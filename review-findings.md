### Who held a duty at the moment of a decision is unrecoverable, so segregation of duties cannot be evidenced
**Raised by:** Audit and compliance
**Where:** `how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md` — People; and `records.md` — the People declaration row
**What is wrong or missing:** Every other record that decides who may do what is versioned and never edited: gate policy is a chained policy version at each owner write, a safeguard is never edited but withdrawn by a second record, so is a constraint, a halt, a legal hold, a role prompt version. The People declaration — which duties each human holds, who gates what, who does UAT, the key-to-name mapping, and the spend ceiling — is one graph record edited in place by the owner, versioned by nothing and appended to no chain, and a write there appends no policy version (that row is written "at each write an owner makes" with Factory as caller, and People is a different screen). A decision's open event names a duty; the close event names an actor key. Whether that actor held that duty then, whether a second holder existed when the self-approval refusal was evaluated, and whether a safeguard's withdrawal was in fact routed away from its author, are all read against a declaration that has since been overwritten. `what-humans-do.md` records a narrower residue — that nobody separately approves the assignment write — and stops short of noticing that the assignment leaves no history at all.
**What turns on it:** Segregation of duties is the claim an auditor tests, and it is tested by reconstructing authorization as of the transaction, not as of the audit. Here the trail proves a key closed a row and cannot prove the key was ever entitled to. The same gap swallows the spend ceiling and the per-model cost rate, authored values with no version row, which contradicts _What the customer runs it under_'s claim that "every such value was either authored by the customer (8) or supplied, and the policy version and score version every decision names say which."
**Migration:** A declaration version chained in the log at each People write, named by every decision the way the policy version is, cannot be attached to decisions already written — a decision read back against today's roster is not the decision that was made, which is the document's own reason for versioning policy.
**Also reached separately by:** Security engineering — "The People declaration decides segregation of duties and is the one owner-written record that is neither versioned nor chained".

### The People declaration decides segregation of duties and is the one owner-written record that is neither versioned nor chained
**Raised by:** Security engineering
**Where:** `how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md` — _People_; `records.md` — the `People declaration` row
**What is wrong or missing:** Gate policy writes append a chained policy version, for the reason the document states — "a decision names them by identifier and nothing else". The duty roster gets no such treatment: it is written at People, it is edited in place, and the log's eight shapes do not include it. Yet the gate component's self-approval refusal fires only "wherever another holder of the row's duty exists", a safeguard's rows route to "the duty or the named human", and pages widen to the owner where no holder is recorded — all reads of that mutable record at decision time. `what-humans-do.md` names the exposure ("no rule reads the write that decided who holds a row's duty. The party who assigns every approver is not separated from approving") and answers it with a sentence rather than a mechanism.
**What turns on it:** The only segregation-of-duties control in the factory can be defeated without leaving a trace: remove the second holder of a duty at People, close your own row (the close event then carries a self-approval field that reads as the ordinary one-owner install), restore the holder. An auditor reading a decision back resolves its actor through today's mapping and has no way to ask what the roster said when the row closed, which is precisely the failure _Traceability_ exists to rule out.
**Migration:** A chained row per People write — duty assignment by opaque key, leaving the key-to-name mapping outside the chain so erasure still works — cannot be backfilled, and every decision closed before it exists stays unreadable against the roster in force at its close.
**Also reached separately by:** Audit and compliance — "Who held a duty at the moment of a decision is unrecoverable, so segregation of duties cannot be evidenced".
