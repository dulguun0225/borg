# What the factory does

A **fully autonomous software factory and operations**: it refines intent, produces the software, deploys it, monitors it, finds issues, and fixes bugs, on its own. It also runs itself: it decomposes [intent](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) into [items](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md), dispatches its own [agents](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) onto them, [gates](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) its own output against a [score](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score/README.md) it learns, revises [what those agents are told](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md) from what its own decisions record, and escalates what it cannot finish. It does not compose its own [fleet](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet/README.md): what an agent is told is the factory's, what it runs on is a human's — the sharpest division in the document. The factory is a product — each customer runs an isolated, self-hosted install. There is no tenancy: no shared install, no multi-tenant model, nothing about one customer's data another's factory could reach. The absence is a decision, not a gap left to fill. What it buys is that the [one graph](#tight-integration) of records — every record, every end-user report, every line an agent read — is one customer's, reachable by no other customer and by nobody hosting a shared install, the condition a regulated customer installs under at all. What it costs is that nothing learned in one install reaches another: the [per-author prior](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) an install narrows over months is that install's alone, and a provider retiring a model version destroys it in every install at once with nothing to amortize it across them. The one channel is the product itself: the [role prompts](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md), [evaluation sets, and starting priors](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet/02-a-model-under-a-name.md) it ships carry what [the builder](#what-the-customer-runs-it-under) measured on the builder's own work, never what a customer's factory learned.

## Tight integration

**Tight integration is the first of the two properties the product is built around.** One system, not a bundle of tools with connectors between them. Intent, item, [spec](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec.md), [build](how-humans-do-it/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md), [gate decision](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md), deploy, [incident](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/06-incidents.md), and score are records in one graph, linked to each other.

Because they are linked, a question a connector answers by matching records across systems is answered here by following links: which services consume a contract, so [what a change breaks](how-humans-do-it/07-contracts/04-enforcement.md) is a query rather than an estimate; which items came from one request, so [work that spans services](how-humans-do-it/07-contracts/10-work-that-spans-services.md) needs no record type of its own; which release each service is running, so a candidate's environment is composed from the [current releases](how-humans-do-it/06-releases/05-the-deploy-record.md) of its dependencies.

A bundle of tools can report all of that. What one graph does is act on it, with no human copying a fact from one system to another: what a consumer assumes is [derived from its build](how-humans-do-it/07-contracts/06-what-a-consumer-declares.md) rather than entered by hand, [the health monitor](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) finding a problem [after its window closed](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/04-after-the-analysis-window.md) writes an intent at the start of [the pipeline](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md), and the score learns from the same log that serves as [the audit trail](deferred.md).

**One record has one writer, and a boundary between two is declared.** Every record in the graph has one **writer** — the single component that writes it — and no second component writes it. Where two genuinely have to reach one record — an owner and a component, or two components — it is split by field or by event, and that split is a [seam](deferred.md) stated in the section that owns the record. The rule keeps a fact single-valued: a record two components wrote can disagree with itself, and every check downstream reads whichever half it happened to read. What it costs is a record where a field would have done — a subject two writers reach becomes a record of its own with a query behind it, which [_A safeguard is a record_](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) pays and states. Sections below invoke this rule rather than re-deriving it.

The downside is that one graph is one trust domain. Almost every check the factory makes reads a record the factory wrote, so a wrong record is read as true by every check downstream of it. [_One pipeline_](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) accepts the same exposure for the path — a defect in it reaches every item that goes through — and one graph is that exposure over the records. The one exception is [_Drift detection_](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/08-drift-detection.md), which compares two of those records against what the factory cannot rewrite: the production [deploy record](how-humans-do-it/06-releases/05-the-deploy-record.md) against what is actually running, and [the log](deferred.md)'s chain — each record naming its predecessor — against the head it recorded on its last pass.

## Traceability

**Traceability is the second**, the testable form of the first: every artifact links back to the intent that caused it and forward to what it produced, under the [policy](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy/README.md) and score in force at the time.

Testable is what keeps it from being a discipline anyone has to maintain: it holds where a question is answered by following links, and fails where an answer has to be reconstructed. Two records supply those links — the [intent](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) every item points back to, and the [release record](how-humans-do-it/06-releases/02-the-release-record.md) linking the item, the build, the gate decisions, the [contract versions](how-humans-do-it/06-releases/03-what-a-build-is-called-and-when.md), and every deploy. An [incident](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/06-incidents.md) is the same links followed from the other end.

_In force at the time_ is the harder part, because policy and score both change: an owner re-authors [gate policy](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy/README.md), and the score is [learned from every outcome that arrives](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md). A decision read back against today's values is not the decision that was made, so each decision record names the policy version and the [score version](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) it was decided under, next to [the actor](deferred.md) and for the same reason — no such field can be added to a history written without it. Both versions are rows of the [log](deferred.md) the decision is a row of, chained like it, so a version rewritten after the fact breaks the chain rather than silently moving what every decision naming it is read against.

What traceability rules out is any action that skips a record. A human who wants different code [authors it upstream](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) rather than patching one at a gate, an emergency is [approve now rather than skip](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md), and undoing something already shipped is a [rollback](how-humans-do-it/06-releases/06-rollback.md) while its [control](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) is still running or a [revert item](how-humans-do-it/06-releases/06-rollback.md) after. Every shortcut is an item — that is what keeps the links unbroken.

## One change, end to end

The claim above is checkable only against a change followed the whole way, so here is one. An end user of the customer's product types a [report](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) saying a screen is slow. A grouper — the component that reads the [report store](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) and decides which reports are one problem — finds four more saying the same thing and calls [intake](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) once for the group; intake writes one intent whose statement summarizes them and points at all five. The intent is unrefined, so [_the interview_](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md) asks the owner what "slow" is worth fixing to, each question a record and each round counted against [the attempt limit](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) (8). The answer arrives, [decomposition](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) proposes two items — one per service — and the [Decomposition gate](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/01-decomposition.md) fires over the set, the score reading the [factors](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) it can compute from a set with no build yet and putting a human at it or not.

Follow one item. [Dispatch](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) matches the [stage](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) the item is at against a [fleet entry](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet/README.md) and puts a model in a [role](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) on it. The spec stage authors [acceptance criteria](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec.md) in the six patterns, and the [Spec gate](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec.md) is where a human confirms them (6) if the score is above the [risk threshold](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy/01-what-is-in-it.md) an owner authored. Everything below leans on one step earlier that is not score-gated: the reading of the intent the requester confirmed at [the interview's last round](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md), which the criteria were authored from. Plan and tasks follow, each an artifact, each a gate. Implementation authors the code, the encodings of those criteria — the code that decides each criterion against an observed run — and the [consumer contracts](how-humans-do-it/07-contracts/06-what-a-consumer-declares.md) the factory derives from the build; the [Implementation gate](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation.md) decides over the item's first build, and the score computes its change factors from that build's diff against [master](how-humans-do-it/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md).

The candidate deploys to [an environment of its own](how-humans-do-it/05-environments/02-an-environment-per-candidate.md), composed from the [current release](how-humans-do-it/06-releases/05-the-deploy-record.md) of every service it depends on. The encodings run there and decide each criterion; a criterion that comes back both ways is [undecided](how-humans-do-it/05-environments/04-what-the-candidate-environment-decides.md) and rejects the way a failure does, where the disagreement appeared. [The merge queue](how-humans-do-it/05-environments/03-the-merge-queue.md) re-verifies the candidate against the master it will actually merge into, and the [Merge to master gate](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/07-merge-to-master.md) reads that run — the criteria, the consumer contracts of every service consuming what this one publishes, and the [UAT](what-humans-do.md) assignment (7) where a human holds one. The queue fast-forwards, writes the [release record](how-humans-do-it/06-releases/02-the-release-record.md), and mints [the number](how-humans-do-it/06-releases/04-the-release-number.md) with it.

[Deploy to production](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/08-deploy-to-production.md) is the last gate and has no Reject: the number already exists, so the actions are approve and hold. The [deployer](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) performs the deploy on the [strategy](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/02-the-rollout-strategy.md) the score picked, starts a [control](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) if that strategy keeps one, and writes the deploy record. The [health monitor](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) opens an [analysis window](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) over the deploy and compares the release against the control until the window closes at one of its four [exits](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md). Say it closes passed, the exit meaning the comparison ruled out a regression of the size worth catching: the control is torn down, the [per-author prior](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) of the model that wrote the change narrows, and the window's own parameters move on what it took to reach that exit.

Now read it backwards — the test. From the [incident](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/06-incidents.md) that would have been written had the comparison crossed its boundary, to the deploy, to the release, to the item, to the intent, to the five reports one stranger's sentence started; and from any gate decision on the way, to the [policy version](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) and score version it was decided under and the [vector of factors](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) the human argued with. Every step is a link that already exists because some component had to write it to do its own job. Nothing in that traversal is reconstructed, and nothing in it was written to make traceability true.

## What the factory does not build

The two properties above say what the factory is; this says what it is made of, and the record divides the two halves. Where a thing writes or keeps a record the factory has to be the only writer of, it is the factory's and this document describes it. Where a thing stores bytes, runs a process, moves traffic, or counts requests, it comes from outside and the factory reaches it. So nothing here reimplements a repository, a container runtime, a scheduler, or a store of numbers, and nothing outside decides anything the factory records.

| What comes from outside | What the factory does with it |
|---|---|
| **git** — one repository per [service](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md), and the transport an agent clones and pushes through | Names the repository on the [service record](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md), commits a candidate branch into it, and fast-forwards [master](how-humans-do-it/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) at [the merge queue](how-humans-do-it/05-environments/03-the-merge-queue.md) |
| Somewhere to run a build or a test — a container runtime, or the host itself | Calls [the deployer](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) to build, and decides a [criterion](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec.md) by running its encoding |
| Whatever runs instances on a [deploy target](how-humans-do-it/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) — a scheduler, or a host | Reaches it only through the named operations of seam 4 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](deferred.md), which is where policy attaches later |
| Whatever decides what fraction of arriving traffic reaches each of two builds | Picks [a rollout strategy](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/02-the-rollout-strategy.md) the platform can perform, every deploy going [without a control](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/02-the-rollout-strategy.md) where none can |
| Wherever the numbers [the health monitor](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) reads are kept | Instruments the software it writes so that it emits them, and computes the comparison over them |
| Wherever a build stays reachable after it was made | Names it on the release record, and how long one stays reachable is [_What survives a lost disk or a factory upgrade?_](open.md) |
| A store of published [advisories](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/03-advisories.md) against third-party packages, one per registry the services resolve from | Reads it on a pass of its own and matches each entry against what every service's current release resolved, raising an intent per advisory that matches |
| The models an agent runs on, the [effort](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet/01-what-an-agent-runs-on.md) each runs at, and the credential each is reached with | Reads a [fleet](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet/README.md) entry an owner wrote, and writes none back — where [what the agent is told](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md) is a record on this side of the division, written by the factory alone |

Everything on the factory's side of that division is what the rest of this document describes: the graph and the [append-only decision log](deferred.md), [dispatch](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md), the [gate component](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md), the queue's ordering and [the release number](how-humans-do-it/06-releases/04-the-release-number.md), the score, [the analysis window](how-humans-do-it/08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md), the drift detector, and the [four screens](how-humans-do-it/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md). No product supplies that set, and every part of it writes a record or decides one.

A forge — one product that is repository host, review tool, pipeline, and registry at once — has parts on both sides of that division, which is why the factory neither adopts one nor rewrites one. Two of its parts are rows above: the repository, and somewhere to run a build. Its other parts are records here — a review is a gate decision with an actor, a policy version, and a score version on it; what may reach master is the queue's exclusivity rather than a rule configured per repository; and what a [stage](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) does is the pipeline. Taking those would put a decision in two stores, which the log refuses for the reason it has one writer, and the two would disagree the first time either was read alone.

What that costs is that everything a forge supplies around a repository is neither built nor bought, with one exception. [_Work_](how-humans-do-it/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) shows an item's artifacts on a timeline, so a human confirming criteria (6) or performing [UAT](what-humans-do.md) (7) reads those and not code. At the one gate whose artifact is the code — [_Implementation_](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation.md) — Work renders the build's diff against master, read from the repository and written nowhere: a diff is bytes the repository already holds, outside the division above, and rendering it is a read like the clone an agent makes. It is built because the alternative was a human deciding over material the product would not show them — approving, or rejecting with feedback they had no basis to write — and the actor field recording a human decision over what no human read. What it costs is one part of a forge built after all. Nothing administers a repository either — creating one and holding write access to it is part of hosting the factory, substrate rather than one of [the twelve duties](what-humans-do.md).

Master's only inbound path being the queue rests on how the repository is administered, not on a record. What refuses another push to a plain repository is who holds write access to it — a forge would refuse one by configured rule instead — and the factory reads no evidence either way. That exclusivity is what makes what was verified ship, and it is half of what makes a release record exist for every commit on master: it keeps every other writer out, and [the queue completing its own unfinished merge](how-humans-do-it/05-environments/03-the-merge-queue.md) on start covers the writer it cannot keep out, itself stopped between the fast-forward and the record write. On a repository where humans can also push, the exclusivity holds by convention and nothing in the factory can tell.

Where a service runs also differs per install, so two installs of one product do not measure alike. [_The rollout strategy_](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/02-the-rollout-strategy.md) states one half of that already — which strategies a service can take is bounded by where it runs — and the health monitor is the other half: numbers reaching no store the factory can read leave a pipeline that ships changes and measures none of them.

## What the customer runs it under

The factory is a product a customer installs and runs, so the terms it runs under are stated here rather than left to a milestone. **The licence** is one file, `LICENSE` at the root of the repository the product is built in, saying under what terms a customer may run, change, and redistribute it. The design states none of those terms — a licence is decided in that file and nowhere else — and does not restate what the licence allocates: warranty and liability between the customer and whoever builds the product — **the builder** from here on — are the licence's terms too.

What the design owes that allocation is the fact a dispute over shipped software turns on: whose decision each value the factory decided under was. Every such value was either authored by the customer (8) or supplied, and the [policy version](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) and [score version](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) every decision names say which — the authored one carries who wrote it, the supplied one is the score's, and both are chained rows of the [log](deferred.md), so neither can be rewritten after the fact to move a value from one side to the other. The same division runs through the code itself. The model that wrote a change, the [effort](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet/01-what-an-agent-runs-on.md) it ran at, and the credential it ran through are the customer's, written onto a [fleet entry](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet/README.md) by the customer and never by the factory; [what the agent was told](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md) is the builder's in its first version and the factory's in every version after, each decided at [a gate of its own](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/09-a-role-prompt-or-a-skill.md); and the decision that shipped the change names its [actor](deferred.md), a human or the score. So each fact a liability question asks for — who chose the author, who wrote its instructions, who approved the change, under whose value — is on a record with one writer, read from the log rather than argued.

What the design does not decide is what a jurisdiction makes of that division — whether the builder of a product that ships software is the producer of the software it ships is a question of law, answered against the licence and per jurisdiction, by counsel rather than by a design document. What it costs is that the records answer whose decision a value was and never whether that decision was reasonable: a threshold the customer never authored is the score's on every row, and a customer who left every value supplied has a log that says so.
