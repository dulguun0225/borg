# What the factory does

A **fully autonomous software factory and operations**: it refines intent, produces the software, deploys it, monitors it, finds issues, and fixes bugs, on its own. It also runs itself: it decomposes [intent](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#intake) into [items](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md), dispatches its own [agents](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) onto them, [gates](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it) its own output against a [score](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md) it learns, and escalates what it cannot finish. The factory is a product — each customer runs their own isolated, self-hosted setup. There is no tenancy: no shared install, no multi-tenant model, and nothing about one customer's data that another's factory could reach. The absence is a decision, not a gap left to fill.

## Tight integration

**Tight integration is the first of the two properties the product is built around.** One system, not a bundle of tools with connectors between them. Intent, item, [spec](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#spec), [build](how-humans-do-it/05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch), [gate decision](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it), deploy, [incident](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#incidents), and score are records in one graph, linked to each other.

Because they are linked, a question that connectors answer by matching records across systems is answered here by following links. Which services consume a contract, so [what a change breaks](how-humans-do-it/07-contracts.md#enforcement) is a query rather than an estimate. Which items came from one request, so [work that spans services](how-humans-do-it/07-contracts.md#work-that-spans-services) needs no record type of its own. Which release each service is running, so a candidate's environment is composed from the [current releases](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#the-deploy-record) of its dependencies.

A bundle of tools can report all of that. What one graph does is act on it, with no human copying a fact from one system into another: what a consumer assumes is [derived from its build](how-humans-do-it/07-contracts.md#what-a-consumer-declares) rather than entered by hand, a comparison that finds a problem [after its window closed](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#after-the-watch-window) writes an intent at the start of [the pipeline](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md), and the score learns from the same log that serves as [the audit trail](deferred.md).

The downside is that one graph is one trust domain. Almost every check the factory makes reads a record the factory wrote, so a record that is wrong is read as true by every check downstream of it. [_One pipeline_](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) accepts the same exposure for the path — a defect in it reaches every item that goes through — and one graph is that exposure over the records. The one exception is [_The reconciler_](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-reconciler), which reads what is actually running rather than what was recorded, and compares the two.

## Traceability

**Traceability is the second**, and is the testable form of the first: every artifact links back to the intent that caused it and forward to what it produced, under the [policy](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md) and score that were in force at the time.

Testable is what keeps it from being a discipline anyone has to keep: it holds where a question is answered by following links, and fails where an answer has to be reconstructed. Two records supply those links — the [intent](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#intake) every item points back to, and the [release record](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#the-release-record) linking the item, the build, the gate decisions, the [contract versions](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#what-a-build-is-called-and-when), and every deploy. An [incident](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#incidents) is the same set of links followed from the other end.

_In force at the time_ is the harder part, because policy and score both change: an owner re-authors [gate policy](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md), and the score is [learned from every outcome that arrives](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md#how-it-learns). A decision read back against today's values is not the decision that was made, so each decision record names the policy version and the [score version](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) it was decided under, next to [the actor](deferred.md) and for the same reason — no such field can be added to a history written without it.

What traceability rules out is any action that skips a record. A human who wants different code [authors it upstream](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#what-a-gate-may-change) rather than patching one at a gate, an emergency is [approve now rather than skip](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md), and undoing something already shipped is a [rollback](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#rollback) while its [control](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-signal) is still running or a [revert item](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#rollback) after. Every shortcut is an item, and that is what keeps the links unbroken.

## What the factory does not build

The two properties above say what the factory is; this says what it is made of, and what divides the two halves is the record. Where a thing writes or keeps a record the factory has to be the only writer of, it is the factory's and this document describes it. Where a thing stores bytes, runs a process, moves traffic, or counts requests, it comes from outside and the factory reaches it. So nothing here reimplements a repository, a container runtime, a scheduler, or a store of numbers, and nothing outside decides anything the factory records.

| What comes from outside | What the factory does with it |
|---|---|
| **git** — one repository per [service](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#the-cut), and the transport an agent clones and pushes through | Names the repository on the [service record](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#the-cut), commits a candidate branch into it, and fast-forwards [master](how-humans-do-it/05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch) at [the merge queue](how-humans-do-it/05-environments.md#the-merge-queue) |
| Somewhere to run a build or a test — a container runtime, or the host itself | Calls [the deploy agent](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-signal) to build, and decides a [criterion](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#spec) by running its encoding |
| Whatever runs instances on a [deploy target](how-humans-do-it/05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch) — a scheduler, or a host | Reaches it only through the named operations of seam 4 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](deferred.md), which is where policy attaches later |
| Whatever decides what fraction of arriving traffic reaches each of two builds | Picks [a rollout strategy](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy) the target can perform, every deploy being [straight](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy) where none can |
| Wherever the numbers [the health signal](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-signal)'s comparison reads are kept | Instruments the software it writes so that it emits them, and computes the signal over them |
| Wherever a build stays reachable after it was made | Names it on the release record, and how long one stays reachable is [_What survives a lost disk or a factory upgrade?_](open.md) |
| The models an agent runs on, and the credential each is reached with | Reads a [fleet](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md) entry an owner wrote, and writes none back |

Everything on the factory's side of that division is what the rest of this document describes: the graph and the [append-only decision log](deferred.md), [dispatch](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#dispatch), the [gate component](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md), the queue's ordering and [the number](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#the-number), the score, [the watch window](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-watch-window), the reconciler, and the [four surfaces](how-humans-do-it/11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people). No product supplies that set, and every part of it writes a record or decides one.

A forge — one product that is repository host, review tool, pipeline, and registry at once — has parts on both sides of that division, which is why the factory neither adopts one nor rewrites one. Two of its parts are rows above: the repository, and somewhere to run a build. Its other parts are records here — a review is a gate decision with an actor, a policy version, and a score version on it; what may reach master is the queue's exclusivity rather than a rule configured per repository; and what a [stage](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) does is the pipeline. Taking those would put a decision in two stores, which that log refuses for the reason it has one writer, and the two would disagree the first time either was read alone.

What that costs is that everything a forge supplies around a repository is neither built nor bought. Nothing renders code: [_Work_](how-humans-do-it/11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people) shows an item's artifacts on a timeline, so a human confirming criteria (6) or performing [UAT](what-humans-do.md) (7) reads those rather than the diff view a forge would have had. Nothing administers a repository either — creating one and holding write access to it is part of hosting the factory, which is substrate rather than one of [the twelve duties](what-humans-do.md).

Master's only inbound path being the queue then rests on the substrate and not on a record. What refuses another push to a plain repository is who holds write access to it, where a forge would refuse one by configured rule, and the factory reads no evidence either way. That exclusivity is what makes a release record exist for every commit on master and makes what was verified be what ships, so on a repository where humans can also push, both hold by convention and nothing in the factory can tell.

The substrate also differs per install, so two installs of one product do not measure alike. [_The rollout strategy_](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy) states one half of that already — which strategies a service can take is bounded by where it runs — and the comparison itself is the other half: numbers that reach no store the factory can read leave a pipeline that ships changes and measures none of them.
