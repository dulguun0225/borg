# No single item may break a contract

**No single item may break a contract.** A breaking change is four items: the producer adds the new **form** — what the interface's build declares, its fields, operations or messages and their types — beside the old, each consumer migrates, the producer runs a [brownout](08-deprecation.md) of the old form, and the producer removes it. Each ships alone, is verified on an environment of its own, and is independently reversible. This is the same discipline [no-batching](../06-releases/01-one-item-per-release.md) already forces — an item that cannot ship by itself was [decomposed](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) wrong — and the two rules support each other. Where a change genuinely cannot be decomposed, that is an [escalation](../03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) (12), not permission to batch — and taking it over gives a human no power the factory lacks, since a human authoring a [stage](../01-one-pipeline.md) instead of an [agent](../01-one-pipeline.md) changes the author and not the stages, the gates, or this rule. What the escalation asks for is a re-decomposition, not a co-deploy built by hand — and a stage that finds it [names decomposition](../03-gates/06-going-back-up.md) rather than escalating, a re-decomposition being something the factory performs on an intent in progress and not only a thing to ask a human for.

The four items are also where the version moves, and it moves twice: the addition is a minor, each consumer's migration leaves it where it is — so does the brownout, which changes behaviour and not the form — and the removal is the major. Inside the factory that major warns nobody — the removal was raised only after the list [_Deprecation_](08-deprecation.md) keeps had emptied and the brownout had run uncrossed, so every consumer the factory can read had already migrated — and what it records is that the old form is gone. Its promise is kept for the consumer the factory cannot read, which is the one case where major means what semver says it does.
