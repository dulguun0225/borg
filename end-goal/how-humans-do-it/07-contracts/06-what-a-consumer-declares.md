# What a consumer declares

**A consumer contract is a predicate, not prose.** It is contract testing's consumer-driven contract — what a consumer assumes of a producer, as against the [contract](01-two-versioned-things.md) the producer publishes — and it has to be decidable by the factory against one observed exchange, and it is drawn from the list of allowed predicate kinds the factory owns: the field is read at all, it arrives populated, it has this unit, its values stay inside this domain or this range. The list grows with the rest of [gate policy](../09-gate-policy/README.md) (8); a consumer picks from it and cannot invent a kind of assertion when one is derived. An assumption that will not fit a predicate is not in a consumer contract at all — it stays the consumer's own acceptance criteria, and an item against the producer if it is ever violated.

**It is derived from the consumer's build, not entered by hand.** A **consumer contract** is a record and an artifact of the consumer's item, written through the [artifact store](../01-one-pipeline.md) at the [implementation stage](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation.md) from that item's build — by whoever authors the stage, an agent or a human backstopping it, and derived by the factory either way rather than typed. It has no field naming the release it entered force at: no release exists when it is written, and the merge queue writing one later would be a second writer. Which release a consumer contract entered force at is reached through the release record naming the same item — the release names the merge queue's [re-verification](../05-environments/03-the-merge-queue.md) build where the consumer contract names the implementation stage's, and [_One item per release_](../06-releases/01-one-item-per-release.md) keeps that join single-valued — so a consumer contract derived for a candidate that never merges is filtered by there being no release naming its item. A service derives one against its own store contract exactly as against another service's interface, which is what makes the store's consumer — the service's own past — readable by the same query and lets a deprecated column's list empty. The factory reads the consumer and derives what it assumes, again at every release, so a consumer contract cannot outlive the code that motivated it.

Which consumer contracts bind at a moment is a query: for one service, those derived from every release from its [_last known-good release_](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) — what a rollback could return to at all — up to its newest release. A service none of whose windows has closed [passed](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) or timed out has no last known-good release, and the range is then every release it has, which is the direction a first release's missing [rollback target](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) already takes. One range answers what runs now, what has merged and will run, and what a rollback can still restore, and it is the same query for an interface and for a store. The cost is that a removal waits until every consumer's migration release is that consumer's last known-good release, so on a quiet service the last step of a migration waits out the window's [cap](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md), and that the range is as wide as the windows a service has open and wider wherever a release has merged and not deployed, so a producer is held to the assumptions of releases that are not running. A consumer that stops reading a field stops declaring it: the list [_Deprecation_](08-deprecation.md) waits on shortens itself, and its emptying raises the brownout alone — the removal follows only on the brownout's own evidence, the way [_Deprecation_](08-deprecation.md) sets out — with nobody having to remember either. A consumer with no items for a year declares what its last release read, which is right — its code has not changed, so neither have its assumptions.

**Its authority comes from the gate, not from the derivation being right.** A derived consumer contract is an artifact of the consumer's item, ratified at the gates that item already passes — factory-authored, scored like anything else, read by a human where a safeguard (9) puts one there. Ratified that way it can reject a producer's candidate; before that it rejects nothing, which is true of every artifact here. A rejection later found wrong is a bad decision the score learns from, like a change a human undid after it shipped (10).

**Two baselines, kept apart.** A producer's own diff runs against the contract version its current release publishes, because the promise is to what is running. A consumer contract is checked against the contract version its producer's newest release publishes, because that is what the consumer will meet. Neither is written down and neither produces a record: both are computed at the moment the gate fires, and again inside the merge queue at re-verification, which rejects on the same terms with itself as the actor. So the race between the two resolves either way round it happens: a consumer that newly declares a field the producer is part-way through removing fails at its own gate, or the producer's removal candidate fails at its. What that costs: a candidate which waited in the queue can fail on a baseline that moved after its own run passed, with no record of the earlier pass to point at. One gap is left rather than caught: a candidate whose form breaks against a merged but undeployed release of its own service passes the production diff, and minting its version against the newest one records the break as a major without rejecting anything — what catches it, where anything does, is a consumer contract naming the **element** — one field, one operation, or one message of the form.

**Where derivation cannot see, an owner supplies the predicate with a safeguard.** How much of a consumer's reading is visible is a property of that interface's toolchain rather than of the factory — a generated stub turns a field read into an accessor call, hand-parsed access hides it. A read the derivation misses is an unprotected assumption; one it invents holds a field until the next derivation drops it or the producer's blocked removal item asks the consumer to confirm. A safeguard is authored and maintained by an owner, so the blind case does not go unnoticed. Deriving at all assumes the consumer's build is the factory's to read, which [_Two versioned things_](01-two-versioned-things.md) settles: a consumer outside the factory is blind for a harder reason — no build to read at all — so declared meaning and the producer's own observables are all that cover it, with a safeguard's predicate replacing the consumer contract.

**A derivation that could not run records that it could not, and the record resolves toward the human.** The derivation is per toolchain, so an extractor is a component of the factory, shipped with it, and which toolchains have one is published as a fact of the factory's version — readable before a service is adopted rather than discovered at its first removal. Where no extractor covers a consumer's build, or extraction fails outright, the outcome is **could not derive**: a record, not an empty list, because "no consumer reads this" and "no consumer's read was visible" call for opposite responses and a record that cannot tell them apart licenses the wrong one silently. What the record does is nothing new. The [context factor](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) reads which services consume what a candidate publishes; a consumer nobody could read makes that unknowable rather than zero, and [a factor the score cannot compute](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) already resolves to the value that puts a human at the gate, the [vector the decision records](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) naming the consumer nobody could read. Nothing bounds what an unreadable consumer consumes, so that reaches every candidate touching a published contract in the install, and a marked element's [deprecation list](08-deprecation.md) can no longer be known empty, so no removal ships mechanically while the record stands. The cost is deliberate: one unsupported toolchain puts a human at contract-touching gates across the whole factory, which is standing, visible pressure to add the extractor or retire the service — where the silent reading, a consumer that could not be read scored as one that assumes nothing, made the changes most likely to break it the ones most likely to auto-pass.
