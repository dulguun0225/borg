# Intent into items

How what arrives becomes the [items](01-one-pipeline.md) the factory builds, and everything that happens to it on the way.

## Intake

What arrives is an **intent**: a request from an owner (1), the end-user [_reports_](#reports) a grouper turned into one (4, 5), or something the factory found itself, raised by a **detector** — the [health monitor](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) still running after its [window](08-operations.md#the-analysis-window) closed, a [consumer](07-contracts.md#two-versioned-things) breaking in its own error rate, a permanent [constraint](#constraints-and-the-design-system) in force with shipped services inside its reach and no intent on its record, an [_advisory_](#advisories) naming a package version a shipped build resolved. One record, three sources, and one writer — intake writes every intent, and the three sources are three callers of one entrance rather than three components writing the same record. What it writes at that event is the source, the actor (seam 1 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../deferred.md)), whatever constraint arrived with the request and binds only what is decomposed from it, and for an intent the factory raised, a link to the evidence that raised it. An intent arrives unrefined, and nothing is judged on the way in, because judging it is what the interview is for.

It also writes the [project](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) the request came in under, where a source supplies one — the project an owner raised it under, the project of the [service](#decomposition) a grouped report came from, or the project named in a detector's evidence. That field has one job: it is where [_Decomposition_](#decomposition) places a service the work creates, there being no existing service to reach a project through. It is not a claim about where the work is, because a [contract migration](07-contracts.md#no-single-item-may-break-a-contract)'s decomposed items may be in two projects, and it is not what scopes the constraints [_The interview_](#the-interview) reads. Where no source supplies one the field stays unwritten, and intake fills it once if decomposition has to place a created service and nothing else answers; it is never rewritten, so an approval keeps pointing at what was approved. Putting the project on the intent and not on the item is not a contradiction of [_What an item names_](#what-an-item-names): an item names a service and reaches a project through it, and an intent names no service.

One writer costs a dependency edge from the far end of [the pipeline](01-one-pipeline.md) back to its start: [operations](08-operations.md) calls intake to raise what it found. What several writers would cost is one record's rules — the actor always populated, [the chain](../deferred.md), the links — implemented once per writer and drifting apart, on the record every other record links back to.

The intent is what [the graph](../what-the-factory-does.md#tight-integration) joins on. Every item links back to it, which is why one request producing four items in four services needs no record type of its own — the same point [_Work that spans services_](07-contracts.md#work-that-spans-services) makes from the other end.

The factory's own source repeats where the other two do not: a health monitor still running is still running tomorrow, and a consumer breaking in its own error rate goes on breaking. So a detector raises an intent for a condition and not for an observation — before raising one it looks for an intent on the same evidence that has not finished, and attaches to that one instead. The evidence is what keys it: this service, this consumer, this [contract](07-contracts.md#two-versioned-things), this [release](06-releases.md#the-release-record), this constraint — and for [_Deprecation_](07-contracts.md#deprecation)'s raises, the element, two marked elements of one contract being two removals. Keyed too finely it raises an intent per observation; too coarsely it attaches two problems to one intent, which decomposition then has to split.

### Constraints and the design system

Constraints (2) arrive here too and are not intents: they are never decomposed into items and never ship. One record with a reach field rather than two record types — a constraint names what it binds, and the four values it may take are the ones duty 2 sets out in [_What humans do_](../what-humans-do.md), where the three widest make a constraint permanent and one intent is the narrowest, a constraint arriving with a single request and binding every item decomposed from it. Intake writes it at arrival whichever [screen](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) an owner supplied it on, because the constraint's own rules — the actor, the chain — are implemented once rather than once per entrance, and a second write withdraws it. A permanent one is withdrawn at [_Factory_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), which lists the constraints in force by reach, so ending one is done from that list rather than by finding the request it arrived with; one whose reach is a single intent is read and withdrawn at [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), on the intent it arrived with. It is never edited: correcting one is a withdrawal and a second record, so what was in force when a [stage](01-one-pipeline.md) was authored is reconstructible from arrival and withdrawal alone.

What the reach decides is what the factory reads when it drafts, and drafting — a stage authoring one of an item's [artifacts](01-one-pipeline.md) — is per item: the constraints in force over the factory, over the item's service's project, over the item's area or any area above it, plus whatever arrived with this intent. The interview reads less than that, and deliberately — the factory's own and the intent's own, and not the project's or the area's, because before decomposition which project the work is in is a guess and an intent's items may not share one.

A constraint's other field is its **kind**, and it decides which [gate](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it) can reject on one. A build the factory can diff is decided against the implementation and rejects at [_Implementation_](03-gates.md#implementation). A licence policy is of that kind and not a document: the [build](05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch) names every package it resolved and the licence each declares, so a constraint over what may be depended on is diffed against that set and rejects there. A predicate the factory can decide against an observed run is decided on [_the candidate environment_](05-environments.md#what-the-candidate-environment-decides) beside the [acceptance criteria](03-gates.md#spec), and rejects at the [Merge to master](03-gates.md#merge-to-master) gate on the same terms — rejecting on what the run shows, one the run never exercised reading as nothing, the reading and its cost stated where the run is decided. A document is decided by nobody, and what enforces it is a [safeguard](09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them) (9) putting a human at a gate. The cost is that an owner who supplies a predicate learns of a breach one gate later than one who supplies a build, and everything spent between the two is thrown away.

A law is a constraint whose reach is the factory, and three things about a law the reach and the kind do not carry, so a constraint carries three more fields, each optional and each read by something. A constraint may name the date it binds from: drafting reads it as in force from that date and not from its arrival, and the conformance raise below runs at that date, so a regulation phased in next year is supplied now and reaches shipped services then. It may name a date it is to be reviewed by: past that date, with no withdrawal and no replacement naming it, it is a row in [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) for whoever holds (2), delivered by mail and chat and never a page, nothing deployed being worse for a constraint nobody re-read — and the constraint goes on binding, a review date ending nothing on its own, because a law that lapsed in the owner's diary has not lapsed. And it may bind the factory's own conduct rather than its items: a period, and what starts it. Most of what a law asks of a customer is conduct with a deadline — a breach reported within so many hours, an erasure performed within so many days — and conduct has no drafting moment for a constraint to be read at, so the constraint states the period and a trigger, and what the trigger reads is a record: an [advisory](#advisories) matching a shipped build, an [incident](08-operations.md#incidents) on a service in the constraint's reach, or a human marking an intent at Work as meeting the trigger — the human being the only reader of a report's prose the design admits, the reports floor at [Spec](03-gates.md#spec) already putting one there. The deadline is a field of the intent, written by intake when the trigger occurs — the trigger's own time plus the period, the time of a report's arrival and not of the mark, because the clock a law starts started then — and it is the one field in the factory computed from a calendar, the [page hours](08-operations.md#pages) being authored and read against one but computing nothing. What a deadline does is two things and no third. It pages whoever holds (2) for that constraint at the moment it is written, which [_Pages_](08-operations.md#pages) admits as the one condition outside its own — the software is no worse, the customer is, for every hour of the wait — and the page is answered when the intent ends or the human marks the obligation met. And Work shows the deadline on the intent and every item under it, so a human ordering a queue has it to order by. It moves no priority itself, gates nothing, and teaches the score nothing: an obligation is the owner's to meet and the factory's to make visible. What it costs is a calendar read by one field, three fields most constraints leave empty, a page that reaches a human for an obligation the factory cannot itself discharge, and a trigger that is a human's mark for the case that matters most — a breach reported by an end user is a sentence a human reads, and a factory whose grouper read it for that would be the classifier [_Reports_](#reports) refuses.

Drafting reads a constraint forward, and arrival is what reaches back. A permanent constraint in force whose record no intent names, with shipped services inside its reach, is a [detector](#intake)'s condition: a detector reads the constraints in force on a pass of its own, the way the [health monitor](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) reads a service's numbers, and calls intake to raise one intent where the condition holds — the evidence is the constraint record, which keys the raise like every detector's, and the condition stands until the intent exists, so a pass that stopped half-done raises nothing twice and finishes on the next one. The condition is deliberately stricter than the unfinished-intent rule [_Intake_](#intake) states for a repeating detector: an intent that ended — delivered, partly delivered, or dropped — is not raised over again, a second raise being the factory reopening work a human ended, and the residue is stated rather than looped on: what a dropped conformance intent left out of conformance stays so until the owner asks again or withdraws the constraint. The constraint is the intent's evidence, so the interview and every stage hold it through that link rather than through the project's list [_The interview_](#the-interview) does not read. Decomposition is what enumerates the reach: an item goes to each service inside it that the work changes, and a service already conforming gets none, which is the floor decomposition already sets. It may also find nothing departing at all — this intent alone can ask for a state that already holds — and then it yields no items: the finding attaches to the intent, which waits at [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) until a human ends it as dropped, the end [_The interview_](#the-interview) gives an intent, deciding nothing was outstanding. What an item can be checked against is what the kind already decides: a build is diffed, a predicate is decided against a run, and a document's items end at a safeguard (9) putting a human at a gate, like the rest of that kind. Withdrawal ends the condition with the record. A replacement names the record it replaces — the pair is one write — and where that record's intent has not finished, the replacement's raise attaches its evidence and sends the intent back the way a [rework request](03-gates.md#going-back-up) naming it does: unrefined again, the interview reopened, decomposition run again against what is now in force and superseding what it replaces — an intent that is escalated or re-decomposing takes only the attachment, an escalation staying a human's to clear and an open Decomposition firing closing first, the send-back landing then — so conformance work in progress is re-aimed at the constraint in force rather than finished against one withdrawn, and never doubled. The narrowest reach needs no raise: a constraint arriving with one intent binds what is decomposed from it, and nothing shipped precedes it. What the raise costs is work the owner did not ask for item by item — a factory-wide constraint on a mature install decomposes into conformance items across every service that departs from it, each holding an environment like any other item and ordinary in everything after: interviewed, decomposed, gated, counted in [cost per feature](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people). And the raise buys enumeration, not proof: an item of that work whose kind nothing mechanical could decide — a document, a predicate its run never exercised — ships on whatever human its gates held, so an intent delivered means the reach was worked through and not that the constraint is met, and a departure that survives is found the way any shipped defect is. Without it a constraint binds forward only, and every service already in production stays out of conformance indefinitely, its criteria unchanged and still passing, with nothing anywhere saying which services are outstanding.

A design system is not a record of its own: it is a constraint whose reach is one project and whose subject is what every screen of the product is built from, and its kind decides everything below. It is the permanent constraint a project with a user interface always has, and it is chosen as much as supplied: the project uses one the factory ships with or one an owner uploads. One the factory ships with is content of the product rather than a record — picking it writes a constraint naming it, the way a [fleet entry](10-fleet.md) names a credential the factory does not hold, and it moves only when the owner upgrades the factory. Moving is a withdrawal and a second record, never an edit under the standing name, and each mover writes its own pair: an owner uploading over the one in force writes it in the upload, and an upgrade that changed the shipped one is detected by the factory at its first start on the new version, which calls intake to write the pair itself — itself, because an upgrade is applied by whoever hosts the install and opens no screen, and a record standing unchanged while the content it names is replaced is exactly the break in reconstructibility that correcting by a second record exists to prevent. The second record is a permanent arrival like any other, so the raise above runs against it and the services whose screens depart from the new version get their conformance items: the migration set the dependency with the widest fan-out in a product with screens needs, the way [_No single item may break a contract_](07-contracts.md#no-single-item-may-break-a-contract) makes a contract migration several items. What is uploaded decides what can ever be checked against it, and a design system spans the three kinds rather than taking one. As code — tokens and components with a build — it is diffable, and what the diff decides is bounded the way [_What a diff cannot see_](07-contracts.md#what-a-diff-cannot-see) bounds a contract's: that a screen is built from the system's components, styled from its tokens, and calls no component against its declared interface. It does not decide that they were used correctly — a dialog nested inside a dialog, a destructive variant on Save, two tokens paired unreadably are all made of the right parts — and the rules for using them, the third thing [_What humans do_](../what-humans-do.md) says a design system is, are a document however the rest arrived: decided by nobody, enforced by a safeguard (9) putting a human at the gate, a residue stated rather than promised away. Between the two runs the predicate kind above, and a screen is where it earns its keep: a contrast floor over the colours a render actually resolves, a name on every control, a focus order, a target size are each decidable against a rendered run of the [candidate](06-releases.md#what-a-build-is-called-and-when) and reject at the [Merge to master](03-gates.md#merge-to-master) gate like any predicate. That is also the part of an accessibility regulation a constraint can carry as something a machine decides; the rest of such a constraint is a document with the [compliance officer](../what-humans-do.md)'s safeguard, like any rule a machine cannot read. As a document entire — an export, a page of rules — there is no build and no predicate, nothing checks the result, and the safeguard is the whole of the enforcement. Nothing about that is particular to design: no permanent constraint is checked mechanically unless it arrives as something a machine can read, which is why the compliance officer holding (2) over a regulated area holds a safeguard with it.

The kind decides what may reject on a constraint once it is inside an artifact, and nothing decides whether it reached one. A constraint is read when a stage drafts and is not itself a criterion, so a [spec](03-gates.md#spec) authored without it has nothing a check can name as missing: its criteria fit the six patterns, the set is complete on its own terms, and what the owner asked for is simply absent. The two kinds a machine can read do not help here either — they reject an implementation or a run, and there is no implementation yet. What catches it is the human confirming the criteria (6) at Spec, holding the request beside what was drafted from it, and in the steady state the [score](04-risk-score.md) auto-passes that gate and nobody holds anything. An owner who wants the check has the answer the design gives everywhere: a safeguard (9). The residue is that a safeguard is drawn on an area and stands until it is removed, where the constraint it was placed for arrived with one intent — so buying the check for one request puts a human in front of every item in that area until someone takes them out.

So a designer's say continues after the gate they decided at, but unevenly. The screen's state machine, confirmed at [_Spec_](03-gates.md#spec) (6), is enforced at [_Implementation_](03-gates.md#implementation); what the screen looks like is checked mechanically as far as the kinds reach — provenance and tokens by the diff where the design system came as code, rendered properties by the predicates supplied — and past that by nobody without a safeguard. The cheap kind requires a human at a safeguarded gate for as long as the safeguard lasts; the enforceable ones require a designer to produce code, or predicates a run can decide.

### Reports

An end user's bug report (4) or complaint (5) is a **report**, and a report is not an intent. The way in is thin and embedded in the deployed software: what it writes is a report, into the **report store**, one of its own sized for what a population of end users sends rather than for what a graph joins on. A **grouper** reads that store and calls intake once per group, so five hundred reports of one slow button are one intent and not five hundred.

The grouper is an [agent](01-one-pipeline.md), because deciding whether two free-text reports are one problem is model work, and model work outside [the fleet](10-fleet.md) would run on words no gate ever decided. It runs under a [fleet entry](10-fleet.md) like any other agent — a model, an [effort](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-runs-on), a credential — in a role put on neither an intent nor an item: its scope is a project, and it reads the reports that arrived under that project and nothing else. Its [role prompt](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told) is versioned and decided at [a gate of its own](03-gates.md#a-role-prompt-or-a-skill), so a safeguard (9) at that gate reaches the words it works from — words [_What a version is authored from_](10-fleet.md#what-a-version-is-authored-from) keeps a report out of. What grouping does not do is move the [per-author prior](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least): no gate decides a grouping and no artifact carries one, so its two failure directions surface one stage down — a group [decomposition](#decomposition) has to split, and duplicates linked as recurrences — where they are repaired rather than scored.

This source alone has a record in front of the intent. An owner's request is one thing because one person typed it, and a factory finding is keyed by its evidence. Only an end user's report arrives in a quantity nobody upstream has collapsed, in words the factory has to read to know whether two of them are one problem.

A report raises an intent on arrival rather than waiting for a batch: the first of a group raises it, and later matching ones attach. Nothing waits for a count, because grouping is deduplication and not triage — one report from one end user is an intent, and the count is evidence on it rather than a rank: nothing orders work by it on its own, though a human reordering a queue can read it. A batch would group better and delay every fix by the length of the interval.

A report is marked with the intent it was grouped into and kept. Deleting one on grouping would cost the measurement this store makes possible — the rate of reports before and after the release meant to fix what they describe — so what removes a report is a retention setting an owner authors, and never the grouping. That setting is a field of the one policy list the factory owns, which [_Gate policy_](09-gate-policy.md#what-is-not-in-it) sets out, and its only reader is the report store — the component that writes a report is the only one that may remove one. Where an owner authors nothing, reports are kept for the life of the install: no outcome teaches a retention period, so there is nothing for the [score](04-risk-score.md) to supply.

Grouping takes no gate. The interview, decomposition, and [_Spec_](03-gates.md#spec) all read what it produced, which is the argument [_Dispatch_](#dispatch) makes for itself. The two ways it goes wrong are not symmetric, so the grouper prefers to group: two problems in one intent are separated one stage down, where decomposition yields an item each or a spec comes back too large, while duplicates that were never grouped are separated by nobody — each takes its own interview, its own [timeline](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), and its own item, several of them on the same code and serialized behind each other in [_The merge queue_](05-environments.md#the-merge-queue).

A later report attaches to the intent and never rewrites it. What refines an intent is attached to it and every reader reads both, so the five hundredth report changes what is built by being read rather than by editing the record — which is what keeps an approval pointing at what was approved. Decomposition is the boundary. Before it, the grouper may still split a group it got wrong; after it, a report matching work already decomposed attaches and raises the count, and one that does not is a new intent, linked to the first as a recurrence. A report arriving after the fix shipped is that same rule: the timeline is finished, so evidence that the fix did not work is a new intent and never a reopening. What the boundary costs is a run of reports just after a narrow decomposition showing as two linked timelines where a human would call it one problem.

For an intent grouped from reports, the reports are what the interview and the spec are authored against and the intent's own **statement** — the field holding what was asked for — summarizes them; for an owner's request the statement is the thing itself.

An intent grouped from reports never auto-passes the [Spec](03-gates.md#spec) gate. The source intake wrote is a context factor the [score](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) reads, and reports is the value that is resolved rather than weighed: it takes the treatment [a factor the score cannot compute](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) takes — toward the gate — so a human confirms the criteria (6) whatever the rest of [the vector](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) says, and the [held-out sample](04-risk-score.md#how-it-learns) cannot select past them, never selecting what a resolved factor put there. This is the one part of the channel's safety that does not rest on how good any reader is. That an agent treats a report as content and never as instruction stays [an instruction to the agent](01-one-pipeline.md), worth what an instruction is worth — a reason to hand report text onward quoted rather than folded into anything an agent is told, and not what stops an injected report from shipping code; the human at Spec is. What the floor costs is stated plainly: the source that arrives in the greatest volume is the one whose items cost the most human attention, the opposite direction from the rest of the design, and taken because the alternative is an anonymous sentence reaching production with nobody on the path.

Arrival is bounded at the channel. Two rates are fields of the [factory-wide settings record](09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them) beside [retention](09-gate-policy.md#what-is-not-in-it) — one applied per service, one over the whole channel — and the report store enforces them at the way in: a report over either rate is refused rather than queued without bound. A report also carries an opaque key where the deployed software supplies one, derived from its own session and salted, so that it marks a session's reports as one source's and carries nobody's identity; the store rates a source by it, and a bad actor is limited through it. The key is not a reporter handle — nothing joins it to a person, it cannot be added to reports already written, and it is the most the channel can carry without trading away the anonymity it exists to offer, which is why the two authored rates stay coarse: what they bound is arrival, never any individual. A surge therefore drops real reports, a real loss preferred to an unbounded one — unbounded, the channel's volume is linear in what a stranger sends at no cost, read by the grouper report by report against [a quota](10-fleet.md#an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold) every stage shares and whose exhaustion pages nobody. The factory-wide rate authored to zero closes the channel, and a safeguard (9) narrowing the per-service rate to zero closes one service's. This is protection at the channel and not a ceiling on spend: what admitted reports go on to cost is bounded, where an owner authored one, by [the spend ceiling](10-fleet.md#a-spend-ceiling) and by nothing else.

An owner who wants a human before the pipeline spends anything on a report adds a safeguard (9) whose subject is the report store: with one in force a report-derived intent arrives and waits, [dispatch](#dispatch) putting no agent on it and no interview round running, until a human admits it at [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) — one action per group, the group already being one intent. It is a safeguard rather than the default because a regulated install wants it and an ordinary one should not have to pay it, and what it buys over the floor at Spec is that nothing is spent refining what a human would never admit.

Retention removes reports in bulk and on age; what removes one report's words is a **redaction** — a second record naming a report, the statement summarizing it, or an [artifact version](01-one-pipeline.md) quoting it, and the spans removed. The bytes named are destroyed, every reader reads the target through its redactions, and the redaction carries the actor and the reason and never the words. No link breaks, because what is erased is text inside a record and never the record: the report row, its grouping link, its count, and the before-and-after measurement all stand with the words gone. It is the correction idiom a [constraint](#constraints-and-the-design-system) already uses — a second record, never an edit — pointed at content, with one difference stated plainly: the original is destroyed rather than superseded, so this is the one mechanism in the factory that removes information a decision was made on. The decision still names its versions, and what those versions can no longer show, the redaction record says why. An owner performs one erasure as one action, and the factory walks the links that exist — the report to its intent, the statement, the artifact versions authored against them, which is the query the intent's source already marks out. What it costs is a read-through on every reader of stored text, and an auditor reading a decision back meeting a stated gap where words were.

What a redaction reaches ends at the install's edge, and both halves of that edge are on the record. Which providers received the words is answerable: report-derived work spends through named credentials — the field every [interview](#the-interview) round and every stage writes — and [_People_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) records who lent each, so the recipients are enumerable without a provider being named anywhere in the factory. What a provider retained is a residue the factory does not reach: recalling anything from one is the owner's agreement with that provider, which is [substrate](../what-humans-do.md) the way hosting is. And nobody — the factory included — can list a person's reports, because the channel carries no identity by design: an access request is answered by that fact, and an erasure request needs what every redaction needs, content pointed at.

Three things stay refused here, each for a reason already on the record. A subject key — indexing reports by the people their text mentions — would have the factory identify people in free text, building exactly the join the opaque key refuses to be; whoever the words are about is served by the redaction and not by an index. Screening content before an agent or a screen reads it is a moderation pipeline a one-customer install does not carry: the admission safeguard above is that screen where an owner wants one, an agent takes report text as [content and never instruction](01-one-pipeline.md), and the human the Spec floor keeps is the backstop. And a reporter still gets nothing back — no acknowledgment, no status, no way to contest a grouping — because each would need the reporter handle the channel refuses; the residue is that a misgrouped report is contested by nobody outside, and what repairs it is [decomposition](#decomposition) splitting the group and a recurrence link joining what it missed.

A report that is never grouped is work nobody sees, which is how a store in front of a pipeline fails wherever it is built. So every report is either linked to the intent it was grouped into or counted as ungrouped, and that count is a number on [_Factory_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people). One report appears in [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) under its intent and nowhere else: a browsable list of reports would put end-user volume on the screen that is one item per timeline, and make the grouping a human's job again.

### Advisories

An **advisory** is a published statement that a named version range of a third-party package has a vulnerability, and the fourth detector reads them. The feed it reads is a thing from outside, on the far side of the division [_What the factory does not build_](../what-the-factory-does.md#what-the-factory-does-not-build) draws — a store of advisories somebody else keeps, one per package registry the factory's services resolve from, named by the owner the way a deploy [target](05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch) is. What it is read against is a field of the [build](05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch): every package a build resolved and the version it resolved to, written by the [deployer](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) from the build it performed. The condition is mechanical and no model reads a word of it: an advisory names a package and a range, a service's [current release](06-releases.md#the-deploy-record) names a build, and the build's resolved set holds that package inside that range. The detector reads the feed on a pass of its own, the way the conformance raise in [_Constraints and the design system_](#constraints-and-the-design-system) reads the constraints in force, and calls intake once per advisory that matches anything: the evidence is the advisory, which keys the raise, and [_Decomposition_](#decomposition) enumerates the services whose current release resolves it, an item to each, like the reach of a constraint. The condition follows the conformance raise's stricter rule — it stands until the intent exists, and an intent that ended is not raised over again, an advisory the owner dropped staying dropped until they ask — and an advisory the feed withdraws ends it.

The trust boundary is where it is for a [report](#reports), and narrower. An advisory is a stranger's text arriving through a source the factory does not authenticate, and where its prose reaches an agent it reaches it as content, under [_What an agent reads is content, never instruction_](01-one-pipeline.md). What separates it from a report is that the match is decided against a record the factory wrote before any model reads a word: an advisory raises nothing unless a shipped build resolved the package it names, so a fabricated one can make the factory move a version it did not need to and nothing else, and a feed that lies stays a feed the owner named.

What the raise buys is the match and not the judgement. Whether the vulnerable path is reached by the service's own code is decided by nobody mechanical — the item ships the version the advisory clears, or a human ends the intent as dropped at [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), the end [_The interview_](#the-interview) gives one. It reaches Work as an unrefined intent and never as a [page](08-operations.md#pages): the factory does not page to inform, and the advisory's severity travels on the evidence for a human to read there. What it costs is a resolved set on every build, repeated across rebuilds of one item; a pass reading a feed the factory does not keep; and a gap it states rather than closes — the detector reads advisories and not code, so a vulnerability nobody has published, and the interval between publication and the named feed carrying it, are invisible to it, and a candidate not yet shipped is read only once it is a current release, on the next pass after it becomes one. A registry with no feed named is not watched, and which registries are is a list on [_Factory_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) beside the constraints in force. Without the detector, nothing in the factory observes a vulnerability in something already shipped: the numbers are clean, no window crosses, no gate fires, and the only route from an advisory to a fix is an owner reading it and typing a request — work assigned to nobody and counted nowhere.

## The interview

The factory runs the interview and decides when it has asked enough. It asks (3) until it has enough to decompose the intent and author a spec per item whose criteria fit the six patterns [_Spec_](03-gates.md#spec) sets out — and what ends the questioning is having enough to author, not a human declaring the intent clear. An owner cannot know what those patterns will demand of an answer; the factory is the one that has them.

What ends the interview is one more round, fixed in form: the factory states, in the requester's own terms, what it has understood is wanted and what it will build, and the requester's answer confirms that reading or corrects it. Confirmed, the intent is refined; corrected, the answer attaches like any other and the factory asks again, the correction counting a round like every round. It is owed on an intent that has a requester — an owner's request (1) — and on an intent grouped from [_reports_](#reports) it reaches whoever holds (3), the reporter being reachable by nothing; an intent the factory raised has no requester, its evidence is the request, and it takes no such round, nobody being able to say the evidence was misread. No gate fires on it and no [score](04-risk-score.md) reads it, and that is the point rather than an omission: the two places a human could otherwise ratify the factory's reading of a request — confirming the criteria at [_Spec_](03-gates.md#spec) (6), [UAT](../what-humans-do.md) at [Merge to master](03-gates.md#merge-to-master) (7) — are score-gated and in the steady state auto-pass, so without this round the requester never says whether what was asked for was understood, and a wrong thing built well surfaces after production as a new intent. What it costs is a round on every requested intent, including the ones the factory could have decomposed without asking anything; a wait on the requester, unbounded the way every silence at the interview is; and that what is confirmed is the reading in the requester's terms and never the criteria, so a reading confirmed and criteria that miss it is caught at Spec where a human is there, and otherwise not before production.

The interview is not a stage of its own. It is the state an intent is in, which is why it has no gate and no artifact a gate decides over: intake writes the intent, the interview refines it, decomposition turns it into items. So the state is a field on the intent, and it has five values. An intent arrives **unrefined**. It is **refined** when the factory has enough to decompose it and author a spec per item, which is the same thing as ready to decompose and needs no second name. It is **re-decomposing** while a re-decomposition of a set in progress is being decided, which is what stops that intent's items while the [Decomposition](03-gates.md#decomposition) firing is open. It is **escalated** where the rounds exceeded [_the attempt limit_](03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit), or where [decomposition](#decomposition)'s re-decompositions did, and that value is written rather than recomputed from either count: the limit is authored with gate policy (8) and changes, and a decision read back against a value that was not in force when it was taken is not the decision that was taken. And it is **dropped** where a human ends it for good, the value an [item](#what-an-item-names) can already take and for the same reason: some work stops short of anything its items could show, and an end read from absence reads as nothing at all.

The state is the intent's own and runs past decomposition in four cases and no others: exhausting either of the counts the intent keeps writes **escalated** here, the limit exceeded being the intent's own rather than any stage's; anything sent back to the intent — a [rework request](03-gates.md#going-back-up) naming it, a gate's reject naming it, or a replacement [constraint](#constraints-and-the-design-system)'s raise landing on it, on the terms that section states — writes **unrefined** again, which is what reopens the interview; anything sent back to decomposition writes **re-decomposing** until that Decomposition firing closes; and a human at [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) ends one for good, intake writing **dropped** with Work as the caller — an escalation nobody clears, or a conformance intent whose decomposition found nothing departing. Ending an intent ends its unmerged items with it, Work calling dispatch to write dropped on each in the same action, so nothing of it waits for a second decision or holds a branch. Everything else after decomposition is read from the items — [an intent partly delivered](#when-an-intents-items-do-not-all-ship) is computed from whether its items all reached a release, and whether an intent was decomposed at all is answered by the items that exist. So the record keeps one writer: a transition is a write to the intent and goes through intake like every other, and decomposition writes items and calls intake for the one exception that is its own.

The interview reopens where a stage cannot author from what the intent says, which is the ambiguity no amount of re-authoring settles. A rework request names the intent, or a human at a gate rejects to it, the state goes back to unrefined, and the factory asks — a question record like any other and a round counted against the same limit, so a stage reaching for the intent every time exhausts the rounds and the intent escalates rather than asking forever. Decomposition then runs again on the answer, superseding only what it actually replaces, because an answer that moves what was asked for moves what the set should have been and leaving the old decomposition standing would ratify it against a request nobody made. Every unmerged item of that intent waits while it is open: the state is already what puts an [agent](01-one-pipeline.md) on an intent still unrefined, and it is read here to put none on an item whose intent has gone back. Work shows each of them stopped on the intent's state and not merely as a timeline that is not advancing, which is what an item matching no [fleet entry](10-fleet.md) looks like and is the one thing it must not be confused with. What that costs is one item's ambiguity stopping siblings that were fine, which is the direction worth erring in — an answer that changes what was asked for changes what all of them are building, and a sibling that shipped in the meantime is a release nobody wanted. An intent the factory raised itself has nobody who asked, so its question reaches Work with no [holder](../what-humans-do.md) and widens to the owner, the way an unheld row does everywhere else. The sharper cost is that this is the one place work stops with nothing running and no [page](08-operations.md#pages) raised — the [notifier](08-operations.md#pages) still reaches the holder on the two channels that are not the page, nothing deployed being worse for an unanswered question, but no limit applies to an owner's silence, for the reason none applies to it before decomposition either, so an item can sit on one longer than any escalation would have taken.

What is written once is the statement and whatever is attached to it, which is what keeps an approval pointing at what was approved; the state advances. Nothing reads the state back to justify a decision — it says where the intent is, which is what [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) answers and what puts an [agent](01-one-pipeline.md) on the intents still unrefined.

A **question** is a record, one per question asked, attached to the intent and naming the round it was asked in, with the answer written onto it when the answer arrives. Intake writes both, called by the factory at the question and by Work at the answer, because a question is attached to the intent and takes the intent's one writer — the arrangement [_Intake_](#intake) already makes for every write to that record, the interview being a state and not a component able to write one. Each round also writes what it spent onto the intent, the field a stage keeps on an item, with the name of the credential it spent through. It is not an artifact: nothing gates it, no attempt is counted on it, and the spec is still what the refinement produces. What that costs is a record written twice, so a reader tells an unanswered question from an answered one by a field rather than by the record existing — and the answer is write-once, so an owner who answers badly waits to be asked again.

How many rounds have been asked is a field of the intent, written by intake at each round like every other write to it, and it is what [_the attempt limit_](03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit) is compared against here. Counting the questions instead would count a round that asked three as three, and the limit counts rounds.

Questions appear in [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) under the intent that raised them, and are answered there. The spec is the artifact, so there is no interview gate and none is missing: the requester confirms the reading at the last round, and approving the spec confirms the criteria authored from it (6), which is what [_Where a gate is, and what decides it_](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it) already assumes.

The drawback is that an owner cannot shorten it. An intent whose questions go unanswered does not proceed, and the end condition belonging to the factory is what makes that wait the owner's to end.

Two things go wrong with an interview, and only one of them spends anything. An owner who stops answering spends nothing — no build, no environment, no agent is running — so no limit applies to that wait and none should: a limit would turn their silence into an [escalation](03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit) (12) they have to clear anyway, which is the human work the factory exists to remove. An interview that is answered and still never reaches enough to author does spend, round after round, and it has [_the attempt limit_](03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit) like any other stretch of work the factory can fail at — the same parameter, authored with gate policy (8), counting rounds here rather than retries. Exceeding it appears in Work as an escalation (12), the factory saying it cannot refine this one; it still takes no gate, the spec being the artifact that ratifies the refinement. A human who answers clears the escalation and the count starts again, because the escalation is what put them there and answering it is a decision to spend more — the limit is still the only one the factory holds, and an owner who keeps clearing is choosing to spend past it, limited then by the [spend ceiling](10-fleet.md#a-spend-ceiling) where one is authored on the credential the agent runs on — a stop on units spent through it, restarted by a clearing of its own and not by this one — and by the quota on the [provider account](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-runs-on) behind it otherwise. The drawback is that a limit low enough to stop an interview going in circles will also stop one that was about to converge.

## Decomposition

One intent becomes one item or several — none only for the conformance intent [_Constraints and the design system_](#constraints-and-the-design-system) raises, whose decomposition may find nothing departing, attach that finding, and write no item, no gate firing over an empty set — and an item names exactly one service. How many go to one service is a floor and not a cap: a service the work changes gets at least one item, and gets more where one item would be larger than decomposition is aiming at, or where a change cannot ship as one. Decomposition is where [_No single item may break a contract_](07-contracts.md#no-single-item-may-break-a-contract) is applied rather than discovered: "an item that cannot ship by itself was decomposed wrong" is a statement about this stage, and a migration with one consumer is four items over two services, three of them on the producer, which adds the new form in one item, runs the [brownout](07-contracts.md#deprecation) of the old one in a second, and removes it in a third — the last two the factory raises itself, the brownout when the derived list empties and the removal on the brownout's evidence, the way [_Deprecation_](07-contracts.md#deprecation) sets out, not written here ahead of conditions nobody can schedule. Until decomposition got a gate of its own, nothing ratified what it produced.

A service the work changes may not exist yet, and nothing about decomposition changes: one item creates it, and whatever calls it is a second item declared to wait on the first. Decomposition writes the service record in the same write as that item, because an item names one service and the record has to exist for the item's only outbound link to point at anything. Its other writer is an owner at [_Factory_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), putting the [window limit](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows) and the analysis window's parameters on it (8), and the seam between the two is the field: decomposition writes the service's identity and never a parameter, an owner writes parameters and never creates a service. A service is one repository with one long-lived branch inside it, and no repository holds two: [master](05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch), [the release number](06-releases.md#the-release-number), and the queue's ordering are all per service, and two services sharing a repository would give it two divergent trunks, so a build of either would be made from a tree that contains the other's stale code. The drawback is that a twelve-service project is twelve repositories, and a customer arriving with a monorepo has a split to perform that nothing in the factory does for them. Master itself does not exist until the first release: the [implementation role](01-one-pipeline.md) commits the candidate branch with no base, and the queue's first fast-forward is what creates master. Nothing writes the service's place in an environment either — a [deploy record](06-releases.md#the-deploy-record) is keyed by service and environment, so a service is in an environment by having been deployed there. The item ships by itself, a service nothing calls yet being exactly the kind of thing that can. The drawback is that the factory's weakest measurement applies to the release the rest of the work waits on: a first release is [_without a control_](03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy) whatever the score would prefer, nothing can close its window [_passed_](08-operations.md#the-analysis-window), where no safeguard sets a threshold (9) it is [_unmeasured altogether_](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor), and it has no [_rollback target_](06-releases.md#rollback). Each of those is stated where it arises, and this is where they arrive together. How long they last is stated too: until the second release closes a [window](08-operations.md#the-analysis-window) on evidence, which a service with organic traffic — requests real users are already making — reaches at its second release and a service with none does not reach at all. What the customer has meanwhile is a factory gating on the prior the product shipped for each model version rather than on a wide one, which [_A model under a name_](10-fleet.md#a-model-under-a-name) supplies, and no measurement of what it ships. A customer whose software is already running and already has traffic is past that state at the second release, which is why [_A service that already exists_](#a-service-that-already-exists) is the ordinary way a factory starts and not the exception.

### A service that already exists

Most software a customer brings has been running for years, and it is adopted through the same pipeline as anything else: an owner raises an intent (1) naming a repository and the environment it is running in, and the interview asks what it does — the criteria a human can state for behaviour nobody wrote down, which may be none. Decomposition writes the service record for it, the repository already existing, and yields one item, whose implementation stage authors nothing: the repository's head is the implementation, the candidate branch is its trunk, and the [deployer](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) builds it as it builds any commit, so the item has a [build](05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch), the [consumer contracts](07-contracts.md#what-a-consumer-declares) the factory derives from one, and the resolved set the [advisory](#advisories) detector reads. Every gate fires as it fires on any item, the [Implementation](03-gates.md#implementation) gate's diff being the whole tree against nothing, and the queue's first fast-forward makes the head master and the item release one. Its production deploy is where adoption differs from a first release the factory built: the software is already running, so where the [platform](03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy) can shift a share, the release takes traffic [with a control](03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy) — the instances already running are the control, kept serving as any control is — and the window compares the factory's build of the head against the customer's, which is a comparison between two builds of one commit and establishes that the factory builds and deploys this service the way its owner did. From that deploy the service is inside the factory's checks: it has a current release for [a deploy gate's hold on a dependency](03-gates.md#deploy-to-production) to read, a deploy record for [_Drift detection_](08-operations.md#drift-detection) to compare, and a build for the next release's control to start from, so the second release is measured completely — on a service with organic traffic, sooner than a service the factory built from nothing is ever measured. The [design system](#constraints-and-the-design-system) an owner picks for the project reaches it through the conformance raise above, an item per screen that departs.

What adoption does not give a service is stated rather than promised. Release one has no [rollback target](06-releases.md#rollback) — what ran before it is not a release — so a window that fails on it finds nothing to return to and takes the [page](08-operations.md#pages) that exit already has. The interfaces arrive without [declared meaning](07-contracts.md#what-a-diff-cannot-see), a consumer's contracts are checked against what was never declared, and naming a field afterwards is a breaking change and a migration's four items. Every parameter of the service starts where a new service's does — the [window limit](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows) at one, the window's size supplied — because nothing the factory can read says what ten years of shipping taught. And the service is one repository with one trunk: a customer arriving with a monorepo splits it before adoption, the split being work nothing in the factory does for them, for the reason above. What adoption costs the factory is one item that changes nothing and pays for an environment, a run, and a release number anyway, and a first window whose passed exit says only that the factory reproduced what was running. Following the links out of an incident in adopted code ends at the adoption item, which is where the factory's history of that service starts, and nothing recovers what was behind it.

Shipping by itself is a minimum and not a size — it admits an item that touches forty files and one that changes a string. What sizes an item between them is a target the score supplies, which an owner authors, or safeguards per area (9), with the rest of gate policy (8). It learns from what a bad size does in each direction, and both are already among the numbers [_Factory_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) reports: a decomposition too coarse shows as attempt-limit escalations (12), with everything spent on the item thrown away, and one too fine shows as cost per feature and rework rate — an environment, a spec, four gates, and a release number for a one-line change. What that number reaches is model spend and nothing the host bills — the environment and the fleet a release keeps are hosting the factory does not read, and the spend ceiling does not reach them either — so the target can be learned finer than the install can afford while Factory shows the number falling. That is stated as a cost and not repaired: the owner's lever is to author the target over what the score learned (8). The minimums do not move with the target. It ships by itself, and no single item may break a contract, which is four items for an interface and three for a store, however small the target. What the target is not is a definition: an item is not one feature. A feature that changes a contract is several items by the rule above, so a definition of that shape is broken by the case this section already works through, and Factory computes cost per feature as what the intent and its items spent, which already makes a feature an intent. What refusing it costs is that the target has two named failure directions and no stated unit, and [_In what unit is the item-size target authored?_](../open.md#in-what-unit-is-the-item-size-target-authored) is that question.

Decomposition also records the order. Where one item cannot be verified until another has shipped — the producing release of a migration — that dependency is declared here, and both deploy gates hold on it: [_Deploy to candidate environment_](03-gates.md#deploy-to-candidate-environment) until the dependency is live, [_Deploy to production_](03-gates.md#deploy-to-production) if it has stopped being. Ordering is a property of decomposition, not something discovered at deploy time.

The declared order is checked where the set is decided: a set whose dependencies form a cycle is rejected at [the Decomposition gate](03-gates.md#decomposition) mechanically, the way a forbidden transition is at [_Implementation_](03-gates.md#implementation), because two items each holding a deploy gate on the other is a wait nothing lifts and no instrument shows — a factory hold over an existing record writes nothing, counts no attempt, and pages nobody. A re-decomposition also repoints what stands: an item left standing that waited on one now superseded waits on the replacements, written by decomposition in the same write that creates them — the inverse of the pointer a superseded item already carries — and an item left waiting on one replaced by nothing has that wait resolved in the set the gate decides, what waits on what being part of what is approved. Without the repointing, an item waiting on a superseded sibling waits on something that will never be current, forever, and every instrument reads it as an ordinary dependency hold about to lift itself.

Decomposition has a gate of its own, named for the stage and scored like every other, and it fires where the stage yielded more than one item. What is approved is the set: how many items, which service each changes, and what waits on what. A rejection re-decomposes the set rather than sending one item back, because what the gate decides on is the decomposition and not an item, and each round counts against the attempt limit on the intent rather than on an item — the items of a rejected round are superseded and their replacements start at nothing, so a count kept on them would never reach a limit however many rounds were spent. It is the parameter the interview already counts its rounds against, one stage later and on the same record, in a field of its own beside the rounds and never the same field: the two are different stretches of work, an owner answering an escalated interview clears that count alone, and one field would spend an interview's rounds out of decomposition's budget. Intake writes it, called by decomposition, which is the one write decomposition makes to an intent rather than to items. While rounds remain, a human re-decomposes by hand with [Edit in place](03-gates.md#actions-at-each-gate), the gate still deciding and the set still editable. An intent that exceeds the limit gets **escalated** written on it and appears in Work as an escalation (12) — the second of the two things that write that value, and the two are told apart by which of the intent's counts exceeded the limit rather than by the value itself.

Decomposition also runs again on an intent in progress, which is what three other sections send a problem to: a group [_Reports_](#reports) says the grouper preferred to make, a spec that comes back too large, and a change [_No single item may break a contract_](07-contracts.md#no-single-item-may-break-a-contract) says cannot ship as one. A [rework request](03-gates.md#going-back-up) naming decomposition re-decomposes the set, as does a gate's reject naming it, and what either produces is decided at this gate whatever it yields — the one-item condition is the first decomposition's, and a re-decomposition of work in progress always fires, because what it decides is which existing items are superseded rather than how many are proposed. The intent goes to re-decomposing and every unmerged item of it stops while that firing is open, the way they stop while the interview is: without it a sibling merges between the rework request and the verdict, and the set approved would supersede a release. The item that sent it back stops with them, its stage unchanged and its attempt booked on the intent, so it resumes where it was if the re-decomposition does not replace it. The items it replaces are superseded and point at their replacements, the record shape a rejected round already uses; what differs is that an item in progress has spent artifacts, a build, and an environment, and superseding it throws all of them away. A merged item is out of reach — a rework request may be raised no later than the merge to master — so the re-decomposition leaves shipped work alone and declares the new set's order against it, which is what [_When an intent's items do not all ship_](#when-an-intents-items-do-not-all-ship) already says about the siblings that got there first. Each round counts against the same re-decomposition count, so a set that keeps being re-decomposed escalates the intent rather than dividing forever, and its items stop there rather than resuming — they appear in Work under it until a human takes one over (12) or drops them, the factory not going on to build a decomposition it has just said it cannot make. Against a decomposition that fires once per intent this costs a gate every builder implements as re-firing and a set that can change under work already approved; what it buys is that a wrong decomposition is repaired on the intent that caused it rather than restarted as a new one with the interview, the grouped reports, and the timeline left behind.

One verdict covers the whole decomposition, however many services it changes. Splitting it up — one verdict per service, each holder deciding only the items in theirs — would rebuild here the wait [_Who owns a contract_](07-contracts.md#who-owns-a-contract) refused: approvals that must all arrive before anything proceeds, in a graph where a four-service decomposition is ordinary, and the attempt limit turning a long enough wait into escalations (12). A holder who wants to decide at decomposition for their own service gets that with a safeguard (9) on their area, which is what a safeguard does everywhere else. The drawback is that they first see the work one stage down, with the authoring already done — never blind, since the item links back to its intent and Work shows the sibling timelines under the same decision, but reading a spec rather than a proposal.

Decomposition writes the items, and the gate decides over records that already exist. Every other [artifact gate](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it) does — Spec, [Implementation plan](03-gates.md#implementation-plan), [Tasks](03-gates.md#tasks), and Implementation each decide over an artifact already written — and the alternative gives the Decomposition decision fields describing the set it proposes, which is what an item already records. It would also split one record's writer in two: a decomposition yielding one item fires no gate, so items would appear at approval where a gate fired and at decomposition where none did.

A rejected set is superseded rather than discarded. Each rejected item points at the items of the re-decomposition that replaced it — a list, since a re-decomposition may replace four items with two — written by decomposition in the same write that creates the replacements, so what was decomposed wrong is readable beside what replaced it rather than only in the decision's feedback. Where a re-decomposition replaced an item with nothing the pointer stays unwritten, and what says why is the item's superseded stage beside the decision that rejected the set. What the pair of choices costs is item records that no gate approved, a superseded set per rejected round, and a service record decomposition wrote for an item the re-decomposition did not replace, which no item in progress then names: every reader of items — [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) first — has to tell an item in progress from one of those.

The Spec gate cannot do this job — it is per item, so approving one item's spec never ratifies the decomposition that produced four. Without a gate here, a wrong decomposition is discovered several specs later and re-decomposing means abandoning items that were already approved. So the gate fires where there is a set to ratify and nowhere else: a decomposition that yields one item produced none, and putting a gate in front of every intent — most of them single-item — would be a decision about nothing. Scoring it was the cheaper defence and it is the wrong one: a gate the score auto-passes is still a gate every builder implements and every intent passes through. What the condition costs is the decomposition that should have yielded four and yielded one, which now reaches a human one stage down as a spec too large, at [_Spec_](03-gates.md#spec) rather than here.

### What an item names

An item names one service, and its area is a field of the item that decomposition writes. An area is free to cut across services, so it cannot be reached through the service an item names — the work is in a part of the software an owner grouped, and decomposition is what knows which part. The project is not a field: a service is in exactly one, so an item naming its own would let the two disagree, and a service moved between projects would leave every item naming the old one. What that costs is a join — a safeguard (9) or a [_scope_](01-one-pipeline.md) drawn on an area reads the item alone, and one drawn on a project reads the item's service.

An area is a record with one writer, an owner declaring one at [_Factory_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) (9), and its readers are decomposition, dispatch, and every mechanism a safeguard or a scope drawn on one reaches. Areas form a chain, each naming the area or the project it lies inside, so which of two is narrower is a link rather than a judgment over prose. Decomposition writes the narrowest area in the chain whose declaration covers the work, and only an area inside the project of the service the item names, so the item's area and its service agree by construction. A safeguard or a scope drawn on any area in that chain reaches the item: the gate and dispatch follow it from the item's area up to the project. Without the chain an owner who declared a narrower area inside a safeguarded one would lose the safeguard the moment decomposition wrote the narrower name, and nothing in the design may remove a human from a gate. Two groupings that genuinely cross-cut need a third area declared for the intersection.

One intent's items may be in more than one project. A contract migration decomposes two of its four items from one intent — the brownout and the removal, both the producer's, are [raised by the factory on evidence of its own](07-contracts.md#deprecation) — and [_Contracts_](07-contracts.md#two-versioned-things) puts a contract's consumers anywhere in the factory, so decomposition writes items wherever the work is and the intent stays what joins them. What that costs is that a migration's items are decided under two projects' gate thresholds and two sets of safeguards, and that a fleet whose entries are scoped to one project cannot be dispatched onto the item in the other — which shows in Work as an item that does not advance, indistinguishable from a fleet too small.

Which stage an item is at is a field on the item, and **dispatch** is what writes it — every stage reports a transition to it, and the component that reads the field on every move is the one that keeps it. Two more fields go with it, written at the same transitions and both kept per stage: how many attempts each stage has taken, and what each stage spent, with the name of the credential it spent through, which is what [_A spend ceiling_](10-fleet.md#a-spend-ceiling) sums by. An attempt is counted when a stage is entered to author, so a stage reached once and passed stands at one, and what the events below do is send the item back to be entered again rather than increment anything themselves — which is what makes more than one attempt at a stage mean the work there was redone. The current stage's count is what a gate compares against [_the attempt limit_](03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit), because an attempt that failed before a gate fired wrote no decision to count, and it is where an agent's failed attempt, a gate's reject, and [_the merge queue_](05-environments.md#the-merge-queue)'s rejection all report — each at the stage the item is sent to rather than at the one it leaves, which is what books a [rework request](03-gates.md#going-back-up) against the stage that was wrong; the stages below a rework request's target re-author counting nothing, a [hold](03-gates.md#what-a-gate-may-change) reports nothing, and neither does a candidate re-verifying behind someone else's failure. A human taking an escalated item over (12) clears the escalation and the limit counts from there. The attempts already taken stay on the stage, so what the clearing writes through dispatch is the count it cleared at, and the limit is compared against the attempts since. An owner answering an escalated [_interview_](#the-interview) clears one too, where nothing but the limit reads the rounds and the count may simply start again. Kept rather than reset at each advance, those counts are what [_Factory_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people)'s rework rate reads. Dispatch matches the stage against the role — and matches nothing where that role's prompt has no [version in force](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told), an agent being told one thing — and reads the item and its intent's state, which with [a credential already known unreachable](10-fleet.md#an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold), that missing prompt, and a stage no [fleet entry](10-fleet.md) matches is all that stops an item from outside it; the intent's state is how an intent that is not refined gets no agent put on anything below it — unrefined, re-decomposing, escalated, and dropped alike, escalated being the intent that exhausted the counts it keeps and dropped one a human ended for good, so that enumerating only the first two would put an agent back on items the factory has said it cannot decompose, or a human has ended. Deriving it instead — a query over which artifacts exist and which gate decisions passed — would put that join in every reader and would not remove the field: an item that exceeded [_the attempt limit_](03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit) is escalated (12), and that value is written for the reason the interview's is, that a limit authored with gate policy (8) changes and a decision read back against a value that was not in force when it was taken is not the decision that was taken. A superseded item is a value here too, and it is what tells one from an item decomposition wrote a moment ago, neither having an artifact yet. An item can also stop for good without reaching a release, and **dropped** is the value that says so — written by dispatch where a human ends work for good: with Work as the caller, where a human stops one that escalated (12) and nobody took over or ends [the intent above it](#the-interview), and with [_Ops_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) as the caller, where [a mark](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows) ends a revert item before it ships. Merged, dropped, or superseded by a re-decomposition is what frees an item's branch and its environment for good, and [_An environment per candidate_](05-environments.md#an-environment-per-candidate) tears one down on exactly those events — and reclaims one meanwhile from an item running nothing, so an escalated item holds its branch until a human ends it one of those ways and holds no environment while it waits.

The item has two writers and the seam is the event: decomposition writes it when it creates one — the intent, the service, the area, the branch name, and what it waits on — and writes an item again only to point a superseded one at what replaced it and to repoint what a standing item waits on where [a re-decomposition](#decomposition) superseded its dependency, reporting those transitions to dispatch like every other; every write that is not one of those two is dispatch's. An owner reordering a queue writes the [priority](05-environments.md#the-merge-queue) through dispatch rather than beside it, the way Work calls intake to answer a question. One writer after decomposition costs a dependency edge from every stage back to dispatch, which is the cost [_Intake_](#intake) already accepts on the intent and for the same reason — the record's rules implemented once rather than once per stage — and it puts a match the design calls not a judgment on the write path of every item, unavailable exactly when nothing can move. What the fields cost is that they can disagree with the artifacts and with the decisions: a transition that fails to write leaves an item naming a stage its artifacts do not show, and a reader asking why an item escalated still reads the decisions to see what the attempts were.

## When an intent's items do not all ship

An intent whose items did not all ship is an outcome rather than a fault: one item exceeds the attempt limit and appears as an escalation (12), or a human [undoes it after it shipped](../what-humans-do.md) (10), while its siblings are already live. Nothing writes it down — an intent is partly delivered when at least one of its items stopped without reaching production and at least one sibling is live, an item being live when a production [deploy record](06-releases.md#the-deploy-record) names its release — as the one it deployed, or in the list of releases a [revert](06-releases.md#rollback)'s deploy delivered — and marks it complete on every production target, and not before. An intent whose items are all still moving is in progress rather than partly delivered, one where nothing shipped is stopped, and one whose superseded items were replaced by a re-decomposition is judged on the replacements. It is not terminal either: a human taking over an escalated item (12) can finish it, and the intent stops being partly delivered with no event anywhere, which is why a field would have to be unwritten and is not a field. The cost is three joins — intent to items, item to release, release to that service's production deploy record, and a rule over that record's completion per target — so the reading is only as cheap as those inbound edges are indexed, and an item merely held at the production deploy gate never makes its intent one of these however long it waits. The shipped ones stay shipped. Each was decomposed to ship by itself and is worth something by itself — and where it is not, the decomposition was wrong, which is what the gate above exists to catch. Undoing the whole thing is one revert item per shipped sibling, joined by the same intent, and never a single undo.

The declared order is what keeps that survivable. An item that fails stops only its dependents: whatever it depended on shipped before it, and whatever depended on it was still held at a deploy gate. So what this leaves is a feature half delivered, not production half broken.

## Dispatch

With the decomposition approved, the factory puts its own agents onto the items — a model in a role with a scope, as [_One pipeline_](01-one-pipeline.md) has it. Dispatch gets no gate: it decides who authors, and everything authored is reviewed at a gate anyway, where the score reads the [per-author prior](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) of the model that wrote it.

What limits how many items move at once is infrastructure — an environment per candidate — and that cost is the factory's rather than a human's. Where a human authors a stage instead of an agent (11, 12), nothing about dispatch changes: authorship is an attribute of the stage, and the item is still one timeline.

Dispatch is report-driven — [_What an item names_](#what-an-item-names) has every stage reporting a transition to it — and an agent can stop without reporting anything: its host restarts, its process dies, its provider connection hangs. So a dispatch is a claim that expires unless the agent renews it, renewal being activity rather than progress, and a claim that expires unrenewed is itself the report: dispatch sends the item back to the stage to be entered again, the way an agent's failed attempt does, so the count rises on re-entry and enough silent stops walk the item to an [escalation](03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit) (12) instead of leaving a timeline that never advances and never escalates. A stage with an unexpired claim is matched to nobody, which is what keeps a slow agent and its replacement from authoring one stage at once. What it costs is a renewal on every working agent, and that a stop is discovered at the expiry rather than at the death — an expiry generous enough to never cut off honest work leaves a dead stage undiscovered for exactly that long.

The [_Factory_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) screen is where an owner watches this: [_the fleet_](10-fleet.md), what each agent is doing, and how well.
