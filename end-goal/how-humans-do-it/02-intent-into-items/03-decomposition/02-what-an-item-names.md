# What an item names

An item names one service, and its area is a field of the item that decomposition writes. An area is free to cut across services, so it cannot be reached through the service an item names — the work is in a part of the software an owner grouped, and decomposition is what knows which part. The project is not a field: a service is in exactly one, so an item naming its own would let the two disagree, and a service moved between projects would leave every item naming the old one. What that costs is a join — a safeguard (9) or a [_scope_](../../01-one-pipeline.md) drawn on an area reads the item alone, and one drawn on a project reads the item's service.

An area is a record with one writer, an owner declaring one at [_Factory_](../../11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) (9), and its readers are decomposition, dispatch, and every mechanism a safeguard or a scope drawn on one reaches. Areas form a chain, each naming the area or the project it lies inside, so which of two is narrower is a link rather than a judgment over prose. Decomposition writes the narrowest area in the chain whose declaration covers the work, and only an area inside the project of the service the item names, so the item's area and its service agree by construction. A safeguard or a scope drawn on any area in that chain reaches the item: the gate and dispatch follow it from the item's area up to the project. Without the chain an owner who declared a narrower area inside a safeguarded one would lose the safeguard the moment decomposition wrote the narrower name, and nothing in the design may remove a human from a gate. Two groupings that genuinely cross-cut need a third area declared for the intersection.

One intent's items may be in more than one project. A contract migration decomposes two of its four items from one intent — the brownout and the removal, both the producer's, are [raised by the factory on evidence of its own](../../07-contracts.md#deprecation) — and [_Contracts_](../../07-contracts.md#two-versioned-things) puts a contract's consumers anywhere in the factory, so decomposition writes items wherever the work is and the intent stays what joins them. What that costs is that a migration's items are decided under two projects' gate thresholds and two sets of safeguards, and that a fleet whose entries are scoped to one project cannot be dispatched onto the item in the other — which shows in Work as an item that does not advance, indistinguishable from a fleet too small.

Which stage an item is at is a field on the item, and **dispatch** is what writes it — every stage reports a transition to it, and the component that reads the field on every move is the one that keeps it. Two more fields go with it, written at the same transitions and both kept per stage: how many attempts each stage has taken, and what each stage spent, with the name of the credential it spent through, which is what [_A spend ceiling_](../../10-fleet.md#a-spend-ceiling) sums by. An attempt is counted when a stage is entered to author, so a stage reached once and passed stands at one, and what the events below do is send the item back to be entered again rather than increment anything themselves — which is what makes more than one attempt at a stage mean the work there was redone. The current stage's count is what a gate compares against [_the attempt limit_](../../03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit), because an attempt that failed before a gate fired wrote no decision to count, and it is where an agent's failed attempt, a gate's reject, and [_the merge queue_](../../05-environments.md#the-merge-queue)'s rejection all report — each at the stage the item is sent to rather than at the one it leaves, which is what books a [rework request](../../03-gates.md#going-back-up) against the stage that was wrong; the stages below a rework request's target re-author counting nothing, a [hold](../../03-gates.md#what-a-gate-may-change) reports nothing, and neither does a candidate re-verifying behind someone else's failure. A human taking an escalated item over (12) clears the escalation and the limit counts from there. The attempts already taken stay on the stage, so what the clearing writes through dispatch is the count it cleared at, and the limit is compared against the attempts since. An owner answering an escalated [_interview_](../02-the-interview.md) clears one too, where nothing but the limit reads the rounds and the count may simply start again. Kept rather than reset at each advance, those counts are what [_Factory_](../../11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people)'s rework rate reads. Dispatch matches the stage against the role — and matches nothing where that role's prompt has no [version in force](../../10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told), an agent being told one thing — and reads the item and its intent's state, which with [a credential already known unreachable](../../10-fleet.md#an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold), that missing prompt, and a stage no [fleet entry](../../10-fleet.md) matches is all that stops an item from outside it; the intent's state is how an intent that is not refined gets no agent put on anything below it — unrefined, re-decomposing, escalated, and dropped alike, escalated being the intent that exhausted the counts it keeps and dropped one a human ended for good, so that enumerating only the first two would put an agent back on items the factory has said it cannot decompose, or a human has ended. Deriving it instead — a query over which artifacts exist and which gate decisions passed — would put that join in every reader and would not remove the field: an item that exceeded [_the attempt limit_](../../03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit) is escalated (12), and that value is written for the reason the interview's is, that a limit authored with gate policy (8) changes and a decision read back against a value that was not in force when it was taken is not the decision that was taken. A superseded item is a value here too, and it is what tells one from an item decomposition wrote a moment ago, neither having an artifact yet. An item can also stop for good without reaching a release, and **dropped** is the value that says so — written by dispatch where a human ends work for good: with Work as the caller, where a human stops one that escalated (12) and nobody took over or ends [the intent above it](../02-the-interview.md), and with [_Ops_](../../11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) as the caller, where [a mark](../../08-operations.md#overlapping-windows) ends a revert item before it ships. Merged, dropped, or superseded by a re-decomposition is what frees an item's branch and its environment for good, and [_An environment per candidate_](../../05-environments.md#an-environment-per-candidate) tears one down on exactly those events — and reclaims one meanwhile from an item running nothing, so an escalated item holds its branch until a human ends it one of those ways and holds no environment while it waits.

The item has two writers and the seam is the event: decomposition writes it when it creates one — the intent, the service, the area, the branch name, and what it waits on — and writes an item again only to point a superseded one at what replaced it and to repoint what a standing item waits on where [a re-decomposition](README.md) superseded its dependency, reporting those transitions to dispatch like every other; every write that is not one of those two is dispatch's. An owner reordering a queue writes the [priority](../../05-environments.md#the-merge-queue) through dispatch rather than beside it, the way Work calls intake to answer a question. One writer after decomposition costs a dependency edge from every stage back to dispatch, which is the cost [_Intake_](../01-intake/README.md) already accepts on the intent and for the same reason — the record's rules implemented once rather than once per stage — and it puts a match the design calls not a judgment on the write path of every item, unavailable exactly when nothing can move. What the fields cost is that they can disagree with the artifacts and with the decisions: a transition that fails to write leaves an item naming a stage its artifacts do not show, and a reader asking why an item escalated still reads the decisions to see what the attempts were.
