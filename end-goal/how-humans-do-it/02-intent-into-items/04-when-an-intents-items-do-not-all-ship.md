# When an intent's items do not all ship

An intent whose items did not all ship is an outcome rather than a fault: one item exceeds the attempt limit and appears as an escalation (12), or a human [undoes it after it shipped](../../what-humans-do.md) (10), while its siblings are already live. Nothing writes it down — an intent is partly delivered when at least one of its items stopped without reaching production and at least one sibling is live, an item being live when a production [deploy record](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record.md) names its release — as the one it deployed, or in the list of releases a [revert](../06-releases/06-rollback.md)'s deploy delivered — and marks it complete on every production target, and not before. An intent whose items are all still moving is in progress rather than partly delivered, one where nothing shipped is stopped, and one whose superseded items were replaced by a re-decomposition is judged on the replacements. It is not terminal either: a human taking over an escalated item (12) can finish it, and the intent stops being partly delivered with no event anywhere, which is why a field would have to be unwritten and is not a field. The cost is three joins — intent to items, item to release, release to that service's production deploy record, and a rule over that record's completion per target — so the reading is only as cheap as those inbound edges are indexed, and an item merely held at the production deploy gate never makes its intent one of these however long it waits. The shipped ones stay shipped. Each was decomposed to ship by itself and is worth something by itself — and where it is not, the decomposition was wrong, which is what [the Decomposition gate](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/01-decomposition.md) exists to catch. Undoing the whole thing is one revert item per shipped sibling, joined by the same intent, and never a single undo.

The declared order is what keeps that survivable. An item that fails stops only its dependents: whatever it depended on shipped before it, and whatever depended on it was still held at a deploy gate. So what this leaves is a feature half delivered, not production half broken.
