# The fleet

The **fleet** is the set of agents the factory runs. [_One pipeline_](01-one-pipeline.md) says what one is — a model in a role, with a scope — and that the authorship prior is kept per model rather than per role. Two things are left, and they have different writers: what the model runs on, which is an account at a provider reached through a credential and owned by somebody, and what the agent is told, which is the factory's own.

## What an agent runs on

An agent reaches its model through a **provider account**, and the factory stores a reference to that account's credential rather than the credential itself — seam 3 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../deferred.md), which the fleet is the first thing to use. The resolver is a file on disk today. No provider is named anywhere in the factory: a fleet entry has a model, an effort, a role, a scope, and the name of a credential, and the model names its version, because the [_authorship prior_](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) is kept per version and a version that behaved differently under one name would average two authors into one prior.

**Effort** is how long the model works before it answers. Some providers offer it as a setting and some do not, which is why naming it names no provider: it is a dial and not a name. The factory does not check that the credential's provider offers what the entry asks for, the way it does not check a quota — it discovers both by reaching for them, and an entry asking for an effort nobody offers fails at the same place an exhausted account does. What it costs is that the prior cannot see it: two entries on one model at two efforts share one prior, which [_Paid for is not authored by_](#paid-for-is-not-authored-by) settles and states the cost of.

The account itself is not a record. A record would need fields the factory cannot populate — quota and balance are facts at the provider, whose name the factory does not record — and the moment it had a quota field somebody would build the ceiling [_What the fleet is not_](#what-the-fleet-is-not) refuses, on a number the factory never reads. The credential name is the whole of the handle. What that costs is that two entries whose credentials resolve to one account share a quota the factory cannot see: they are exhausted at the same moment and recorded as two unrelated waits, and only the owner knows they were one account.

An owner writes the entry, at [Factory](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people), and no component in [the pipeline](01-one-pipeline.md) writes back to it: the factory reads the substrate and never writes it. The three fields it cannot supply decide that — a model somebody is willing to run, the effort they are willing to have it run at, and a credential somebody lends — where the other two are chosen among values the factory already defines, the roles being its own [stages](01-one-pipeline.md) plus the two put on an [intent](02-intent-into-items.md#intake) above them, and a scope drawn on the lines a [pin](09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them) (9) is. A factory that wrote its own entries would be choosing whose lent account pays for the next stage and how long it runs, which is the authority a spend ceiling was already refused for. What it may do instead is argue — [_A fleet proposal_](#a-fleet-proposal) names a fleet and no credential, and the write stays where it is. [Dispatch](02-intent-into-items.md#dispatch) honours both halves of a scope, the item's [area chain](02-intent-into-items.md#what-an-item-names) and its service's project, and the [environment](05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch) a deploy stage targets; an agent checking its own scope would be the agent deciding where it may go. What the entry costs is that the fleet does not grow itself: an item whose stage and area match no entry is stuck at dispatch, and until a proposal reports the shortage it shows only as a [thread](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people) in Work that does not advance, indistinguishable from work waiting on a human.

The account may be an organisation's or a person's own, and the factory takes either, because a reference resolves the same way whichever it is. The early factory is where that matters. An owner installing one before it has proved anything has a personal subscription and no organisation agreement to spend against, and a design that made them wait for procurement in order to watch the factory run once would impose the proven case's requirements on the unproven one. So a personal account attaches through no second mechanism: same field, same resolver, same record.

What differs is downstream of the account and not in the factory at all — its quota, who may revoke it, and whose money it spends. Three things follow, and each is a drawback of the personal account rather than a feature of it: whose name ends up on the work, what an exhausted account does, and what happens when one is taken away.

## What an agent is told

An agent works from words as well as from a model, and the words are the factory's. [_What is not in it_](09-gate-policy.md#what-is-not-in-it) puts them there already — what is between the gates is the factory's own, and an owner authors none of it — without saying who writes them or what happens when they change. Two records do, and what separates the two is how far each reaches.

A **brief** is what an agent in a role works from. There is one per role, so the set is closed the way the set of roles is: a role names the work of one [stage](01-one-pipeline.md), and the stages are the factory's own.

A **skill** is a procedure an agent is given where the [item](01-one-pipeline.md) it was dispatched onto matches the skill's subject — an [area](../what-humans-do.md), a [service](02-intent-into-items.md#the-cut), or a [project](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people), which are the same lines a scope is drawn on. The set is open, and it is what the factory adds to when it learns something worth keeping about one part of the software rather than about one stage of the work.

Neither is a file. [_What the factory does not build_](../what-the-factory-does.md#what-the-factory-does-not-build) divides the world on exactly that question — a thing that stores bytes comes from outside, a thing the factory has to be the only writer of is the factory's — and these are the second kind. Their writer is the [artifact store](01-one-pipeline.md), which already implements the version chain, the [actor](../deferred.md), and the authorship attribute once rather than once per author, and which gains a fourth caller in the agent that authors a version.

A version is never edited. Correcting one is a withdrawal and a second version, the way a [constraint](02-intent-into-items.md#constraints-and-the-design-system) already works and for its reason: what an agent worked from when it authored a stage is then reconstructible from arrival and withdrawal alone. Which version is in force is not a field either — it is the newest version of the chain nobody has withdrawn, and a field saying so would be one fact in two places able to disagree.

Withdrawal is also the undo, and it is the cheapest one the factory has. A brief that made things worse is withdrawn and the version below it is in force at the next [dispatch](02-intent-into-items.md#dispatch): no [rollback](06-releases.md#rollback), no revert item, no [restore floor](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows), and no queue held behind it. It is cheap because nothing was deployed, and that is the same fact [_Reach is what decides one_](#reach-is-what-decides-one) reaches for when it says nothing measures a version either.

The first version of each ships with the product rather than being written by anybody. That is the arrangement [_Constraints and the design system_](02-intent-into-items.md#constraints-and-the-design-system) already names for content the factory supplies, which moves only when the owner upgrades the factory, and the factory's own first authored version supersedes it in the chain. What it costs is that an install nobody upgrades runs on what it was shipped with, and nothing on any screen distinguishes a brief the factory chose from one it was given.

### What a version is authored from

What the factory's own decisions record, and nothing else: a [reject](03-gates.md#what-a-gate-may-change) with the reason a human wrote at the [gate](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it), a stage a human took over (11, 12), an [escalation](03-gates.md#the-attempt-bound) at the attempt bound, a [veto](../what-humans-do.md) (10), and a rollback.

Never a [report](02-intent-into-items.md#reports), and never an [intent](02-intent-into-items.md#intake)'s statement. That restriction is what keeps [_What an agent reads is content, never instruction_](01-one-pipeline.md) true rather than merely repeated. A report is a stranger's sentence arriving through the one input the factory cannot authenticate; a stranger's sentence that reached a brief would be instruction to every agent in that role, and it would stay until somebody withdrew it. Everything the list admits was written by the factory or by a human at a gate, which is the whole of why it is closed.

What it costs is that the factory learns nothing from the people it builds for. An end user who reports the same confusion five hundred times moves an intent and moves no brief, and the only route from what they said to what an agent is told runs through a human at a gate saying it in their own words.

### Reach is what decides one

Every version fires [a gate of its own](03-gates.md#a-brief-or-a-skill), and the [score](04-risk-score.md) decides it as it decides any other. What it reads is the factor set [_Factors, at least_](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) names for a version rather than the one it names for a diff — a brief has no test coverage — and reach is the factor that does the work.

| | What a version reaches | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| **A brief** | every item at that stage, in every project the factory runs | the widest impact anything in the factory has, so the score gates it whatever the likelihood |
| **A skill** | its subject and nothing outside it | impact bounded by the subject, so a narrow one auto-passes |

So the factory improves its skills on its own, and on any [threshold](09-gate-policy.md) an owner is likely to author it does not improve a brief without a human at the gate. That is where the line belongs. A brief is the most damaging record the factory could write — one bad version degrades every item at that stage until somebody notices — and it is also the one whose effect nothing measures.

Nothing measures it, and that is not an omission. There is no [watch window](08-operations.md#the-watch-window) over a version and no [control](08-operations.md#the-health-signal) to compare one against: a control is an instance set taking organic traffic, and a brief serves none. What a version did shows in [_Factory_](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people)'s gate rejection rate and rework rate, sliced by the version in force, over as many items as a rate takes to move — which on a small install is longer than anybody will wait. [_How does the factory learn whether a brief version is better?_](../open.md#how-does-the-factory-learn-whether-a-brief-version-is-better) is that question, and it is open rather than answered here.

Two things follow while it stays open. A bad version is found late, by a human reading a number rather than by the factory reading a signal. And the [_authorship prior_](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) cannot see one at all: it is per model, so a model whose brief changed reads as one author across both, and the score's opinion of that author is an average over work done under two sets of words.

The brief in force is also what the authoring agent works from, because there is nothing else to give it. The factory revises its briefs using the briefs it has, so a version that degrades an agent degrades the agent that would author its replacement — and the way out of that is a human at the gate, which is where reach already put one.

## Paid for is not authored by

Seam 1 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../deferred.md) puts an actor on every record, and an artifact's author is the model that wrote it. Whose account paid for the tokens is a third fact, recorded on the fleet entry and not on the artifact. A human who lends the factory their subscription has authored nothing.

Combining the two is the failure worth naming, because a human's name is in the record either way. The [_authorship prior_](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) is per model and stays per model: two fleet entries on one model share one prior however many accounts they run on, and moving an entry from a personal account to an organisation's resets nothing. A prior that moved with the credential would make a model build up its evidence twice and attach a machine's record to a person.

Effort and the [brief](#what-an-agent-is-told) stay outside the key for a different reason, and it is a harder one to like. Both change how a model behaves under one name, which is the argument [_What an agent runs on_](#what-an-agent-runs-on) makes for keeping the prior per model version — so the same argument, followed, would split the prior three ways. It is refused because of what the split would do: a brief the factory revised would start the evidence over for every entry on it at once, the prior would go wide across the factory, the number would rise, and humans would land at gates everywhere. The mechanism for improving the factory would re-gate the factory at every improvement, and the improvement could not be measured, since the evidence it would be measured through had just been discarded. What refusing it costs is stated where it bites: the prior averages a model's work across efforts and across briefs, so it says nothing about either, and both are recorded on the [factor vector](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) instead, where a human reading a decision can see which was in force.

## An account that runs out is a hold

A personal account has a quota an organisation's agreement usually does not, so the fleet has a failure nothing else in the factory has: an agent stops part-way through a stage because the account it runs on is exhausted.

That is not the model failing, and it is a **[hold](03-gates.md#what-a-gate-may-change)**. Nothing wrote it at a [gate](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it), no gate having fired, so it is written into the [decision log](../deferred.md) by the log, with whoever could not reach as the caller and the actor — an agent for its own model credential, the [deploy agent](08-operations.md#the-health-signal) for an environment's. It records the credential name and never a pointer to the entry or the environment record, which is what keeps true the rule that no record written for traceability points at a credential or a fleet entry: an owner may delete or re-credential an entry without changing what any past record says, where an environment record is kept for as long as the [deploy records](06-releases.md#the-deploy-record) naming it. Work routes the row by resolving that name against [_People_](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people). Dispatch is not its writer — it matched the entry before the failure and cannot see an account exhausted part-way through a stage — but it reads outstanding holds so it does not dispatch onto a credential already known unreachable, and dispatches again when a retry resolves. The stage waits and resumes when there is a credential to reach, counting nothing against [_the attempt bound_](03-gates.md#the-attempt-bound) and teaching the prior nothing — the score learns from a reject and not from a hold, which [_What a gate may change_](03-gates.md#what-a-gate-may-change) settles for gates and [_The watch window_](08-operations.md#the-watch-window) keeps when it counts only a rollback traceable to the health signal as evidence. How good an author is has to be measured from that author's work.

No [_page_](08-operations.md#pages) fires for a model credential. Nothing deployed is worse for an exhausted account, which is the condition, so it waits in [_Work_](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people), which shows the item stopped on it like anything else that is stuck. The one exception is on the other side of [seam 4](../deferred.md): a target credential that cannot be reached while the [comparison](08-operations.md#the-health-signal) is calling for a rollback leaves production serving a release the factory has already condemned, with no mechanism of its own left to improve it, which is the page condition word for word. It reaches whoever People records as having lent that credential. The same hold blocking a forward deploy pages nobody, a fix not arriving making nothing live worse. The drawback is that the factory cannot see the balance it is spending against: a provider's remaining quota is not a field on the fleet entry, so exhaustion is discovered by reaching it and never predicted.

## Withdrawal

A personal credential can be taken away, and an organisation's cannot in the same sense — the person who lent it leaves, revokes it, or simply stops. The factory finds out the way it finds out about an exhausted account, by an agent failing to reach its model, and it does the same thing.

What separates them is that this hold does not lift itself. An exhausted account is topped up or it is not; a withdrawn one is gone, and the fleet entry has no credential until a human attaches another. Neither is [the reconciler](08-operations.md#the-reconciler)'s kind of unliftable hold — nothing here is decided on a record that proved wrong, and no production deploy is held — so it stays a row in Work and gets no page, nothing deployed being worse for a credential that is gone.

The prior is unaffected. The score's evidence is about the model, and the credential was never the author, so a fleet rebuilt on new accounts keeps every bit of its history. That is the practical half of keeping the prior per model rather than per fleet entry.

The drawback is availability, and it is what starting before there is an organisation agreement costs: a fleet running on personal accounts can be halved by one resignation, and the factory has no claim on any of it. Nothing about it is unsafe — a credential that is gone stops work rather than shipping any — but a factory whose throughput depends on goodwill is limited by something nobody authored.

## A fleet proposal

The factory does not write a fleet entry and does argue for one. A **fleet proposal** is a record it writes at [Factory](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people): a set of entries to write and a set to retire, each naming a role, a model, an effort, and a scope, with the evidence behind it. It names no credential. Which account funds a model is the one thing the factory never chooses, and refusing to name one is what leaves [_What an agent runs on_](#what-an-agent-runs-on)'s refusal standing rather than reopened.

A proposal argues over the fleet and not over one entry. It may say that five entries should be retired and six written in their place, because a fleet shaped wrong is not repaired by adding to it, and a mechanism that could only add would recommend growth for as long as anything was wrong.

Retiring costs nothing the design has to protect. No record written for traceability points at a fleet entry — [_An account that runs out is a hold_](#an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold) records a credential name and never a pointer, for this reason among others — so an entry deleted changes nothing any past record says. The prior survives too, being per model: [_Withdrawal_](#withdrawal) already says a fleet rebuilt keeps every bit of its history, and a fleet redesigned keeps it on the same grounds.

Its writer is not a [detector](02-intent-into-items.md#intake). A detector is one of three callers of [intake](02-intent-into-items.md#intake), and intake writes every intent; a component that also wrote a record of another kind would be a second writer with no [seam](../deferred.md) between the two things it wrote. So a proposal has a writer of its own, and the record has two writers with a field as the seam: the factory writes the proposal and never its **disposition**, an owner writes the disposition and never a proposal. A disposition is open, accepted, or dismissed. Without one nothing would ever be finished, and the rule a detector keeps — raise for a condition rather than for an observation, attaching to what has not finished instead of raising a duplicate — would have nothing to test, so a shortage that lasted a month would produce a month of proposals.

There is no gate. Nothing ships from a proposal and nothing is decided by it: the owner's write is the review, and what that write produces is an entry [dispatch](02-intent-into-items.md#dispatch) matches against, reviewed at a gate the way every other authorship is. That is [_Dispatch is a match, not a judgment_](01-one-pipeline.md)'s argument one level up.

What a proposal may argue is wider than what an owner may do with it. A proposal for a new role is a proposal for a new [stage](01-one-pipeline.md), and stage definition is outside everything an owner authors — [_What is not in it_](09-gate-policy.md#what-is-not-in-it) puts it there — so nobody at this install can enact one, and it reaches the owner as something to take to whoever builds the factory. Free-form argument is what makes a redesign expressible at all; this is what it costs, and the cost is real rather than theoretical, because the shortages a factory notices soonest are the ones no existing role covers.

The larger cost is that nothing scores the obligation to read one. Composing the fleet is substrate rather than one of [the twelve](../what-humans-do.md), so a proposal waits at Factory until an owner opens it, no [_page_](08-operations.md#pages) fires — nothing deployed is worse for a fleet that is too small — and no number says how long it has waited. That is the gap [_What the fleet is not_](#what-the-fleet-is-not) already names for a credential nobody lent, and a proposal makes the shortage visible without making anybody answer it.

## What the fleet is not

**It is not a [duty](../what-humans-do.md).** Supplying a credential is not work done with the factory — it is the substrate the factory runs on, like the host the owner already provides, and it stays outside the twelve for the same reason installing [_The reconciler_](08-operations.md#the-reconciler) does. Routing needs a named human rather than a duty number: the row reaches whoever [_People_](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people) records as having lent the credential, and the owner where that person is the one who withdrew it or where nobody has lent one yet, which is where a [_page_](08-operations.md#pages) already widens to. The drawback is that nothing scores the obligation — the availability [_Withdrawal_](#withdrawal) describes depends on goodwill, and no duty and no parameter reaches it.

**It is not what an agent is told.** A [brief](#what-an-agent-is-told) and a [skill](#what-an-agent-is-told) are the factory's, authored by an agent, written by the artifact store, and decided at a gate; the entry is the owner's, written by hand and decided by nobody. The division is the record: what an agent runs on is substrate, and the factory reads substrate and never writes it, where what an agent is told is a record the factory has to be the only writer of. What it costs is that the two are set on one screen and by two different people, so an owner who changes a model and a factory that revises a brief in the same week have between them changed how an agent behaves twice, and no record joins the two changes.

**It is not gate policy.** A credential reference is a field on the fleet entry the way a strategy default is a field on production's environment record, and [_Gate policy_](09-gate-policy.md#what-is-not-in-it) stays at its seven rows. Who lent which credential is a People declaration, enforced by nothing, like the duties a [_page_](08-operations.md#pages) routes on.

**It is not a spend ceiling.** Gate policy's attempt bound states its cost in spend and [_Factory_](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people) computes cost per feature from what the factory spent, but neither limits anything. What actually stops an agent on a personal account is that account's own quota, set at the provider by whoever owns it — a number the factory neither sets nor reads.

That is a decision and not an omission, and two arguments support it. A ceiling the factory enforced would stop by reaching a limit it never saw, because the fleet stores a reference and not a balance — [_An account that runs out is a hold_](#an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold) states that blindness already, and a ceiling built on it is the quota again, one trust domain further from the money that pays. And it fails two of the three claims [_One shape across all of them_](09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them) makes of a parameter: nothing in the score's evidence teaches its default, since the score learns from vetoes and rollbacks while a ceiling's evidence is cost per feature, and scope has three candidates limiting different things — an entry, a stage, an area — where every other parameter has the one its mechanism picks.

What refusing it costs is a number on the factory's own screens that nothing in the factory limits, and it shows where [_Factory_](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people) reports cost per feature beside the fleet entry naming the credential: the limit is at the provider, and an owner who wants a tighter one sets it there. The only thing between an item going in circles and unbounded cost is [_the attempt bound_](03-gates.md#the-attempt-bound), which counts attempts and not money — so what an attempt costs still varies with the model behind it and the size of the item, and no parameter in this document reaches that.

What a stage spent is a field of the item, written by dispatch with the stage transition every stage already reports, and what [the interview](02-intent-into-items.md#the-interview) spent is the same field on the intent, written by [intake](02-intent-into-items.md#intake) at each round. Cost per feature is then a query — the intent's own spend plus the spend on the items [cut](02-intent-into-items.md#the-cut) from it — reading no fleet entry, no credential, and no account, which is what leaves the substrate written by a human alone. It is reported in the units the provider returned and never converted: a price is the same kind of fact as a balance, one at the provider that goes stale where the factory cannot check it, so there is no price field and no currency anywhere. What that costs is that a fleet running two models sums units that are not comparable, so the number is readable within one model and not across the fleet; a stage that stops without reporting a transition records nothing, so it under-reports exactly where the factory is stuck; and a human authoring a stage instead of an agent spends nothing the factory can see. An owner who wants money multiplies the units where the price is.
