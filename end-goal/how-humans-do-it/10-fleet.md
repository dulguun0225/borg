# The fleet

The **fleet** is the set of agents the factory runs. [_One pipeline_](01-one-pipeline.md) says what one is — a model in a role, with a scope — and that the per-author prior is kept per model rather than per role. Two things are left, and they have different writers: what the model runs on, which is an account at a provider reached through a credential and owned by somebody, and what the agent is told, which is the factory's own.

## What an agent runs on

An agent reaches its model through a **provider account**, and the factory stores a reference to that account's credential rather than the credential itself — seam 3 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../deferred.md), which the fleet is the first thing to use. The [resolver](../deferred.md) is a file on disk today. No provider is named anywhere in the factory: a fleet entry has a model, an effort, a role, a scope, and the name of a credential, and the model names its version, because the [_per-author prior_](04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) is kept per version and a version that behaved differently under one name would average two authors into one prior.

**Effort** is how long the model works before it answers. Some providers offer it as a setting and some do not, which is why naming it names no provider: it is a dial and not a name. The factory does not check that the credential's provider offers what the entry asks for, the way it does not check a quota — it discovers both by reaching for them, and an entry asking for an effort nobody offers fails at the same place an exhausted account does. What it costs: the prior cannot see it — two entries on one model at two efforts share one prior, which [_Paid for is not authored by_](#paid-for-is-not-authored-by) settles and states the cost of.

The account itself is not a record. A record would need fields the factory cannot populate — quota and balance are facts at the provider, whose name the factory does not record — and a quota field would have [_A spend ceiling_](#a-spend-ceiling) compared against a number the factory never reads, where it compares against what the factory recorded itself. The credential name is the whole of the handle. What that costs: two entries whose credentials resolve to one account share a quota the factory cannot see — they are exhausted at the same moment and recorded as two unrelated waits, and only the owner knows they were one account.

An owner writes the entry, at [Factory](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), and no component in [the pipeline](01-one-pipeline.md) writes back to it: the factory reads the [substrate](../what-humans-do.md) and never writes it. The three fields it cannot supply decide that — a model somebody is willing to run, the effort they are willing to have it run at, and a credential somebody lends — where the other two are chosen among values the factory already defines, the roles being its own [stages](01-one-pipeline.md) plus the two put on an [intent](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) above them and [the grouper](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) above those — none of them a deploy, which the [deployer](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) performs and no entry is put on — and a scope drawn on the lines a [safeguard](09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them) (9) is. A factory that wrote its own entries would be choosing whose lent account pays for the next stage and how long it runs, which is the authority [_A spend ceiling_](#a-spend-ceiling) keeps with the owner: an owner authors what may be spent through a credential, and the factory never does. What it may do instead is argue — [_A fleet proposal_](#a-fleet-proposal) names a fleet and no credential, and the write stays where it is. [Dispatch](02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) honours both halves of a scope, the item's [area chain](02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/02-what-an-item-names.md) and its service's project; an agent checking its own scope would be the agent deciding where it may go. What the entry costs is that the fleet does not grow itself: an item whose stage and [area](../what-humans-do.md) match no entry is stuck at dispatch, and until a proposal reports the shortage it shows only as a [timeline](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) in Work that does not advance, indistinguishable from work waiting on a human.

The account may be an organisation's or a person's own, and the factory takes either, because a reference resolves the same way whichever it is. The early factory is where that matters. An owner installing one before it has proved anything has a personal subscription and no organisation agreement to spend against, and a design that made them wait for procurement in order to watch the factory run once would impose the proven case's requirements on the unproven one. So a personal account attaches through no second mechanism: same field, same resolver, same record.

What differs is downstream of the account and not in the factory at all — its quota, who may revoke it, and whose money it spends. Three things follow, and each is a drawback of the personal account rather than a feature of it: whose name ends up on the work, what an exhausted account does, and what happens when one is taken away.

## A model under a name

The version an entry names is a name the provider serves behind and not a function it promises to keep: what answers under one pinned version moves when the provider changes how it is served, and the name does not. The [_per-author prior_](04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) is kept per version so that two authors are never averaged into one, and a version that drifts under its name is that averaging arriving anyway, with the wrong sign — a new model enters with a wide prior and is put in front of a human at more [gates](03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md), which is right, while a drifted one keeps the narrow prior it earned before the drift and is gated less at the moment it became worse. The outcomes on the version's work are the factory's only other instrument, and they read as a rate over as many items as a rate takes to move. So the factory holds one instrument that is not an outcome: an **evaluation set** per [role](01-one-pipeline.md), a fixed set of tasks with known answers, shipped with the product as [what an agent is told](#what-an-agent-is-told) is shipped — content and not a record, moved only at an upgrade, authored by nobody at the install. It is run against an entry's model where the entry is written or its model or effort changed, and again after every so many dispatches onto the entry, that count being a field of the entry the owner writes with the rest of it — the entry is the owner's record and the run spends the owner's credential, so how often to spend it is theirs and no default is supplied beyond the run at the write. The run is dispatched like a stage's work and spends through the entry's credential like one, and its result is a record keyed by model version, effort, and the set's version, written by dispatch, which performed it. What the score reads from it is the departure of a result from that version's earlier results on the same set — the reading [_How it learns_](04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md) already makes for a factor whose distribution moved — and a version found departed takes the treatment a drifted factor takes: its prior reads as an unfamiliar author's, toward the gate, with the [factor vector](04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) on each decision naming why, until the evidence since the departure narrows it again. What it costs: spend on work that ships nothing, at a rate the owner sets; a set the provider may have trained on, which reads as a model that did not drift; a fixed set that measures the set and not the work, so a departure says the model changed and never in which direction; and a first run with nothing to depart from — the entry's first result is the baseline, and a model that arrived already drifted is caught by outcomes or not at all.

The other end of the same fact is a version that stops being served. An owner moves every entry naming it to its successor, and [_Paid for is not authored by_](#paid-for-is-not-authored-by) says what a prior starting over on every entry at once does: the number rises and humans land at gates across the factory. The product ships a starting prior per model version, beside the evaluation set and the first role prompts, measured by [the builder](../what-the-factory-does.md#what-the-customer-runs-it-under) on the builder's own work, and an entry naming a version the product shipped a prior for starts there rather than wide; the install's own evidence moves it from there as it would from wide, and a shipped prior never replaces evidence the install already holds, an upgrade seeding only the versions the install has none on. What it costs: a prior measured on the builder's work and not this customer's, narrower than the evidence justifies wherever the two differ; a version the builder never measured starting wide as before; and the one channel by which anything learned outside an install reaches it, stated in [_What the factory does_](../what-the-factory-does.md) beside the tenancy decision it is the price of.

## What an agent is told

An agent works from words as well as from a model, and the words are the factory's. [_What is not in it_](09-gate-policy.md#what-is-not-in-it) puts them there already — what is between the gates is the factory's own, and an owner authors none of it — without saying who writes them or what happens when they change. Two records do, and what separates the two is how far each reaches.

A **role prompt** is what an agent in a role works from. There is one per role, so the set is closed the way the set of roles is: a role names the work of one [stage](01-one-pipeline.md), of the two put on an [intent](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md), or of [the grouper](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md), and all of them are the factory's own.

A **skill** is a procedure an agent is given where the [item](01-one-pipeline.md) it was dispatched onto matches the skill's subject — an [area](../what-humans-do.md), a [service](02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md), or a [project](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), which are the same lines a scope is drawn on. The set is open, and it is what the factory adds to when it learns something worth keeping about one part of the software rather than about one stage of the work.

Neither is a file. [_What the factory does not build_](../what-the-factory-does.md#what-the-factory-does-not-build) divides the world on exactly that question — a thing that stores bytes comes from outside, a thing the factory has to be the only writer of is the factory's — and these are the second kind. Their writer is the [artifact store](01-one-pipeline.md), which already implements the version chain — the versions of one thing in the order they arrived — the [actor](../deferred.md), and the authorship attribute once rather than once per author, and which gains another caller in the agent that authors a version.

A version is never edited. Correcting one is a withdrawal and a second version, the way a [constraint](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) already works and for its reason: what an agent worked from when it authored a stage is then reconstructible from arrival and withdrawal alone. Which version is in force is not a field either — it is the newest version of the chain that its [gate](03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/09-a-role-prompt-or-a-skill.md) approved and nobody has withdrawn, a version not approved being not in force with the one below it in force still — nothing at all where an upgrade started the chain — and the entries install wrote standing without an approval, a factory with nothing decided in it having to run — and a field saying so would be one fact in two places able to disagree.

Withdrawal is also the undo, and it is the cheapest one the factory has. A role prompt that made things worse is withdrawn and the version below it is in force at the next [dispatch](02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md): no [rollback](06-releases.md#rollback), no revert item, no [last known-good release](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows), and no queue held behind it. It is cheap because nothing was deployed, and that is the same fact [_Reach is what decides one_](#reach-is-what-decides-one) reaches for when it says nothing measures a version either.

The first version of each ships with the product rather than being written by anybody. That is the arrangement [_Constraints and the design system_](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) already names for content the factory supplies, and the factory's own first authored version supersedes it in the chain. An upgrade that changed the shipped words moves nothing by itself: at the factory's first start on the new version, what shipped enters the chain on the same authoring-nothing call that entered the first version at install — the artifact store writing it, the author field empty as the exception [_One pipeline_](01-one-pipeline.md) states — and it enters as a version awaiting [the gate every version fires](03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/09-a-role-prompt-or-a-skill.md): a version not approved is not in force, so where it extends a chain the words the install ran on stand until the gate decides, and where it starts one — the role or the skill an upgrade adds — nothing is in force until then, only the work that would have used it waiting: a new role's items undispatched in [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), [dispatch](02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) matching nothing whose prompt has no version in force, and an item matching a new skill's subject running without it. The install's entries alone enter in force ungated — a factory with nothing decided in it has to run, the acceptance [_Gate policy_](09-gate-policy.md) already makes for the values the score supplies. What decides it is the gate's ordinary arithmetic, and an empty author is a factor the score cannot compute, resolved the way [an unavailable factor](04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) always is — toward the gate — so the changed words of a shipped role prompt or skill reach force through a human whichever way its reach alone would have gone. The same event makes [_Constraints and the design system_](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) write a withdrawal-and-arrival pair; a role prompt has the version chain and a gate a constraint does not, so here the gate is the answer. What it costs: an install nobody upgrades runs on what it was shipped with, nothing on any screen distinguishes a role prompt the factory chose from one it was given, and an upgrade adding a stage stalls every item reaching it until a human decides the shipped words and writes a fleet entry for the new role — no page fires and no attempt counts while they wait, and both ends of the wait are at [_Factory_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people).

### What a version is authored from

What the factory's own decisions record, and nothing else: a [reject](03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) with the reason a human wrote at the [gate](03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md), a stage a human took over (11, 12), an [escalation at the attempt limit](03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md), a human's [undo of a shipped change](../what-humans-do.md) (10), and a rollback.

Never a [report](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md), and never an [intent](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md)'s statement. That restriction is what keeps [_What an agent reads is content, never instruction_](01-one-pipeline.md) true rather than merely repeated. A report is a stranger's sentence arriving through the one input the factory cannot authenticate; a stranger's sentence that reached a role prompt would be instruction to every agent in that role, and it would stay until somebody withdrew it. Everything the list admits was written by the factory or by a human at a gate, which is the whole of why it is closed.

What it costs: the factory learns nothing from the people it builds for. An end user who reports the same confusion five hundred times moves an intent and moves no role prompt, and the only route from what they said to what an agent is told runs through a human at a gate saying it in their own words.

### Reach is what decides one

Every version fires [a gate of its own](03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/09-a-role-prompt-or-a-skill.md), and the [score](04-risk-score/README.md) decides it as it decides any other. What it reads is the factor set [_Factors, at least_](04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) names for a version rather than the one it names for a diff — a role prompt has no code to have sized — and reach is the factor that does the work.

| | What a version reaches | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| **A role prompt** | every item at that stage, in every project the factory runs | the widest impact anything in the factory has, so the score gates it whatever the likelihood |
| **A skill** | its subject and nothing outside it | impact bounded by the subject, so a narrow one [auto-passes](03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) |

So the factory improves its skills on its own, and on any [threshold](09-gate-policy.md) an owner is likely to author it does not improve a role prompt without a human at the gate. That is where the line belongs. A role prompt is the most damaging record the factory could write — one bad version degrades every item at that stage until somebody notices — and it is also the one whose effect nothing measures.

Nothing measures it, and that is not an omission. There is no [analysis window](08-operations.md#the-analysis-window) over a version and no [control](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) to compare one against: a control is instances taking organic traffic, and a role prompt serves none. What a version did shows in [_Factory_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people)'s gate rejection rate and rework rate, sliced by the version in force, over as many items as a rate takes to move — which on a small install is longer than anybody will wait. [_How does the factory learn whether a role prompt version is better?_](../open.md#how-does-the-factory-learn-whether-a-role-prompt-version-is-better) is that question, and it is open rather than answered here.

Two things follow while it stays open. A bad version is found late, by a human reading a number rather than by the factory reading a signal. And the [_per-author prior_](04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) cannot see one at all: it is per model, so a model whose role prompt changed reads as one author across both, and the score's opinion of that author is an average over work done under two sets of words.

The role prompt in force is also what the authoring agent works from, because there is nothing else to give it. The factory revises its role prompts using the role prompts it has, so a version that degrades an agent degrades the agent that would author its replacement — and the way out of that is a human at the gate, which is where reach already put one.

## Paid for is not authored by

Seam 1 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../deferred.md) puts an actor on every record, and an artifact's author is the model that wrote it. Whose account paid for the tokens is a third fact, recorded on the fleet entry and not on the artifact. A human who lends the factory their subscription has authored nothing.

Combining the two is the failure worth naming, because a human's name is in the record either way. The [_per-author prior_](04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) is per model and stays per model: two fleet entries on one model share one prior however many accounts they run on, and moving an entry from a personal account to an organisation's resets nothing. A prior that moved with the credential would make a model build up its evidence twice and attach a machine's record to a person.

Effort and the [role prompt](#what-an-agent-is-told) stay outside the key for a different reason, and it is a harder one to like. Both change how a model behaves under one name, which is the argument [_What an agent runs on_](#what-an-agent-runs-on) makes for keeping the prior per model version — so the same argument, followed, would split the prior three ways. It is refused because of what the split would do: a role prompt the factory revised would start the evidence over for every entry on it at once, the prior would go wide across the factory, the number would rise, and humans would land at gates everywhere. The mechanism for improving the factory would re-gate the factory at every improvement, and the improvement could not be measured, since the evidence it would be measured through had just been discarded. What refusing it costs, stated where it bites: the prior averages a model's work across efforts and across role prompts, so it says nothing about either, and both are recorded on the [factor vector](04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) instead, where a human reading a decision can see which was in force.

## An account that runs out is a hold

A personal account has a quota an organisation's agreement usually does not, so the fleet has a failure nothing else in the factory has: an agent stops part-way through a stage because the account it runs on is exhausted.

That is not the model failing, and it is a **[hold](03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md)**. Nothing wrote it at a [gate](03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md), no gate having fired, so it is written into the [decision log](../deferred.md) by the log, with whoever could not reach as the caller and the actor — an agent for its own model credential, the [deployer](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) for an environment's. It records the credential name and never a pointer to the entry or the environment record, which is what keeps true the rule that no record written for traceability points at a credential or a fleet entry: an owner may delete or re-credential an entry without changing what any past record says, where an environment record is kept for as long as the [deploy records](06-releases.md#the-deploy-record) naming it. Work routes the row by resolving that name against [_People_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people). Dispatch is not its writer — it matched the entry before the failure and cannot see an account exhausted part-way through a stage — but it reads outstanding holds so it does not dispatch onto a credential already known unreachable, and dispatches again when a retry resolves. The stage waits and resumes when there is a credential to reach, counting nothing against [_the attempt limit_](03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) and teaching the prior nothing — the score learns from a reject and not from a hold, which [_What a gate may change_](03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) settles for gates and [_The analysis window_](08-operations.md#the-analysis-window) keeps when it counts only a rollback traceable to the [health monitor](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) as evidence. How good an author is has to be measured from that author's work.

No [_page_](08-operations.md#pages) fires for a model credential. Nothing deployed is worse for an exhausted account, which is the condition, so it waits in [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), which shows the item stopped on it like anything else that is stuck. The one exception is on the other side of [seam 4](../deferred.md): the credential for a [deploy target](05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md), held on that environment's record and never on an entry, that cannot be reached while the [health monitor](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) is calling for a rollback leaves production serving a release the factory has already failed, with no mechanism of its own left to improve it, which is the page condition word for word. It reaches whoever People records as having lent that credential. The same hold blocking a forward deploy pages nobody, a fix not arriving making nothing live worse. The drawback: the factory cannot see the balance it is spending against — a provider's remaining quota is not a field on the fleet entry, so exhaustion is discovered by reaching it and never predicted. What stops before it is [_A spend ceiling_](#a-spend-ceiling), where an owner authored one, and that stops on the factory's own records rather than on the provider's.

## A credential taken back

A personal credential can be taken back, and an organisation's cannot in the same sense — the person who lent it leaves, revokes it, or simply stops. The factory finds out the way it finds out about an exhausted account, by an agent failing to reach its model, and it does the same thing.

What separates them is that this hold does not lift itself. An exhausted account is topped up or it is not; one taken back is gone, and the fleet entry has no credential until a human attaches another. Neither is [the drift detector](08-operations.md#drift-detection)'s kind of unliftable hold — nothing here is decided on a record that proved wrong, and no production deploy is held — so it stays a row in Work and gets no page, nothing deployed being worse for a credential that is gone.

The prior is unaffected. The score's evidence is about the model, and the credential was never the author, so a fleet rebuilt on new accounts keeps every bit of its history. That is the practical half of keeping the prior per model rather than per fleet entry.

The drawback is availability — what starting before there is an organisation agreement costs: a fleet running on personal accounts can be halved by one resignation, and the factory has no claim on any of it. Nothing about it is unsafe — a credential that is gone stops work rather than shipping any — but a factory whose throughput depends on goodwill is limited by something nobody authored.

## A fleet proposal

The factory does not write a fleet entry and does argue for one. A **fleet proposal** is a record it writes at [Factory](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people): a set of entries to write and a set to retire, each naming a role, a model, an effort, and a scope, with the evidence behind it. It names no credential. Which account funds a model is the one thing the factory never chooses, and refusing to name one is what leaves [_What an agent runs on_](#what-an-agent-runs-on)'s refusal standing rather than reopened.

A proposal argues over the fleet and not over one entry. It may say that five entries should be retired and six written in their place, because a fleet shaped wrong is not repaired by adding to it, and a mechanism that could only add would recommend growth for as long as anything was wrong.

Retiring costs nothing the design has to protect. No record written for traceability points at a fleet entry — [_An account that runs out is a hold_](#an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold) records a credential name and never a pointer, for this reason among others — so an entry deleted changes nothing any past record says. The prior survives too, being per model: [_A credential taken back_](#a-credential-taken-back) already says a fleet rebuilt keeps every bit of its history, and a fleet redesigned keeps it on the same grounds.

Its writer is not a [detector](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md). A detector is one of three callers of [intake](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md), and intake writes every intent; a component that also wrote a record of another kind would be a second writer with no [seam](../deferred.md) between the two things it wrote. So a proposal has a writer of its own, and the record has two writers with a field as the seam: the factory writes the proposal and never its **disposition**, an owner writes the disposition and never a proposal. A disposition is open, accepted, or dismissed. Without one nothing would ever be finished, and the rule a detector keeps — raise for a condition rather than for an observation, attaching to what has not finished instead of raising a duplicate — would have nothing to test, so a shortage that lasted a month would produce a month of proposals.

There is no gate. Nothing ships from a proposal and nothing is decided by it: the owner's write is the review, and what that write produces is an entry [dispatch](02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) matches against, reviewed at a gate the way every other authorship is. That is [_Dispatch is a match, not a judgment_](01-one-pipeline.md)'s argument one level up.

What a proposal may argue is wider than what an owner may do with it. A proposal for a new role is a proposal for a new [stage](01-one-pipeline.md), and stage definition is outside everything an owner authors — [_What is not in it_](09-gate-policy.md#what-is-not-in-it) puts it there — so nobody at this install can enact one, and it reaches the owner as something to take to whoever builds the factory. Free-form argument is what makes a redesign expressible at all; this is what it costs, and the cost is real, because the shortages a factory notices soonest are the ones no existing role covers.

The larger cost is that nothing scores the obligation to read one. Composing the fleet is substrate rather than one of [the twelve](../what-humans-do.md), so a proposal waits at Factory until an owner opens it, no [_page_](08-operations.md#pages) fires — nothing deployed is worse for a fleet that is too small — and no number says how long it has waited. That is the gap [_What the fleet is not_](#what-the-fleet-is-not) already names for a credential nobody lent, and a proposal makes the shortage visible without making anybody answer it.

## A spend ceiling

A **spend ceiling** is a limit an owner authors on what the factory spends through one credential, in the units the provider returns, and reaching it is a [hold](03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md). It is authored on the [_People_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) declaration that records who lent the credential, which is already per credential name and owner-written, and it has no default: where none is authored nothing is compared, and the provider's quota is the only stop, which is what every install had before. The credential is the scope because it is where the money is: one credential is one account at one provider and one invoice, where an entry, a stage, or an area each draw on accounts whose invoices are separate. It reaches a model credential only, since what is recorded is what a stage and an interview spent; authored on the credential an environment reaches its targets through it compares nothing, no deploy recording spend. What the scope costs: a credential carrying two models sums units the provider prices differently, so the ceiling is exact on a credential running one model and a bound on units rather than money otherwise — an owner who wants it exact lends one credential per model.

What it is compared against is the factory's own record. What a stage spent is a field of the item, written by dispatch with the stage transition every stage already reports, and what [the interview](02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md) spent is the same field on the intent, written by [intake](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) at each round. Each carries the name of the credential the stage ran on — a name and never a pointer, the rule [_An account that runs out is a hold_](#an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold) keeps for the same reason, so an entry retired leaves the field readable. The sum the ceiling is compared against is a query over the items and intents whose stages ran on that name since the newest clear, and it reads nothing at the provider — the same construction [_the attempt limit_](03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) uses when it compares against the item's own count rather than the log — so the fleet storing a reference and not a balance is no obstacle, since no balance is read. Cost per feature is the same field read the other way: the intent's own spend plus the spend on the items [decomposed](02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) from it, reading no fleet entry and no account, the name on the field resolving nothing, which leaves the substrate written by a human alone. It is reported per model version and never summed across models — a feature's stages run in more than one role, a role may run on a different model, and one number over them would add units that do not add. It is in the units the provider returned and never converted: a price is the same kind of fact as a balance, one at the provider that goes stale where the factory cannot check it, so there is no price field and no currency anywhere, and an owner who wants money multiplies the units where the price is. What that costs: a feature on a fleet of two models has two numbers and no total; a stage that stops without reporting a transition records nothing, so the field under-reports exactly where the factory is stuck; and a human authoring a stage instead of an agent spends nothing the factory can see.

Reaching the ceiling is the hold [_An account that runs out is a hold_](#an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold) describes, written and routed the same way: into the [decision log](../deferred.md) by the log, naming the credential, no [_page_](08-operations.md#pages), no attempt counted, the prior taught nothing, and [dispatch](02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) putting nothing further onto that name until it clears. The owner clears it at [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), and clearing restarts the count, the pattern an [escalated interview](02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md) already uses: clearing is a decision to spend more, and a ceiling that went on counting would be reached again at the next stage. There is no period and no clock. The ceiling is a total since the last clear and not a rate, because nothing else in the factory reads a calendar and a rate would be the first thing that did.

It is not one of [gate policy](09-gate-policy.md#what-is-in-it)'s eight. Every row there has a value the score supplies and a safeguard narrows; the score learns from rollbacks and the changes humans undo, and a ceiling's evidence is cost, which the score does not read — so there is nothing to supply and nothing to safeguard, and it is authored outright, beside the [service level objective](08-operations.md#service-level-objectives) and the [fraction of its instances a release keeps](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows), which [_What is not in it_](09-gate-policy.md#what-is-not-in-it) lists.

What it costs: a stop a human has to clear. It bounds model spend and nothing on the host — the [environment per candidate](05-environments/02-an-environment-per-candidate.md) and the fleet a release keeps are hosting the owner pays for outside the factory, so the ceiling bounds the provider's invoice and never the hosting one. It is reached late by whatever a stage that stopped without reporting left unrecorded. Two entries on one account under two names are two ceilings over one quota, and only the owner knows they were one. And it reads agent work alone, a human's stage spending nothing it can see.

## What the fleet is not

**It is not a [duty](../what-humans-do.md).** Supplying a credential is not work done with the factory — it is the substrate the factory runs on, like the host the owner already provides, and it stays outside the twelve for the same reason installing [_Drift detection_](08-operations.md#drift-detection) does. Routing needs a named human rather than a duty number: the row reaches whoever [_People_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) records as having lent the credential, and the owner where that person is the one who took it back or where nobody has lent one yet, which is where a [_page_](08-operations.md#pages) already widens to. The drawback: nothing scores the obligation — the availability [_A credential taken back_](#a-credential-taken-back) describes depends on goodwill, and no duty and no parameter reaches it.

**It is not what an agent is told.** A [role prompt](#what-an-agent-is-told) and a [skill](#what-an-agent-is-told) are the factory's, authored by an agent — or entered unauthored from what shipped, the exception above, install's entries alone ungated — written by the artifact store, and decided at a gate; the entry is the owner's, written by hand and decided by nobody. The division is the record: what an agent runs on is substrate, and the factory reads substrate and never writes it, where what an agent is told is a record the factory has to be the only writer of. What it costs: the two are set on one screen and by two different people, so an owner who changes a model and a factory that revises a role prompt in the same week have between them changed how an agent behaves twice, and no record joins the two changes.

**It is not gate policy.** A credential reference is a field on the fleet entry the way a strategy default is a field on production's environment record, and [_Gate policy_](09-gate-policy.md#what-is-not-in-it) stays at its eight rows. Who lent which credential is a People declaration, enforced by nothing, like the duties a [_page_](08-operations.md#pages) routes on; the [spend ceiling](#a-spend-ceiling) authored beside it is the one field on that declaration the factory enforces.

**It is not what limits spend.** Two limits reach what an agent spends — [_the attempt limit_](03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md), which counts attempts and is gate policy, and [_A spend ceiling_](#a-spend-ceiling), which counts units and is not — and the entry holds neither: the ceiling is authored on the People declaration beside who lent the credential, and the entry names the credential and says nothing about what may be spent through it.
