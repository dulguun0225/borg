# The screens as software

The four screens are one client, built and versioned with the factory and upgraded with it, so there is never a screen reading records whose shape it was not built for; what that costs: a screen cannot be upgraded alone. Every place a human can be on them is an address — an item, a decision, a service on an environment, a constraint — so a row the notifier delivers by mail or chat opens at the row and not at the home view, going back returns to where the human was, and reopening after a refresh shows what the store holds now rather than what the screen held, the screen holding nothing a record does not. Who a human is on them is what they declare at [_People_](01-work-ops-factory-people.md), carried on every call as the principal seam 5 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../../deferred.md) puts there, and enforced by nothing until that seam is — the client is where authentication attaches then, once, and no screen holds a session the store does not. The screens are held to what the factory holds a product's screens to: the [design system](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) the product ships with is the one the screens are built from, and the predicates [a run on a candidate environment](../05-environments/04-what-the-candidate-environment-decides.md) can decide — a contrast floor, a name on every control, a focus order, a target size — are decided over them the way they are over a candidate's, which is what makes an accessibility regulation reachable on the factory's own screens and not only on what it builds. Every time shown is a record's own, written in UTC where the record was written, and rendered in the viewer's locale and zone by the client and by nothing else; the text is in one language until an owner supplies another, translation being content the product ships and not a record.
