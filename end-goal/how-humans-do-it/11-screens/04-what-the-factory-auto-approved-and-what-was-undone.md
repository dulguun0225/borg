# What the factory auto-approved, and what was undone

The Factory screen reports two rates side by side: how much the factory auto-approved, and how often a human later undid it (10) or the factory rolled it back. How much autonomy to allow is decided from the pair, and every outcome the second of them counts is one the [risk score](../04-risk-score/README.md) learns from. The human's load reported beside them is what tells an owner which side of the pair they are on: an auto-approval rate of four in five reads one way when the fifth row waited an hour and another when it waited a week, and without the wait the rate is compatible with an owner who is the factory's bottleneck and cannot tell. The [risk threshold](../09-gate-policy/01-what-is-in-it.md) is authored against the pair and this number together — the score [refuses to loosen it on cost](../04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md) because the owner authors what human review is worth, and a wait per duty is the number they author it with. The same pair per human, beside how long each row was open in front of them, is what says whether the review is worth anything: a human whose approvals are undone at the factory's auto-pass rate, in the time it takes to open a row and close it, is a signature at that gate and not a check, and [_Where a gate is, and what decides it_](../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) says what that reading is for and what it is not.
