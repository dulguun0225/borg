# An environment per candidate

Every candidate gets an environment of its own, created from master plus that candidate and torn down when the item merges, is dropped, or is superseded by a [re-decomposition](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md). Nothing is shared, so nothing queues behind anything: a candidate being repaired delays nobody, and a candidate waiting on a human delays only itself. Nothing shares because nothing else is candidate-fed — the environments a customer defines are deploy targets for master, so no persistent slot exists for candidates to take turns on.

The environment is composed for the candidate running in it — the [_current releases_](../06-releases.md#the-deploy-record) of its dependencies, what is running rather than what is newest, never another service's candidate, and wherever those dependencies are, a service in another project included. That is what makes a twelve-service project testing twelve items at once safe, and it is only safe because no item may break a contract. What it was composed from is a field the deployer writes at creation and rewrites when the environment is recomposed at a re-verification, naming each dependency and the release of it that was current then; no deploy record is written for a dependency put in place that way, a deploy record being what a gate decided and there being one gate here, over the item's own build. The cost is that a field the deployer writes about its own work can disagree with what is actually on that environment, and nothing checks it — the drift detector reads production targets only.

Its [store](../07-contracts.md#the-store-is-a-contract-too) is created with it, and what that store holds is stated. The deployer applies the service's schema to it the way a production deploy would — every change master carries plus the item's own, the same step and the same actor [the deploy record names](../06-releases.md#the-deploy-record) — so an item's schema change runs first where a failure costs a candidate and not an incident. What the store starts with is authored per service on the [service record](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md), beside [the explicit threshold](../08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) and at the same cost: a snapshot the owner has anonymised, or synthetic rows they supply, and where they author nothing it starts empty. It is stated rather than silent because two checks read the store's state — the [criteria](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec.md), and the store [declarations in force](../07-contracts.md#what-a-consumer-declares) decided against it — and a check over a store nobody seeded decides over whatever the run happened to write. What a candidate's store cannot rehearse is production's scale: a change that completes in milliseconds over the seed can hold a lock for minutes over production's rows, and what catches that is [the reading against the service's own recent history](../08-operations.md#the-health-monitor), not this run.

Torn down for good on those three events, an environment is also reclaimed meanwhile from an item that is running nothing. An item stopped above Implementation — sent back to [Spec](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec.md), waiting on its intent's [interview](../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md), or [escalated](../03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) with nobody having taken it over — holds no run its environment is serving, and the environment is recomposable, being created from master plus the candidate and composed from dependencies' current releases, which is what [_The merge queue_](03-the-merge-queue.md)'s re-verification already rebuilds. The deployer tears one down where the item's stage says nothing can be running on it and composes one again when the item next reaches [Deploy to candidate environment](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/06-deploy-to-candidate-environment.md); the branch and the builds persist, the environment being the one part that can be discarded and composed again. Without reclamation the [platform](../03-gates/02-the-rollout-strategy.md)'s room is consumed monotonically by items waiting on humans, every wait the design leaves unbounded — an owner's silence on an interview, an escalation nobody takes, the re-authoring above an item sent back — holding one environment each: ten such items on a platform with room for ten hold every later item at that gate indefinitely, no [page](../08-operations.md#pages) fires, and the factory can free nothing itself. What reclamation costs is composition paid twice on an item that resumes, and that the deployer reads whether a run is in progress before tearing down rather than assuming it.

What composing one takes is written, not estimated: the environment record carries when the deployer began composing it and when the run could start, written by the deployer as it does the work, so [_Factory_](../11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) reports composition time per service beside throughput — a number an owner reads before deciding whether the platform is the bottleneck, and the one the throughput argument above rests on. What an install has to provide is stated the same way, as a shape rather than a figure: the factory itself, which is one process and one store, and then per item in progress one environment holding that service's build, its store, and the current release of each dependency in the transitive closure — so the platform an operator provisions is linear in items in progress with the closure as the coefficient, and the bound on items in progress is the room the platform has, held at [_Deploy to candidate environment_](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/06-deploy-to-candidate-environment.md) when it runs out. The figure is the install's, because the closure and the release are: the design refuses to state a capacity for the reason it refuses to model one below.

The cost is infrastructure per item in progress rather than one shared environment per service. It is the factory's cost and not a human's: where the score or a safeguard puts a human on a candidate, that human's delay is that item's alone, where a single shared environment would have imposed it on every item behind them.

Nothing an owner authors bounds it, and that is refused rather than missing. A per-install cost floor and an authored limit on how many environments run at once would each need the factory to know what an environment costs on the hosting the owner pays for outside the factory, and it [does not model capacity](../08-operations.md#the-health-monitor), so any value it stated would be a guess; a limit it cannot derive is one the owner sets at their hosting provider, where the bill is, and the platform having no room already holds an item at [_Deploy to candidate environment_](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/06-deploy-to-candidate-environment.md). What that costs is that the [spend ceiling](../10-fleet.md#a-spend-ceiling) — the one authored bound counted in spend rather than attempts, set on the credential an agent reaches its [model](../10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-runs-on) through — reaches model spend alone, and what an environment per candidate costs in hosting is bounded by nothing the factory reads.
