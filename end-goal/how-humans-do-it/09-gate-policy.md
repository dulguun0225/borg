# Gate policy

Everything an owner authors (8), in one place. Each parameter is defined beside the mechanism it limits, sections apart, which is right for a reader meeting the mechanism and wrong for an owner setting the policy — the duty is one line and looks far smaller than it is. Gathered, it is eight rows.

## What is in it

The eight, each with what it limits and what a bad value does at either end.

| Parameter | What it limits | What goes wrong at each end |
|---|---|---|
| **[risk threshold](04-risk-score.md)** | where the score stops auto-passing and puts a human at the gate | gate more and the human work the factory exists to remove comes back; gate less and a change reaches production with nobody having watched it run |
| **[attempt limit](03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit)** | how many times a stage is retried, how many rounds [_the interview_](02-intent-into-items.md#the-interview) asks, and how many times [_decomposition_](02-intent-into-items.md#decomposition) runs again on a rejected set, before the item or the [intent](02-intent-into-items.md#intake) appears in [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) as an escalation (12) | low turns solvable work into human work and stops an interview that was about to converge; high spends agent time before anyone sees the item |
| **[item-size target](02-intent-into-items.md#decomposition)** | how large an item is meant to be, above the minimum that it ships by itself | too coarse shows as attempt-limit escalations (12), with everything spent on the item thrown away; too fine shows as cost per feature and rework rate |
| **[the list of allowed predicate kinds](07-contracts.md#what-a-consumer-declares)** | what kinds of assertion a consumer contract may draw from | narrow leaves assumptions undeclared, discovered only once a producer has broken one; wide admits an assertion that cannot be decided against one observed exchange, and enforcement stops being mechanical |
| **[the analysis window's size and confidence](08-operations.md#the-analysis-window)** | the smallest regression the comparison must rule out to close [passed](08-operations.md#the-analysis-window), and how sure it must be | coarser detects less, and the traffic a comparison needs scales as the inverse square of what it must detect; finer keeps every release under watch longer |
| **[the analysis window's cap](08-operations.md#the-analysis-window)** | the elapsed time that ends a window which will never reach its volume | short closes more windows unresolved; long holds the next deploy behind a window a quiet service was never going to resolve |
| **[window limit](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows)** | how many analysis windows one service may hold open at once | 1 is the serial factory; each increment adds throughput on a high-frequency service and increases how many releases one rollback undoes |
| **[held-out sample rate](04-risk-score.md#how-it-learns)** | how often the score auto-passes a change it would have gated, to keep unbiased signal on the authors and areas it has stopped trusting | low produces almost no usable outcome, so the risk threshold can fall on what goes wrong and never rise; high sends changes the score wanted gated to production with nobody at the gate |

## One shape across all of them

**The score supplies what an owner does not.** Authoring is an override rather than a requirement: a factory with nothing authored in it runs, on values the score sets and moves as outcomes arrive. The drawback is that a default nobody chose is still a decision, and it can stay invisible until it takes effect, the [_window limit_](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows) being the clearest case of that.

**A safeguard (9) can only add.** A **safeguard** is what an owner puts on top of what this section gathers — a human required at a gate, or a parameter narrowed — and the whole of what it may do is add protection. The direction differs per parameter and points the same way in each: a ceiling over the window's size, a floor under the confidence it requires, a floor under its cap, a ceiling over the window limit, a ceiling over the held-out sample rate, a ceiling over the attempt limit, a ceiling over the item-size target, a floor under the list of allowed predicate kinds, a human at a gate, a [_rollout strategy_](03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy) that keeps a control, an [explicit health threshold](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) applied in addition to the comparison rather than instead of it. A safeguard clamps and does not take precedence: the value in force is what an owner authored where they authored one and what the score supplies otherwise, clamped by the safeguard — a ceiling caps it, a floor raises it, and a safeguard never replaces a value already narrower than itself. Read as a precedence, a ceiling of five over the window limit would override an authored two and raise the number, which is a safeguard adding throughput and removing safety. The score keeps moving inside a safeguard and never past it, so an owner who adds one on a single value has not frozen the rest.

**A safeguard is a record.** One record with one writer, [Factory](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), naming its subject — a stage, a service, a project, an [area](../what-humans-do.md), a [contract element](07-contracts.md#two-versioned-things), this section's own list, [the report store](02-intent-into-items.md#reports), or [the drift detector](08-operations.md#drift-detection)'s last check — what it binds, its direction, and, where it puts a human at a gate, the [duty](../what-humans-do.md) or the named human its rows route to. That last field is what lets the person who authored a check receive it: without it the rows go to the duty the design names at that gate and to the owner everywhere else, so a [compliance officer](../what-humans-do.md) who safeguards a regulated area authors a check somebody else answers, and an owner who safeguards a production deploy gate stands in front of every release on that service personally and cannot delegate it. The routing already exists — a [decision](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it)'s open event names the duty or named human it waits on — so this is a field on the safeguard rather than a mechanism, and what it costs is that a safeguard can name a human who later holds no duty, which routes the same way an unheld row already does: it widens to the owner.

**A safeguard is not edited.** Withdrawing one is a second record naming the first, the treatment a [constraint](02-intent-into-items.md#constraints-and-the-design-system) already gets — correcting one is a withdrawal and a second record — and the treatment a [role prompt](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told) version gets. The value in force is a read of the newest record for that subject. A field flipped in place would leave nothing saying the check ever existed, when it was removed, or by whom, which matters here more than anywhere else: a withdrawal is the one operation in the factory that removes a human from a gate, and the document refuses that mechanism twice elsewhere. So a withdrawal is decided and not merely written: it takes a gate row of its own, [_A safeguard's withdrawal_](03-gates.md#a-safeguards-withdrawal), held by a human always and routed to the human the safeguard names rather than to whoever wrote the withdrawal, and until that row approves it the safeguard stands. What the second record costs is that a subject with a long history is a query rather than a field read, and that the actor on it is self-asserted until [seam 1](../deferred.md) carries a principal. The alternative was a field on the record its scope names, which works for five subjects and breaks for two: a safeguard on a predicate kind names a contract element as its subject, and that record's writer is [the merge queue](05-environments.md#the-merge-queue), so an owner authoring on it would be a second writer with no [seam](../deferred.md); and a safeguard setting a maximum age on the drift detector's last check would have the factory writing into a store it may never write. Five readers and one writer is the shape the [release record](06-releases.md#the-release-record) already has. What it costs is that what applies here is a query over safeguards by subject rather than a field read on the record already in hand, so every mechanism a safeguard binds does one more read, and a safeguard naming a subject nobody declared is a dangling reference nothing detects until the mechanism looks for it.

Six of those directions have to be named rather than read off the row, because both ends of each are bad in their own way. A ceiling over the window's size is the one that reads backwards: the size is the smallest regression the comparison must rule out, so capping it holds the window to catching something at least that small, where a floor would let the value be raised until it caught nothing worth catching. A floor under the window's cap keeps a release under watch on a service too quiet to reach that volume, and the next deploy waits for it — protection is the longer end here, which is why the floor is the direction and not the ceiling. A ceiling over the attempt limit adds human work to stop spending on retries, which is what a safeguard does everywhere else. A ceiling over the item-size target produces smaller items, each shipping by itself and passing its own gates. A safeguard on that list may only extend it: a kind of assertion added is coverage added, and one removed would invalidate consumer contracts already [ratified at a gate](07-contracts.md#what-a-consumer-declares). What keeps a wider list from losing the mechanical enforcement its own cell describes is that list's own rule — a predicate has to be decidable against one observed exchange — which is a floor no safeguard goes below. A ceiling over the held-out sample rate is the one whose bad end is not a loss of protection: sampling less keeps humans at gates, and what it costs is the evidence a threshold needs to rise at all, so the safeguard buys caution and pays for it in calibration. A safeguard on the report store points one of three ways, each adding protection: a human admitting a report-derived intent before anything is spent on it, a human at [the gate that decides the grouper's role prompt](03-gates.md#a-role-prompt-or-a-skill), or a narrowing of [the channel's rates](02-intent-into-items.md#reports) — to zero where an owner closes one service's way in — and what each costs is stated where the channel is defined.

**Scope follows the mechanism, not the duty.** Each parameter is a field of the record its scope names, holding what an owner authored and nothing else: where the field is empty the value in force is what the score supplies at that moment, so a factory with nothing authored runs and the score never writes another component's record.

**What an owner authored is versioned the way the score's own values are.** A **policy version** is a row of the [decision log](../deferred.md), appended by the log at each owner write — Factory being the component that calls for the append — and chained like every row there, naming every authored parameter and the scope it was authored on, and the field the mechanism reads is a read of the newest one — the same construction the [score version](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) already has for the values the score supplies. It is a row of the log and not a record of its own because a decision names it by identifier, and a version rewritten outside the chain would move the meaning of every decision naming it while the log verified clean. Every [decision](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it) names the policy version in force, which [_Traceability_](../what-the-factory-does.md#traceability) makes the harder half of the claim it rests on, and a version is what that name can point at: overwriting the field in place would leave an auditor told a change was decided under a version and shown nothing, since a decision read back against today's values is not the decision that was made. It also carries who wrote it, so what an owner set and when is a fact of the record rather than of somebody's memory. What it costs is a version per authored write, most of them differing in one number, and a value in force that is a read of a version rather than a field — the same cost the score version already pays for the other half of the same pair. The window limit and the analysis window's parameters are per service and are fields of the [_service record_](02-intent-into-items.md#decomposition); the item-size target is per area and is a field of the [_area record_](02-intent-into-items.md#what-an-item-names). A gate row's threshold is a field of an environment record — a deploy row into a persistent environment reads the environment it deploys into, and every other row reads production's, which [_Records, and one long-lived branch_](05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch) says exists everywhere, so it is there before the item is. All eight rows of the [default path](03-gates.md#actions-at-each-gate) read production's, [Decomposition](03-gates.md#decomposition) firing before any [candidate environment](05-environments.md#an-environment-per-candidate) exists and a candidate's own environment being created at the gate that decides its deploy, so unable to hold the threshold deciding it. A row a customer's extra environment adds reads that environment. [_A role prompt or a skill_](03-gates.md#a-role-prompt-or-a-skill) reads the factory-wide settings record this section defines, having no project and so no production environment to read, and it is still the same parameter: the eight rows are eight whatever a row's threshold is a field of.

Two parameters have no customer record their scope reaches, and they share one. The attempt limit is per stage — with the interview's rounds and decomposition's re-decompositions counted against it too — and the stages are the factory's own; the list of allowed predicate kinds is one list the factory owns and an owner extends. Both are fields of the **factory-wide settings record**, written by Factory, which exists before any project does and which an owner may never open. Putting the limit on production's environment record instead would make it per project as well as per stage — a scope this section does not give it, and one that duplicates by field what a safeguard already does. The threshold [_A role prompt or a skill_](03-gates.md#a-role-prompt-or-a-skill) reads is a field of it as well, and belongs there for the reason the other two do — the row is the factory's own and reaches every project at once. Report retention, [decision-log](../deferred.md) retention, and the [report channel's two rates](02-intent-into-items.md#reports) — per service and factory-wide, bounding arrival at the way in — are fields of it too, being authored, factory-wide, and not gate policy. A safeguard (9) narrows any of them further, on any subject a safeguard may name.

**The duty does not shrink, and its reach grows.** The [backstop duties](../what-humans-do.md) (11, 12) fall away as the factory improves. This one does not, and what it governs widens as they go: these eight rows are what the factory may do with no human deciding anywhere, so the better it gets, the more of its behaviour they describe.

## What is not in it

Three things are next to gate policy and are not it. **The score.** An owner authors what the number is compared against, never how it is computed — the formula is published so a human can disagree with it, and the [vector](04-risk-score.md) behind it is learned from outcomes. **The stages.** A gate is where policy applies; what is between the gates is the factory's own — including [what an agent is told](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told) there, which the factory writes, versions, and puts through a gate of its own, and which an owner reaches only by adding a safeguard at that gate (9). **The form a criterion takes.** The [six patterns](03-gates.md#spec) are a closed set and the factory owns the form, which is what lets it end the interview — an owner confirms criteria (6) and does not author the shape one may have.

A [_strategy_](03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy) default is a field on production's environment record beside gate policy, and the explicit health threshold, the [service level objective](08-operations.md#service-level-objectives), the [fraction of its instances a release keeps](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows) while a rollback could return to it, and the [hours within which the service pages](08-operations.md#pages) are fields on the service record beside the window limit and the analysis window's parameters, as are the [backlog cap](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows) and the [search budget](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows), each supplied where nothing is authored and each lowered and never raised by a safeguard (9). A safeguard on the strategy default or the threshold is (9); the objective, the fraction, and the hours are authored outright, there being no supplied value for a safeguard to constrain — the fraction's default is all of them, fixed rather than supplied, because the score does not model capacity, and the hours' default is every hour, because nothing the factory observes says which service may wait for morning. A [_spend ceiling_](10-fleet.md#a-spend-ceiling) — a limit on what the factory spends through one credential, reaching which is a [hold](03-gates.md#what-a-gate-may-change) — is a field of the [_People_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) declaration that records who lent the credential, and is authored outright for the same reason: its evidence is cost, which the score does not learn from, so nothing supplies a value and there is nothing for a safeguard to constrain. None of these is authored here — each is authored and is not among the eight, the way retention below is.

**Retention is authored and is not one of the eight.** An owner may set how long the [_report store_](02-intent-into-items.md#reports) keeps a report and how long the decision log is kept, both fields of the factory-wide settings record. Where they author neither, both are kept for the life of the install. The report channel's two rates sit beside them, authored the same way and bounding what arrives rather than what is kept; where an owner authors neither rate, arrival is unbounded, because no outcome teaches a rate and so there is nothing for the score to supply. Truncating the log is to a checkpoint and the [chain](../deferred.md) restarts there — the truncation itself appending a chained row naming who authored the value, the value it enforced, the boundary it cut to, and the policy version and score version in force at the cut, so what was removed and under what authority stays readable, and a decision after the cut naming a version before it is still read against what it was decided under — but nothing before the checkpoint is evidence any more and the score loses its oldest outcomes — which are the ones a narrow [per-author prior](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) was built from, so an owner truncating for storage widens the priors of the authors the factory knows best, and nothing on the screen says the setting did that. The checkpoint is not a field: it is the oldest record the log still holds, and storing it beside the log would be one fact in two places able to disagree. Everything else in [the graph](../what-the-factory-does.md#tight-integration) is kept, because deleting any of it breaks a link a record already points at — which is what one graph exchanges for the customer's own disk. Kept means the records: what is removable inside a kept record is its text, by [a redaction](02-intent-into-items.md#reports), which destroys words and never a link.
