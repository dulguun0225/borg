# A fleet proposal

The factory does not write a fleet entry and does argue for one. A **fleet proposal** is a record it writes at [Factory](../11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people): a set of entries to write and a set to retire, each naming a role, a model, an effort, and a scope, with the evidence behind it. It names no credential. Which account funds a model is the one thing the factory never chooses, and refusing to name one is what leaves [_What an agent runs on_](01-what-an-agent-runs-on.md)'s refusal standing rather than reopened.

A proposal argues over the fleet and not over one entry. It may say that five entries should be retired and six written in their place, because a fleet shaped wrong is not repaired by adding to it, and a mechanism that could only add would recommend growth for as long as anything was wrong.

Retiring costs nothing the design has to protect. No record written for traceability points at a fleet entry — [_An account that runs out is a hold_](05-an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold.md) records a credential name and never a pointer, for this reason among others — so an entry deleted changes nothing any past record says. The prior survives too, being per model: [_A credential taken back_](06-a-credential-taken-back.md) already says a fleet rebuilt keeps every bit of its history, and a fleet redesigned keeps it on the same grounds.

Its writer is not a [detector](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md). A detector is one of three callers of [intake](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md), and intake writes every intent; a component that also wrote a record of another kind would be a second writer with no [seam](../../deferred.md) between the two things it wrote. So a proposal has a writer of its own, and the record has two writers with a field as the seam: the factory writes the proposal and never its **disposition**, an owner writes the disposition and never a proposal. A disposition is open, accepted, or dismissed. Without one nothing would ever be finished, and the rule a detector keeps — raise for a condition rather than for an observation, attaching to what has not finished instead of raising a duplicate — would have nothing to test, so a shortage that lasted a month would produce a month of proposals.

There is no gate. Nothing ships from a proposal and nothing is decided by it: the owner's write is the review, and what that write produces is an entry [dispatch](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) matches against, reviewed at a gate the way every other authorship is. That is [_Dispatch is a match, not a judgment_](../01-one-pipeline.md)'s argument one level up.

What a proposal may argue is wider than what an owner may do with it. A proposal for a new role is a proposal for a new [stage](../01-one-pipeline.md), and stage definition is outside everything an owner authors — [_What is not in it_](../09-gate-policy/03-what-is-not-in-it.md) puts it there — so nobody at this install can enact one, and it reaches the owner as something to take to whoever builds the factory. Free-form argument is what makes a redesign expressible at all; this is what it costs, and the cost is real, because the shortages a factory notices soonest are the ones no existing role covers.

The larger cost is that nothing scores the obligation to read one. Composing the fleet is substrate rather than one of [the twelve](../../what-humans-do.md), so a proposal waits at Factory until an owner opens it, no [_page_](../08-operations/07-pages.md) fires — nothing deployed is worse for a fleet that is too small — and no number says how long it has waited. That is the gap [_What the fleet is not_](09-what-the-fleet-is-not.md) already names for a credential nobody lent, and a proposal makes the shortage visible without making anybody answer it.
