# A spend ceiling

A **spend ceiling** is a limit an owner authors on what the factory spends through one credential, in the units the provider returns, and reaching it is a [hold](../03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md). It is authored on the [_People_](../11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) declaration that records who lent the credential, which is already per credential name and owner-written, and it has no default: where none is authored nothing is compared, and the provider's quota is the only stop, which is what every install had before. The credential is the scope because it is where the money is: one credential is one account at one provider and one invoice, where an entry, a stage, or an area each draw on accounts whose invoices are separate. It reaches a model credential only, since what is recorded is what a stage and an interview spent; authored on the credential an environment reaches its targets through it compares nothing, no deploy recording spend. What the scope costs: a credential carrying two models sums units the provider prices differently, so the ceiling is exact on a credential running one model and a bound on units rather than money otherwise — an owner who wants it exact lends one credential per model.

What it is compared against is the factory's own record. What a stage spent is a field of the item, written by dispatch with the stage transition every stage already reports, and what [the interview](../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md) spent is the same field on the intent, written by [intake](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) at each round. Each carries the name of the credential the stage ran on — a name and never a pointer, the rule [_An account that runs out is a hold_](05-an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold.md) keeps for the same reason, so an entry retired leaves the field readable. The sum the ceiling is compared against is a query over the items and intents whose stages ran on that name since the newest clear, and it reads nothing at the provider — the same construction [_the attempt limit_](../03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) uses when it compares against the item's own count rather than the log — so the fleet storing a reference and not a balance is no obstacle, since no balance is read. Cost per feature is the same field read the other way: the intent's own spend plus the spend on the items [decomposed](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) from it, reading no fleet entry and no account, the name on the field resolving nothing, which leaves the substrate written by a human alone. It is reported per model version and never summed across models — a feature's stages run in more than one role, a role may run on a different model, and one number over them would add units that do not add. It is in the units the provider returned and never converted: a price is the same kind of fact as a balance, one at the provider that goes stale where the factory cannot check it, so there is no price field and no currency anywhere, and an owner who wants money multiplies the units where the price is. What that costs: a feature on a fleet of two models has two numbers and no total; a stage that stops without reporting a transition records nothing, so the field under-reports exactly where the factory is stuck; and a human authoring a stage instead of an agent spends nothing the factory can see.

Reaching the ceiling is the hold [_An account that runs out is a hold_](05-an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold.md) describes, written and routed the same way: into the [decision log](../../deferred.md) by the log, naming the credential, no [_page_](../08-operations/07-pages.md), no attempt counted, the prior taught nothing, and [dispatch](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) putting nothing further onto that name until it clears. The owner clears it at [_Work_](../11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), and clearing restarts the count, the pattern an [escalated interview](../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md) already uses: clearing is a decision to spend more, and a ceiling that went on counting would be reached again at the next stage. There is no period and no clock. The ceiling is a total since the last clear and not a rate, because nothing else in the factory reads a calendar and a rate would be the first thing that did.

It is not one of [gate policy](../09-gate-policy/01-what-is-in-it.md)'s eight. Every row there has a value the score supplies and a safeguard narrows; the score learns from rollbacks and the changes humans undo, and a ceiling's evidence is cost, which the score does not read — so there is nothing to supply and nothing to safeguard, and it is authored outright, beside the [service level objective](../08-operations/05-service-level-objectives.md) and the [fraction of its instances a release keeps](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md), which [_What is not in it_](../09-gate-policy/03-what-is-not-in-it.md) lists.

What it costs: a stop a human has to clear. It bounds model spend and nothing on the host — the [environment per candidate](../05-environments/02-an-environment-per-candidate.md) and the fleet a release keeps are hosting the owner pays for outside the factory, so the ceiling bounds the provider's invoice and never the hosting one. It is reached late by whatever a stage that stopped without reporting left unrecorded. Two entries on one account under two names are two ceilings over one quota, and only the owner knows they were one. And it reads agent work alone, a human's stage spending nothing it can see.
