# What an agent runs on

An agent reaches its model through a **provider account**, and the factory stores a reference to that account's credential rather than the credential itself — seam 3 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../../deferred.md), which the fleet is the first thing to use. The [resolver](../../deferred.md) is a file on disk today. No provider is named anywhere in the factory: a fleet entry has a model, an effort, a role, a scope, and the name of a credential, and the model names its version, because the [_per-author prior_](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) is kept per version and a version that behaved differently under one name would average two authors into one prior.

**Effort** is how long the model works before it answers. Some providers offer it as a setting and some do not, which is why naming it names no provider: it is a dial and not a name. The factory does not check that the credential's provider offers what the entry asks for, the way it does not check a quota — it discovers both by reaching for them, and an entry asking for an effort nobody offers fails at the same place an exhausted account does. What it costs: the prior cannot see it — two entries on one model at two efforts share one prior, which [_Paid for is not authored by_](04-paid-for-is-not-authored-by.md) settles and states the cost of.

The account itself is not a record. A record would need fields the factory cannot populate — quota and balance are facts at the provider, whose name the factory does not record — and a quota field would have [_A spend ceiling_](08-a-spend-ceiling.md) compared against a number the factory never reads, where it compares against what the factory recorded itself. The credential name is the whole of the handle. What that costs: two entries whose credentials resolve to one account share a quota the factory cannot see — they are exhausted at the same moment and recorded as two unrelated waits, and only the owner knows they were one account.

An owner writes the entry, at [Factory](../11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), and no component in [the pipeline](../01-one-pipeline.md) writes back to it: the factory reads the [substrate](../../what-humans-do.md) and never writes it. The three fields it cannot supply decide that — a model somebody is willing to run, the effort they are willing to have it run at, and a credential somebody lends — where the other two are chosen among values the factory already defines, the roles being its own [stages](../01-one-pipeline.md) plus the two put on an [intent](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) above them and [the grouper](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) above those — none of them a deploy, which the [deployer](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) performs and no entry is put on — and a scope drawn on the lines a [safeguard](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) (9) is. A factory that wrote its own entries would be choosing whose lent account pays for the next stage and how long it runs, which is the authority [_A spend ceiling_](08-a-spend-ceiling.md) keeps with the owner: an owner authors what may be spent through a credential, and the factory never does. What it may do instead is argue — [_A fleet proposal_](07-a-fleet-proposal.md) names a fleet and no credential, and the write stays where it is. [Dispatch](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) honours both halves of a scope, the item's [area chain](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/02-what-an-item-names.md) and its service's [project](../11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people); an agent checking its own scope would be the agent deciding where it may go. What the entry costs is that the fleet does not grow itself: an item whose stage and [area](../../what-humans-do.md) match no entry is stuck at dispatch, and until a proposal reports the shortage it shows only as a [timeline](../11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) in Work that does not advance, indistinguishable from work waiting on a human.

The account may be an organisation's or a person's own, and the factory takes either, because a reference resolves the same way whichever it is. The early factory is where that matters. An owner installing one before it has proved anything has a personal subscription and no organisation agreement to spend against, and a design that made them wait for procurement in order to watch the factory run once would impose the proven case's requirements on the unproven one. So a personal account attaches through no second mechanism: same field, same resolver, same record.

What differs is downstream of the account and not in the factory at all — its quota, who may revoke it, and whose money it spends. Three things follow, and each is a drawback of the personal account rather than a feature of it: whose name ends up on the work, what an exhausted account does, and what happens when one is taken away.
