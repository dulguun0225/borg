# Environments

Where software runs, and how a change reaches each place it runs.

## Records, and one long-lived branch

Environments are records, not names in code. One record type with a **kind** fixed at creation and three values — production, a deploy target a customer defines, and a [candidate](06-releases.md#what-a-build-is-called-and-when)'s own. [Gate](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it) thresholds, credential references, and the targets it is reached at are fields of both persistent kinds; the strategy default is production's alone, because a [strategy](03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy) decides whether a [control](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) runs and a control exists only where organic traffic does. A candidate's environment holds nothing an owner authored, being created at the gate that decides its deploy and so unable to hold the threshold deciding it. What it does hold is what no deploy can happen without: the targets the [deployer](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) created for it and the credential reference it reaches them with, both written by the deployer at creation rather than authored by anybody. An environment with neither is one nothing can deploy into, and keeping the two facts on the record rather than implied by the creator is what lets the same readers read all three kinds. Production exists everywhere, every candidate gets one of its own, and customers define more per [project](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people).

An environment names its **targets**, plural — the addresses a deploy is performed against — and they are a field, not records of their own, so nothing holds a reference that must survive an address change: a [deploy record](06-releases.md#the-deploy-record) is keyed by service and environment rather than by target and carries completion per target as a field, a mismatch names the address that differs, and [_Drift detection_](08-operations.md#drift-detection) reads each in turn. The cost is that an owner declares every target before the factory can deploy into that environment, and a target added at the provider and never declared is deployed to by nothing and read by the drift detector as nothing — a real target running old software named by no mismatch.

The kind is also the seam, two writers never writing a record of the other's kind. An owner writes the persistent kinds on [_Factory_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people): production's at the creation of the project, which an owner does not choose because production exists everywhere, and each further one when they define it. The [deployer](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) writes a candidate's, creating it at the approval of [Deploy to candidate environment](03-gates.md#deploy-to-candidate-environment), tearing it down for good when the [item](01-one-pipeline.md) merges, is dropped, or is superseded by a [re-decomposition](02-intent-into-items.md#decomposition), and reclaiming one meanwhile as [_An environment per candidate_](#an-environment-per-candidate) sets out — the only component that reaches a deploy target is the one that reaches environments, and a gate decides an event and edits nothing. What that costs is three creation paths for one record type, so every reader excludes the kinds it does not want, and a torn-down candidate record is kept rather than deleted because the deploy records naming it would otherwise point at nothing.

A persistent environment contains a project — every service in it — while a [release](06-releases.md#the-release-record), a [release number](06-releases.md#the-release-number), a master branch, and an [analysis window](08-operations.md#the-analysis-window) are all per service. A deploy puts one service's build on one environment, so an environment fed by a twelve-service project has twelve independent promotion paths. A candidate's environment contains no project: it is composed for one candidate, which [_An environment per candidate_](#an-environment-per-candidate) sets out. The gate row a customer's environment adds describes that environment; what fires is one decision per deploy into it. A customer's extra environments are deploy targets and not ordered stops — nothing makes a release pass through one before production, and the [production deploy gate](03-gates.md#deploy-to-production) gains no [hold](03-gates.md#what-a-gate-may-change) on another environment's deploy. An owner who wants a human to look at a release running somewhere first adds a [safeguard](09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them) putting one at that gate (9). What that costs is that a staging environment enforces nothing: a release can reach production having never been deployed there.

Its history is not a field. The deploys that name a record are inbound edges, the arrangement the release record already uses, and [incidents](08-operations.md#incidents) and [rollbacks](06-releases.md#rollback) exist on production environments alone, because the [health monitor](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) that opens an incident and the strategy that keeps a control both attach to a production deploy and to no other. The cost is that a pre-production environment where something misbehaves has no record of it — what is written there is a rejected candidate or nothing.

One long-lived branch is each service's promotion path: master. A candidate has a branch of its own until it merges, is dropped, or is superseded by a re-decomposition. Merging and deploying are separate events and so are separate gates — a merge admits a change to master, a deploy puts a build on an environment, and either can happen without the other. A deploy can be rerun; a merge cannot be unmerged the same way.

A candidate is not a record. It names an item and the build currently in force for it, and a build on a candidate branch belongs to one item, so the pair and the build pick out the same thing — a record per pair would repeat what the build and the item say already. The [criteria](03-gates.md#spec) results and the candidate deploy attach to the build. The branch and the environment are the item's and persist across a rebuild, the environment being a record already. What that costs is that [_The merge queue_](#the-merge-queue)'s rejection has no record of its own: no gate fires, so it is written into [the log](../deferred.md) by the log with the queue as caller and [actor](../deferred.md), the way [a wait no gate could compute](03-gates.md#what-a-gate-may-change) is, and not as a [decision](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it) — there is no firing to open a row at, the [Merge to master](03-gates.md#merge-to-master) gate's own having closed as an approval, and no [factor vector](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) computed for it. It is counted as an attempt against [_the limit_](03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit) at the [stage](02-intent-into-items.md#what-an-item-names) the item is sent back to. What that costs is that [_Factory_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people)'s gate rejection rate, being rejects over firings per gate row, does not see it: one of the three ways an item goes back is absent from the number an owner reads.

A **build** is a record, one per commit built, written by the deployer when it is called to build. Four callers: the implementation stage when it finishes, so the [Implementation gate](03-gates.md#implementation) decides over a build that exists and the [score](04-risk-score.md) has a diff to compute the [change factors](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) from; the candidate deploy where a rebuild is needed; the merge queue at re-verification, which builds the candidate branch onto the master it will actually merge into; and the [search](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows), whose builds are of commits on no branch, decided by no gate and bounded there rather than here. A rebuild is a new build, so an item has as many builds as it was built and the release names the last one — which is what makes the commit that was verified being the commit that merges a property of the queue. Every record naming a build says which: a gate decision names the one in force at the firing, a factor vector is computed from that same build, a [consumer contract](07-contracts.md#what-a-consumer-declares) names the one it was derived from. The commit is a field of the build and not a record, one commit per build and nothing pointing at a commit that has no build. So is what the build resolved: every third-party package, the version resolved to, and the licence the package declares, written by the deployer from the build it performed, because a commit resolving an unpinned range on two days is two builds shipping two things, and the [advisory](02-intent-into-items.md#advisories) detector reads a shipped build's set and nothing else could say what it holds. The same set, diffed against the [current release](06-releases.md#the-deploy-record)'s build's, is the dependency change the score's [exposure](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) factor reads, and a licence policy is decided against it as a build-kind [constraint](02-intent-into-items.md#constraints-and-the-design-system); which services ship a package at a version is a query over current release, build, and set, and not an estimate. What the build costs is that a component named for reaching an environment is called once per item where no environment is reached, and that an item rebuilt four times leaves three builds nothing points at.

What a run on a candidate environment produced attaches to the build — each criterion decided, each consumer contract checked against the exchanges observed there, and the composition in force at the run, copied from the field [_An environment per candidate_](#an-environment-per-candidate) has the deployer write, because that field is rewritten at a recomposition and what an earlier run ran against survives on the build or nowhere — written by the deployer when the run finishes, and not a record of its own: the identity is the build plus the criterion id, and which environment the run happened on is the item's, which the build names. The build could not name one itself — it is written when the implementation stage finishes, one gate above the approval that creates the environment. The producer's own [contract diff](07-contracts.md#enforcement) is not among them. It needs no run and no environment, and a verdict stored at run time is not a check made when the gate fires, which is what [_Two baselines, kept apart_](07-contracts.md#what-a-consumer-declares) requires. What is written is what was observed; what is decided is decided at the gate.

## An environment per candidate

Every candidate gets an environment of its own, created from master plus that candidate and torn down when the item merges, is dropped, or is superseded by a [re-decomposition](02-intent-into-items.md#decomposition). Nothing is shared, so nothing queues behind anything: a candidate being repaired delays nobody, and a candidate waiting on a human delays only itself. Nothing shares because nothing else is candidate-fed — the environments a customer defines are deploy targets for master, so no persistent slot exists for candidates to take turns on.

The environment is composed for the candidate running in it — the [_current releases_](06-releases.md#the-deploy-record) of its dependencies, what is running rather than what is newest, never another service's candidate, and wherever those dependencies are, a service in another project included. That is what makes a twelve-service project testing twelve items at once safe, and it is only safe because no item may break a contract. What it was composed from is a field the deployer writes at creation and rewrites when the environment is recomposed at a re-verification, naming each dependency and the release of it that was current then; no deploy record is written for a dependency put in place that way, a deploy record being what a gate decided and there being one gate here, over the item's own build. The cost is that a field the deployer writes about its own work can disagree with what is actually on that environment, and nothing checks it — the drift detector reads production targets only.

Its [store](07-contracts.md#the-store-is-a-contract-too) is created with it, and what that store holds is stated. The deployer applies the service's schema to it the way a production deploy would — every change master carries plus the item's own, the same step and the same actor [the deploy record names](06-releases.md#the-deploy-record) — so an item's schema change runs first where a failure costs a candidate and not an incident. What the store starts with is authored per service on the [service record](02-intent-into-items.md#decomposition), beside [the explicit threshold](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) and at the same cost: a snapshot the owner has anonymised, or synthetic rows they supply, and where they author nothing it starts empty. It is stated rather than silent because two checks read the store's state — the [criteria](03-gates.md#spec), and the store [declarations in force](07-contracts.md#what-a-consumer-declares) decided against it — and a check over a store nobody seeded decides over whatever the run happened to write. What a candidate's store cannot rehearse is production's scale: a change that completes in milliseconds over the seed can hold a lock for minutes over production's rows, and what catches that is [the reading against the service's own recent history](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor), not this run.

Torn down for good on those three events, an environment is also reclaimed meanwhile from an item that is running nothing. An item stopped above Implementation — sent back to [Spec](03-gates.md#spec), waiting on its intent's [interview](02-intent-into-items.md#the-interview), or [escalated](03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit) with nobody having taken it over — holds no run its environment is serving, and the environment is recomposable, being created from master plus the candidate and composed from dependencies' current releases, which is what [_The merge queue_](#the-merge-queue)'s re-verification already rebuilds. The deployer tears one down where the item's stage says nothing can be running on it and composes one again when the item next reaches [Deploy to candidate environment](03-gates.md#deploy-to-candidate-environment); the branch and the builds persist, the environment being the one part that can be discarded and composed again. Without reclamation the [platform](03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy)'s room is consumed monotonically by items waiting on humans, every wait the design leaves unbounded — an owner's silence on an interview, an escalation nobody takes, the re-authoring above an item sent back — holding one environment each: ten such items on a platform with room for ten hold every later item at that gate indefinitely, no [page](08-operations.md#pages) fires, and the factory can free nothing itself. What reclamation costs is composition paid twice on an item that resumes, and that the deployer reads whether a run is in progress before tearing down rather than assuming it.

What composing one takes is written, not estimated: the environment record carries when the deployer began composing it and when the run could start, written by the deployer as it does the work, so [_Factory_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) reports composition time per service beside throughput — a number an owner reads before deciding whether the platform is the bottleneck, and the one the throughput argument above rests on. What an install has to provide is stated the same way, as a shape rather than a figure: the factory itself, which is one process and one store, and then per item in progress one environment holding that service's build, its store, and the current release of each dependency in the transitive closure — so the platform an operator provisions is linear in items in progress with the closure as the coefficient, and the bound on items in progress is the room the platform has, held at [_Deploy to candidate environment_](03-gates.md#deploy-to-candidate-environment) when it runs out. The figure is the install's, because the closure and the release are: the design refuses to state a capacity for the reason it refuses to model one below.

The cost is infrastructure per item in progress rather than one shared environment per service. It is the factory's cost and not a human's: where the score or a safeguard puts a human on a candidate, that human's delay is that item's alone, where a single shared environment would have imposed it on every item behind them.

Nothing an owner authors bounds it, and that is refused rather than missing. A per-install cost floor and an authored limit on how many environments run at once would each need the factory to know what an environment costs on the hosting the owner pays for outside the factory, and it [does not model capacity](08-operations.md#the-health-monitor), so any value it stated would be a guess; a limit it cannot derive is one the owner sets at their hosting provider, where the bill is, and the platform having no room already holds an item at [_Deploy to candidate environment_](03-gates.md#deploy-to-candidate-environment). What that costs is that the [spend ceiling](10-fleet.md#a-spend-ceiling) — the one authored bound counted in spend rather than attempts, set on the credential an agent reaches its [model](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-runs-on) through — reaches model spend alone, and what an environment per candidate costs in hosting is bounded by nothing the factory reads.

## The merge queue

Candidates verified in parallel cannot all fast-forward, because each was built on a master that has since moved. So merging is a queue: a candidate entering it re-verifies against master plus every candidate ahead of it, and fast-forwards only if that passes. A failure invalidates the speculation behind it, and those candidates re-verify against the master that actually resulted.

A candidate that fails its own re-verification — against the master that actually resulted, not against a speculation ahead of it — is rejected by the queue: the item [goes back up](03-gates.md#going-back-up) [the pipeline](01-one-pipeline.md) to Implementation, the queue naming no other stage and there being none between, and counts an attempt there against [_the limit_](03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit) — the rework is real work, which is what the limit bounds. That is the stop the [Merge to master](03-gates.md#merge-to-master) gate can no longer give, having already approved, and without it a candidate failing here would have no path. Candidates behind a failure count nothing, for the same reason they re-verify without an attempt counted against them: they failed because of someone else.

That it failed on its merits is established rather than assumed, because a re-verification is never a repeat of the run that passed: the build is new and the environment recomposed, so a criterion that passed on the candidate environment and fails here changed its answer between two runs that differ, and what differed is on the record — each run's results name [the composition it was performed against](#records-and-one-long-lived-branch), and each build its commit. Such a criterion is run once more, over the re-verification's own build and composition — once, and never until green. A repeat that disagrees is the disagreement over one build that makes a criterion [undecided](#what-the-candidate-environment-decides), and the way back is authoring the criterion's encoding — the check [_Implementation_](03-gates.md#implementation) authors beside the code to decide it — again: the score learns from it the way it learns from a [reject](03-gates.md#what-a-gate-may-change), an encoding that cannot hold its answer being a defect of the item's own build. A failure that repeats is real, and what the score learns turns on the two compositions. Where they match, the candidate failed against the master it will actually merge into, which is the queue doing its job, and the score learns as from a reject. Where a dependency's release moved between the runs, the score learns as from a [hold](03-gates.md#what-a-gate-may-change) and the [per-author prior](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) does not move: what changed is a release the author's work never saw, and a prior kept per model version and factory-wide would carry one moved dependency's noise onto every item that model authors. The rejection the queue writes into the log names which reading it was, and the moved release where there is one, so the one rejection [_Factory_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people)'s gate rejection rate cannot see is legible on the item's timeline in [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people). What this costs is one more run per disagreeing criterion and three exits where one stood. The attempt counts on every exit, so an item a moving dependency keeps rejecting still walks toward an [escalation](03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit) — deliberately: work the environment will not let finish is work a human should see, and the limit bounds spend, which the rework is. What the three exits remove is the booking of that movement as the author's defect. On the third reading the attempt and the prior part ways — counted like a reject, taught like a hold, a combination no gate produces — because the two record different things: the limit what was spent, the prior who authored what failed, and here what failed is not what was authored.

The queue restores what one shared environment used to provide — the commit that was verified is the commit that merges. It costs compute on speculative runs that a failure ahead of them throws away, which is the better of the two: the shared environment imposed the same serialization on human time, and this imposes it on machine time.

Its order is settable, like the queue at any gate. Reordering changes when a candidate re-verifies, never what it has to pass. The settable order is one priority field of the item, written through [dispatch](02-intent-into-items.md#dispatch) with [Work](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) as the caller, and it orders every queue an item waits in as an item — this one and the queues at the gates up to and including Merge to master. It orders nothing after the merge: numbered releases waiting to deploy are ordered by the release number and by nothing else, [_the revert a rollback is waiting on_](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows) excepted. So an owner who rushes an item to the front here has rushed it at every gate it has left, and has no way to reorder a deploy — an urgent fix behind three numbered releases waits for them.

The queue is a component and not a record. Its membership is the items whose [stage](02-intent-into-items.md#what-an-item-names) says Merge to master approved them and whose fast-forward has not happened, its order is that priority and then the time of the approval in [the log](../deferred.md), and its speculative re-verifications are its own state, read by nothing outside it. The two outcomes that outlast a run are records already: a fast-forward writes the release record, and a failure on the candidate's own merits is the rejection the queue writes into the log, with the queue as caller and actor and no decision opened — the arrangement stated where the candidate is said not to be a record. What that costs is that nothing records how long a candidate waited, so why something took a day is reconstructed from the approval time against the release record's own.

Master's head the queue reads from git, the system that holds it. Deriving it from the highest-numbered release record was refused because the two systems move at one event with nothing spanning them: a queue that stops between the fast-forward and the record write leaves a derived head one commit stale, and the next candidate would merge a change no release names inside its own release. The next number is a query and not a field — one above the highest among that service's release records, numbers never reused and a rolled-back release keeping its own, the queue's per-service ordering being what stops two candidates reading one — because a counter beside the records would be the same fact written twice at one event, and a lost counter write mints a duplicate.

Two things guard that query where the ordering cannot, because the ordering lives only in a running queue. A second release naming one service and one number is refused where the records are kept, so a duplicate mint is a failed write and never two releases answering to one name. And the fast-forward and the record write are one operation restartable from either side: the record is keyed on the commit, so writing it again writes nothing, and on start — every start, a crash and an upgrade of the self-hosted factory being the same stop — the queue reads master before it mints. A commit on master with no release record is the queue's own unfinished merge, and the queue completes it in master's order with the write the fast-forward already implied. The same reading is what makes records restored from a backup safe to start on, when every record since the backup is a lost write at once while git and the deploy targets roll back with nothing: the queue completes the records master's order still derives — master is linear, so the number a commit takes is its position — and mints nothing for any service while a commit's record cannot be derived, the item or build it named being among what the restore lost. That wait ends at a human, and what reaches one is not a page of the queue's own: the same restore leaves every deploy record behind what runs, which is a [mismatch](08-operations.md#drift-detection) per service, and the mismatch pages and holds already. What this costs is that the queue reads two systems where it read one — git at every start and every mint — and that a restore holds every merge on a service until that service's history is whole again.

## What the candidate environment decides

[The graph](../what-the-factory-does.md#tight-integration) is not uniform. Up to the merge, what moves is a candidate and deploys are plain — no strategy, no rollout, and no [_analysis window_](08-operations.md#the-analysis-window), because a candidate environment has no organic traffic and a comparison computed from one human exercising a screen is noise that looks like evidence. What is not that comparison is a [quality criterion](03-gates.md#spec) decided under load its own encoding generates — an absolute reading under a condition the criterion states, decided here with the rest of the criteria and establishing nothing past this environment's hardware. What the environment decides is the criteria: the candidate runs on production-like infrastructure against the current releases of its dependencies, and every consumer contract is checked against it there, with the predicate-kind [constraints](02-intent-into-items.md#constraints-and-the-design-system) in force decided against what the same run shows. A [store's](07-contracts.md#the-store-is-a-contract-too) is checked the same way and against a state rather than an exchange — the store the run leaves behind, seeded as [_An environment per candidate_](#an-environment-per-candidate) states and carrying the item's own schema change — so the reads and writes of every release a rollback could restore are decided here, before production rather than at the rollback. Deciding them is running the encoding [_Implementation_](03-gates.md#implementation) authored beside the code, and a criterion that fails is a rejection at the Merge to master gate on the same terms a consumer contract failing there is.

A criterion has a third outcome and it is not a kind of pass. An encoding that produced a failure and a pass over the same build decided nothing — what a repeated run measures is the repetition — so the criterion is **undecided** for that build, and undecided rejects the way a failure does. For a criterion the disagreement has one place to appear: [the queue's confirming run](#the-merge-queue) is the only repeat over one build there is — a run over one build is otherwise something nothing performs twice — and it runs after the Merge to master gate approved, so a criterion goes undecided at the queue and is rejected there. What still reads at that gate the way a failure does is a [consumer contract](07-contracts.md#what-a-consumer-declares) or a [store declaration](07-contracts.md#the-store-is-a-contract-too) undecided by a path the run never exercised, whose disagreement needs no repeat. A predicate-kind constraint the run never exercised reads as nothing instead — it is a bound over what a run shows, never a claim every item must demonstrate, so an item whose run renders no screen is not stopped on a contrast floor it never touched. What that costs is that a departure no run has shown ships unchecked, caught at the first item whose run reaches it. Recording either as a pass is what would make the verdict rest on nothing, since a passing criterion is the whole of what that gate reads about the item's own behaviour. What it costs is that an encoding whose own defect has nothing to do with the change stops the item, and the way out is to author the encoding again rather than to run it again: retrying until green is available to nobody, the factory included, because the factory is what would otherwise do it at scale.

A run that could not be performed produced no outcome, this third one included, and what could not be performed is narrow: the environment failed to compose, or a dependency it needed was unreachable before the run began. A run that composed, started, and ended without the encodings finishing is not among them — the item's own service not answering is behaviour of the build, and its criteria fail as criteria — because reading a deterministic hang as an unperformed run would retry a real defect with nothing counting and nobody reaching it. For the narrow kind, nothing is recorded against any criterion, no attempt is counted, and the run is performed again: an absent input is not a failed check, the reading [an unavailable factor](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) already gets, and performing it again is not the retrying refused above, nothing having been decided that a second run could contradict. A run that cannot be performed twice over stops retrying and waits — [a wait no gate could compute](03-gates.md#what-a-gate-may-change), written into the log by the log with the deployer as caller and actor, counting no attempt and teaching the score nothing, read in [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) like any other wait — and what ends it is the run performing. What it costs is that the wait is bounded by nothing but the dependency coming back, and at the queue it stands in that service's merge order while it lasts.

Merge to master is where a candidate becomes a release and gets its number. Everything from there is machine: numbering, strategy selection, rollout, monitoring, rollback.

The verdict is score-gated like every other gate, so it is not the last place a human decides and there is no last one: the same score decides at each gate whether a human decides there, and a safeguard (9) puts one back. Where one is there, what they are doing is [UAT](../what-humans-do.md) (7) — the environment is no longer named for it, because in the steady state nobody is there. Where it auto-passes, the verdict is the factory's own, taken against acceptance criteria authored from a reading of the [intent](02-intent-into-items.md#intake) that the person who asked confirmed at [the interview's last round](02-intent-into-items.md#the-interview) — and confirmed as criteria by a human (6) only where the score or a safeguard put one at [Spec](03-gates.md#spec), which in the steady state it does not.

The alternative was to separate that verdict by origin — permanent for human-originated features, auto-passable for factory-originated fixes — and that is the second, invisible path the pipeline forbids, separated by a worse predictor than the score's own factors. Scoring it costs this: a change can reach production with nobody having watched it run. The analysis window covers half of that and says so: it catches a change that misbehaves, never one that behaves perfectly and is the wrong thing. What catches a wrong thing built well is the person who asked confirming the factory's reading of the intent at [the interview's last round](02-intent-into-items.md#the-interview), which is not score-gated; the criteria, where the score or a safeguard put a human at the [Spec gate](03-gates.md#spec) to confirm them (6); end users (4, 5); and [a human undoing the change after it shipped](../what-humans-do.md) (10) — and an owner who wants more adds it back per service or per area with a safeguard. Of the four, only the first is present on every item: the second is absent in the steady state, and the last two are after production.
