# Service level objectives

The reading against a service's own recent history says whether that service is failing now. It says nothing about how often it has failed, and a service that fails a fraction of its requests every day crosses nothing: those failures are in the history it is read against.

A **service level objective** is a target an owner authors on the [service record](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) — the proportion of a quantity that must be good over a stated period, 99.9% of requests served without error over thirty days being the ordinary shape. The factory computes what is left of it from the emission the health monitor already reads, and that remainder is the **error budget**. It does one thing: while the budget is exhausted, that service's production deploys are held, and the hold lifts itself when the period rolls forward far enough to restore it. Nothing is decided and no [_page_](07-pages.md) fires, which is the shape the hold a [declared dependency that is not current](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/08-deploy-to-production.md) sets already has.

It is authored and never supplied, which is the opposite of the division the window's size and the [window limit](03-overlapping-windows.md) use, and the reason is that the score learns from the factory's own outcomes: a target inferred that way is set to whatever the service already achieves and never binds. What an objective states is what the customer promised the people using the software, and the factory observes nothing that says what that is. Where an owner authors none there is no budget and nothing is held — that reading and the window are the whole of what protects that service. It is authored on the service record beside the explicit threshold and is not one of [gate policy](../09-gate-policy/README.md)'s eight rows, at the same cost that threshold carries.

What the hold costs: the fix for whatever exhausted the budget is itself a production deploy. So two items pass the hold: a revert, which passes the hold [a rollback leaves](03-overlapping-windows.md) for the same reason, and an item whose intent the health monitor raised on that service. Without them the budget would hold hardest exactly where production is worst. What the exception costs: a fix making things worse takes the same path, so a service can spend what is left of its budget on its own repairs, bounded by the [attempt limit](../03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) and by nothing else.
