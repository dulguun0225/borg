# Pages

One **notifier** delivers everything waiting on a human out of the product, on three channels: mail, chat, and the page. It routes all three the same way — on the [_People_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) declaration, by the [duty](../../what-humans-do.md) the row belongs to, or by the named human where the row belongs to none — so the routing is implemented once rather than beside each thing that waits. Its callers are the components that create a wait: the [gate component](../01-one-pipeline.md) when it opens a decision a human must close, intake when it writes a round of [_interview_](../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md) questions or an intent escalated, [dispatch](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) when it writes an item escalated, and an owner firing one from [Ops](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md). The drift detector calls nothing, so the notifier reads its store itself. A **page** is the narrow channel: what qualifies for one is a wait where the deployed software is worse until a human ends it, and nothing else fires one. It is the only channel that widens and the only one that writes a record — mail and chat write nothing, a delivery that changes no state being no evidence, and a record per delivery would size [the log](../../deferred.md) by how often the factory notifies. What one component costs: a fault in it stops all three channels at once and the factory falls back to whoever remembers to open Work, which is the poll [push-not-poll](../11-screens/02-three-properties-every-screen-needs.md) was written to remove. So the notifier writes a last check record too, beside the health monitor's and the deployer's, and nothing pages when the notifier is what stopped: the channel that would carry that page is the thing that stopped. What makes it visible instead is [Ops](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) reading those records beside the drift detector's own — a poll, and the one place push-not-poll cannot reach, because a component cannot announce its own silence.

A page is not a record; it is the name for the sequence of page events on one row. A **page event** is one append to the log naming the row, the human reached, and which of four it is — reached, acknowledged, widened, answered — each with an [actor](../../deferred.md). The first delivery reaches every human holding the duty at once; unacknowledged it widens exactly once, to the owner; there is no narrower first recipient and no second widening. Acknowledged is written when a human says they have the row, which is an act in the product and nothing else — it ends no wait, decides nothing, and stops only the widening. Answered is written when the row stops waiting, and the component that ends the wait calls the notifier at the same write it ends it with. Widening on unanswered instead would make a human already working the problem indistinguishable from one asleep, because every page whose remedy takes longer than the delay would widen — so the channel that exists to be rare would be loudest exactly where somebody is already on it. What acknowledgement costs is a state a human can set and then walk away from, which is why it stops the widening and never the page: the row still waits, still shows in [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), and still reads as production being worse until somebody ends it. A caller that fails to make the closing call leaves a page unanswered against a row already resolved, which Ops shows as a wait with nothing waiting on it.

One wait ends where nothing calls, and it is the one the notifier already reads for itself: a mismatch is cleared by a human inside [the drift detector](08-drift-detection.md), which writes into a store no factory component may write and calls nothing. So that page's answered event is written by the notifier the next time it reads that store and finds the mismatch cleared, and its time is the time of that read rather than of the clearing. Every other caller makes the closing call.

That condition is already the document's, and two refusals above follow from it rather than being added to it. A [declared dependency](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) that is not its service's current release holds the production deploy, nothing is decided, and the hold lifts itself when the dependency is current again — there is no wait for a human to end. An owner's silence at [_The interview_](../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md) consumes no compute, which is why no limit applies to that wait; a page would be the same mistake made more loudly.

Six things meet that condition today, four of them here, one in [_Rollback_](../06-releases/06-rollback.md), where a failed exit finds no release to return to and the failed release goes on serving, and the sixth in [_An account that runs out is a hold_](../10-fleet/05-an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold.md), where a target credential cannot be reached while the health monitor is calling for a rollback; the condition is what governs — the list follows from it and is not the test. An escalation (12) on an item the factory raised from an incident — the factory saying it cannot fix a defect that is live. A human deciding at a gate on a revert while the rollback that removed the defect still holds: the service is running the build that was restored, master still contains the defect, and nothing ships past that human. And a mismatch [_Drift detection_](08-drift-detection.md) found, which holds that service's production deploys until a human clears it. The fourth is a window past its cap that nothing has evaluated, which is what a stopped health monitor looks like from outside: the release under watch is unmeasured, the rollback that would undo it is not going to happen, the service's production deploys are held at the window limit, and no mechanism the factory has ends any of it — the component that would is the one that stopped. Its own last check record is what makes the state readable rather than inferred. In all four, production is worse than it should be and no mechanism the factory has will improve it.

The first is not special for having come from an incident. An item [_After the analysis window_](04-after-the-analysis-window.md) raises is not one, and takes the same shape as an intent grouped from end-user [_reports_](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) (4, 5); a defect an end user reported is that shape exactly. An item from any of those three, stopped at the attempt limit, is the factory giving up on a defect while the software containing it is live, so it meets the condition without being on the list. What separates these is never which of intake's three sources the intent came from: the refusal that an escalation on a feature item has nothing live that is worse already separates on whether something live is worse, and that is the whole of the test.

One wait meets a condition of its own and is admitted beside these rather than under them: a deadline a [constraint](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) put on an intent, where a law started a clock. The software is no worse for the hour, and the customer is — a breach notified late is a breach notified late whatever production is doing — so it pages whoever holds (2) for that constraint when the deadline is written, at any hour, and is answered when the intent ends or the human marks the obligation met. It is the one page the condition above does not cover, admitted because the condition was written for what the factory can make worse and a statutory period is worse on its own; what it costs is a page class whose remedy the factory cannot perform, so the page delays nothing the factory would otherwise have done, and every one of them reaches a human at whatever hour the trigger happened to arrive.

A rollback the factory performed on its own is reported, not requested, and reporting is not paging — **the factory does not page to inform.** Nothing else waiting on a human meets the condition either: a [UAT](../../what-humans-do.md) assignment (7) delays its own item and holds its own item's environment, an escalation on a feature item has nothing live that is worse, a deploy queued behind its service's window limit is waiting on the factory, and a [_fleet entry with no credential to reach_](../10-fleet/05-an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold.md) stops work rather than making anything live worse — the row most often mistaken for a page and still not one. Those wait in Work, and mail or chat delivers them.

A page has no duty of its own — inventing a thirteenth would be a mechanism where routing already exists. It reaches whoever holds the duty the row belongs to, the same routing that showed them the row in Work.

Not every row belongs to a duty. A mismatch [_Drift detection_](08-drift-detection.md) found holds the production deploy gate, which none of [the twelve](../../what-humans-do.md) owns: confirming criteria (6) and performing UAT (7) are the duties at a gate, and [_What the candidate environment decides_](../05-environments/04-what-the-candidate-environment-decides.md) puts the UAT one at the [Merge to master](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/07-merge-to-master.md) gate. Installing the drift detector is [substrate](../../what-humans-do.md) outside the twelve, so the page reaches whoever [_People_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) records as having installed it, and widens to the owner where nobody is recorded. That is what [_What the fleet is not_](../10-fleet/09-what-the-fleet-is-not.md) already does for a lent credential — routing needs a named human rather than a duty number — and it is why a thirteenth duty stays refused: a duty would score an obligation the twelve deliberately leave unscored, where a named human only has to be reachable.

A duty may be held by more than one human, and a page reaches all of them at once. There is no rotation naming which one it reaches first: a rotation would be a People declaration enforced by nothing, and what a stale one does — widen to every holder — is what happens here without it. The parameter's failure mode is the behaviour that replaces it, so the parameter is what can be dropped. Unacknowledged, a page widens once more, to the owner. The drawback: a page nobody needed reaches everybody, accepted so that none is ever lost, and acknowledgement narrows it only where somebody is awake to send one.

An unanswered page never makes the factory act alone, because the state it is paging about is stable. Where a rollback has run, the service runs the last release whose window closed without failing it and not the one just failed, so the wait delays the fix and never the recovery; where none has, what is live is what was live when the page fired, and the wait delays the fix there too.

That split is also what decides whether a page waits for morning. Where production serves a release the health monitor has called for a rollback on and the rollback has not run — a failed exit with nothing to return to, a target credential that cannot be reached, a window past its cap with the health monitor stopped — the software is worse now and worse for every hour of the wait, and the page fires whenever the condition arises, on every service. Where the rollback has run, or nothing was live to remove — the revert at a gate, an escalation on an item raised from an incident, a mismatch the drift detector found — the software is worse until a human ends the wait and no worse for the hour at which they do. The factory tells the two apart from what it already holds, the release the production deploy record names and whether a rollback the health monitor called for on it has run, and nothing authors the urgency and nothing scores it; the score computes impact for every decision it makes, but which of the two a wait is turns on state and not on likelihood. For the second kind an owner authors on the [service record](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md), beside the explicit threshold and the objective, the hours within which that service pages. A wait of the second kind arising outside them goes out by mail and chat at once, waits in [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) as every row does, and pages at the next hour the service allows; where an owner authors none, it pages at any hour, which is what every service did before there was anything to author. The hours are authored and never supplied, for the reason the [objective](05-service-level-objectives.md) is: nothing the factory observes says which service the customer minds losing overnight, and a value inferred from outcomes would be set to whatever the service already gets. What it costs is another authored value that goes stale the way a People declaration does, and a page that waited until morning on a service whose hours were authored too narrow — the owner who authored them chose that over a page reaching every holder at 02:40 for work that could not have needed doing before morning, which is the thing that makes the next page slower to answer.

A human may fire one on their own judgment, from [_Ops_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), which is the parallel a human's undo (10) already has: the rollback the factory would perform, on a human's judgment instead of the health monitor's. Nothing scores it and no limit applies to it. What limits it is that a page nobody needed makes its recipient slower to answer the next one.

A page is an event on the row it belongs to — who was reached, when it widened, who answered — each recording an actor, which is seam 1 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../../deferred.md) and nothing new. The hours are the one authored thing in it, and they are on the service record; who holds a duty is a People declaration and the widening is the factory's own, so gate policy stays at the rows it has.
