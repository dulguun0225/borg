# Risk score

A vector of named factors, reduced to one number by a published formula. Both halves matter — the number is what a [gate](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it) compares against a threshold, the vector is what a human reads when they disagree with the number. A score nobody can argue with is a score nobody will trust.

## Factors, at least

Three groups of factor, named because a human has to be able to argue with them, and the set is open.

- **The change** — size, how much of the system it can affect, area churn, test coverage, reversibility.
- **Authorship** — a prior, per human and per AI model version, computed from that author's own work with its [vetoes](../what-humans-do.md) and [rollbacks](06-releases.md#rollback) marked. A prior read as a list of only the bad outcomes could never narrow toward trust: what narrows it is the work that was not vetoed, so every outcome on that author's [artifact](01-one-pipeline.md) moves it, a [window](08-operations.md#the-watch-window) closing without harm included. It is kept per model version and not per family, which is how a new version accumulates a prior of its own; the cost is that moving a [fleet entry](10-fleet.md) to a newer version starts its evidence over, and keeping the old name for a new version reads the old version's outcomes as the new one's. The [effort](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-runs-on) an entry runs at and the [brief](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told) an agent works from change how one model behaves under one name too, and both are deliberately left out of the key — [_Paid for is not authored by_](10-fleet.md#paid-for-is-not-authored-by) refuses the split and states what refusing it costs. Both are recorded on the vector a gate firing writes instead, which is where a fact a human argues with belongs. It starts wide for an author the factory has not seen and narrows with evidence. The restriction [_The watch window_](08-operations.md#the-watch-window) keeps — that only a rollback or veto traceable to the [health signal](08-operations.md#the-health-signal) counts — is that window's parameters' rule and not this one's: the window learns from what the [comparison](08-operations.md#the-health-signal) could have seen, the prior from every outcome on that author's work, a change vetoed for being the wrong feature included, because building the wrong thing well says something about the author and nothing about the comparison.
- **Context** — what this change touches in this customer's business, and which sibling services consume what it publishes. The same diff is a different risk in a payments path than on a marketing page.

A version of what an agent is told is scored on a set of its own, because the change group is computed from a diff and a [brief](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told) has no test coverage and no area to have churned. Three factors replace that group at [_A brief or a skill_](03-gates.md#a-brief-or-a-skill) — **reach**, how much of the factory works from this version; **departure**, how far it differs from the version in force; and reversibility, which is high for every version, [withdrawal](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told) being a second record and nothing having been deployed. The authorship and context groups are unchanged, the author being the agent that wrote the version. That the set is different here rather than partly unavailable is the point: a factor the score cannot compute puts a human at the gate, and treating test coverage as missing rather than as inapplicable would put one at every version forever, which is a decision nobody made arriving as a side effect. What it costs is a second factor set to publish, calibrate, and argue with, on a row that fires far less often than the eight of the [default path](03-gates.md#actions-at-each-gate).

Likelihood and impact stay separate until the last step. They answer different questions and call for different responses: likely-wrong but cheap to undo should ship and let rollback handle it; unlikely but catastrophic should be gated regardless. This is also why one score decides three things about a change — the gate reads mostly likelihood against impact, the [rollout strategy](03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy) reads mostly impact against reversibility and how fast a problem would appear, and the boundary of the [_watch window_](08-operations.md#the-watch-window) reads mostly impact. Reading different halves is what keeps them from weakening together: a change misjudged on likelihood, which is what an authorship prior and an unfamiliar area affect, loses its gate and keeps its watch.

The defaults the score supplies where an owner authored nothing are not a fourth factor: [_Gate policy_](09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them)'s seven parameters are each a field of the record its scope names — a service, an area, an environment, or the factory policy record — and six of them move as outcomes arrive, where the three factors are computed from one change. They are fields of a **score version** — a record of the score's own, append-only, naming the published formula, the factor set, and every value the score supplies, with a supplied value for six of the seven rows and none for the [predicate catalog](07-contracts.md#what-a-consumer-declares), which no outcome teaches. Every decision names the version in force. Keeping the supplied value here rather than in the record that holds the authored one is what leaves both single-writer, and it is what keeps a [pin](09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them)'s floor distinguishable from the value moving inside it — one field two components wrote could not express that. The cost is a long sequence of versions, most differing in one number the decisions naming them never read, and an [effective parameter](09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them) that is a read of three things rather than one.

The vector is recorded where it was computed and never recomputed. A gate firing writes it onto that firing's [_decision_](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it) beside the number, the score version, and the [policy version](../what-the-factory-does.md#traceability), so every firing produces a vector of its own. Where an agent authored what is under decision, the vector also names the effort its entry ran at and the versions of the brief and the [skills](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told) it worked from — the two facts the prior deliberately averages over, put where a human arguing with the number can see them, and where a query can group decisions by the version in force without any of it moving the prior. Recomputing one later reads today's prior, today's factor set, and today's formula, which is not the vector the decision was made on — and the vector a human argues with has to exist while they are deciding, which is why the decision is opened at the firing rather than at the verdict. At the four [artifact gates](03-gates.md#actions-at-each-gate) the change factor is computed from the artifact under decision and at [Decomposition](03-gates.md#decomposition) from the set [the cut](02-intent-into-items.md#the-cut) proposed, so it is weakest exactly where the decision is cheapest to get wrong.

A factor the score cannot compute resolves to the value that puts a human at the gate, and the vector records which factor was unavailable and why rather than leaving a gap a reader has to interpret. That covers an input the score could not read, a [build](05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch) whose diff it could not take, and an evaluation that failed outright. The reason is what the number is for: it is compared against a threshold, so a factor that silently reads as absent reads as low risk, and the gate a failure would remove is the gate that failure is evidence for needing. Nothing here decides how far the resolved value moves the number — that is the formula's, published like the rest of it — only its direction, which is the one thing that must not depend on a component being up. What it costs is that a fault in whatever supplies a factor puts humans at gates across the factory at once, and from [_Work_](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people) that looks like the score having changed its mind rather than like an outage; what says otherwise is the vector on each decision naming the same unavailable factor, which is why the naming is not optional.

## How it learns

The score is learned, not fixed. Every bad decision feeds back and refines it: an [auto-passed](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it) change that a human vetoes, a low-risk change its watch window rolled back, a gate the factory would have passed but a human rejected. Outcome feedback is the sharpest signal but not the only one — any source that improves the score is admissible, and the input set stays open by design.

A supplied value moves on both ends of its own row, and [gate policy](09-gate-policy.md#what-is-in-it)'s table is what says where the second end is: every row there states what goes wrong at each end, and both are evidence. One end is something going wrong — a rollback, a veto, a crossing the [comparison](08-operations.md#the-health-signal) could have seen and did not, an [item](01-one-pipeline.md) the [attempt bound](03-gates.md#the-attempt-bound) gave up on. The other is the parameter costing more than it returns, which is observable for most of them: attempts nothing has ever needed, a [cap](08-operations.md#the-watch-window) far longer than any window of that service takes to resolve, a [window's size](08-operations.md#the-watch-window) finer than its traffic can rule anything out at. Reading only the first end would make every value move one way and stay there, and a value that only tightens ends up costing what it was set to protect against — the clearest case being a [watch window](08-operations.md#the-watch-window) so fine that it never resolves, which watches every release for the whole cap and catches nothing.

Two of them have no second end here and are stated as such rather than left to be discovered. No outcome says [the confidence a comparison must reach](08-operations.md#the-watch-window) was too high: what would show it is a rollback that turned out unnecessary, and nothing ever establishes that a condemned release was fine. And the other end of the [item-size target](02-intent-into-items.md#the-cut) is cost per feature and rework rate, both of which are [_Factory_](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people)'s to report and neither of which anything measures yet. Both are allowed to move one way because neither compounds — the traffic a comparison needs grows as the log of the confidence where it grows as the inverse square of the size — so what an owner may have to do is author over one of them, which [authoring being an override](09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them) is exactly the case for.

The [risk threshold](09-gate-policy.md#what-is-in-it) is a third exception and not for want of an observable. Its own row states the loose end plainly — gate more and the human work the factory exists to remove comes back — and how often a row puts a human at it is a count, needing no outcome and no counterfactual. What it is not is evidence about safety, and the difference from the two above is the difference between protection that has become worthless and protection that has become expensive: a window too fine to resolve rules nothing out, so loosening it gives up nothing, where a human at a gate really does read the change and really does stop some of them. Loosening the threshold on its cost alone would be the score deciding that human review is not worth its price, which is a judgment about what the factory is for rather than a reading of what happened — and the tree already answers a cost complaint the other way, by having the owner author the value (8). So the threshold moves down on outcomes and up on one thing only, which is the next paragraph.

Scoring on authorship reinforces itself if left alone: a distrusted author is gated more, gated work draws more scrutiny, more scrutiny finds more faults, and the distrust is confirmed. The factory holds out a random sample — occasionally auto-passing what it would have gated, under the longest watch window there is — to keep unbiased signal on the authors and areas it has stopped trusting.

The sample is also the only evidence there is for raising a threshold, which is more than the paragraph above asks of it. A change that was gated and turned out well is consistent with three things at once — that it was never risky, that the human at the gate caught what would have made it risky, and that its author was more careful for knowing a human would read it — and no record tells those apart, so none of them is evidence that the gate was unnecessary. What does tell them apart is a change the score wanted gated and did not gate, and the sample is the only thing that produces one. So a factory whose sample never selects has a threshold that can fall and cannot rise: it goes on tightening on every outcome it gets wrong and never loosens on one it got right, which is the same shape as a [window](08-operations.md#the-watch-window) whose size only ever narrows. That is worth saying because the sample reads like a fairness mechanism for authors the factory has stopped trusting, and it is also the only reason one half of the calibration is possible at all.

The sample is the score's own to hold out of, and it reaches nothing an owner pinned. A pin (9) can only add, and a gate pinned always-on is a human added; a sample that could pass one would be the single mechanism in [the tree](../glossary.md) that removes a human from a gate, which is what a pin exists to prevent. So what the sample may auto-pass is what the score itself would have gated, and the drawback is that the areas an owner distrusts most are the ones it learns least about.

The selection is recorded on the decisions rather than as a set of its own, in two fields that must not be one. **Held out** says the score selected this [item](01-one-pipeline.md), written on every decision on it from the selection onward, so an item selected once auto-passes every gate the score would have gated and reaches production with a human removed at each of them. **Auto-passed by** says, at this firing, whether the auto-pass came from the number being under the threshold or from the selection, and reads threshold at a gate the score would have passed anyway. The comparison runs the window to the [cap](08-operations.md#the-watch-window) on the selection and not on the provenance, because what the sample is evidence about is a [release](06-releases.md#the-release-record) reaching production with a human removed somewhere above it. A held-out release also takes a strategy that keeps a [control](08-operations.md#the-health-signal) wherever there is a build to start one from, whatever the vector's impact half would have picked: the sample exists to produce outcome evidence, and a [straight](03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy) deploy leaves only [the weak fallback](08-operations.md#the-health-signal). What that costs is a second [instance set](08-operations.md#the-health-signal) for a window that runs to the longest [exit](08-operations.md#the-watch-window) there is, and a sample measured better than the population it represents — so what its evidence supports is that a comparison was available, not that an unsampled release on the same author would have shown the same. A service's first release is straight whatever the score prefers, so the sample cannot usefully select one.
