# Risk score

A vector of named factors, reduced to one number by a published formula. Both halves matter — the number is what a gate compares against a threshold, the vector is what a human reads when they disagree with the number. A score nobody can argue with is a score nobody will trust.

## Factors, at least

- **The change** — size, how much of the system it can affect, area churn, test coverage, reversibility.
- **Authorship** — a prior, per human and per AI model version, computed from that author's own work with its vetoes and rollbacks marked. Read as a list of only the bad outcomes it could never narrow toward trust: what narrows it is the work that was not vetoed, so every outcome on that author's artifact moves it, a window closing without harm included. The key is the model version and not the family, which is how a new version accumulates a prior of its own; the cost is that moving a fleet entry to a newer version starts its evidence over, and keeping the old name for a new version hands the new one evidence it did not earn. It starts wide for an author the factory has not seen and narrows with evidence. The restriction [_The watch window_](08-operations.md#the-watch-window) keeps — that only a rollback or veto traceable to the health signal counts — is that window's parameters' rule and not this one's: the window learns from what the comparison could have seen, the prior from every outcome on that author's work, a change vetoed for being the wrong feature included, because building the wrong thing well says something about the author and nothing about the comparison.
- **Context** — what this change touches in this customer's business, and which sibling services consume what it publishes. The same diff is a different risk in a payments path than on a marketing page.

Likelihood and impact stay separate until the last step. They answer different questions and call for different responses: likely-wrong but cheap to undo should ship and let rollback handle it; unlikely but catastrophic should be gated regardless. This is also why one score decides three things about a change — the gate reads mostly likelihood against impact, the rollout strategy reads mostly impact against reversibility and how fast a problem would appear, and the boundary of the [_watch window_](08-operations.md#the-watch-window) reads mostly impact. Reading different halves is what keeps them from weakening together: a change misjudged on likelihood, which is what an authorship prior and an unfamiliar area affect, loses its gate and keeps its watch.

The defaults the score supplies where an owner authored nothing are not a fourth factor: [_Gate policy_](09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them)'s seven parameters apply per stage, per service, or per area and move as outcomes arrive, where the three above are computed from one change. They are fields of a **score version** — a record of the score's own, append-only, naming the published formula, the factor set, and every value the score supplies, with a supplied value for six of the seven rows and none for the predicate catalog, which no outcome teaches. Every decision names the version in force. Keeping the supplied value here rather than in the record that holds the authored one is what leaves both single-writer, and it is what keeps a pin's floor distinguishable from the value moving inside it — one field two components wrote could not express that. The cost is a long sequence of versions, most differing in one number the decisions naming them never read, and an effective parameter that is a read of three things rather than one.

The vector is recorded where it was computed and never recomputed. A gate firing writes it onto that firing's [_decision_](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it) beside the number, the score version, and the policy version, so one item's eight firings produce eight vectors. Recomputing one later reads today's prior, today's factor set, and today's formula, which is not the vector the decision was made on — and the vector a human argues with has to exist while they are deciding, which is why the decision is opened at the firing rather than at the verdict. At the four artifact gates the change factor is computed from the artifact under decision and at Decomposition from the set the cut proposed, so it is weakest exactly where the decision is cheapest to get wrong.

## How it learns

The score is learned, not fixed. Every bad decision feeds back and refines it: an auto-passed change that a human vetoes, a low-risk change its watch window rolled back, a gate the factory would have passed but a human rejected. Outcome feedback is the sharpest signal but not the only one — any source that improves the score is admissible, and the input set stays open by design.

Scoring on authorship reinforces itself if left alone: a distrusted author is gated more, gated work draws more scrutiny, more scrutiny finds more faults, and the distrust is confirmed. The factory holds out a random sample — occasionally auto-passing what it would have gated, under the longest watch window there is — to keep unbiased signal on the authors and areas it has stopped trusting.

The sample is the score's own to hold out of, and it reaches nothing an owner pinned. A pin (9) can only add, and a gate pinned always-on is a human added; a sample that could pass one would be the single mechanism in the tree that removes a human from a gate, which is what a pin exists to prevent. So what the sample may auto-pass is what the score itself would have gated, and the drawback is that the areas an owner distrusts most are the ones it learns least about.

The selection is recorded on the decisions rather than as a set of its own, in two fields that must not be one. **Held out** says the score selected this item, written on every decision on it from the selection onward, so an item selected once auto-passes every gate the score would have gated and reaches production with no human above it. **Auto-passed by** says, at this firing, whether the auto-pass came from the number being under the threshold or from the selection, and reads threshold at a gate the score would have passed anyway. The comparison runs the window to the cap on the selection and not on the provenance, because what the sample is evidence about is a release reaching production with a human removed somewhere above it. A held-out release also takes a strategy that keeps a control wherever there is a build to start one from, whatever the vector's impact half would have picked: the sample exists to produce outcome evidence, and a straight deploy leaves only the weak fallback. What that costs is a second instance set for a window that runs to the longest exit there is, and a sample measured better than the population it represents — so what its evidence supports is that a comparison was available, not that an unsampled release on the same author would have shown the same. A service's first release is straight whatever the score prefers, so the sample cannot usefully select one.
