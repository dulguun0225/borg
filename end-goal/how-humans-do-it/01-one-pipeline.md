# One pipeline

One path, taken by everything the factory builds: what moves along it, who authors each step, and why there is no second path.

**Item and agent.** An **item** is the unit the factory moves: one thing that can ship by itself, one [thread](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people), one [release](06-releases.md#the-release-record). An **agent** is a worker the factory runs — a model in a role, with a scope, working from a [_brief_](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told) the factory wrote. Two agents on the same model share one [authorship prior](04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least): the [score](04-risk-score.md) is kept per model, not per role, and not per brief either — which [_Paid for is not authored by_](10-fleet.md#paid-for-is-not-authored-by) settles, against the argument that would split it.

**Role and scope.** A **role** is what an agent is put on, a **scope** is where it may be put. A role names the work of one stage — the artifact it authors, or the [environment](05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch) it reaches through seam 4 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../deferred.md) — so an owner composing a [fleet entry](10-fleet.md) chooses among roles and never invents one, the stages being the factory's own. It is not a duty: a human holds one of [the numbered twelve](../what-humans-do.md), an agent holds a role, and nothing about a role is scored, because the prior is the model's. A scope is drawn on the same lines a [pin](09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them) (9) is — a [project](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people), an [area](../what-humans-do.md) — and limits which items an entry may be put on and which environments it may reach. Two roles are put on an intent rather than an item, because [the interview](02-intent-into-items.md#the-interview) and [the cut](02-intent-into-items.md#the-cut) are work done before there is an item to match against, and what scopes them is the intent's [originating project](02-intent-into-items.md#intake) — an intent naming no [service](02-intent-into-items.md#the-cut) and no area until the cut writes them. What that costs is that an area-scoped entry can never take either, and that an intent whose source supplied no project is reachable only by an entry drawn wider than one, so an owner who scoped everything narrowly has intents nothing will refine — a thread that does not advance, which is what [_The fleet_](10-fleet.md) already says a stage with no matching entry looks like.

**Dispatch is a match, not a judgment.** The item's stage against the role, the item's [service](02-intent-into-items.md#the-cut) and area against the scope. That is why [_Dispatch_](02-intent-into-items.md#dispatch) needs no [gate](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it): nothing there is decided that a later gate does not see, since everything the match produces is reviewed at one. Dispatch is also what writes the field it matches on — [_What an item names_](02-intent-into-items.md#what-an-item-names) puts the item's stage, the attempts each stage has taken, and what each stage spent there, every stage reporting a transition to it. Writing a transition somebody else reported is not a judgment either, and the component that reads the field on every move is the one that keeps it. What a scope costs is that it binds nothing yet — dispatch honours it and no mechanism stops an agent reaching past it. It is stated now because a policy attaching at the seam needs a subject already named, which is what every [deferred seam](../deferred.md) provides and this is the fleet entry's half of.

**What an agent reads is content, never instruction.** An agent is told one thing and reads everything else. What it is told is its [_brief_](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told), and the [_skills_](10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told) that match the item — records the factory writes, versioned, decided at a gate, and authored from the factory's own decisions rather than from anything that arrived from outside. Everything a stage hands it is material to work from instead — the [intent](02-intent-into-items.md#intake)'s statement, the [reports](02-intent-into-items.md#reports) grouped into it, an owner's answer to an [interview](02-intent-into-items.md#the-interview) question, the repository being changed, what a run printed. Text arriving through any of them that tells the agent to skip a stage, author something else, or reach past its scope is content that says so, and the agent reports it and continues with the stage it was dispatched onto. The line between the two is where the text came from and not what it says: a sentence in a brief is instruction because a gate decided it, and the same sentence in a report is content because nobody did. The widest exposure is the [report store](02-intent-into-items.md#reports), because it takes words from end users, nothing about it is authenticated, and nothing about it can be: a report is a stranger's sentence, and the [grouper](02-intent-into-items.md#reports) and every stage below it read that sentence. This is a property required of an agent rather than a check on the text — a check would have to decide what a sentence is trying to do, which is the thing the factory is asking a model to do in the first place. What it costs is that the exposure [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../deferred.md) names for one path — nothing outside the pipeline disagrees with the pipeline — arrives here through what the pipeline reads rather than through what it is, and no field populated today makes it cheaper to answer later.

**Authorship is per stage.** Every item goes through the pipeline. A human-authored change, an AI-authored change, and one the two write together take the same stages, the same gates, and the same score. Authorship is an attribute of each stage, not a mode on the item: an item can have an AI [spec](03-gates.md#spec), a co-authored [plan](03-gates.md#implementation-plan), and a human implementation, and it is still one thread.

**One entrance for every artifact.** Three things can author a version of an artifact — the agent in the stage's role, a human [backstopping](../what-humans-do.md) that stage (11, 12), and the **gate component**, which fires every gate and is where a human takes [Edit in place](03-gates.md#actions-at-each-gate) — so the writer cannot be the position they occupy. The **artifact store** is that writer, and the three are callers of it. It is [_Intake_](02-intent-into-items.md#intake)'s arrangement applied to artifacts and taken for the same reason: the version chain, the [actor](../deferred.md#security-comes-last), the authorship attribute, the author, and the criteria a spec version introduces are implemented once rather than once per author. What it costs is a dependency edge from every authoring agent and from the gate component back to one component, and no way to hand the factory a spec written somewhere else without it passing through the same call.

**A version names its author.** The authorship attribute says which of the three wrote it; the author says which one — the model version for an agent, the person for a human at the stage or at a gate. Both are fields, because an authorship prior is computed from that author's own work and the attribute cannot answer whose work a version is. Two agents in different roles on one model are one author under two actors, which is the same fact _Item and agent_ states above, and it is why the author is the model version rather than the role or the [fleet entry](10-fleet.md) the agent ran as. What it costs is that a version whose author the factory cannot name is a version no prior can read, so the field is required and there is nothing to submit without one.

**Taking over.** Backstop duties (11, 12) are this and nothing more — a human authors one stage instead of an agent. Taking over is not leaving the factory.

**Self-raised work.** A bug the factory finds and fixes itself is an item like any other. It appears in [_Work_](11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people), takes the same stages, and is [auto-passed](03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it) only where the score allows. There is no second, invisible path, and nothing ships that the [trust number](11-surfaces.md#the-trust-number) cannot see.

**Emergencies.** There is no bypass, including for incidents. A human at a gate is not a delay: the emergency action is approve now, not skip. A change that should not have shipped is caught by the [_watch window_](08-operations.md#the-watch-window), not by a faster route around the pipeline.

**Queue order.** Waiting in [the merge queue](05-environments.md#the-merge-queue) is one place a queue forms, and its order is settable — an urgent item goes to the front. That is not a bypass. Reordering a queue changes when an item reaches the gates; it does not change which gates it passes through. [Numbered releases](06-releases.md#the-number) are the other queue, and it forms only where something is holding deploys: the service already has its K windows open — the limit [_Overlapping windows_](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows) sets — or a [rollback](06-releases.md#rollback) whose revert has not shipped is holding every deploy but that revert, or a [declared dependency](02-intent-into-items.md#the-cut) is not its service's [current release](06-releases.md#the-deploy-record), or [_The reconciler_](08-operations.md#the-reconciler) has found the service's record disagreeing with what runs, or a human is holding at the [production deploy gate](03-gates.md#deploy-to-production). That queue's order is not settable: the number orders builds, so they deploy to production in that order, [_the revert a rollback is waiting on_](08-operations.md#overlapping-windows) excepted.
