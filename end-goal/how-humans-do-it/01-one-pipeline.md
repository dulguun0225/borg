# One pipeline

One path, taken by everything the factory builds: what moves along it, who authors each step, and why there is no second path.

**Item and agent.** An **item** is the unit the factory moves: one thing that can ship by itself, one [timeline](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), one [release](06-releases/02-the-release-record.md). An **agent** is a worker the factory runs — a model in a role, with a scope, working from a [_role prompt_](10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md) the factory wrote. Two agents on the same model share one [per-author prior](04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md): the [score](04-risk-score/README.md) is kept per model, not per role or role prompt — which [_Paid for is not authored by_](10-fleet/04-paid-for-is-not-authored-by.md) settles, against the argument that would split it.

**Role and scope.** A **role** is what an agent is put on, a **scope** is where it may be put. A role names the work of one stage, the artifact it authors — no role reaches a [deploy target](05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md), a deploy being performed by the [deployer](08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md), the component that reaches [environments](05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md), and not a stage an agent is dispatched to — so an owner composing a [fleet entry](10-fleet/README.md) chooses among roles and never invents one, the stages being the factory's own. It is not a duty: a human holds one of [the numbered twelve](../what-humans-do.md), an agent holds a role, and nothing about a role is scored, because the prior is the model's. A scope is drawn on the same lines a [safeguard](09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) (9) is — a [project](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), an [area](../what-humans-do.md) — and limits which items an entry may be put on. Two roles are put on an intent rather than an item, because [the interview](02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md) and [decomposition](02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) happen before there is an item to match against, and what scopes them is the [project the intent's request came in under](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) — an intent naming no [service](02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) and no area until decomposition writes them. A third, [the grouper](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md), is put on neither: it runs before an intent exists, and what scopes it is the project whose reports it reads. What that costs is that an area-scoped entry can never take any of the three, and that an intent whose source supplied no project is reachable only by an entry drawn wider than one, so an owner who scoped everything narrowly has intents nothing will refine — a timeline that does not advance, which is what [_The fleet_](10-fleet/README.md) says a stage with no matching entry looks like.

**Dispatch is a match, not a judgment.** The item's stage against the role, the item's [service](02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) and area against the scope. That is why [_Dispatch_](02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) needs no [gate](03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md): nothing there is decided that a later gate does not see, since everything the match produces is reviewed at one. Dispatch is also what writes the field it matches on — [_What an item names_](02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/02-what-an-item-names.md) puts the item's stage, the attempts each stage has taken, and what each stage spent there, every stage reporting a transition to it. Writing a transition somebody else reported is not a judgment either, and the component that reads the field on every move is the one that keeps it. What a scope costs is that it binds nothing yet — dispatch honours it and no mechanism stops an agent reaching past it, though every call the agent makes carries it as the principal seam 5 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../deferred.md) puts on a call, so what stops it later has the call to read. It is stated now because a policy attaching at the seam needs a subject already named, which every [deferred seam](../deferred.md) provides and this is the fleet entry's half of.

**What an agent reads is content, never instruction.** An agent is told one thing and reads everything else. What it is told is its [_role prompt_](10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md), and the [_skills_](10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md) that match the item — records the factory writes, versioned, decided at a gate, and authored from the factory's own decisions rather than from anything that arrived from outside. Everything a stage hands it is material to work from instead — the [intent](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md)'s statement, the [reports](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) grouped into it, an owner's answer to an [interview](02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md) question, the repository being changed, what a run printed. Text arriving through any of them that tells the agent to skip a stage, author something else, or reach past its scope is content that says so, and the agent reports it and continues with the stage it was dispatched onto. The line between the two is where the text came from and not what it says: a sentence in a role prompt is instruction because a gate decided it, and the same sentence in a report is content because nobody did. The widest exposure is the [report store](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md), because it takes words from end users, nothing about it is authenticated, and nothing about it can be: a report is a stranger's sentence, and the [grouper](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) and every stage below it read that sentence. This is a property required of an agent rather than a check on the text — a check would have to decide what a sentence is trying to do, which is the thing the factory is asking a model to do in the first place. The grouper is an agent so that the property reaches the first reader on that path, and the property is still an instruction, worth what an instruction is worth: what stops a report read as instruction from shipping code is the human [_Reports_](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) keeps at the [Spec](03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec.md) gate for every intent grouped from them. What it costs is that the exposure [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../deferred.md) names for one path — nothing outside the pipeline disagrees with the pipeline — arrives here through what the pipeline reads rather than through what it is, and no field populated today makes it cheaper to answer later.

**What did not fit is recorded.** Reading everything else is bounded by what the model an [entry runs on](10-fleet/01-what-an-agent-runs-on.md) can read at once, and the material a stage hands an agent has no such bound: five hundred grouped reports, years of [constraints](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) in force, a mature repository, a long run's output. The factory assembles what an agent is given, so it is the one that knows what it left out, and an artifact authored from a truncated read is complete in form and missing whatever fell off — the omission indistinguishable, on every record, from a decision taken on complete input. So the artifact version records what material was withheld from its author, and the [score](04-risk-score/README.md) reads that the way it reads [a factor it cannot compute](04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) — toward the gate, named on the decision's [factor vector](04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) — because an input that silently did not fit reads as an input that was read. What it costs is the direction that treatment always costs: the intents with the most evidence behind them are the likeliest to land a human at a gate, and a factory that has accumulated years of material pays it more and more often.

**Authorship is per stage.** Every item goes through the pipeline. A human-authored change, an AI-authored change, and one the two write together take the same stages, the same gates, and the same score. Authorship is an attribute of each stage, not a mode on the item: an item can have an AI [spec](03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec.md), a co-authored [plan](03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/03-implementation-plan.md), and a human implementation, and it is still one timeline.

**One entrance for every artifact.** Three things can author a version of an artifact — the agent in the stage's role, a human [backstopping](../what-humans-do.md) that stage (11, 12), and the **gate component**, which fires every gate and is where a human takes [Edit in place](03-gates/03-actions-at-each-gate.md) — so the writer cannot be the position they occupy. The **artifact store** is that writer, and the three are callers of it. One more call authors nothing: at install, and at a first start on an upgrade that changed shipped words, the factory itself calls the store to enter what shipped — when, and what the entry then awaits, is [_The fleet_](10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md)'s to say — with the factory's own start as the actor. It is [_Intake_](02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md)'s arrangement applied to artifacts and taken for the same reason: the version chain, the [actor](../deferred.md#security-comes-last), the authorship attribute, the author, and the criteria a spec version introduces are implemented once rather than once per author. What it costs is a dependency edge from every authoring agent, from the gate component, and from the factory's own start back to one component, and no way to hand the factory a spec written somewhere else without it passing through the same call.

**A version names its author.** The authorship attribute says which of the three wrote it; the author says which one — the model version for an agent, the person for a human at the stage or at a gate. Both are fields, because a per-author prior is computed from that author's own work and the attribute cannot answer whose work a version is. Two agents in different roles on one model are one author under two actors, which is the same fact _Item and agent_ states above, and it is why the author is the model version rather than the role or the [fleet entry](10-fleet/README.md) the agent ran as. What it costs is that a version whose author the factory cannot name is a version no prior can read, so the field is required and there is nothing to submit without one. The one exception is the entry nobody wrote — the shipped version the factory enters at install or at an upgrade's first start, per the call above — which carries the author and the authorship attribute both empty, the empty pair saying exactly that, and is read by no prior. The entries install writes precede any gate and enter in force, a factory with nothing decided in it having to run; every entry an upgrade writes awaits its gate, the empty author resolved rather than weighed there — a human decides whatever the formula returns, as [_the score_](04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) states — and a chain the upgrade starts has nothing in force until that human does, only the work that would have used it waiting.

**Taking over.** Backstop duties (11, 12) are this and nothing more — a human authors one stage instead of an agent. Taking over is not leaving the factory.

**Self-raised work.** A bug the factory finds and fixes itself is an item like any other. It appears in [_Work_](11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), takes the same stages, and is [auto-passed](03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) only where the score allows. There is no second, invisible path, and nothing ships unrecorded.

**Emergencies.** There is no bypass, including for incidents. A human at a gate is not a delay: the emergency action is approve now, not skip. A change that should not have shipped is caught by the [_analysis window_](08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md), not by a faster route around the pipeline.

**Queue order.** Waiting in [the merge queue](05-environments/03-the-merge-queue.md) is one place a queue forms, and its order is settable — an urgent item goes to the front. That is not a bypass. Reordering a queue changes when an item reaches the gates; it does not change which gates it passes through. [Numbered releases](06-releases/04-the-release-number.md) are the other queue, and it forms only where something is holding deploys: the service already holds as many windows open as its [window limit](08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) allows, or its [error budget](08-operations/05-service-level-objectives.md) is exhausted, or a [rollback](06-releases/06-rollback.md) whose revert has not shipped is holding every deploy but that revert, or a [declared dependency](02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) is not its service's [current release](06-releases/05-the-deploy-record.md), or [_Drift detection_](08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) has found the service's record disagreeing with what runs, or a human is holding at the [production deploy gate](03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/08-deploy-to-production.md). That queue's order is not settable: the release number orders builds, so they deploy to production in that order, [_the revert a rollback is waiting on_](08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) excepted.
