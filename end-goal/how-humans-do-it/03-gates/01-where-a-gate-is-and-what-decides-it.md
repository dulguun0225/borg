# Where a gate is, and what decides it

There is a gate at every [stage](../01-one-pipeline.md) boundary: after [decomposition](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) into [items](../01-one-pipeline.md), after the spec, implementation plan, tasks, and implementation are authored, and before each merge and each deploy. Two kinds: an **artifact gate** decides over a document a stage has written, and an **event gate** decides whether an event — a merge, a deploy — happens at all, which is what makes [hold](04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) a stop rather than an undo. The mechanism is permanent — it does not weaken as the factory improves.

The factory scores each change and auto-passes what it judges low risk. The same [score](../04-risk-score/README.md) picks the [_rollout strategy_](02-the-rollout-strategy.md). Humans override by adding a safeguard — a gate held always-on, or a strategy that keeps a [control](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) — and can undo a change after it shipped.

A gate **firing** — one gate deciding one thing at one moment — writes one **decision** into the append-only log seam 2 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../../deferred.md) describes, which is that log's single [writer](../../what-the-factory-does.md#tight-integration) with the gate component as its caller. A decision is two rows and not one: an **open event** appended when the gate fires, and a **close event** appended when the **verdict** is given — approve, reject, or hold — naming the row it closes. A row is chained when it is appended, so a verdict written onto the open event afterwards would be a rewrite of a chained record — the rewrite [_Drift detection_](../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md)'s check on the chain exists to catch, made the ordinary path for every gate in the factory. Two appends is what a [_page_](../08-operations/07-pages.md) already does for the same reason: a page is a sequence of rows about one wait, each saying which of them it is. The [_deploy record_](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record.md) advances in place instead, and that is not a counterexample — it is an ordinary record rather than a row of the log, and nothing chains it. What forces the write at the firing is the [factor vector](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md): a human is meant to argue with the score's factors before deciding, so the vector has to exist while they are deciding, and it cannot be computed when they open the row because the score version moves as outcomes arrive. Opened, it names the gate row, the item, the [build](../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) where one exists, the [artifact version](../01-one-pipeline.md) under decision at an artifact gate, the vector and the number, the [policy version](../../what-the-factory-does.md#traceability) and the [score version](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md), the values actually applied, and the [duty](../../what-humans-do.md) or named human the row waits on. The artifact version is there because the record is open across two events and artifacts are editable at a gate: [Edit in place](03-actions-at-each-gate.md) authors a new version through the [artifact store](../01-one-pipeline.md) while the row is still waiting, so a verdict that named only the item would point at whatever the artifact says when someone reads it rather than at what was decided over. Naming it costs one field, the version chain already existing; what it costs a reader is that an approval names a version rather than an artifact, so asking what was approved reads the chain rather than the current artifact. At Decomposition it names the [intent](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) and the set decomposition proposed rather than one item, that row being the one gate above a [timeline](../11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) rather than on it. At [_A role prompt or a skill_](07-what-particular-gates-decide/09-a-role-prompt-or-a-skill.md) it names the version under decision and no item at all, that row belonging to no timeline, no [service](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md), and no build. The close event names the open event, the verdict, its actor, when that actor opened the row where the actor is a human, and where the verdict sends an item back [what it returns to](06-going-back-up.md), and names neither the policy version nor the score version: what a decision was made under is a fact of the firing, so it is written once where the firing is and two rows cannot come to disagree about it. Both are written against the item and the build and never against the [release](../06-releases/02-the-release-record.md), including at the production deploy gate, which fires after the release exists — one rule for all eight rows, so no reader has to know which side of the merge a gate is on. What that costs is that a decision is read by joining two rows rather than by reading one, and that a pending gate is an open event with no close event yet — which is how [_Work_](../11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) already describes it. An item stopped at [_the attempt limit_](05-the-attempt-limit.md) leaves an open event that never receives its close event, so absence of a verdict is not by itself evidence that anyone is still deciding. An open event admits exactly one close event — the log's writer refuses a second close naming an open event already closed — so where [a duty is held by more than one human](../08-operations/07-pages.md) the first verdict decides and a later one is refused, and the refusal is a state the screen shows: the row already decided, with its verdict, never an error about nothing. And [Edit in place](03-actions-at-each-gate.md) while the row waits re-fires the gate rather than leaving the verdict to bind to a version the human rejected by editing it: authoring the new version appends a new open event naming it, with a vector computed from what is now under decision, and the superseded open event never receives its close — the reading the attempt limit's abandoned rows already have. The row an edit fires waits on the human whose edit fired it, an edit not being an approval, so a version a human wrote at a gate is decided by one however the number moves. What the pair costs is that approving after editing is two rows where it reads as one action, and that open events accumulate without closes wherever an edit, a supersession, or an escalation moved the decision elsewhere.

A human at a gate is an instrument, and the design reads it rather than assumes it. Three things follow. The vector is shown while the human decides and the number only beside the verdict once it is written: [_Implementation_](07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation.md) derives an encoding's expected behaviour from the criterion's sentence and never from the code, because a reader of the code agrees with it, and a reader shown the score's conclusion first agrees with that — and a human at a gate already knows the number was above the [risk threshold](../09-gate-policy/01-what-is-in-it.md), that being why they are there, so what withholding it costs is only how far above. The close event carries, beside its own time, when the actor opened the row in [Work](../11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people), written with Work as the caller so that it is the screen's report and not the human's, and [Factory](../11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) reports how long a row was open in front of a human before its verdict, per duty and per named human, beside the wait it already reports. And the pair Factory reports for the factory — what it auto-approved and how often that was later undone (10) or rolled back — it reports per human the same way, over the rows each approved. Those are readings and not levers: nothing removes a human from a gate on a number, and a safeguard (9) adds one whatever the readings say. What they change is what a safeguard is understood to buy — a human added, whose detection is measured by the pair rather than assumed — so an owner can see a gate a human signs without reading: rows approved in the time the previous three hundred took, undone at the auto-pass rate. What it costs is a field a row left open in another tab overstates; a number per named human that reads as a measure of a person, kept because the alternative is a defence the design cites and never examines; and a residue the readings show and do not repair — duties 11 and 12 hand a human only what [the fleet](../10-fleet.md) of agents failed at, on practice that shrinks as the factory improves, and nothing here keeps a human practised.

A failing rollout rolls back on its own, inside its [_analysis window_](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) — no human involved, no waiting. The rollback is reported, not requested.

A human's undo (10) assumes the change can still be undone, and what a human may do narrows as later work builds on it: a rollback while the build it would return to is still running, a revert once it is not. Reversibility is a factor of the score and what a human may still do is limited by it, through the strategy it picks and the [instances of the build being replaced](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) that strategy keeps running. It narrows that way and no other: with one item per release, a change is never harder to undo because it shipped alongside nine others. What overlapping analysis windows add is not difficulty but reach — a rollback undoes every release above its target, up to the [window limit](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md): the one set of items the factory ships together.
