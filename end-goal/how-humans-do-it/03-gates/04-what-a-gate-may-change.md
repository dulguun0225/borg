# What a gate may change

At a gate, artifacts are editable by hand. Code is not: a gate approves or rejects an implementation, it never hand-patches one. A human who wants different code authors it upstream and [sends the item back](06-going-back-up.md) to have it built again.

Merge and deploy gates edit nothing — what they decide is an event, not a document. Reject sends the item back to a stage — the one its verdict names, and the nearest authoring stage above the row where it names none, which [_Going back up_](06-going-back-up.md) sets out; hold leaves the event queued with the change still good, which is why only the deploy rows offer it. What a hold waits on differs by gate: at the production deploy gate the service's open windows already at its window limit, a rollback whose revert has not shipped, a declared dependency that is not live, or a record the [drift detector](../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) found disagreeing with what runs; at the candidate deploy gate a dependency that is not live yet — the producing release of a [contract migration](../07-contracts/02-no-single-item-may-break-a-contract.md), since a candidate environment is composed from its dependencies' [current releases](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record.md) and never from another service's candidate — or a platform with no room for another environment, the one condition here decomposition could not have declared. The two are different answers and have to stay distinguishable: the score learns from a reject and should learn nothing from a hold.

There are three kinds of hold, and only one is written as a decision. A hold a human sets is the verdict of that firing's decision record, with that human as the actor. A hold the factory sets over a record that already exists — an open window, an [error budget](../08-operations/05-service-level-objectives.md) exhausted, a dependency that is not current, a rollback whose revert has not shipped, a mismatch the drift detector found — writes nothing and is recomputed at every firing, which is what checking at the moment of firing already requires; a record for it would be a decision where the design says nothing is decided, and re-testing would append one every time the gate re-fired. A wait the factory could not compute at a firing, because no gate fired or because the condition is not a record — a credential that cannot be reached, a platform with no room — is written into the log by the log, with the component that met it as the caller and the actor, so [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) has a row to show. None of the three counts against the attempt limit and none teaches the score. What that costs: the factory's own hold over a record that already exists is written nowhere, so how long it has been holding is answerable for the other two alone.
