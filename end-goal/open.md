# Open

A question belongs here when an [owner](what-humans-do.md) decides it and the design cannot answer it by applying a pattern it already holds. It is phrased as the question and what turns on it, and is deleted from here once folded into the file that owns its subject.

## How does the factory learn whether a role prompt version is better?

The factory authors [what an agent is told](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md), a **role prompt** per [role](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) and a **skill** per subject, each versioned, and has no way to find out whether a version improved the work. The [evaluation set](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/02-a-model-under-a-name.md) reads two versions on one model version as two results on fixed tasks, which measures the set and not the work. The [per-author prior](how-the-factory-works/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) is per model and deliberately cannot see a role prompt; there is no [analysis window](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) over a version and no [control](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) to compare one against, a control being instances taking organic traffic and a role prompt serving none. What is left is [_Factory_](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md)'s gate rejection rate and rework rate grouped by the version in force, which needs many items to move. The shape an answer would take is already in this document and is not a [rollout](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/02-the-rollout-strategy.md): [_How it learns_](how-the-factory-works/04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md)'s held-out sample is a randomised selection the [score](how-the-factory-works/04-risk-score/README.md) makes and records on the [decision](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md), so it could select which items are authored under a new version the same way. That is allowed where a rollout is not, [dispatch](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) still matching rather than judging. What stops that being written down as the answer is arithmetic, not architecture: the volume a comparison needs scales as the inverse square of what it must detect, and a factory producing a few items a day, split two ways, rules out only enormous regressions. What turns on it is whether the factory can ever improve a role prompt on evidence or only ever on a human's judgment at the gate — and, if only on judgment, whether authoring role prompts is worth the mechanism it costs. The owner decides.

## What makes a stopped conformance or advisory check visible?

The [last check](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) record makes a component that stopped running a named row on [the home view](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/02-three-properties-every-screen-needs.md), and four components write one: the [health monitor](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md), the [deployer](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md), the [drift detector](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/08-drift-detection.md), and the [notifier](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/07-pages.md). Two more passes run and write none: the conformance raise reads [the constraints in force](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) on a pass of its own, and the [advisory](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/03-advisories.md) raise reads its feed on another. Either stopping is invisible by the design's own account — _Advisories_ says that without the raise "the numbers are clean, no window crosses, no gate fires", which is equally true of a raise that stopped. What blocks extending the record to them is that nothing names the component either pass belongs to: a [detector](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) is a role and not a component, so there is nobody to write one — the same gap [_What writes a fleet proposal?_](open.md#what-writes-a-fleet-proposal) has. Naming one raises the question that gap always raises. [_A fleet proposal_](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/07-a-fleet-proposal.md) refuses a detector as its writer because "a component that also wrote a record of another kind would be a second writer with no [seam](deferred.md) between the two things it wrote", and the health monitor is a detector writing more than one kind already — the [analysis window](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md), the [incident](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/06-incidents.md), and a last check per service — so either that rule holds and the two raises need a writer of their own, or it does not and the health monitor is why. What turns on it is which: a raise that stopped is found by a named component's last check, or by an owner noticing that nothing has arrived. The owner decides, and the second is what the design says today by saying nothing.

## May the factory write a fleet entry itself?

[_A fleet proposal_](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/07-a-fleet-proposal.md) has the factory arguing for a [fleet](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/README.md) — the entries an agent's model, effort, role and scope are written onto — and an owner writing it; the arrangement was chosen to be staged rather than final: the later step is letting the factory write the entry, one change to one writer and not a second mechanism. What has to be true first is what this question is about. The refusal it would lift is that writing a model and an [effort](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/01-what-an-agent-runs-on.md) onto an entry chooses how long somebody's lent account runs, an authority [_A spend ceiling_](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/08-a-spend-ceiling.md) keeps with the owner — so the question is not whether the factory can compose a fleet well, but whether a ceiling per credential, bounding what every entry on that credential may spend, is bound enough for the factory to write an entry inside it. What turns on it is whether [_What humans do_](what-humans-do.md) keeps composing the fleet as substrate at all, or whether the owner's part shrinks to lending a credential.

## What writes a fleet proposal?

[_A fleet proposal_](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/07-a-fleet-proposal.md) argued from evidence is a record the factory writes, with free-form argument as its evidence, and nothing names its writer: no [role](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) covers composing one, so no [fleet entry](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/README.md) gives the work a model and no [role prompt](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md) decided at a gate gives it words. The proposal a version's first start writes has a writer already, [the install's first-start step](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/07-a-fleet-proposal.md), so this question is about the argued kind alone. Model work with no role is the arrangement [_Reports_](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) refuses for [the grouper](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md), the other model work done on neither an [intent](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) nor an item: it refuses by making the grouper a role of its own. What turns on it is what the argument an owner reads is worth: the proposal argues the fleet should change, and the words that compose it are versioned by nothing and reachable by no [safeguard](how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) (9). The owner decides whether the proposal's writer takes the grouper's treatment or stays outside the fleet with the reason written down.

## What writes a redaction?

A [redaction](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) is the one mechanism in the factory that destroys information a decision was made on, and [_Records and their writers_](records.md) has no writer to put beside it. Its three targets have three writers: a report belongs to [the report store](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md), the statement holding what was asked for is a field of the [intent](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) and so belongs to [intake](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md), and an [artifact version](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) belongs to the [artifact store](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md). One erasure is one action across all three, so there are two candidates. The first is a writer of its own that three components call, the arrangement intake and the artifact store both take. The second is each target's own writer writing the redactions that name what it wrote, which is three writers on one record with no [seam](deferred.md) between them. What turns on it is whether the erasure a law compels is one write the factory can show an auditor it performed, or three writes that can each fail on their own. A writer of its own raises a second question: the three records are ones it may not otherwise write, so what destroys the bytes inside them? The owner decides.

## What survives a lost disk or a factory upgrade?

A lost disk is answered by the [recovery unit](what-the-factory-does/04-what-the-factory-does-not-build.md), the [backup retention](how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/03-what-is-not-in-it.md), and the [erasure list](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) a restore replays against. Upgrading the factory is already the owner's, part of [hosting it](what-humans-do.md), and whether a record written by one version of the factory is readable by the next is answered in [_Tight integration_](what-the-factory-does/01-tight-integration.md) and in [_Components and what they call_](components.md).

What none of that reaches is a build. [_Rollback_](how-the-factory-works/06-releases/06-rollback.md) reaches its target the slow way, by redeploying that release's [build](how-the-factory-works/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md), and nothing here says what keeps one reachable or for how long. Whatever answers this question decides whether a build the factory made is retained as a matter of correctness or discarded as a matter of cost, and a service whose oldest reachable build is newer than its [last known-good release](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) has a rollback target it cannot actually reach. The owner decides.

## In what unit is the item-size target authored?

The [item-size target](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) says how large an item is meant to be above the minimum that it ships by itself, and [_One shape across all of them_](how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) lists it beside every other parameter an owner authors (8) or safeguards (9) — in nothing. The [score](how-the-factory-works/04-risk-score/README.md) needs no unit, because nothing makes it write a value down for a human: it learns from what a bad size does in each direction, [attempt-limit](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) escalations (12) on one side and cost per feature and rework rate on the other, all three already reported at [_Factory_](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md). An owner authoring one has to write something, and the candidates are not the same quantity: files an item changes, [acceptance criteria](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/01-the-record.md) a spec version introduces, or what the [stages](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) before it spent, and none is necessarily what the score is learning. What turns on it is whether this is a parameter an owner authors at all: every other one takes its unit from the mechanism it binds, and a target with none is a learned value an owner can bound rather than one an owner sets. The owner decides.

## Does the explicit health threshold link to what motivated it?

A [quality criterion](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/03-the-six-patterns.md) is decided under load that its own [encoding](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/README.md) generates, so what it establishes is behaviour on [the candidate environment](how-the-factory-works/05-environments/04-what-the-candidate-environment-decides/README.md)'s hardware. What reads production afterwards, the [health monitor](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md)'s comparison and the [explicit threshold](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) a [safeguard](how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) (9) sets, links to no [intent](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) and no criterion: the comparison detects a regression from what production already does, and the threshold is a field of the service with nothing saying which request it was authored to answer. So an intent asking for a slow screen to be fast can pass its criterion, ship, and be no faster at production's scale, with nothing closing the loop back to the intent that asked. What turns on it is whether an explicit threshold may name the criterion or intent it was authored for: a cross-record link with a lifecycle of its own, a threshold outliving the item and a withdrawn criterion leaving the link pointing at history. Or whether validation at production scale is post-production by design instead, reaching the factory as a new intent the way a [consumer](how-the-factory-works/07-contracts/01-two-versioned-things.md)'s break already does. The owner decides.

## Does a mismatch suspend the automatic rollback?

A mismatch [_Drift detection_](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) found holds that service's production deploys, for the reason that every remedy the factory has reads the record just shown wrong — a [rollback](how-the-factory-works/06-releases/06-rollback.md) targets a release computed from the windows the factory itself closed. A rollback is a deploy event, and nothing says whether the hold at [_Deploy to production_](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/08-deploy-to-production.md) stops the one the [health monitor](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) performs on its own when a window [fails](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) while a mismatch is outstanding. What turns on it is which of two bad states production is left in: suspended, a release that just failed its window keeps serving until a human clears the mismatch, the [page](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/07-pages.md) condition already met; not suspended, the factory shifts traffic onto a target computed from records it has just been told are wrong, the reason the deploy hold gives word for word. The owner decides.
