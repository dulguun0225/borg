# Open

A question belongs here when an [owner](what-humans-do.md) is who decides it and the design cannot answer it by applying a pattern it already holds. It is phrased as the question and what turns on it, and it is deleted from here when it is folded into the file that owns its subject.

## How does the factory learn whether a role prompt version is better?

The factory authors [what an agent is told](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told) — a **role prompt** per [role](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) and a **skill** per subject, each versioned — and has no way to find out whether a version improved anything. The [per-author prior](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) is per model and deliberately cannot see a role prompt; there is no [analysis window](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-analysis-window) over a version and no [control](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) to compare one against, a control being instances taking organic traffic and a role prompt serving none. What is left is [_Factory_](how-humans-do-it/11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people)'s gate rejection rate and rework rate grouped by the version in force, which needs many items to move. The shape an answer would take is already in this document and is not a [rollout](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy): [_How it learns_](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md#how-it-learns)'s held-out sample is a randomised selection the [score](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md) makes and records on the [decision](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it), so it could select which items are authored under a new version the same way — legal where a rollout is not, [dispatch](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#dispatch) still matching rather than judging. What stops that being written down as the answer is arithmetic and not architecture: the volume a comparison needs scales as the inverse square of what it must detect, and a factory producing a few items a day, split two ways, rules out only enormous regressions. What turns on it is whether the factory can ever improve a role prompt on evidence or only ever on a human's judgment at the gate — and, if only ever on judgment, whether authoring role prompts is worth the mechanism it costs. The owner decides.

## May the factory write a fleet entry itself?

[_A fleet proposal_](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#a-fleet-proposal) has the factory arguing for a [fleet](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md) — the entries an agent's model, effort, role and scope are written onto — and an owner writing it, and the arrangement was chosen to be staged rather than final: the later step is letting the factory write the entry, which is one change to one writer and not a second mechanism. What has to be true first is what this question is about. The refusal it would lift is that writing a model and an [effort](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-runs-on) onto an entry chooses how long somebody's lent account runs, which is the authority [_A spend ceiling_](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#a-spend-ceiling) keeps with the owner — so the question is not whether the factory can compose a fleet well, but whether a ceiling per credential, bounding what every entry on that credential may spend, is bound enough for the factory to write an entry inside it. What turns on it is whether [_What humans do_](what-humans-do.md) keeps composing the fleet as substrate at all, or whether the owner's part of it shrinks to lending a credential.

## What writes a fleet proposal?

[_A fleet proposal_](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#a-fleet-proposal) is a record the factory writes, with free-form argument as its evidence, and nothing names its writer: no [role](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) covers composing one, so no [fleet entry](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md) gives the work a model and no [role prompt](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told) a gate decided gives it words. That is the arrangement [_Reports_](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#reports) refuses for [the grouper](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#reports) — the other model work done on neither an [intent](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#intake) nor an item — by making it a role of its own. What turns on it is what the argument an owner reads is worth: the proposal is the record that argues the fleet should change, and the words that compose it are versioned by nothing and reachable by no [safeguard](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them) (9). The owner decides whether the proposal's writer takes the grouper's treatment or stays outside the fleet with the reason written down.

## What survives a lost disk or a factory upgrade?

The design keeps every record for the life of the install and lets an owner author [retention](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md#what-is-not-in-it) on two of them, and that is all it says about the store's life: nothing about backing [the graph](what-the-factory-does.md#tight-integration) up, restoring it, or a record written by one version of a self-hosted product being readable by the next — and upgrading the factory is already the owner's, as part of [hosting it](what-humans-do.md). A lost disk today loses the records, the log the [risk score](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md) learns from, and the audit trail, and the design does not say so. It is not only the records. [_Rollback_](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#rollback) reaches its target the slow way by redeploying that release's [build](how-humans-do-it/05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch), and nothing in this document says what keeps one reachable or for how long — so whatever answers this question decides whether a build the factory made is retained as a matter of correctness or discarded as a matter of cost, and a service whose oldest reachable build is newer than its [last known-good release](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#overlapping-windows) has a rollback target it cannot actually reach. What turns on it is whether this is a third subject [_Deferred, but not designed out_](deferred.md) sequences away — stated with what the wait costs, beside security — or work the design answers before anything rests on the log as evidence; the owner decides which.

## In what unit is the item-size target authored?

The [item-size target](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#decomposition) says how large an item is meant to be above the minimum that it ships by itself, and [_One shape across all of them_](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them) lists it beside every other parameter an owner authors (8) or safeguards (9) — in nothing. The [score](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md) needs no unit, because nothing makes it write a value down for a human: it learns from what a bad size does in each direction, [attempt-limit](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#the-attempt-limit) escalations (12) on one side and cost per feature and rework rate on the other, all three already reported at [_Factory_](how-humans-do-it/11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people). An owner authoring one has to write something, and the candidates are not the same quantity — files an item changes, [acceptance criteria](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#spec) a spec version introduces, or what the [stages](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) before it spent — nor is any of them necessarily what the score is learning. What turns on it is whether this is a parameter an owner authors at all: every other one takes its unit from the mechanism it binds, and a target with none is a learned value an owner can bound rather than a value an owner sets. The owner decides.

## Does the explicit health threshold link to what motivated it?

A [quality criterion](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#spec) decided under load its own [encoding](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#implementation) generates establishes behaviour on [the candidate environment](how-humans-do-it/05-environments.md#what-the-candidate-environment-decides)'s hardware, and what reads production afterwards — the [health monitor](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-monitor)'s comparison and the [explicit threshold](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) a [safeguard](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them) (9) sets — links to no [intent](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#intake) and no criterion: the comparison detects a regression from what production already does, and the threshold is a field of the service with nothing saying which request it was authored to answer. So an intent asking for a slow screen to be fast can pass its criterion, ship, and be no faster at production's scale, with nothing closing the loop back to the intent that asked. What turns on it is whether an explicit threshold may name the criterion or intent it was authored for — a cross-record link with a lifecycle of its own, a threshold outliving the item and a withdrawn criterion leaving the link pointing at history — or whether validation at production scale is post-production by design, reaching the factory as a new intent the way a [consumer](how-humans-do-it/07-contracts.md#two-versioned-things)'s break already does. The owner decides.

## Does a mismatch suspend the automatic rollback?

A mismatch [_Drift detection_](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#drift-detection) found holds that service's production deploys, and the reason given is that every remedy the factory has reads the record just shown wrong — a [rollback](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#rollback) targets a release computed from the windows the factory itself closed. A rollback is a deploy event, and nothing says whether the hold at [_Deploy to production_](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#deploy-to-production) stops the one the [health monitor](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) performs on its own when a window [fails](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-analysis-window) while a mismatch is outstanding. What turns on it is which of two bad states production is left in: suspended, a release that just failed its window keeps serving until a human clears the mismatch, which is the [page](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#pages) condition already met; not suspended, the factory shifts traffic onto a target computed from records it has just been told are wrong, which is the reason the deploy hold gives word for word. The owner decides.
