# Open

A question belongs here when an [owner](what-humans-do.md) is who decides it and [the tree](glossary.md) cannot answer it by applying a pattern it already holds. It is phrased as the question and what turns on it, and it is deleted from here when it is folded into the file that owns its subject.

## How does the factory learn whether a brief version is better?

The factory authors [what an agent is told](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told) — a **brief** per [role](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) and a **skill** per subject, each versioned — and has no way to find out whether a version improved anything. The [authorship prior](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) is per model and deliberately cannot see a brief; there is no [watch window](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-watch-window) over a version and no [control](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-signal) to compare one against, a control being an instance set taking organic traffic and a brief serving none. What is left is [_Factory_](how-humans-do-it/11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people)'s gate rejection rate and rework rate grouped by the version in force, which needs many items to move. The shape an answer would take is already in the tree and is not a [rollout](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy): [_How it learns_](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md#how-it-learns)'s held-out sample is a randomised selection the [score](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md) makes and records on the [decision](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it), so it could select which items are authored under a new version the same way — legal where a rollout is not, [dispatch](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#dispatch) still matching rather than judging. What stops that being written down as the answer is arithmetic and not architecture: the volume a comparison needs scales as the inverse square of what it must detect, and a factory producing a few items a day, split two ways, rules out only enormous regressions. What turns on it is whether the factory can ever improve a brief on evidence or only ever on a human's judgment at the gate — and, if only ever on judgment, whether authoring briefs is worth the mechanism it costs. The owner decides.

## May the factory write a fleet entry itself?

[_A fleet proposal_](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#a-fleet-proposal) has the factory arguing for a [fleet](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md) — the entries an agent's model, effort, role and scope are written onto — and an owner writing it, and the arrangement was chosen to be staged rather than final: the later step is letting the factory write the entry, which is one change to one writer and not a second mechanism. What has to be true first is what this question is about. The refusal it would lift is that writing a model and an [effort](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-runs-on) onto an entry chooses how long somebody's lent account runs, which is the authority a [spend ceiling](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#what-the-fleet-is-not) was already refused for — so the question is not whether the factory can compose a fleet well, but whether anything bounds what it spends doing so, and today nothing does. What turns on it is whether [_What humans do_](what-humans-do.md) keeps composing the fleet as substrate at all, or whether the owner's part of it shrinks to lending a credential.

## What survives a lost disk or a factory upgrade?

The tree keeps every record for the life of the install and lets an owner author [retention](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md#what-is-not-in-it) on two of them, and that is all it says about the store's life: nothing about backing [the graph](what-the-factory-does.md#tight-integration) up, restoring it, or a record written by one version of a self-hosted product being readable by the next — and upgrading the factory is already the owner's, as part of [hosting it](what-humans-do.md). A lost disk today loses the records, the log the [risk score](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md) learns from, and the audit trail, and the design does not say so. It is not only the records. [_Rollback_](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#rollback) reaches its target the slow way by redeploying that release's [build](how-humans-do-it/05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch), and nothing in the tree says what keeps one reachable or for how long — so whatever answers this question decides whether a build the factory made is retained as a matter of correctness or discarded as a matter of cost, and a service whose oldest reachable build is newer than its [restore floor](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#overlapping-windows) has a rollback target it cannot actually reach. What turns on it is whether this is a third subject [_Deferred, but not designed out_](deferred.md) sequences away — stated with what the wait costs, beside security and adoption — or work the design answers before anything rests on the log as evidence; the owner decides which.

## In what unit is the item-size target authored?

The [item-size target](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#the-cut) says how large an item is meant to be above the minimum that it ships by itself, and [_One shape across all of them_](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them) lists it beside every other parameter an owner authors (8) or pins (9) — in nothing. The [score](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md) needs no unit, because nothing makes it write a value down for a human: it learns from what a bad size does in each direction, [attempt-bound](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#the-attempt-bound) escalations (12) on one side and cost per feature and rework rate on the other, all three already reported at [_Factory_](how-humans-do-it/11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people). An owner authoring one has to write something, and the candidates are not the same quantity — files an item changes, [acceptance criteria](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#spec) a spec version introduces, or what the [stages](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) before it spent — nor is any of them necessarily what the score is learning. What turns on it is whether this is a parameter an owner authors at all: every other one takes its unit from the mechanism it binds, and a target with none is a learned value an owner can bound rather than a value an owner sets. The owner decides.

## Do the records a decision is read against need the log's integrity?

[Traceability](what-the-factory-does.md#traceability) rests on a decision naming what was in force when it was taken: each [decision](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it) record names the [policy version](what-the-factory-does.md#traceability) and the [score version](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least) it was decided under, because a decision read back against today's values is not the decision that was made. Naming is by identifier, so the claim holds only as far as those two records can be trusted to say what they said. The log itself is defended by each of its records [chaining its predecessor](deferred.md); those two are append-only because nothing in the factory has a statement that edits one, which is a property of the code and not of the store. So a row rewritten in either changes what every decision naming it is read against, while the log verifies clean: the decision's own record is untouched and only its meaning has moved.

What makes this worth asking now is that it used to be partly self-defending and no longer is. The values the [score](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md) supplies were fixed until it [learned them](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md#how-it-learns), so a rewritten row could be checked against what the factory would supply on its own; a learned value exists nowhere but the record that carries it, and there is nothing left to check it against. What turns on it is whether traceability as a claim made to an auditor may rest on records outside the chain at all — and if it may not, whether the answer is to chain those two the way the log is chained, or to say plainly that the claim's reach ends at the log and what that leaves an auditor. It is not forced by construction, which is the one thing that would settle it by itself: a chain may start partway through a history the way the log's own [retention](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md#what-is-not-in-it) restarts one at a checkpoint, so unlike the four seams [_Deferred, but not designed out_](deferred.md) sequences, this one can be added to records written without it. The owner decides.
