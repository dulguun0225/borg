# Tight integration

**Tight integration is the first of the two properties the product is built around.** One system, not a bundle of tools with connectors between them. Intent, item, [spec](../how-the-factory-works/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md), [build](../how-the-factory-works/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md), [gate decision](../how-the-factory-works/03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md), deploy, [incident](../how-the-factory-works/08-operations/06-incidents.md), and score are records in one graph, linked to each other.

Because they are linked, a question a connector answers by matching records across systems is answered here by following links. Which services consume a [contract](../how-the-factory-works/07-contracts/01-two-versioned-things.md), so [what a change breaks](../how-the-factory-works/07-contracts/04-enforcement.md) is a query rather than an estimate. Which items came from one request, so [work that spans services](../how-the-factory-works/07-contracts/10-work-that-spans-services.md) needs no record type of its own. Which release each service is running, so a [candidate](../how-the-factory-works/06-releases/03-what-a-build-is-called-and-when.md)'s environment is composed from the [current releases](../how-the-factory-works/06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md) of its dependencies.

A bundle of tools can report all of that. What one graph does is act on it, with no human copying a fact from one system to another: what a consumer assumes is [derived from its build](../how-the-factory-works/07-contracts/06-what-a-consumer-declares.md) rather than entered by hand, [the health monitor](../how-the-factory-works/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) finding a problem [after its window closed](../how-the-factory-works/08-operations/04-after-the-analysis-window.md) writes an intent at the start of [the pipeline](../how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md), and the score learns from the same log that serves as [the audit trail](../deferred.md).

**One record has one writer, and a boundary between two is declared.** Every record in the graph has one **writer**, the single component that writes it, and no second component writes it. Where two genuinely have to reach one record, whether an owner and a component or two components, the writing is split between them, and that split is a [seam](../deferred.md) stated in the section that owns the record. A split is on a field of the record, on the event that writes it, or on the kind the record was created as. The rule keeps a fact single-valued: a record two components wrote can disagree with itself, and every check downstream reads whichever half it happened to read. What it costs is a record where a field would have done — a subject two writers reach becomes a record of its own with a query behind it, a cost [_A safeguard is a record_](../how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) pays and states. Sections below invoke this rule rather than re-deriving it, and [_Records and their writers_](../records.md) inventories the records they invoke it over. It holds a row per record, naming that record's writer and the seam where two writers reach one. A second writer given to a record already listed there contradicts the list rather than a sentence in a file nobody opened. Each row points at the section that owns the subject and holds no reason of its own, so nothing is decided there. [_Components and what they call_](../components.md) is the same list from the other side: a row per component, what it may call, and the rule that an edge not in a row does not exist.

**Every record in the graph carries a format version, not only the log's.** [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../deferred.md) states the reason for the log's: a reader must not infer a record's shape from which fields are present. The same reader reads every other record, so the same field is on every one of them. A record that a later version updates moves its version only when the whole record is rewritten to the new shape, and that rewrite is part of the migration that performs it.

**An event that writes more than one record has a durable step order, and every step after the first is keyed so that writing it again writes nothing.** A component stopping partway through such an event otherwise leaves records each well-formed and jointly false, which every check downstream reads as true. [_The merge queue_](../how-the-factory-works/05-environments/03-the-merge-queue.md) states the construction for the fast-forward and the release record, and it is the construction every such event takes. The order is chosen so that a stop leaves the event visibly owing rather than visibly done, which makes half-done and finished different states rather than the same one. Three orders follow from it: [the analysis window](../how-the-factory-works/08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md)'s close is the last step of every exit, an item's [stage](../how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/02-what-an-item-names.md) is the last step of the transition that moves it, and an [authored parameter](../how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md)'s field is written after the policy version that records it. What says the event is unfinished is the last step's absence, or its disagreement with a step taken before it. [_Components and what they call_](../components.md) holds the other half, every component's restart being a read of its own records, and that last-written step is what such a read looks for. What it costs is a key per step and an event a reader can catch half-done, where before there was one write and a reader who saw it or did not.

The downside is that one graph is one trust domain. Almost every check the factory makes reads a record the factory wrote, so a wrong record is read as true by every check downstream of it. [_One pipeline_](../how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) accepts the same exposure for the path, a defect in it reaching every item that goes through, and one graph is that exposure over the records. The one exception is [_Drift detection_](../how-the-factory-works/08-operations/08-drift-detection.md), which compares two of those records against what the factory cannot rewrite: the production [deploy record](../how-the-factory-works/06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md) against what is actually running, and [the log](../deferred.md)'s chain against the head the detector recorded on its last pass, each record's chain field hashing its own payload together with the same field on the record before it.
