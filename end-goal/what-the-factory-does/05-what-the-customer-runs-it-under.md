# What the customer runs it under

The factory is a product a customer installs and runs, so the terms it runs under are stated here rather than left to a milestone. **The licence** is one file, `LICENSE` at the root of the repository the product is built in, saying under what terms a customer may run, change, and redistribute it. The design states none of those terms — a licence is decided in that file and nowhere else — and does not restate what the licence allocates: warranty and liability between the customer and whoever builds the product — **the builder** from here on — are the licence's terms too.

What the design owes that allocation is the fact a dispute over shipped software turns on: whose decision each value the factory decided under was. Every such value was either authored by the customer (8) or supplied, and the [policy version](../how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) and [score version](../how-the-factory-works/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) every decision names say which. The authored one carries who wrote it, the supplied one is the score's, and both are chained rows of the [log](../deferred.md), so neither can be rewritten after the fact to move a value from one side to the other. The same division runs through the code itself. The model that wrote a change, the [effort](../how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/01-what-an-agent-runs-on.md) it ran at, and the credential it ran through are the customer's, written onto a [fleet entry](../how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/README.md) by the customer and never by the factory. [What the agent was told](../how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md) is the builder's in its first version and the factory's in every version after, each decided at [a gate of its own](../how-the-factory-works/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/09-a-role-prompt-or-a-skill.md). That claim rests on the shipped-bundle identity [_The product's release channel_](../deferred.md#the-products-release-channel) names: which version shipped an agent's words is on the record, not asserted after the fact. The decision that shipped the change names its [actor](../deferred.md), a human or the score, and beside it whether that name was claimed or verified. So each fact a liability question asks for is on a record with one writer, read from the log rather than argued: who chose the author, who wrote its instructions, who approved the change and under whose value, and whether that approval was of the approver's own work. A fact resting on a claimed name reads as one.

What the design does not decide is what a jurisdiction makes of that division — whether the builder of a product that ships software is the producer of the software it ships is a question of law, answered against the licence and per jurisdiction, by counsel rather than by a design document. What it costs is that the records answer whose decision a value was and never whether that decision was reasonable: a threshold the customer never authored is the score's on every row, and a customer who left every value supplied has a log that says so.

The produced software's licence is the customer's own decision, authored as the [product licence](../how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) on each service record, and what the design owes a rights question over it is the same kind of fact the builder's liability gets. Which licence each dependency shipped under is on the [notice file](../how-the-factory-works/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) a release names, and who wrote each line that shipped is on that release's [authorship rollup](../how-the-factory-works/06-releases/02-the-release-record.md). Whether that combination may lawfully be distributed is again a question of law, answered against the licence and per jurisdiction, by counsel rather than by this document.
