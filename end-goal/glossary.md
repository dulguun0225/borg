# Glossary

Every term the document uses as though already defined, with one line saying what it is and a link to the section that defines it. It is an index and not a second place a decision is written: the line here says what the term names, the section says what follows from it, and where the two could differ the section is right. That is what keeps it clear of `CLAUDE.md`'s rule against a list of answers kept outside the sections that own them — a resolved question is folded into its section and never restated here.

Read it when a sentence uses a name you cannot place. Nothing depends on reading it first.

- **acceptance criterion** — a record of the service stating one testable behaviour in one of six sentence patterns, introduced and withdrawn by spec versions and decided against a run by the encoding written beside the code. [Spec](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#spec)
- **actor** — the human or component recorded as having done a thing, on every record that records one; seam 1. [Deferred, but not designed out](deferred.md)
- **agent** — a worker the factory runs: a model in a role, with a scope, working from a brief. [One pipeline](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md)
- **area** — a record an owner declares (9): a grouping of the software, coarser than a file and free to cut across services; areas form a chain rooted at a project. [What humans do](what-humans-do.md)
- **artifact** — what a stage authors about one item and a gate decides over: the spec, the implementation plan, the tasks, and the implementation, with the criterion encoding authored beside that last one and decided at the same gate. [One pipeline](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md)
- **artifact store** — the one component every artifact version is written through, with three callers: the stage's agent, a human backstopping the stage (11, 12), and the gate component where a human takes Edit in place. [One pipeline](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md)
- **attempt bound** — how many times a stage is retried, how many rounds the interview asks, and how many times a rejected cut is re-cut, before the item or the intent stops being retried and escalates (12). [The attempt bound](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#the-attempt-bound)
- **authorship prior** — the score factor per model version, computed from that author's own outcomes; it starts wide and narrows with evidence. [Factors, at least](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least)
- **auto-passed by** — the decision's field saying whether this firing's auto-pass came from the number being under the threshold or from the held-out selection. [How it learns](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md#how-it-learns)
- **baseline** — what the comparison is made against. The factory starts a control to be one rather than pointing at what is already running. [The health signal](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-signal)
- **boundary** — the threshold the comparison is read against, valid at every point it is read, computed from the window's size and confidence. [The watch window](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-watch-window)
- **brief** — what an agent in a role works from, one per role, versioned and written by the factory rather than by an owner. [What an agent is told](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told)
- **build** — a record, one per commit built, written by the deploy agent when it is called to build. [Records, and one long-lived branch](how-humans-do-it/05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch)
- **candidate** — an item plus its build: identity enough to deploy, test, and reject, from build until merge to master. It is not a record. [What a build is called, and when](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#what-a-build-is-called-and-when)
- **cap** — the elapsed time that ends a watch window which will never reach its volume, and the name of that exit. [The watch window](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-watch-window)
- **clean** — the watch window exit where the comparison ruled out a regression of the size worth catching, closing the window early on evidence. [The watch window](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-watch-window)
- **comparison** — the component that reads production behaviour, computes the health signal, opens and closes every watch window, writes incidents, and calls intake with what it finds. [The health signal](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-signal)
- **constraint** — a record an owner supplies (2) naming what it binds; it is never cut into items and never ships. [Constraints and the design system](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#constraints-and-the-design-system)
- **contract** — a record: one published interface or store of one service, with its kind as the field everything else follows from. [Two versioned things](how-humans-do-it/07-contracts.md#two-versioned-things)
- **contract version** — semver on one published interface: a compatibility promise, moving when the form that interface publishes moves. [What a build is called, and when](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#what-a-build-is-called-and-when)
- **control** — an instance set running the build already in production, started alongside the release and taking comparable traffic, so the comparison measures the change and not the age. [The health signal](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-signal)
- **crossing** — the comparison passing the boundary. Inside a watch window it rolls the release back with no human involved; outside one it raises an item; the comparison writes an incident either way. [The watch window](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-watch-window)
- **current release** — the release a service's production deploy record names: what is running, not what is newest. [The deploy record](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#the-deploy-record)
- **the cut** — the stage that turns one intent into one item or several, at least one per service the work changes, and records the order between them. [The cut](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#the-cut)
- **decision** — what a gate firing writes into the append-only log: an opening row carrying the factor vector, and a closing row naming it and carrying the verdict. [Where a gate is, and what decides it](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it)
- **declaration** — a predicate about a producer's interface, derived from a consumer's build rather than typed, and checked against the producer's candidate. [What a consumer declares](how-humans-do-it/07-contracts.md#what-a-consumer-declares)
- **deploy agent** — the component that builds, creates and tears down candidate environments, performs every deploy, executes the rollout, and writes the deploy record. [The health signal](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-signal)
- **deploy record** — what says which release of which service runs on which environment, written when the deploy starts and advanced to complete or rolled back. [The deploy record](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#the-deploy-record)
- **design system** — the reusable decisions every screen is built from; a standing constraint of one project, supplied by an owner or picked from the ones the factory ships with. [Constraints and the design system](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#constraints-and-the-design-system)
- **detector** — anything of the factory's own that raises an intent: a comparison still running after its window closed, a consumer breaking in its own error rate. It raises one per condition rather than per observation, later evidence attaching to the intent already raised. [Intake](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#intake)
- **dispatch** — the component that matches an item's stage against a role and its service and area against a scope, and writes the item's stage, its attempt counts, and what each stage spent. [Dispatch](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#dispatch)
- **dropped** — the item stage value saying it stopped for good without a release. Merged or dropped is what frees an item's branch and its environment. [What an item names](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#what-an-item-names)
- **duty** — one of the twelve numbered obligations an owner holds or delegates. A bare `(7)` or `(11, 12)` anywhere is a duty number. [What humans do](what-humans-do.md)
- **Edit in place** — the gate action where a human edits the artifact under decision instead of rejecting the item to get a different one. Offered at Decomposition and at every artifact gate but Implementation. [Actions at each gate](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#actions-at-each-gate)
- **environment** — a record with a kind fixed at creation: production, a deploy target a customer defines, or a candidate's own. [Records, and one long-lived branch](how-humans-do-it/05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch)
- **effort** — how long a model works before it answers, a field of the fleet entry beside the model and outside the authorship prior's key. [What an agent runs on](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-runs-on)
- **escalation** — the factory saying it cannot finish an item, written when the attempt bound is exceeded and shown in Work (12). [The attempt bound](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#the-attempt-bound)
- **factory policy** — the record for the two parameters no customer record's scope reaches — the attempt bound and the predicate catalog — plus the two retention settings. [One shape across all of them](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them)
- **the fast-forward** — the merge queue putting a verified candidate onto master. It is the event that mints the number and writes the release record. [The merge queue](how-humans-do-it/05-environments.md#the-merge-queue)
- **fleet** — the set of agents the factory runs, composed by an owner as substrate rather than as one of the twelve duties. [The fleet](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md)
- **fleet proposal** — a record the factory writes arguing for a set of entries to write and retire, with a disposition an owner writes and no credential named. [A fleet proposal](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#a-fleet-proposal)
- **form** — a constraint's field deciding which gate can reject on it: a build, a predicate, or a document. [Constraints and the design system](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#constraints-and-the-design-system)
- **gate** — a decision point at every stage boundary and before every merge and every deploy, where the score decides whether a human decides. [Where a gate is, and what decides it](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it)
- **gate component** — the component that fires every gate, opens and closes the decision, and calls the artifact store where a human takes Edit in place. [One pipeline](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md)
- **gate policy** — the seven parameters an owner authors (8), gathered in one place from the sections that define each. [Gate policy](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md)
- **the graph** — the one linked set of records the product is built around: intent, item, spec, build, gate decision, deploy, incident, and score, joined by links rather than matched across systems. [Tight integration](what-the-factory-does.md#tight-integration)
- **harm** — the watch window exit where the comparison crossed the boundary against the release; it is rolled back with no human involved. [The watch window](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-watch-window)
- **health signal** — the comparison of a release against its control, which is what the factory reads to decide whether a rollout is failing. [The health signal](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-signal)
- **held out** — the score's record that it selected this item for the random sample, auto-passing every gate it would have gated. [How it learns](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md#how-it-learns)
- **hold** — a stop that leaves the event queued with the change still good, offered at the deploy rows alone. Three kinds, and only a human's is written as a decision. [What a gate may change](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#what-a-gate-may-change)
- **incident** — a record on a production environment, written by the comparison at a crossing against the release running there. [Incidents](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#incidents)
- **instance set** — the running processes serving one build on a target. A control is one, and the control a rollback shifts traffic onto becomes what serves the release returned to. [The health signal](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-signal)
- **intake** — the single writer of every intent, and of the questions and answers attached to one, called by all three sources. [Intake](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#intake)
- **intent** — what arrives and what everything links back to: one record from three sources — an owner's request, a group of end-user reports, or something the factory found. [Intake](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#intake)
- **the interview** — the state an intent is in while the factory asks (3) until it has enough to cut and author a spec per item. Not a stage, and it has no gate. [The interview](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#the-interview)
- **item** — the unit the factory moves: one thing that can ship by itself, one thread, one release. [One pipeline](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md)
- **K** — how many watch windows one service may hold open at once, and so how many releases one rollback undoes. [Overlapping windows](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#overlapping-windows)
- **kind** — an environment's fixed value, and the seam between its writers; also a contract's, deciding which promise it makes. [Records, and one long-lived branch](how-humans-do-it/05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch)
- **last comparison** — the reconciler's record per production target, overwritten each pass, so a reconciler that stopped is visible rather than silent. [The reconciler](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-reconciler)
- **master** — a service's one long-lived branch and its promotion path; its only inbound path is the merge queue. [Records, and one long-lived branch](how-humans-do-it/05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch)
- **merge gate** — Merge to master: the release event, where a candidate becomes a numbered release and where UAT (7) is performed. [Merge to master](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#merge-to-master)
- **merge queue** — the component that re-verifies each candidate against the master it will actually merge into, performs the fast-forward, writes the release record, and mints the number. [The merge queue](how-humans-do-it/05-environments.md#the-merge-queue)
- **mismatch** — what the reconciler writes when a production target is not running that service's current release; it holds the service's production deploys and pages. [The reconciler](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-reconciler)
- **notifier** — the one component delivering everything waiting on a human out of the product, on three channels: mail, chat, and the page. [Pages](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#pages)
- **the number** — the per-service ordinal minted at merge to master. It orders builds and names rollback targets, and is never reused. [The number](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#the-number)
- **originating project** — the project a request was raised under, written on the intent; it is where the cut places a service the work creates. [Intake](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#intake)
- **page** — the narrow channel, for a wait where the deployed software is worse until a human ends it. The only channel that widens and the only one that writes a record. [Pages](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#pages)
- **page event** — one append to the log naming the row, the human reached, and which of three it is: reached, widened, answered. [Pages](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#pages)
- **partial intent** — an intent whose items did not all ship, with at least one sibling live. Nothing writes it; it is computed. [A partial intent](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#a-partial-intent)
- **per-intent** — the narrowest constraint reach: one that arrived with a single request and binds every item cut from it. [Constraints and the design system](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#constraints-and-the-design-system)
- **pin** — an owner's addition (9) — a human at a gate, or a bound on a parameter — that can only add protection and never remove it. It is a record. [One shape across all of them](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them)
- **the pipeline** — the one path every item takes: the stages that author it and the gate at each boundary. There is no second path and no bypass. [One pipeline](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md)
- **policy version** — which version of gate policy was in force at a decision, named on that decision beside the score version, because a decision read back against today's values is not the decision that was made. [Traceability](what-the-factory-does.md#traceability)
- **predicate catalog** — the kinds of assertion a consumer's declaration may draw from; one list the factory owns and an owner extends. [What a consumer declares](how-humans-do-it/07-contracts.md#what-a-consumer-declares)
- **project** — a grouping of work. A service is in exactly one, and an item reaches its project through its service. [Work, Ops, Factory, People](how-humans-do-it/11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people)
- **provider account** — what an agent reaches its model through, stored as a reference to a credential rather than the credential itself. Not a record. [What an agent runs on](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-runs-on)
- **question** — a record, one per interview question asked, written by intake and attached to the intent, with the answer written onto it when it arrives. [The interview](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#the-interview)
- **the reconciler** — one process outside the pipeline, installed by the owner beside the factory, that reads what is actually running on each production target and compares it against the record. It can stop things and is evidence about nothing. [The reconciler](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-reconciler)
- **release** — the name a build has on master, from merge onward, and the record where the graph joins. [The release record](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#the-release-record)
- **report** — an end user's bug report (4) or complaint (5). A report is not an intent: a grouper reads the report store and calls intake once per group. [Reports](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#reports)
- **report store** — the store a report is written into, sized for what a population of end users sends rather than for what a graph joins on. Its writer is the only component that may remove a report, and how long it keeps one is authored. [Reports](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#reports)
- **restore floor** — a service's standing rollback target: the release watched by the newest closed window whose exit is clean or at the cap. [Overlapping windows](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#overlapping-windows)
- **return** — a stage's author ending an attempt with no artifact and naming what owns the defect: an earlier stage, the cut, or the intent. No gate fires, and the attempt is counted against whatever was named. [Going back up](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#going-back-up)
- **revert** — an ordinary item that undoes a release whose control is gone. Nothing on the record says it is one. [Rollback](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#rollback)
- **risk score** — a vector of named factors reduced to one number by a published formula. The number is what a gate compares against a threshold; the vector is what a human argues with. [Risk score](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md)
- **role** — what an agent is put on. A role names the work of one stage, so an owner composing a fleet entry chooses among them and never invents one. [One pipeline](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md)
- **rollback** — a deploy event that shifts traffic onto the control of the window immediately above its target, minting and retiring no number. [Rollback](how-humans-do-it/06-releases.md#rollback)
- **rollback's target** — computed for one rollback: the newest release below the one being rolled back whose window closed clean or at the cap. [Overlapping windows](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#overlapping-windows)
- **rollout strategy** — how a release takes live traffic from the build it replaces: with a control, or straight. It attaches to a production deploy and to no other. [The rollout strategy](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy)
- **scope** — where an agent may be put: a project or an area, drawn on the same lines a pin is. [One pipeline](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md)
- **score version** — the score's own append-only record naming the published formula, the factor set, and every value the score supplies. Every decision names the version in force. [Factors, at least](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score.md#factors-at-least)
- **seam** — one of the four places [_Deferred, but not designed out_](deferred.md) leaves an interface for something not designed yet: the actor, the log, the credential resolver, and reaching a substrate. [Deferred, but not designed out](deferred.md)
- **service** — one repository with one long-lived branch, and no repository holds two. Master, the number, and the queue's ordering are all per service. [The cut](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#the-cut)
- **source** — the field naming what caused a record: on an intent, which of the three raised it; on a rollback, the comparison or the named human. [Intake](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#intake)
- **skill** — a procedure an agent is given where its item matches the skill's subject — an area, a service, or a project — versioned and written by the factory. [What an agent is told](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#what-an-agent-is-told)
- **stage** — one step of the pipeline, authoring one artifact of an item, with a gate at its boundary; the stages are the factory's own and a role names the work of one. [One pipeline](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md)
- **standing** — the three widest constraint reaches together: the factory, a project, an area. A standing constraint is permanent the way the rules (8) are. [What humans do](what-humans-do.md)
- **state flow** — a screen's states, the events that move it between them, and what each state forbids; authored as a state machine at Spec and enforced at Implementation. [Spec](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#spec)
- **store** — a contract whose consumer is the service's own past, which is why it promises forward as well as backward. [The store is a contract too](how-humans-do-it/07-contracts.md#the-store-is-a-contract-too)
- **straight** — the rollout strategy that takes all traffic in place, with none of the replaced build left running, so no control and no fast rollback. [The rollout strategy](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#the-rollout-strategy)
- **surfaces** — the four places a human sees the factory: Work, Ops, Factory, People. [Work, Ops, Factory, People](how-humans-do-it/11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people)
- **swept** — the watch window exit where a rollback aimed below the release undid it: closed with the release neither condemned nor running. [The watch window](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-watch-window)
- **targets** — the addresses an environment is reached at, a field of the environment rather than records of their own. [Records, and one long-lived branch](how-humans-do-it/05-environments.md#records-and-one-long-lived-branch)
- **task** — an internal step of one item, never a unit that ships: no build, no number, no environment of its own. [Tasks](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#tasks)
- **thread** — one item's whole timeline, from intent to the numbered release it ends in, which is how Work shows it. One item is one thread. [Work, Ops, Factory, People](how-humans-do-it/11-surfaces.md#work-ops-factory-people)
- **the tree** — this design document: the Markdown files under `end-goal/` that [README](README.md) indexes. Sections use the word to refer to the document as a whole.
- **trust number** — how much the factory auto-approved and how often that was later vetoed or rolled back. [The trust number](how-humans-do-it/11-surfaces.md#the-trust-number)
- **UAT** — duty 7, performed at the merge gate against the candidate's own run. [What humans do](what-humans-do.md)
- **veto after the fact** — duty 10, in two phases: a rollback while the release's control is still running, a revert once it is gone. [What humans do](what-humans-do.md)
- **watch window** — a record, one per production deploy of a release its service has not watched before, opened and closed by the comparison at exactly one of four exits: harm, clean, cap, swept. [The watch window](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-watch-window)
