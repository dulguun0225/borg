# Two versioned things

A [project](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md)'s software is one or more services, and services reach each other through published interfaces — REST under OpenAPI, gRPC under protobuf, Kafka under topic schemas. Those interfaces have consumers, and the consumers are other services in the same factory, whichever project each is in. Nothing here permits or forbids one service calling another: code calls what it calls, and the factory is not in the network path. What the reach decides is what the factory checks and records — the version, the [consumer contract](06-what-a-consumer-declares.md) checked at the producer's [Merge to master](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/07-merge-to-master.md) gate, the [deprecation list](08-deprecation.md), the producer's [current release](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md) running in the consumer's [candidate environment](../05-environments/02-an-environment-per-candidate/README.md). A call it does not cover still happens and breaks the way a third-party interface breaks. What covering a cross-project call costs: the [items](../01-one-pipeline.md) decomposed from one [intent](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) are decided under two projects' thresholds and [safeguards](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md), and that a [fleet](../10-fleet/README.md) scoped to one project cannot be dispatched onto the item in the other.

A contract is a record: one published interface of one service, identified by that service and the interface's own name in its [build](../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md), with its kind — a published interface or a [store](09-the-store-is-a-contract-too.md) — as the field everything else follows from. [The merge queue](../05-environments/03-the-merge-queue.md) writes it at the fast-forward of the first [release](../06-releases/02-the-release-record.md) that publishes it, in the same write as that release's first version, because a contract changes only inside its service's items and every write to it happens at a release. A [writer](../../what-the-factory-does/01-tight-integration.md) of its own would be a component with one caller and the per-service ordering implemented again inside it, which is the argument that already put the [release record](../06-releases/02-the-release-record.md) and its number in the queue. The kind has to be single-valued across versions, which is why it is a record and not a name for a service plus an interface name: two versions disagreeing about whether the thing is a store would enforce two promises on one interface. What it costs: a contract exists only from the merge that first published it, so an interface a rejected [candidate](../06-releases/03-what-a-build-is-called-and-when.md) publishes points at nothing.

So there are two versioned things, and they must not be combined into one. A release number orders the builds of a single service. A contract version is a compatibility promise to whoever calls it, and semver is precisely what that is for: major means a consumer breaks. One service can publish several contracts — an API, a gRPC service, three topics — and they move at their own rates. A single number per service cannot express several independent promises, and a promise that moves for unrelated reasons stops being read. Most releases publish no new contract version at all.
