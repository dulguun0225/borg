# Deprecation

Deprecation is an obligation of the contract, not a note someone leaves. When the producer adds the new form, the old one is **marked** — a field of the contract version, per element, derived from the build like the rest of the form, because no diff shows which new form supersedes which old one and the producer's own item is the only thing that knows. Where a toolchain has no place to state it, a store schema being the ordinary case, the build states the mark beside the migration in the same file the derivation already reads. A mark is itself a change to the form, so it mints a version and it is a minor: a consumer sees a bump for an annotation nothing breaks on, which is the cost of the version tracking the form rather than the promise.

The list is then a query and nothing writes it — for one marked element, the consumers whose contracts in force still name it, the consumers whose contracts in force over that producer are [partial](12-what-the-derivation-records.md), plus any safeguard's predicate naming it. Computed, it cannot go stale: a consumer that stops reading a field leaves the list because its next release derives nothing, with nobody remembering to remove it. A maintained list would need two writers to agree about a fact both read out of records that already say it. A detector of its own, the pass over the deprecation list, raises both, and has a row in [_Components and what they call_](../../components.md). It raises the brownout when the derived consumer contracts naming the element are gone, and the removal when the brownout's window reached its cap with no crossing and with volume received. A row of its own because the two raises fall at two events no other component sees both of, and one evidence key wants one raiser; the cost is one more detector to keep running. It writes a [last check](../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) of its own each pass, so a stopped pass is a named row rather than a brownout that never arrives. It raises neither again for an element whose brownout failed, the failed window standing in the record for the detector to read, both raises being a human's from then on.

Each raise is keyed by the contract and the element, the [evidence key intake states](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) for these raises, and the key is what stops a second brownout arriving beside one already raised. A safeguard stops neither item existing, only passing: the candidate is rejected at the Merge to master gate on the safeguard, counts an attempt, and appears in [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) as an escalation (12) naming the safeguard and its author, which is the blocked item asking the consumer to confirm. Nobody has to remember step three. What a stale safeguard costs is a full pass of [the pipeline](../01-one-pipeline.md) and the attempt limit spent on work the factory raised itself, and a removal blocked for as long as nobody withdraws or confirms it.

The **brownout** is why the emptied list raises no removal by itself. The list is derived from builds, and [_What a consumer declares_](06-what-a-consumer-declares.md) concedes what a build can hide: a read through reflection or configuration empties a list its consumer is still on. So the last inference before something is destroyed is checked against the running world instead of trusted.

The brownout is an item of the producer whose release disables the marked element in behaviour while leaving the form unchanged (the field unpopulated, the operation erroring), deliberately the change [_What a diff cannot see_](05-what-a-diff-cannot-see.md) names, made this once on purpose. It ships, deploys, and rolls back like any release, under whatever [rollout strategy](../03-gates/02-the-rollout-strategy.md) the score and an owner's safeguards pick. A read that lands on a [control](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md)'s instances errs nothing, which thins the signal the way partial exposure thins any window's. Its [window](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) is the one window that reads more than the producer's own numbers, the exception stated beside the rule it excepts in [_The analysis window_](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md). It runs to the [cap](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) rather than stopping where the [boundary](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) would allow, the way the [held-out sample](../04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md)'s does, its evidence being the point. A service breaking anywhere in the install while it is open fails it, and the failed exit is the ordinary one: the [rollback](../06-releases/06-rollback.md) restores the element unattended, the [revert](../06-releases/06-rollback.md) and the [hold](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) follow as after any failed window. The failed brownout stands in the record as a consumer read the derivation missed, so raising that element's brownout or removal again is from then on a human's act, never the detector's.

The detector raises the removal only on a brownout window that reached the cap uncrossed having received volume (one whose comparison received none says nothing about the element, which is the reading [_The analysis window_](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) gives an empty comparison). That removal reaches [Implementation](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/README.md) with a human deciding it there: the drop's diff destroys stored data, so [reversibility is resolved rather than valued](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) there. A [could not derive](06-what-a-consumer-declares.md) record still puts a human at the gates [_What a consumer declares_](06-what-a-consumer-declares.md) already reaches, on its own terms. The brownout's evidence still raises the item; what changed is that nobody looked is no longer the steady state for the drop itself. The exit that reports no outcome is elsewhere not evidence; here the silence is the finding sought, because the brownout exists to give it a bounded period and a watch, and what it establishes stays bounded too: no read was exercised, never that none exists.

The brownout is the one release whose intended effect is a caller's failure — the factory degrading production on purpose, which nothing else may do — and the exception is stated the way [the search](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md)'s is, named and bounded: it ships as an item through every gate, one window bounds it, the ordinary rollback reverses it, and the removal it precedes would fail the same consumer permanently, with nothing watching and nothing left to restore. What it cannot establish is a read the window never saw — a consumer whose path runs monthly survives a shorter brownout and breaks at the removal — so it narrows what the blind case leaves unprotected and does not close it, and both levers over the rest are already an owner's: the cap sizes the period, and a safeguard (9) naming the element blocks the removal outright for the consumer an owner knows about. A false failure fails safe — a service erroring for reasons of its own restores an element nobody read and blocks a removal that was fine, at the cost any failed window carries, and the human who re-raises the removal is who tells the two apart. Brownouts hold windows open, so the [window limit](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) already bounds how many elements a producer clears at once — and at the supplied limit of 1 a brownout is the producer's whole window budget for a full cap, its other releases holding at the production deploy gate meanwhile, the price the [held-out sample](../04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md) already pays for the same run-to-cap watch. A store's old form takes no brownout: its consumer is the service's own past release, which is not running to err, and the release range [_What a consumer declares_](06-what-a-consumer-declares.md) queries is already what covers it.

The list contains the consumers the factory can derive, so a consumer outside the factory is on it only where an owner's safeguard supplies a predicate replacing that consumer's contract — the blind case [_What a consumer declares_](06-what-a-consumer-declares.md) sets out, and blocking the removal item is what the safeguard does here. Without one, it is not on the list at all: the list empties while that consumer still reads the old form, and the factory proceeds to the brownout and the removal on schedule. The brownout reaches it as a warning where the major version cannot — behavioural, needing no build to read — but nothing watches it: an outside consumer's error rate is not the factory's to read, so its break during the brownout restores nothing by itself, and what the brownout gives it is a bounded period in which the element still exists to be restored and a human to complain to, rather than a removal nothing can put back.
