# A halt's withdrawal

The third row outside every item, and it takes [_A safeguard's
withdrawal_](10-a-safeguards-withdrawal.md)'s shape for the same reason. What it decides is
the second record that ends a [halt](../../09-gate-policy/04-stopping-the-factory.md), and
a withdrawal is not in force until this row approves it, so a halt nobody has ended stands.
Its actions are Approve and Reject with feedback: there is no Edit in place, a withdrawal
naming one record having nothing in it to edit, and no Hold, a withdrawal not approved
being already not in force.

It is a row of its own rather than the safeguard row widened, because the two rows decide
different records and route differently. A safeguard's withdrawal routes to the human that
safeguard's own routing field names. A halt has no such field, its subject being the
factory, so this row routes to the owner, which is what an [unheld
row](../../08-operations/07-pages.md) already does. It reads no factor set and no
threshold, for the reason the safeguard row reads none: a row the [score](../../04-risk-score/README.md)
could auto-pass would be the factory deciding to stop being stopped.

The actor on the withdrawal is never the human this row waits on, the separation the
safeguard row already makes between who writes a record and who decides it. Where an
install has one owner the two are one person, the row still fires, and what it buys is a
chained [decision](../01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) with an actor and a time
on it rather than a second pair of eyes. What it costs is a gate row between an owner and
a factory they have decided to trust again, which is the direction that mistake should
fall in.
