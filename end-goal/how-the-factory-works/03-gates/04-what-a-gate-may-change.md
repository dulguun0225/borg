# What a gate may change

At a gate, artifacts are editable by hand. Code is not: a gate approves or rejects an implementation, it never hand-patches one. A human who wants different code authors it upstream and [sends the item back](06-going-back-up.md) to have it built again.

Merge and deploy gates edit nothing — what they decide is an event, not a document. Reject sends the item back to a stage — the one its verdict names, and the nearest authoring stage above the row where it names none, which [_Going back up_](06-going-back-up.md) sets out; hold leaves the event queued with the change still good, which is why only the deploy rows offer it. What a hold waits on differs by gate. At the production deploy gate it is the service's open windows already at its window limit, a rollback whose revert has not shipped, a declared dependency that is not live, a [halt](../09-gate-policy/04-stopping-the-factory.md) or a [change freeze](../09-gate-policy/04-stopping-the-factory.md) an owner authored, or a record the [drift detector](../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) found disagreeing with what runs. At the candidate deploy gate it is a dependency that is not live yet, the producing release of a [contract migration](../07-contracts/02-no-single-item-may-break-a-contract.md), since a candidate environment is composed from its dependencies' [current releases](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md) and never from another service's candidate. It is also a platform with no room for another environment, the one condition here decomposition could not have declared. At both deploy gates it is also a service its owner has not yet marked [provisioned](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md): a repository or a persistent environment's store not yet written as existing. The two are different answers and have to stay distinguishable: the score learns from a reject and should learn nothing from a hold.

There are three kinds of hold, and only one is written as a decision. A hold a human sets is the verdict of that firing's decision record, with that human as the actor.

A hold the factory sets over a record that already exists writes nothing: an open window, an [error budget](../08-operations/05-service-level-objectives.md) exhausted, a dependency that is not current, a rollback whose revert has not shipped, a mismatch the drift detector found. It is recomputed at the firing and at every re-evaluation of the open row while it stands, which is what checking at the moment of firing already requires. A record for it would be a decision where the design says nothing is decided, and re-testing would append one at every re-evaluation. The row stays open while such a hold stands, one open event and never one per re-test. The gate fires once on the arrival, and the gate component re-evaluates the pending row whenever a record a hold of this kind reads changes, and on a fixed interval at the least. When none stands the row closes as it would have at a firing with no hold. Where the row carries no [mark](01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) sending a human and the number on the open event is under the threshold, that is an auto-approve with the gate component as actor. Otherwise the row goes on waiting on the human it names, who may already have approved through the hold by naming it. So a pending row is one open event however long the hold stood, the [pending predicate](01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) returns one row per gate per item, and the score's join of open row to close row has no unpaired rows from a hold.

A wait the factory could not compute at a firing, because no gate fired or because the condition is not a record, is written into the log by the log: a credential that cannot be reached, a platform with no room, a [stage dispatch matched nothing for](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md). The component that met it is the caller and the actor, so [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) has a row to show. It is two rows rather than one, the shape a [page](../08-operations/07-pages.md) already has for the same reason. The first row is written when the component meets the condition, naming the condition and the values it was met on. The second is written when the condition is found gone, by whichever component next reaches the work the wait stopped rather than by the one that met it, which may have stopped with it. That is [dispatch](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) for every wait that stops a dispatch, reading the outstanding ones before it matches; the deployer for a target it can reach again or a platform with room again; and in general the component that would have acted, which is the one that has to read the wait anyway. A first row with no second is what Work reads as still holding, and the pair is what makes how long it held a subtraction. Those values are what an owner reads to know which record to write or repair. Neither row points at a [credential](../10-fleet/01-what-an-agent-runs-on.md) or a [fleet entry](../10-fleet/README.md), which is what keeps the rule that no record written for traceability points at either.

None of the three counts against the attempt limit and none teaches the score. What that costs: the factory's own hold over a record that already exists is written nowhere, so how long it has been holding is answerable for the other two alone, and a condition that lifts and returns is a new pair rather than one wait that resumed.

An approve at a row where a hold stands names every hold standing, and holds at a deploy row are a set and not one: a dependency not current, an advisory match, and a freeze stand together in an ordinary incident. The conditions that hold a production deploy differ in what approving accepts: approving one accepts a deploy that waits, approving another accepts redelivering a defect the factory has just removed. So one Approve across all of them records a gesture rather than a decision, and so would an approve naming one of three. The verdict is an approve naming the set it goes through, on the [close event](01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) beside the verdict, and the gate refuses two shapes: an approve naming a hold not standing at that re-evaluation, and an approve whose set leaves out one that is, of which a bare approve while one stands is the case with nothing named. So a later reader of the close event reads which holds the human accepted, each by name, and the drift mismatch below is not passed under cover of naming a cheaper one beside it. The factory's own holds are recomputed at every re-evaluation of the row, so the set to name from is already in hand, and a hold that arrived between the human's reading and their verdict refuses the approve, the human naming it or not on a second reading. Where the set holds the rollback hold below, the row waits on that hold's decider, an approve through the set being an approve through it. What it costs is one field on the close event holding a set, a screen state per standing hold, and an operator who cannot clear the most damaging hold with the gesture that clears the cheapest.

A [halt](../09-gate-policy/04-stopping-the-factory.md) and a [change freeze](../09-gate-policy/04-stopping-the-factory.md) are holds of that second kind. Each is a record that already exists, neither writes anything at the firing, and both are recomputed at the firing and at every re-evaluation like the rest. The halt is the one exception to the approve above, and the gate refuses a third shape for it: an approve at a firing where a halt stands. There is no verdict a halt admits, because approving through one is ending it, and ending it is decided at [_A halt's withdrawal_](07-what-particular-gates-decide/11-a-halts-withdrawal.md) by the human that row routes to. A freeze takes no exception at all and is approved through by naming it, the way an exhausted error budget is.

[A rollback whose revert has not shipped](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) is the one hold with a routing of its own, for what approving through it does. Master still carries the change the rollback removed, so the deploy redelivers a defect the factory found in production, and this is the hold most likely to be reached for during an incident. Its row waits on the [duty](../../what-humans-do.md) that undoes a shipped change (10), on the named human a [safeguard](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md)'s routing field gives where one names it, and on the owner where nobody holds it. It never waits on whoever is at [Ops](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) when the hold is met, which is the separation [_A safeguard's withdrawal_](07-what-particular-gates-decide/10-a-safeguards-withdrawal.md) already makes between the actor and the decider, and the verdict carries the reason that human states. What it costs: the fastest way through the most damaging hold is slower than through the other four, and an install with one owner separates nobody and buys a chained decision with an actor, a reason and a time on it rather than a second pair of eyes.
