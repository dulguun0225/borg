# Where a gate is, and what decides it

There is a gate at every [stage](../01-one-pipeline.md) boundary: after [decomposition](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) into [items](../01-one-pipeline.md), after the spec, implementation plan, tasks, and implementation are authored, and before each merge and each deploy. Two kinds: an **artifact gate** decides over a document a stage has written, and an **event gate** decides whether an event — a merge, a deploy — happens at all, which is what makes [hold](04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) a stop rather than an undo. The mechanism is permanent — it does not weaken as the factory improves.

The factory scores each change and auto-passes what it judges low risk. Two things reach a gate from the score, not one: the number against the [risk threshold](../09-gate-policy/01-what-is-in-it.md), and the [factor vector](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) it was computed from — a vector recording any factor [resolved rather than valued](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) is decided by a human whatever the number is, so no threshold an owner authors and no formula the score publishes can auto-pass it. The same [score](../04-risk-score/README.md) picks the [_rollout strategy_](02-the-rollout-strategy.md). Humans override by adding a safeguard — a gate held always-on, or a strategy that keeps a [control](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) — and can undo a change after it shipped.

A gate **firing** is one gate deciding one thing at one moment, and it writes one **decision** into the append-only log seam 2 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../../deferred.md) describes, which is that log's single [writer](../../what-the-factory-does/01-tight-integration.md) with the gate component as its caller. What fires it is the item arriving at the row: at an artifact gate, the artifact store writing the version the stage submits; at an event gate, the event it decides being next, the row above approved and, at a merge, the candidate's run ended. A gate fires again on the same item and row for three reasons and no fourth: [Edit in place](03-actions-at-each-gate.md), below; a row re-fired to another holder of its duty, below; and a [hold](04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) a human set, the row firing again when that human or another holder of the row's duty releases it at [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md). The gate component fires no row on an item while an open event on that row is pending, the one exception being the open event Edit in place appends naming the row it supersedes, so one gate on one item has at most one pending row, and the rows one item writes are bounded by its arrivals and those three. A hold the factory sets holds the open row rather than firing the gate again, which _What a gate may change_ sets out.

A decision is two rows and not one: an **open event** appended when the gate fires, and a second row appended when the decision ends, naming the row it ends. That second row is a **close event** where a **verdict** is given: approve, reject, or hold. A row is chained when it is appended, so a verdict written onto the open event afterwards would be a rewrite of a chained record, the rewrite [_Drift detection_](../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md)'s check on the chain exists to catch, made the ordinary path for every gate in the factory. Two appends is what a [_page_](../08-operations/07-pages.md) already does for the same reason: a page is a sequence of rows about one wait, each saying which of them it is. The [_deploy record_](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md) advances in place instead, and that is not a counterexample — it is an ordinary record rather than a row of the log, and nothing chains it.

What forces the write at the firing is the [factor vector](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md): a human is meant to argue with the score's factors before deciding, so the vector has to exist while they are deciding, and it cannot be computed when they open the row because the score version moves as outcomes arrive. Opened, it names the gate row, the item, the [build](../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) where one exists, the [artifact version](../01-one-pipeline.md) under decision at an artifact gate, the vector and the number, the [policy version](../../what-the-factory-does/02-traceability.md) and the [score version](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md), the values actually applied, and the [duty](../../what-humans-do.md) or named human the row waits on. The artifact version is there because the record is open across two events and artifacts are editable at a gate. [Edit in place](03-actions-at-each-gate.md) authors a new version through the [artifact store](../01-one-pipeline.md) while the row is still waiting, so a verdict naming only the item would point at whatever the artifact says when it is read rather than at what was decided over. Naming it costs one field, the version chain already existing; what it costs a reader is that an approval names a version rather than an artifact, so asking what was approved reads the chain rather than the current artifact.

The version is named by its identifier and by its content digest, a hash the [artifact store](../01-one-pipeline.md) computes over the version's text when it writes it and keeps on the version; the row at [_A role prompt or a skill_](07-what-particular-gates-decide/09-a-role-prompt-or-a-skill.md) names the version under decision the same way. The identifier says which version was decided over and the digest says what it said, so the chain covers the text and not only the name. A version rewritten in the store after its approval disagrees with the digest on the chained row, the rewrite seam 2 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../../deferred.md) exists to catch, where an identifier alone points at whatever the store holds under it. An [acceptance criterion](07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/01-the-record.md)'s provenance, read from the decision that introduced it, is covered the same way, the criterion being written in the spec version's own write. What it costs is a hash per version, and a reader that verifies an approval by hashing the version it reads.

At Decomposition it names the [intent](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) and the set decomposition proposed rather than one item, that row being the one gate above a [timeline](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) rather than on it. At [_A role prompt or a skill_](07-what-particular-gates-decide/09-a-role-prompt-or-a-skill.md) it names the version under decision and no item at all, that row belonging to no timeline, no [service](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md), and no build. The close event names the open event, the verdict, its actor, when that actor opened the row where the actor is a human, and where the verdict sends an item back [what it returns to](06-going-back-up.md). It names neither the policy version nor the score version: what a decision was made under is a fact of the firing, so it is written once where the firing is and two rows cannot come to disagree about it. Both are written against the item and the build and never against the [release](../06-releases/02-the-release-record.md), including at the production deploy gate, which fires after the release exists — one rule for all eight rows, so no reader has to know which side of the merge a gate is on. What that costs is that a decision is read by joining two rows rather than by reading one.

On a reject or a hold the close event also carries the reason: the text a human wrote, which is the feedback the action's name promises, or the check the gate component rejected on where the reject was mechanical. The log's writer refuses a reject carrying none, so no item is sent back with nothing saying why. The reason is on the chained row from the first one because three things read it and no other record holds it: the stage the item returns to, which [_One pipeline_](../01-one-pipeline.md) hands it beside the rejected version; a [role prompt version](../10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/01-what-a-version-is-authored-from.md), authored from rejects and their reasons; and an auditor reading a decision back. What it costs is a field a human has to fill before a reject closes, and prose on a chained row that the [log](../../deferred.md)'s retention cut removes with the decision.

A decision that will never receive a verdict is ended in the log rather than left open. Its second row is an **abandonment**: it names the open event and why no verdict is coming, with the component that ended the decision as caller and actor, and it carries no verdict, a close event being reserved for one. Three things write one. An item stopped at [_the attempt limit_](05-the-attempt-limit.md) leaves a row nobody is deciding, ended by the component that writes the escalation onto the item. An item superseded by a [re-decomposition](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) or dropped ends its open rows with it, written by the component that wrote that value onto the item. And [Edit in place](03-actions-at-each-gate.md) while the row waits re-fires the gate rather than leaving the verdict to bind to a version the human rejected by editing it. Authoring the new version appends a new open event naming the version and the row it supersedes, with a vector computed from what is now under decision, and an abandonment ends the superseded row.

A decision may carry a third kind of row between its open event and the row that ends it. An **acknowledgement** names the open event and a human, and is appended when a holder of the row's duty says at [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) that they have the row, the gate component calling for it as it calls for a close. It is the act a [_page_](../08-operations/07-pages.md) already records as acknowledged, given to every gate row because a duty held by more than one human delivers the row to all of them at once. It decides nothing, ends no wait, and excludes nobody: the row stays pending, stays in Work for every holder, and the first verdict still decides. What it changes is what the other holders see, the row shown to each of them as acknowledged and by whom, so a holder who reads it knows another is on it and a row acknowledged by nobody reads as such. An open event admits one acknowledgement per human and any number of humans, and a second from the same human is refused as a second close is. Where the row also pages, one act at Work writes both, the gate component calling the notifier for the page's acknowledged event as the component that ends a wait calls it.

[Factory](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) reads them beside the close event. The rows a human acknowledged and another holder decided are reported per named human beside the rows each decided, so time a shared duty spent is charged to everyone who took the row and not only to whoever closed it. And the wait on a shared duty is split at the first acknowledgement, the way [_The page channel, and what reached a human_](../11-screens/05-the-page-channel-and-what-reached-a-human.md) splits it at the first accepted delivery: before it the wait is the duty's, after it a human's. What it costs is a chained row per acknowledgement, a state a human can set and walk away from, which the page accepts on the same terms and Factory shows as an acknowledgement with no verdict after it, and a holder who reads a row without acknowledging it, who stays as invisible as before.

The log's writer refuses four closes and nothing else. A reject carrying no reason is refused, as above. An open event admits exactly one close event, so a second close naming one already closed is refused, and where [a duty is held by more than one human](../08-operations/07-pages.md) the first verdict decides and a later one does not. A close naming an open event an abandonment has ended is refused, so no verdict binds to a version its human edited away or to an item that stopped. And a close event whose actor is the author named on the artifact version its open event names is refused wherever another holder of the row's duty exists, the row re-firing to that holder rather than closing to its own author. That is the rule the gate component applies at Decomposition, Spec, Implementation plan, Tasks, and [_A role prompt or a skill_](07-what-particular-gates-decide/09-a-role-prompt-or-a-skill.md); [_A safeguard's withdrawal_](07-what-particular-gates-decide/10-a-safeguards-withdrawal.md) already applies it to one row alone. Where no second holder exists, the row still fires to the editor and the close event carries a self-approval field, which [Factory](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) reports per human beside what each approved and how often it was later undone; an [install](../../what-the-factory-does/README.md) with one owner is allowed, and its trail says what it is.

Each refusal is a state the screen shows: the row already decided, already ended, or waiting on a different holder, with what caused it, never an error about nothing. A pending gate is then an open event with neither a close event nor an abandonment, a predicate over the log alone that needs no join against the item's stage. Absence of a verdict is evidence that somebody is still deciding, which it was not while an abandoned row sat open with nothing saying so. What it costs is that approving after editing is two rows where it reads as one action, that every abandoned decision costs a row where it used to cost none, and that every human edit at those five gates costs a second person wherever one exists.

A gate fires only where the item's intent permits it. [The intent's state](../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md) is read before the firing, and no row of an item whose intent is unrefined, re-decomposing, escalated, or dropped is fired at all: no open event is appended, so nothing is decided, no attempt is counted, and the score learns nothing. Reading that state at [dispatch](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) alone would stop authoring and nothing else, and Merge to master, the row nearest a release and one the score auto-passes in the steady state, would decide an item whose intent is being decomposed again.

A human at a gate is an instrument, and the design reads it rather than assumes it. Four things follow. The vector is shown while the human decides and the number only beside the verdict once it is written: [_Implementation_](07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/README.md) derives an encoding's expected behaviour from the criterion's sentence and never from the code, because a reader of the code agrees with it, and a reader shown the score's conclusion first agrees with that. A human at a gate already knows why they are there, so what withholding the number costs is only the magnitude.

What put them there is a mark on the row and never the absence of one, which is what makes it readable without the number. A vector naming a resolved factor says the number decided nothing. A [safeguard](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) (9) among the values actually applied says an owner put them there whatever the number was. A row an [Edit in place](03-actions-at-each-gate.md) fired waits on another holder of the row's duty, or on the editor where none exists, however the recomputed number moves. A row the review sample below selected says the score would have auto-passed it and an authored rate sent it to a human anyway. And [_A safeguard's withdrawal_](07-what-particular-gates-decide/10-a-safeguards-withdrawal.md) reads no threshold at all. Where a row waiting on a human carries none of them, what sent them is the number against the [risk threshold](../09-gate-policy/01-what-is-in-it.md).

What the list deliberately does not cover is a [hold](04-what-a-gate-may-change.md), which is a fact about the event and not about the score: its three kinds differ in what each writes and _What a gate may change_ is where that is settled, so what a held row records is read there rather than here. One condition inside one of those kinds does send a human — [a mismatch the drift detector found](07-what-particular-gates-decide/08-deploy-to-production.md) pages and waits on a human and on nothing else, where the four conditions beside it in that kind lift themselves and page nobody. So a held row is read for its hold before it is read for a mark, and the residual above is a statement about the rows the score sent somebody to.

A human deciding on an input the factory could not read has to have the vector, since arguing with a factor that was never computed is not what they are there to do, and so does one an owner posted at a gate the score would have passed. The close event carries, beside its own time, when the actor opened the row in [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), written with Work as the caller so that it is the screen's report and not the human's. [Factory](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) reports how long a row was open in front of a human before its verdict, per duty and per named human, beside the wait it already reports. The pair Factory reports for the factory is what it auto-approved and how often that was later undone (10) or rolled back, and it reports the same pair per human, over the rows each approved and split by what put the human at the row. Those are readings and not levers: nothing removes a human from a gate on a number, and a safeguard (9) adds one whatever the readings say.

The split is what makes the pair worth reading, because only one of the kinds is comparable with the factory's own rate. A threshold sends a human the changes the score put above it, where the auto-pass rate is over the changes below it, so a human who is a perfect check on the harder half still shows the higher undo rate. What removes the difference is the **review sample rate**: how often a change the score would have auto-passed is put in front of that duty's human anyway, with the vector and without the number, as a threshold-gated row is. The open event carries that selection as the row's mark, beside the [_why it auto-passed_](../04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md) field the rows that did auto-pass carry. Those rows are the changes the factory itself judged fine, so a human whose undo rate on them matches the factory's, in the time it takes to open a row and close it, is a signature at that gate and not a check. That is what a safeguard (9) is understood to buy: a human added, whose detection is measured rather than assumed.

The rate is per duty and authored with the rest of [gate policy](../09-gate-policy/01-what-is-in-it.md) (8), the score supplies it where an owner authors none, and a safeguard (9) is a floor under it, sampling more being a human added. Two rules keep it clear of the [held-out sample](../04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md), which selects in the opposite direction. It selects no row on an item that sample selected: that sample's evidence is a release that reached production with a human removed, and a review-sampled row would put one back at a gate the selection removed them from. And it moves the risk threshold one way only. A rejection at a review-sampled row is a gate the factory would have passed and a human did not, which the score already learns from; an approval at one is consistent with the human having caught nothing, so nothing rises on it.

What it costs is a field a row left open in another tab overstates; a number per named human that reads as a measure of a person, kept because the alternative is a defence the design cites and never examines; and human time spent on changes the factory was right about, which is the work the factory exists to remove. The rate is what bounds that last one, and the practice it buys is the answer to a residue the readings would otherwise only show: duties 11 and 12 hand a human only what [the fleet](../10-fleet/README.md) of agents failed at, on work that shrinks as the factory improves, where a review-sampled row is ordinary work the factory would have passed.

A failing rollout rolls back on its own, inside its [_analysis window_](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) — no human involved, no waiting. The rollback is reported, not requested.

A human's undo (10) assumes the change can still be undone, and what a human may do narrows as later work builds on it: a rollback while the build it would return to is still running, a revert once it is not. Reversibility is a factor of the score and what a human may still do is limited by it, through the strategy it picks and the [instances of the build being replaced](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) that strategy keeps running. It narrows that way and no other: with one item per release, a change is never harder to undo because it shipped alongside nine others. What overlapping analysis windows add is not difficulty but reach — a rollback undoes every release above its target, up to the [window limit](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md): the one set of items the factory ships together.
