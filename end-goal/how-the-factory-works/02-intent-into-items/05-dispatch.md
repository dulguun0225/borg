# Dispatch

With the decomposition approved, the factory puts its own agents onto the items — a model in a role with a scope, as [_One pipeline_](../01-one-pipeline.md) has it. Dispatch gets no gate: it decides who authors, and everything authored is reviewed at a gate anyway, where the score reads the [per-author prior](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) of the model that wrote it.

Where the intent's state and an unexpired claim do not already stop it, six things stop a dispatch, each a [hold](../03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) in the [decision log](../../deferred.md) rather than a timeline that stops. They are a stage no [fleet entry](../10-fleet/README.md) covers, a stage whose role has no [role prompt version in force](../10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md), [a credential already known unreachable](../10-fleet/05-an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold.md), [a credential at its spend ceiling](../10-fleet/08-a-spend-ceiling.md), a [constraint](01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) of the document kind requiring [seam 5](../../deferred.md#security-comes-last) enforced on an item within its reach, and a constraint of the dispatch-decided kind whose predicate no entry's processing location satisfies. Dispatch writes one row per item and stage for each of the six, with itself as the caller and the actor; for a run on no item it writes one per intent and role, or one per project for [the grouper](01-intake/02-reports.md), under whichever of the six conditions reaches a run of that kind. The row names which condition held and the values the match was made on: the role, the item's [area chain](03-decomposition/02-what-an-item-names.md) and its service's [project](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) where no entry's scope covered them, the credential name where that was the cause, and the constraint named where a document-kind constraint was the cause. Where the dispatch-decided kind was the cause, the row also names the processing location no entry's credential satisfied. What would clear it is then read off the values it was matched on rather than followed from a pointer at an entry.

The account exhausted part-way through a stage, and a [spend ceiling](../10-fleet/08-a-spend-ceiling.md) reached part-way through one, are each a row of its own, written by whoever could not reach or proceed, and dispatch's rows are about the stages that never started, so neither stands for the other and a credential twelve waiting items share is twelve rows and not one. Dispatch re-matches its open holds when a record able to clear one arrives: an owner writing a fleet entry at [_Factory_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), [the gate a version fires](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/09-a-role-prompt-or-a-skill.md) putting one in force, a credential reachable again, an owner clearing a ceiling, or the seam 5 field turning on. It then writes the second row of every hold the match lifts, the mid-stage one it did not open included, so no hold outlives its condition and none is left for a component that has stopped to close. No [page](../08-operations/07-pages.md) fires and no attempt counts, for the reason a hold neither pages nor counts: nothing deployed is worse for it, and no agent worked. What it costs is a row per item and stage: fifty items reaching a stage no entry covers write fifty holds, which is why Factory reports the count per cause rather than leaving an owner to count rows.

What limits how many items move at once is infrastructure, an environment per candidate, and that cost is the factory's rather than a human's. Where more is ready than that infrastructure admits, dispatch orders it by [tier](02-the-interview.md) first, an item's [priority](../05-environments/03-the-merge-queue.md) breaking a tie within one. What a tier orders is admission, never a bound already reached: the spend ceiling, [the platform's own room](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/06-deploy-to-candidate-environment.md), and [the backlog cap](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) each stay a stop no tier lifts, the same as a hold above. Where a human authors a stage instead of an agent (11, 12), nothing about dispatch changes: authorship is an attribute of the stage, and the item is still one timeline.

Dispatch is report-driven — [_What an item names_](03-decomposition/02-what-an-item-names.md) has every stage reporting a transition to it — and an agent can stop without reporting anything: its host restarts, its process dies, its provider connection hangs. So a dispatch is a claim that expires unless the agent renews it, renewal being activity rather than progress, and a claim that expires unrenewed is itself the report: dispatch sends the item back to the stage to be entered again, the way an agent's failed attempt does, so the count rises on re-entry and enough silent stops walk the item to an [escalation](../03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) (12) instead of leaving a timeline that never advances and never escalates. A stage with an unexpired claim is matched to nobody, which is what keeps a slow agent and its replacement from authoring one stage at once. A claim on a stage with a hold open against it is the one expiry that is not that report: dispatch releases the claim, counts nothing, and leaves the item on the hold, because an agent that stopped when its credential could not be reached renews nothing and a hold is not a failed attempt — without that, every hold written mid-stage would walk its item to an escalation at the expiry rate. What it costs is a renewal on every working agent, and that a stop is discovered at the expiry rather than at the death — an expiry generous enough to never cut off honest work leaves a dead stage undiscovered for exactly that long.

The [_Factory_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) screen is where an owner watches this: [_the fleet_](../10-fleet/README.md), what each agent is doing, and how well.
