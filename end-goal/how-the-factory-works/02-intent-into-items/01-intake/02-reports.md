# Reports

An end user's bug report (4) or complaint (5) is a **report**, and a report is not an intent. The way in is thin and embedded in the deployed software: what it writes is a report, into the **report store**, one of its own sized for what a population of end users sends rather than for what a graph joins on. A **grouper** reads that store and calls intake once per group, so five hundred reports of one slow button are one intent and not five hundred.

The grouper is an [agent](../../01-one-pipeline.md), because deciding whether two free-text reports are one problem is model work, and model work outside [the fleet](../../10-fleet/README.md) would run on words no gate ever decided. It runs under a [fleet entry](../../10-fleet/README.md) like any other agent — a model, an [effort](../../10-fleet/01-what-an-agent-runs-on.md), a credential — in a role put on neither an intent nor an item: its scope is a project, and it reads the reports that arrived under that project and nothing else. Its [role prompt](../../10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md) is versioned and decided at [a gate of its own](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/09-a-role-prompt-or-a-skill.md), so a safeguard (9) at that gate reaches the words it works from — words [_What a version is authored from_](../../10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/01-what-a-version-is-authored-from.md) keeps a report out of. What grouping does not do is move the [per-author prior](../../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md): no gate decides a grouping and no artifact carries one, so its two failure directions surface one stage down — a group [decomposition](../03-decomposition/README.md) has to split, and duplicates linked as recurrences — where they are repaired rather than scored.

This source alone has a record in front of the intent. An owner's request is one thing because one person typed it, and a factory finding is keyed by its evidence. Only an end user's report arrives in a quantity nobody upstream has collapsed, in words the factory has to read to know whether two of them are one problem.

A report raises an intent on arrival rather than waiting for a batch: the first of a group raises it, and later matching ones attach. Nothing waits for a count, because grouping is deduplication and not triage — one report from one end user is an intent, and the count is evidence on it rather than a rank: nothing orders work by it on its own, though a human reordering a queue can read it. A batch would group better and delay every fix by the length of the interval.

A report also carries one field the reporter sets on submission, independent of whether it is a bug (4) or a complaint (5): whether it says a person is being harmed by the software. Nothing infers it, and nothing classifies content to set it: it is the reporter's own rank and never the factory's, and grouping stays deduplication and not triage.

A report is marked with the intent it was grouped into and kept. Deleting one on grouping would cost the measurement this store makes possible: the rate of reports before and after the release meant to fix what they describe. So what removes a report is a retention setting an owner authors, and never the grouping, unless a [legal hold](../../09-gate-policy/03-what-is-not-in-it.md) on it stands, which suspends the removal until the hold is withdrawn. That setting is a field of the one policy list the factory owns, which [_Gate policy_](../../09-gate-policy/03-what-is-not-in-it.md) sets out, and its only reader is the report store — the component that writes a report is the only one that may remove one. Where an owner authors nothing, reports are kept for the life of the install: no outcome teaches a retention period, so there is nothing for the [score](../../04-risk-score/README.md) to supply.

The same rate is the intent's **outcome**, computed once at its close: [the acceptance round](../02-the-interview.md)'s verdict for a requested intent, this rate for one grouped from reports, and nothing for one the factory raised, read at [_Factory_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) beside cost per feature.

Grouping takes no gate. The interview, decomposition, and [_Spec_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md) all read what it produced, which is the argument [_Dispatch_](../05-dispatch.md) makes for itself. The two ways it goes wrong are not symmetric, so the grouper prefers to group: two problems in one intent are separated one stage down, where decomposition yields an item each or a spec comes back too large, while duplicates that were never grouped are separated by nobody. Each takes its own interview, its own [timeline](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), and its own item, several of them on the same code and serialized behind each other in [_The merge queue_](../../05-environments/03-the-merge-queue.md).

A later report attaches to the intent and never rewrites it. What refines an intent is attached to it and every reader reads both, so the five hundredth report changes what is built by being read rather than by editing the record — which is what keeps an approval pointing at what was approved. Decomposition is the boundary. Before it, the grouper may still split a group it got wrong; after it, a report matching work already decomposed attaches and raises the count, and one that does not is a new intent, linked to the first as a recurrence. A report arriving after the fix shipped is that same rule: the timeline is finished, so evidence that the fix did not work is a new intent and never a reopening. What the boundary costs is a run of reports just after a narrow decomposition showing as two linked timelines where a human would call it one problem.

For an intent grouped from reports, the reports are what the interview and the spec are authored against and the intent's own **statement** — the field holding what was asked for — summarizes them; for an owner's request the statement is the thing itself.

An intent grouped from reports never auto-passes the [Spec](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md) gate. The source intake wrote is a context factor the [score](../../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) reads, and reports is the value that is resolved rather than weighed: it takes the treatment [a factor the score cannot compute](../../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) takes, resolved and not weighed, a human deciding whatever the formula returns. So a human confirms the criteria (6) whatever the rest of [the vector](../../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) says, and the [held-out sample](../../04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md) cannot select past them, never selecting what a resolved factor put there. The channel is the way in, the report store behind it, and the rates that bound arrival, and this is the one part of its safety that does not rest on how good any reader is. That an agent treats a report as content and never as instruction stays [an instruction to the agent](../../01-one-pipeline.md), worth what an instruction is worth — a reason to hand report text onward quoted rather than folded into anything an agent is told, and not what stops an injected report from shipping code; the human at Spec is. What the floor costs: the source that arrives in the greatest volume is the one whose items cost the most human attention, the opposite direction from the rest of the design, and taken because the alternative is an anonymous sentence reaching production with nobody on the path.

A report carrying the field takes the same resolved treatment at Spec that a report's source already takes: a human confirms the criteria (6) whatever the rest of the vector says, and the field is recorded on the vector beside the source so the human sees which one is marked. It adds no gate a report did not already meet; what it adds is visible to the human deciding it.

The way in is not something a project asks for. It is content the factory ships and versions with itself, built into every service the factory deploys, the way [the four screens](../../11-screens/03-the-screens-as-software.md) are one client built and versioned with the factory — so an install that deployed anything has one, and no item, no request, and no gate stands between a deployed service and a way to report against it. It takes no gate for the reason the screens take none: it is the factory's own software, upgraded with the factory rather than authored by an item. What binds it is the predicate kind of [constraint](01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md), and the factory can guarantee the reading because it ships the thing being read. It knows how to open the way in, so the run on [the candidate environment](../../05-environments/04-what-the-candidate-environment-decides/01-the-third-outcome.md) renders it on every candidate. The predicate-kind constraints in force over that project are a contrast floor, a name on every control, a focus order, and a target size. Each is decided against it and rejects at [Merge to master](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/07-merge-to-master.md) like any predicate, rather than reading as nothing on a run that never reached it. That is what puts the surface with the widest exposure under the same accessibility reading the factory's own screens get, and it reaches the people who are not owners.

What it costs is two things. The way in is not built from the project's [design system](01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) and does not look like the product around it: one way in ships for every install and a design system is one project's, so what reaches it is the part a machine decides and never the rules for using components. And it moves only when the owner upgrades the factory and the service builds again, so a service that has not built since an upgrade serves the way in of the release it was built from. That is the cost the screens pay for being one client, arriving here once per service instead of once, and it is answered the opposite way. [_The screens as software_](../../11-screens/03-the-screens-as-software.md) has the factory refuse a call from a client of another version because a screen can reload; a way in can only rebuild, and a store refusing a stale one would close the channel on every service that has not shipped an item since the upgrade.

So the store keeps the promise the other way round. Every submission names the [shipped-bundle identity](../../../deferred.md#the-products-release-channel) of the release that built the way in, first in the submission and under a shape no release moves, and the store reads the rest under the shape that release wrote. A shape once shipped is added to and never changed under its identity, and the store reads every shape any release ever shipped, which is the forward promise [_The store is a contract too_](../../07-contracts/09-the-store-is-a-contract-too.md) has a schema keep for the release a rollback restores. The report records the identity, so which way in wrote it is on the row. A submission under a shape the store does not read, which only a factory returned to an earlier release can meet, is counted per service beside the refused count on [_Factory_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), because a submission the store could not read is the loss the refused counter cannot see. Beside those counts Factory lists each service whose current release's [build](../../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) names an identity older than the running release's, the build naming the identity of the release that made it, so the services serving an old way in are a list read off records rather than an inference from reports that stopped. What it costs is a store that reads every shape ever shipped, and a field on every build.

Which service a submission counts against is never what the submission says. The way in calls as the deploy that placed it, under [seam 5](../../../deferred.md#security-comes-last): the deployer minted it a token at that deploy and wrote the token's digest on the [deploy record](../../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md), and the store takes the service and the environment from the deploy record that digest finds. A submission naming no deploy the store knows is refused and counted on the whole channel and never on a service, so a safeguard narrowing one service's rate is not evaded by submitting under another service's name.

Arrival is bounded at the channel. Two rates are fields of the [factory-wide settings record](../../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) beside [retention](../../09-gate-policy/03-what-is-not-in-it.md), one applied per service and one over the whole channel, and the report store enforces them at the way in: a report over either rate is refused rather than queued without bound. A report also carries an opaque key where the deployed software supplies one, derived from its own session and salted, so that it marks a session's reports as one source's and carries nobody's identity; the store rates a source by it, and a bad actor is limited through it. The key is not a reporter handle — nothing joins it to a person, it cannot be added to reports already written, and it is the most the channel can carry without trading away the anonymity it exists to offer, which is why the two authored rates stay coarse: what they bound is arrival, never any individual. A surge therefore drops real reports, a real loss preferred to an unbounded one — unbounded, the channel's volume is linear in what a stranger sends at no cost, read by the grouper report by report against [a quota](../../10-fleet/05-an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold.md) every stage shares and whose exhaustion pages nobody. The factory-wide rate authored to zero closes the channel, and a safeguard (9) narrowing the per-service rate to zero closes one service's. This is protection at the channel and not a ceiling on spend: what admitted reports go on to cost is bounded, where an owner authored one, by [the spend ceiling](../../10-fleet/08-a-spend-ceiling.md) and by nothing else.

A share of each rate is reserved for a report carrying the field, so a surge drops an ordinary report before it drops one marking harm: the store refuses an unmarked report once the ordinary share is spent and a marked one only once the whole rate is. The reserved share is fixed and not itself authored, so it costs nothing beyond the rate it divides. What it buys is bounded by what a stranger can do with the field: nothing stops a false mark, so a source that marks every report spends its own share of both rates and no more, the opaque key's own limit standing behind it as it already does for the channel.

A submission gets an answer, and the answer needs no handle. The way in renders the submit result in the session that submitted it — accepted, or refused with the rate as the reason — because nothing is being sent to anybody later, only shown to a session that is still open. The render carries no identity, writes no record about a person, and outlives that session by nothing, so it costs the anonymity nothing and is part of the channel rather than a thing built on top of it. What it buys is that a surge is visible to the people it drops: a reporter told the channel is over its rate knows the words did not arrive, where silence teaches them to submit again or to stop — and both move the measurement this store exists to make, the rate of reports before and after a release moving with submission behaviour as well as with the fix.

An owner who wants a human before the pipeline spends anything on a report adds a safeguard (9) whose subject is the report store: with one in force a report-derived intent arrives and waits, [dispatch](../05-dispatch.md) putting no agent on it and no interview round running, until a human admits it at [_Work_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) — one action per group, the group already being one intent. It is a safeguard rather than the default because a regulated install wants it and an ordinary one should not have to pay it, and what it buys over the floor at Spec is that nothing is spent refining what a human would never admit.

A safeguard may also hold reports before the grouper reads them at all: with it in force, an arrived report waits ungrouped at [_Work_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) until a human admits it, and only an admitted report reaches the grouper. What it buys is that an owner who wants it never has a report's words leave the install unread; what it costs is per-report human triage, the volume the grouper exists to remove.

Retention removes reports in bulk and on age; what removes one report's words is a **redaction** — a second record naming a report, the statement summarizing it, or an [artifact version](../../01-one-pipeline.md) quoting it, and the spans removed. The bytes named are destroyed, every reader reads the target through its redactions, and the redaction carries the actor and the reason and never the words. No link breaks, because what is erased is text inside a record and never the record: the report row, its grouping link, its count, and the before-and-after measurement all stand with the words gone. It is the correction idiom a [constraint](01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) already uses, a second record and never an edit, pointed at content — with one difference: the original is destroyed rather than superseded, so this is the one mechanism in the factory that removes information a decision was made on. The decision still names its versions, and what those versions can no longer show, the redaction record says why. An owner performs one erasure as one action, and the factory walks the links that exist — the report to its intent, the statement, the artifact versions authored against them, which is the query the intent's source already marks out. What it costs is a read-through on every reader of stored text, and an auditor reading a decision back meeting a stated gap where words were. Who had already read them before redaction is now answerable, from the [read event](../../../deferred.md) each such reading appends to the log.

Its writer is [Factory](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), the writer a [safeguard](../../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md), a [halt](../../09-gate-policy/04-stopping-the-factory.md) and a [legal hold](../../09-gate-policy/03-what-is-not-in-it.md) already have, so one erasure is one write by one writer and the auditor is shown one record. The same action writes the matching erasure-list row, and that row lands first, keyed so a step taken again writes nothing — the order [_Tight integration_](../../../what-the-factory-does/01-tight-integration.md) sets for an event that writes more than one record, the copy a restore replays before the read-through it protects. What destroys the bytes is each target's own writer — the report store for a report, [intake](README.md) for the statement, the [artifact store](../../01-one-pipeline.md) for an artifact version. Each reads the redactions naming records it writes, on a pass of its own, and destroys the named spans inside its own records — a record one component writes and another reads, so no component writes another's record. The erasure is effective at the one write regardless, every reader reading the target through its redactions from the moment the redaction exists, so a store whose destruction lags serves nothing meanwhile. What it costs is that the physical destruction is a write per target's store, each finishing on that store's own pass, and what the auditor is shown as performed is the redaction and the read-through, each destruction's completion being that store's own fact.

A redaction outlives a restore. The bytes it destroys stand destroyed only in the store restored from; a backup taken before an erasure still carries the words, and restoring it would put them back unless something outside the backup says otherwise. That something is an **erasure list**: an append-only record on the host, outside the [recovery unit](../../../what-the-factory-does/04-what-the-factory-does-not-build.md), naming what a redaction removed and, since [_People_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) maps a key to a name outside the chain, what an erased mapping removed too. It lives beside the report store and never in the graph, so [the record inventory](../../../records.md) lists no row for it. The same action that writes a redaction, or deletes a mapping, writes the matching row here, the list is never rolled back by a restore, and the store replays it against whatever a restore brought back before serving that store again. What it costs is two things: a second place every erasure writes, and a list that outlives every backup it applies to — itself a copy of what was meant to be gone, kept so that a restore cannot undo an erasure.

What a redaction reaches ends at the install's edge, and both halves of that edge are on the record. Which providers received the words is answerable: report-derived work spends through named credentials, the field every [interview](../02-the-interview.md) round and every stage writes, and [_People_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) records who lent each. Each run's [agent run record](../../10-fleet/01-what-an-agent-runs-on.md) names the [processing location](../../10-fleet/01-what-an-agent-runs-on.md) the credential resolved to, the grouper's own reads of arriving reports included, so the recipients are enumerable and the provider is a named party on the record rather than a name the factory withholds. What a provider retained is a residue the factory does not reach: recalling anything from one is the owner's agreement with that provider, which is [substrate](../../../what-humans-do.md) the way hosting is. And nobody, the factory included, can list a person's reports, because the channel carries no identity by design: an access request is answered by that fact, and an erasure request needs what every redaction needs, content pointed at.

Three things stay refused here, each for a reason already on the record. A subject key — indexing reports by the people their text mentions — would have the factory identify people in free text, building exactly the join the opaque key refuses to be; whoever the words are about is served by the redaction and not by an index. Screening content before an agent or a screen reads it is a moderation pipeline a one-customer install does not carry: the admission safeguard above is that screen where an owner wants one, an agent takes report text as [content and never instruction](../../01-one-pipeline.md), and the human the Spec floor keeps is the backstop. And a reporter gets no status and no way to contest a grouping, because each needs the reporter handle the channel refuses — something to reach, and to recognise, after the submitting session is gone. Acknowledgment is not among them and is not refused: it is the submit result above, rendered while that session is still open, and folding it in with the two would have given up the cheap one to pay for the expensive ones. The residue is that a misgrouped report is contested by nobody outside, and what repairs it is [decomposition](../03-decomposition/README.md) splitting the group and a recurrence link joining what it missed.

A report that is never grouped is work nobody sees, which is how a store in front of a pipeline fails wherever it is built. So every report is either linked to the intent it was grouped into or counted as ungrouped, and that count is a number on [_Factory_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md). A refused report is the same failure one step earlier and gets the same answer: the report store counts a refusal where it makes one, per service and over the whole channel, and both counts are numbers on Factory beside the ungrouped one, so a channel dropping real reports is a number an owner reads rather than silence at both ends — and a rate authored too tight is visible as a number rather than inferred from work that never arrived. The refused count is the only number on that screen the store keeps as a counter rather than the screen computing it at read time, and it has to be: the record a query would count is the write the rate exists to refuse, and a row per refusal is the unbounded write the bound was placed to prevent. What that costs is that it cannot be recomputed — a lost counter is lost, where the ungrouped count and every other number on the screen are derived again from the records. One report appears in [_Work_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) under its intent and nowhere else: a browsable list of reports would put end-user volume on the screen that is one item per timeline, and make the grouping a human's job again.
