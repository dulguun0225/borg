# Constraints and the design system

Constraints (2) arrive here too and are not intents: they are never decomposed into items and never ship. One record with a reach field rather than two record types — a constraint names what it binds, and the four values it may take are the ones duty 2 sets out in [_What humans do_](../../../what-humans-do.md), where the three widest make a constraint permanent and one intent is the narrowest, a constraint arriving with a single request and binding every item decomposed from it. Intake writes it at arrival whichever [screen](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) an owner supplied it on, because the constraint's own rules — the actor, the chain — are implemented once rather than once per entrance, and a second write withdraws it. A permanent one is withdrawn at [_Factory_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), which lists the constraints in force by reach, so ending one is done from that list rather than by finding the request it arrived with; one whose reach is a single intent is read and withdrawn at [_Work_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), on the intent it arrived with. It is never edited: correcting one is a withdrawal and a second record, so what was in force when a [stage](../../01-one-pipeline.md) was authored is reconstructible from arrival and withdrawal alone.

What the reach decides is what the factory reads when it drafts, and drafting — a stage authoring one of an item's [artifacts](../../01-one-pipeline.md) — is per item: the constraints in force over the factory, over the item's service's project, over the item's [area](../03-decomposition/02-what-an-item-names.md) or any area above it, plus whatever arrived with this intent. The interview reads less than that, and deliberately — the factory's own and the intent's own, and not the project's or the area's, because before decomposition which project the work is in is a guess and an intent's items may not share one.

A constraint's other field is its **kind**, and it decides where a constraint of that kind is checked: three kinds reject at a [gate](../../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md), a fourth holds at dispatch instead, and a fifth writes a factory parameter directly. A constraint stated as a diffable build is decided against the implementation and rejects at [_Implementation_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/README.md). A licence policy is of that kind, not a document: the [build](../../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) names every package it resolved and the licence each declares, so a constraint over what may be depended on is diffed against that set and rejects there. A build whose set is **could not derive**, or whose recorded coverage leaves out what the constraint reaches, has nothing to diff, and the constraint then resolves a human to that gate the way an [unavailable factor](../../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) does, the vector naming what the resolver did not cover, rather than rejecting nothing.

A predicate the factory can decide against an observed run is decided on [_the candidate environment_](../../05-environments/04-what-the-candidate-environment-decides/README.md) beside the [acceptance criteria](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/03-the-six-patterns.md), and rejects at the [Merge to master](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/07-merge-to-master.md) gate on the same terms. What the run had to show is not what it happened to show: the [screen state machines](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/04-the-screen-state-machine.md) in force for the build enumerate the states the predicates are decided over, and one the run did not render leaves them undecided and rejects there too, the reading and its cost stated where the run is decided. A document is decided by nobody, and what enforces it is a [safeguard](../../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) (9) putting a human at a gate. A document-kind constraint may also require [seam 5](../../../deferred.md#security-comes-last) enforced, read at [dispatch](../05-dispatch.md), where the item waits until the [factory-wide settings record](../../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) says it is. The cost is that an owner who supplies a predicate learns of a breach one gate later than one who supplies a build, and everything spent between the two is thrown away.

A licence policy is authored against a **product licence**, a field an owner writes on the [service record](../03-decomposition/README.md) the way they write a [deploy target](../../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md), decomposition never writing it. Whether a resolved licence is compatible with the product licence is refused as a mechanical check, because which licences are compatible with which is legal judgment that differs by jurisdiction and by how the product is distributed, and the design keeps that judgment in the owner's constraint rather than deciding it for them. A package whose entry declares no licence rejects at [_Implementation_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/README.md) as a build-kind constraint the factory supplies rather than an owner authors, the shape the [security predicates](../../07-contracts/06-what-a-consumer-declares.md) already take there, because nothing can be authored against an unknown.

A fourth kind is decided at dispatch rather than at any gate: a predicate over the [processing location](../../10-fleet/01-what-an-agent-runs-on.md) a [fleet entry](../../10-fleet/README.md)'s credential resolves to. A constraint of that kind restricts which entries may take the items its reach covers, and [dispatch](../05-dispatch.md) matches an item against only an entry whose processing location satisfies the predicate. An item with no conforming entry waits at dispatch the way an item whose stage matches no entry already does. The cost is that an owner scoping a regulated project has to supply an entry per stage for it, or the project does not move.

A fifth kind takes effect on arrival rather than rejecting or holding anything: intake, in the same write, calls [Factory](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) to set [the retention floor](../../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) on the [factory-wide settings record](../../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md), rather than leaving anything to be read at drafting. A records-retention law is of this kind, its reach always the factory. The cost is a constraint no drafting stage ever reads that still changes what an owner may ever author.

A law is a constraint whose reach is the factory, and three things about a law the reach and the kind do not carry, so a constraint carries three more fields, each optional and each read by something. A constraint may name the date it binds from: drafting reads it as in force from that date and not from its arrival, and the conformance raise below runs at that date, so a regulation phased in next year is supplied now and reaches shipped services then. It may name a date it is to be reviewed by: past that date, with no withdrawal and no replacement naming it, it is a row in [_Work_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) for whoever holds (2), delivered by mail and chat and never a page, nothing deployed being worse for a constraint nobody re-read. The constraint goes on binding regardless, a review date ending nothing on its own, because a law that lapsed in the owner's diary has not lapsed. And it may bind the factory's own conduct rather than its items: a period, and what starts it.

Most of what a law asks of a customer is conduct with a deadline: a breach reported within so many hours, an erasure performed within so many days. Conduct has no drafting moment for a constraint to be read at, so the constraint states the period and a trigger, and what the trigger reads is a record: an [advisory](03-advisories.md) matching a shipped build, an [incident](../../08-operations/06-incidents.md) on a service in the constraint's reach, or a human marking an intent at Work as meeting the trigger. The human is the only reader of a report's prose the design admits, the reports floor at [Spec](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md) already putting one there. The deadline is a field of the intent, written by intake when the trigger occurs: the trigger's own time plus the period, the time of a report's arrival and not of the mark, because the clock a law starts started then. It is the one field in the factory computed from a calendar, the [page hours](../../08-operations/07-pages.md) being authored and read against one but computing nothing. What a deadline does is two things and no third. It pages whoever holds (2) for that constraint at the moment it is written, which [_Pages_](../../08-operations/07-pages.md) admits as the one condition outside its own: the software is no worse, the customer is, for every hour of the wait. The page is answered when the intent ends or the human marks the obligation met.

And Work shows the deadline on the intent and every item under it, so a human ordering a queue has it to order by. It moves no priority itself, gates nothing, and teaches the [score](../../04-risk-score/README.md) nothing: an obligation is the owner's to meet and the factory's to make visible. What it costs is a calendar read by one field, three fields most constraints leave empty, a page that reaches a human for an obligation the factory cannot itself discharge, and a trigger that is a human's mark for the case that matters most. A breach reported by an end user is a sentence a human reads, and a factory whose grouper read it for that would be the classifier [_Reports_](02-reports.md) refuses.

Drafting reads a constraint forward, and arrival is what reaches back. A permanent constraint in force whose record no intent names, with shipped services inside its reach, is a [detector](README.md)'s condition. A detector reads the constraints in force on a pass of its own, the way the [health monitor](../../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) reads a service's numbers, and calls intake to raise one intent where the condition holds. Each pass overwrites a [last check](../../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) of its own, carrying its interval, so a pass that stopped is a named row on [the home view](../../11-screens/02-three-properties-every-screen-needs.md) rather than silence. The evidence is the constraint record, which keys the raise like every detector's, and the condition stands until the intent exists, so a pass that stopped half-done raises nothing twice and finishes on the next one. The condition is deliberately stricter than the unfinished-intent rule [_Intake_](README.md) states for a repeating detector: an intent that ended is not raised over again, whether it was delivered, [partly delivered](../04-when-an-intents-items-do-not-all-ship.md) or dropped, a second raise being the factory reopening work a human ended. The residue is stated rather than looped on: what a dropped conformance intent left out of conformance stays so until the owner asks again or withdraws the constraint. The constraint is the intent's evidence, so the interview and every stage hold it through that link rather than through the project's list [_The interview_](../02-the-interview.md) does not read.

Decomposition is what enumerates the reach: an item goes to each service inside it that the work changes, and a service already conforming gets none, which is the floor decomposition already sets. It may also find nothing departing at all, this intent alone being able to ask for a state that already holds, and then it yields no items. The finding attaches to the intent, which waits at [_Work_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) until a human ends it as dropped — the end [_The interview_](../02-the-interview.md) gives an intent, deciding nothing was outstanding. What an item can be checked against is what the kind already decides: a build is diffed, a predicate is decided against a run, and a document's items end at a safeguard (9) putting a human at a gate, like the rest of that kind.

Withdrawal ends the condition with the record. A replacement names the record it replaces, and the pair is one write. Where that record's intent has not finished, the replacement's raise attaches its evidence and sends the intent back the way a [rework request](../../03-gates/06-going-back-up.md) naming it does: unrefined again, the interview reopened, decomposition run again against what is now in force and superseding what it replaces. An intent that is [escalated](../../03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) or [re-decomposing](../03-decomposition/README.md) takes only the attachment, an escalation staying a human's to clear and an open Decomposition firing closing first, the send-back landing then. So conformance work in progress is re-aimed at the constraint in force rather than finished against one withdrawn, and never doubled. The narrowest reach needs no raise: a constraint arriving with one intent binds what is decomposed from it, and nothing shipped precedes it.

What the raise costs is work the owner did not ask for item by item. A factory-wide constraint on a mature install decomposes into conformance items across every service that departs from it, each holding an [environment](../../05-environments/02-an-environment-per-candidate.md) like any other item and ordinary in everything after: interviewed, decomposed, gated, counted in [cost per feature](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md). One thing is not ordinary: a conformance item's [tier](../02-the-interview.md) sits above every tier a requester or a detector proposes, so the law it answers to is never queued behind a feature the owner ranked higher. And the raise buys enumeration, not proof. An item of that work whose kind nothing mechanical could decide ships on whatever human its gates held, which is a document, or a predicate over a screen no [state machine](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/04-the-screen-state-machine.md) in force declares. So an intent delivered means the reach was worked through and not that the constraint is met, and a departure that survives is found the way any shipped defect is. Without it a constraint binds forward only, and every service already in production stays out of conformance indefinitely, its criteria unchanged and still passing, with nothing anywhere saying which services are outstanding.

A design system is not a record of its own: it is a constraint whose reach is one project and whose subject is what every screen of the product is built from, and its kind decides everything below. It is the permanent constraint a project with a user interface always has, and it is chosen as much as supplied: the project uses one the factory ships with or one an owner uploads. One the factory ships with is content of the product rather than a record — picking it writes a constraint naming it, the way a [fleet entry](../../10-fleet/README.md) names a credential the factory does not hold, and it moves only when the owner upgrades the factory. Moving is a withdrawal and a second record, never an edit under the standing name, and each mover writes its own pair: an owner uploading over the one in force writes it in the upload, and an upgrade that changed the shipped one is detected by the factory at its first start on the new version, which calls intake to write the pair itself — itself, because an upgrade is applied by whoever hosts the install and opens no screen, and a record standing unchanged while the content it names is replaced is exactly the break in reconstructibility that correcting by a second record exists to prevent. The second record is a permanent arrival like any other, so the raise above runs against it and the services whose screens depart from the new version get their conformance items: the migration set the dependency with the widest fan-out in a product with screens needs, the way [_No single item may break a contract_](../../07-contracts/02-no-single-item-may-break-a-contract.md) makes a contract migration several items. What is uploaded decides what can ever be checked against it, and a design system spans the three kinds rather than taking one. As code — tokens and components with a build — it is diffable, and what the diff decides is bounded the way [_What a diff cannot see_](../../07-contracts/05-what-a-diff-cannot-see.md) bounds a contract's: that a screen is built from the system's components, styled from its tokens, and calls no component against its declared interface. It does not decide that they were used correctly — a dialog nested inside a dialog, a destructive variant on Save, two tokens paired unreadably are all made of the right parts — and the rules for using them, the third thing [_What humans do_](../../../what-humans-do.md) says a design system is, are a document however the rest arrived: decided by nobody, enforced by a safeguard (9) putting a human at the gate, a residue stated rather than promised away. Between the two runs the predicate kind above, and a screen is where it earns its keep: a contrast floor over the colours a render actually resolves, a name on every control, a focus order, a target size are each decidable against a rendered run of the [candidate](../../06-releases/03-what-a-build-is-called-and-when.md) and reject at the [Merge to master](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/07-merge-to-master.md) gate like any predicate. That is also the part of an accessibility regulation a constraint can carry as something a machine decides; the rest of such a constraint is a document with the [compliance officer](../../../what-humans-do.md)'s safeguard, like any rule a machine cannot read. As a document entire — an export, a page of rules — there is no build and no predicate, nothing checks the result, and the safeguard is the whole of the enforcement. Nothing about that is particular to design: no permanent constraint is checked mechanically unless it arrives as something a machine can read, which is why the compliance officer holding (2) over a regulated area holds a safeguard with it.

The predicates a design system supplies are part of its content. One the factory ships with ships them beside its tokens and its components, so picking that system is picking what a run of a candidate is decided against, and a project that picked one is never left with nothing to decide. An owner's upload carries whatever predicates were uploaded with it, and one that carried none has no enforceable half over the rendered result. The tokens and the components are still diffed where they arrived as code, and everything a render would show is left to the safeguard (9) the document kind already needs. Which of the two a project is at is read off the constraint in force, which names what arrived, rather than inferred from a run that never failed.

What the system contains is a field of that constraint record, derived when the record is written and never edited after: its tokens, its components, each component's declared interface, and the states each component may be in. Intake derives it from the system's build in the same write, the way the [artifact store](../../01-one-pipeline.md) derives a [consumer contract](../../07-contracts/06-what-a-consumer-declares.md) from a consumer's build, and where no extractor can read that build the field records that it could not be derived, the reading a consumer contract's own non-derivation already gets. A system arriving as a document has no build and so no field, and the diff over it decides nothing, which is what that kind already costs. It is a field and not a record of its own because a constraint is never edited: the record in force names one system in one state, so what the system contains moves only when the pair that moves the system does.

Three things read that field. The diff has a set to check against, so the provenance of a component, the origin of a value and a call against a declared interface are each a comparison rather than a judgment. What the diff compares is read off the service's build by the same per-toolchain extractor the transition check at [_Implementation_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/README.md) runs on, and it is bounded as that check is. Each of the three is decided over what the extractor could follow; a component reached through a construct it could not follow makes the screen **could not derive**, which resolves a human to the gate on the terms stated there rather than reading as conforming. A [safeguard](../../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) (9) has an element to name, which is a component of the system in force, the way it names a [contract element](../../07-contracts/01-two-versioned-things.md) — so an owner who distrusts one component puts a human in front of the items whose builds use it rather than in front of a whole area. And departure becomes a query: the diff of a service's build against the field, read over the current release of each service in the reach.

That query is what decomposition enumerates the conformance reach with, so a service that authored no [spec version](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md) is enumerated like any other. An [adopted service](../03-decomposition/01-a-service-that-already-exists.md)'s screens are in its build whether or not an item ever declared a state machine over them, and the raise yields an item per service whose build departs, carrying what departed. So a service already conforming getting none is a reading of that service's build, and not of a screen record the factory does not hold. The predicate half is still out of reach there: a screen with no state machine declares no state to render, and what brings one into reach is the conformance item itself, which declares a state machine at Spec like any item with a user interface.

[_Factory_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) reports that query per project as three numbers: how many services in the reach depart, how many values their builds resolve outside the token set, and how many uses of a component depart from its declared interface. They are computed at read time over current releases, like the rest of that screen's numbers, and nothing gates on them. Adoption is what a design system is managed by, and a count is what makes drift visible where no check decides it, the treatment the unwanted-condition share already gets at [_Spec_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/03-the-six-patterns.md). What it costs is a number that moves when a service is adopted rather than when anything got worse, so it is read across a project and over time rather than against one release.

The design system's constraint record is its version. A constraint is never edited, so the record in force names one design system in one state, and a move writes a second record rather than changing the first. The [build](../../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) names that record beside the third-party set it resolved, so a [release](../../06-releases/02-the-release-record.md) names the design system its screens were built from by naming its build. Which design system a shipped screen came from is then a link followed from the release. Arrival and withdrawal already make it reconstructible, which is what a constraint never being edited buys; what the field adds is that the question is answered by following a link, which is what [_Traceability_](../../../what-the-factory-does/02-traceability.md) asks and what a reconstruction is defined against. An uploaded system needs no identifier of its own for this, the record that carried the upload being the identifier, and one the factory ships is read the same way. What it costs is a field on every build in a project with screens, and a project without them writes nothing.

A move removes nothing by itself. One write that did would put every screen in the project out of conformance at once and leave nothing to migrate from, the outcome [_No single item may break a contract_](../../07-contracts/02-no-single-item-may-break-a-contract.md) refuses for a contract and the reason a design-system move is a migration set at all. So the pair is still one write and still not an edit: the replaced record's content stays installed beside the content the record in force names, a screen built against the replaced one goes on building, and removal is a later event. The replaced content is removed when no release still restorable names it — finishing the migration is necessary for that and not sufficient. A service's restorable releases are the range the [declarations in force](../../07-contracts/06-what-a-consumer-declares.md) are computed over, together with every release below it that is still some [rollback's target](../../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md), which a window closing out of order leaves there. The removal test reads that set over every service in the reach, and the build's own field is what makes the reading a query rather than an estimate. Reading the current release alone was refused for the reason [_Deprecation_](../../07-contracts/08-deprecation.md) waits on the same range: a rollback can still ask for a release below the current one, and content that release was built against has to be there when it does. For a design system the factory ships, an install therefore carries the one in force and every superseded one a restorable release still names; for an upload, the owner's earlier uploads are kept on the same condition. What it costs is storage for superseded systems, which a project that never finishes a migration never stops accumulating.

What stops the outstanding set growing back is that nothing merges against a replaced record. Decomposition enumerates the reach once, at the raise, so an item drafted before the move and merging after it would leave a departure no conformance item covers. The [merge queue](../../05-environments/03-the-merge-queue.md)'s re-verification is where that is caught, because a re-verification is a new build naming the record in force when it was performed, as the candidate's own build names the one in force when it was made. Where a build names a design-system record at all and the two differ, the candidate fails its own re-verification and goes back the way the queue already sends one back, the attempt counted at [_Implementation_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/README.md). The test is a comparison of two fields and not a judgment about what a diff touched, which is what keeps it mechanical.

Its reading is the one the queue gives a dependency that moved under a candidate: the [score](../../04-risk-score/README.md) learns as from a [hold](../../03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) and the [per-author prior](../../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) does not move, a design-system move being no defect of the author's, and the rejection names the replaced record where it would name a moved release. That reading wins over whatever else failed in the same re-verification, and what it hides, it hides once: a real defect fails the next attempt's re-verification with nothing moved under it, and teaches then as a reject. What it costs is that a move sends back every unmerged item in a project whose builds name a design system, whether or not that item touches a screen. What it does not catch is a move landing before an item's implementation build: the implementation is then built against the record in force and only the spec was drafted under the replaced one, a residue left to the safeguard (9) the document part of a design system already carries.

The kind decides what may reject on a constraint once it is inside an artifact, and nothing decides whether it reached one. A constraint is read when a stage drafts and is not itself a criterion, so a [spec](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md) authored without it has nothing a check can name as missing: its criteria fit the six patterns, the set is complete on its own terms, and what the owner asked for is simply absent. Half of that is checkable now and half is not. A constraint arriving with one request is part of what that request asks for, so what it asks is stated in the reading the requester confirms, is enumerated as a [requirement](../02-the-interview.md) like the rest of that reading, and Spec rejects a spec version leaving a requirement of its item unanswered. A permanent one binds every intent and belongs to none, so it is enumerated on no intent and nothing names it as missing from a spec.

For that half the two kinds a machine can read do not help either: they reject an implementation or a run, and there is no implementation yet. What catches it is the human confirming the criteria (6) at Spec, holding the request beside what was drafted from it, and in the steady state the [score](../../04-risk-score/README.md) auto-passes that gate and nobody holds anything — an intent grouped from [reports](02-reports.md) excepted, whose source resolves a human to Spec whatever the number is. What a safeguard adds on that one path is not the human but which one: a safeguard [names who its rows route to](../../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md), so the officer who supplied the constraint reads the criteria themselves rather than whoever holds (6). An owner who wants the check has the answer the design gives everywhere: a safeguard (9). The residue is that a safeguard is drawn on an area and stands until it is removed, where a permanent constraint may bind far less of that area's work than the safeguard reaches, so buying the check puts a human in front of every item in the area until someone takes them out.

So a designer's say continues after the gate they decided at, but unevenly. The screen's state machine, confirmed at [_Spec_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/04-the-screen-state-machine.md) (6), is enforced at [_Implementation_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/README.md) as far as that gate's extractor follows the build, a human deciding the rest. What the screen looks like is checked mechanically as far as the kinds reach: provenance and tokens by the diff where the design system came as code, rendered properties by the predicates supplied, and past that by nobody without a safeguard. The cheap kind requires a human at a safeguarded gate for as long as the safeguard lasts; the enforceable ones require a designer to produce code, or predicates a run can decide.
