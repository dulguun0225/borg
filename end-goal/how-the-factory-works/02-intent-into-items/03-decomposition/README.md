# Decomposition

One intent becomes one item or several, and an item names exactly one service. None is the outcome only for the conformance intent [_Constraints and the design system_](../01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) raises, whose decomposition may find nothing departing, attach that finding, and write no item, no gate firing over an empty set. How many go to one service is a floor and not a cap: a service the work changes gets at least one item, and gets more where one item would be larger than decomposition is aiming at, or where a change cannot ship as one. Decomposition is where [_No single item may break a contract_](../../07-contracts/02-no-single-item-may-break-a-contract.md) is applied rather than discovered: "an item that cannot ship by itself was decomposed wrong" is a statement about this stage. A migration with one consumer is four items over two services, three of them on the producer: one adds the new form, a second runs the [brownout](../../07-contracts/08-deprecation.md) of the old one, and a third removes it. The factory raises the last two itself, the way [_Deprecation_](../../07-contracts/08-deprecation.md) sets out, the brownout when the [derived consumer contracts](../../07-contracts/08-deprecation.md) naming the old form are gone and the removal on the brownout's evidence; decomposition does not write them ahead of conditions nobody can schedule. Until decomposition got a gate of its own, nothing ratified what it produced.

A service the work changes may not exist yet, and nothing about decomposition changes: one item creates it, and whatever calls it is a second item declared to wait on the first. Decomposition writes the service record in the same write as that item, because an item names one service and the record has to exist for the item's only outbound link to point at anything. The [project](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) the service is in is a field of that record and part of its identity, written in the same write from [the project intake wrote on the intent](../01-intake/README.md), and no later write moves it. Its other writer is an owner at [_Factory_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), putting the [window limit](../../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) and the [analysis window](../../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md)'s parameters on it (8), and the seam between the two is the field: decomposition writes the service's identity, its project among it, and never a parameter, an owner writes parameters and never creates a service.

A service is one repository with one long-lived branch inside it, and no repository holds two: [master](../../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md), [the release number](../../06-releases/04-the-release-number.md), and the queue's ordering are all per service, and two services sharing a repository would give it two divergent trunks, so a build of either would be made from a tree that contains the other's stale code. The drawback is that a twelve-service project is twelve repositories, and a customer arriving with a monorepo has a split to perform that nothing in the factory does for them. Master itself does not exist until the first release: the [implementation role](../../01-one-pipeline.md) commits the [candidate branch](../../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) with no base, and the queue's first fast-forward is what creates master. Nothing writes the service's place in an environment either — a [deploy record](../../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md) is keyed by service and environment, so a service is in an environment by having been deployed there. The item ships by itself, a service nothing calls yet being exactly the kind of thing that can.

Decomposition refuses to create a service record for a deliverable [_What the factory does not build_](../../../what-the-factory-does/04-what-the-factory-does-not-build.md) refuses, the same five kinds and the same reason: no service record and no item are written for it.

The drawback is that the factory's weakest measurement applies to the release the rest of the work waits on: a first release is [_without a control_](../../03-gates/02-the-rollout-strategy.md) whatever the score would prefer, nothing can close its window [_passed_](../../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md), where no safeguard sets a threshold (9) it is [_unmeasured altogether_](../../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md), and it has no [_rollback target_](../../06-releases/06-rollback.md). Each of those is stated where it arises, and this is where they arrive together. How long they last is stated too: until the second release closes a [window](../../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) on evidence, which a service with organic traffic reaches at its second release and a service with none does not reach at all. Organic traffic means requests real users are already making. What the customer has meanwhile is a factory gating on the prior the product shipped for each model version rather than on a wide one, which [_A model under a name_](../../10-fleet/02-a-model-under-a-name.md) supplies, and no measurement of what it ships. A customer whose software is already running and already has traffic is past that state at the second release, which is why [_A service that already exists_](01-a-service-that-already-exists.md) is the ordinary way a factory starts and not the exception.

What the identity points at is outside the factory: the repository, and a store on each persistent [environment](../../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md). Neither is created by anything the factory runs, and the factory cannot read whether either exists before it reaches for it. So the service record carries one more owner-written field, **provisioned**, written at [_Factory_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) once the repository exists with the factory's credentials holding write access to it and the service's store exists on every persistent environment. The field also records which shape the repository host gave those credentials, the two seam 3 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../../../deferred.md) names, master restricted to the merge queue's, or one credential on a host that cannot restrict by branch. Decomposition never writes it, which keeps the seam above. An item on a service not provisioned is stopped where the factory would first reach for what is missing: [dispatch](../05-dispatch.md) does not dispatch its implementation stage, and both deploy gates hold on it as they hold on a declared dependency that is not current. The stop at dispatch is [a wait no gate could compute](../../03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md), written into the log naming the service, so the row [_Work_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) shows says a repository or a store is missing rather than that a stage failed. A wait with no holder widens to the owner, which is where a [substrate](../../../what-humans-do.md) obligation already goes. What it costs is a field the factory cannot verify, and one more thing an owner writes before the first item on a new service moves. A store the owner said exists and does not is found by the deploy that fails against it, and the failure stands on the deploy record as any failed deploy's does.

A further four fields are the deployer's, not the owner's or decomposition's: a target it reaches, instances the platform can replace, a rollback path, and an [emission](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/02-the-encoding-and-the-emission.md) the [health monitor](../../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) can read. The deployer populates them at adoption and at every first release, and no one authors them the way a parameter is authored. They are distinct from provisioned: provisioned says the repository and the store exist, and these four say what runs on them can be reached, replaced, undone, and read.

The set of non-production values the service's configuration takes on a candidate is also owner-written on the service record, beside what [the candidate's store starts with](../../05-environments/02-an-environment-per-candidate/README.md), and the deployer writes it onto the candidate environment when it [composes one](../../05-environments/02-an-environment-per-candidate/README.md). So is the [mutant cap](../../05-environments/02-an-environment-per-candidate/README.md), authored with a fixed default and bounding how many mutants [Implementation](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/README.md)'s mutation score may spend per item. A fixed default is a value the product ships, among [what a release carries](../../../deferred.md), read wherever the field stands unauthored; this document names none of the numbers, and a reader of a decision taken under one reaches it through the release that shipped it.

Another field on the service record, authored the same way as the mutant cap, is the cap on how many distinct keys a release may hold open per interval for its [failure records](../../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md): a fixed default, and a safeguard (9) may lower it and never raise it.

A third, authored the same way, is the bound above which a criterion is [unreliable](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/02-in-force-and-withdrawal.md): a fixed default, and a safeguard (9) may raise it and never lower it, lowering being what takes a criterion out of the gate.

A fourth, authored the same way, is a bound on how long an [incident](../../08-operations/06-incidents.md)-raised item may be worked before a human is reached: a fixed default, on the service record beside [the objective](../../08-operations/05-service-level-objectives.md) and the hours a service pages within. Passing it [pages](../../08-operations/07-pages.md), stated there on the same ground as the other clocks.

A fifth field, authored the same way, is the [product licence](../01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) the service's software ships under: the owner writes it and decomposition never does, so a licence policy constraint has it to author against.

Shipping by itself is a minimum and not a size — it admits an item that touches forty files and one that changes a string. What sizes an item between them is a target the score supplies, which an owner authors, or safeguards per area (9), with the rest of gate policy (8). It learns from what a bad size does in each direction, both among the numbers [_Factory_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) reports: too coarse shows as attempt-limit escalations (12), with everything spent on the item thrown away, and too fine shows as cost per feature and rework rate, a whole pipeline for a one-line change. That number reaches model spend and nothing the host bills: the environment and fleet a release keeps are hosting costs, which the factory does not read and the spend ceiling does not reach. So the target can be learned finer than the install can afford while Factory shows the number falling. That is a cost and not repaired: the owner's lever is to author the target over what the score learned (8). The minimums do not move with the target: it ships by itself, and no single item may break a contract, which is four items for an interface and five for a store, however small the target.

What the target is not is a definition: an item is not one feature. A feature that changes a contract is several items by the rule above, so a definition of that shape is broken by the case [_A service that already exists_](01-a-service-that-already-exists.md) works through. Factory computes cost per feature as what the intent and its items spent, which already makes a feature an intent. The target's unit is the count of its intent's [requirements](../02-the-interview.md) an item answers — the field decomposition already writes per item. So the target binds a quantity already on the record at the moment decomposition aims, and still there when [the Decomposition gate](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/01-decomposition.md) decides the set. Criteria exist only at [_Spec_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md) and files only at a build, both after the aiming, and spend is what [_a spend ceiling_](../../10-fleet/08-a-spend-ceiling.md) already bounds. What the unit costs is that requirements are not commensurable, since one can dwarf another, so the target stays a coarse bound and the two failure directions above stay the real teacher, and an interview that enumerates requirements coarser or finer moves the measured size while the work itself stands still.

Decomposition also records the order. Where one item cannot be verified until another has shipped — the producing release of a migration — that dependency is declared here, and both deploy gates hold on it: [_Deploy to candidate environment_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/06-deploy-to-candidate-environment.md) until the dependency is live, [_Deploy to production_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/08-deploy-to-production.md) if it has stopped being. Ordering is a property of decomposition, not something discovered at deploy time.

The declared order is checked against the whole relation and not against the set. What waits on what is a graph over every unmerged item, since a re-decomposition repoints edges that leave the set, and the invariant is that the graph is acyclic. Two items each holding a deploy gate on the other is a wait nothing lifts and no instrument shows: a [factory hold over an existing record](../../03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) writes nothing, counts no attempt, and pages nobody. Two guards keep the invariant. A write that would leave a cycle in the graph is refused where the items are kept, which is what reaches a decomposition yielding one item, whose gate never fires; decomposition is the relation's only writer, so a check there sees every edge there is. And a set whose dependencies form a cycle is rejected at [the Decomposition gate](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/01-decomposition.md) mechanically, the way a build that does not compile is at [_Implementation_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/README.md), so a wrong order comes back as feedback rather than as a stage that could not write. The comparison is to the compile and not to the forbidden transition that gate also rejects: a cycle in a relation the factory holds whole is decidable, where a transition in a build is decided only as far as an extractor follows it. What the pair costs is that a refusal can name an edge to an item the set never mentions, so what was decomposed wrong is read off the edge rather than off the set.

The relation the two guards check is the union of what decomposition declares and what a [rollback hold](../../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) imposes, not the declared edges alone. While a rollback hold stands on a service, every unmerged item of that service other than the revert waits on the [revert item](../../06-releases/06-rollback.md), and that wait is an edge of the graph, recomputed at each firing the way the hold is and held by no record. Decomposition reads those edges at every write from what the production deploy gate reads the hold from, and refuses a write that would close a cycle through one, naming the hold in the refusal. A hold cannot close a cycle by itself: its edges all lead into the revert item the same rollback raised, so a cycle through one leaves that item by an edge decomposition declared, and that edge was written after the hold stood. So a check over the union at decomposition's write sees every cycle the union can hold, and no record of the hold is written to make it visible. What it refuses is a revert declaring a dependency on a sibling its own hold holds, and two reverts each declaring a dependency on an item the other's hold holds — each a wait nothing lifts, on a service already running a failed release, that the declared edges alone pass. What it costs is a refusal naming an edge nothing declared, read off the hold rather than off the set, and a check that reads another component's state at every write.

A re-decomposition also repoints what stands: an item left standing that waited on one now superseded waits on the replacements, written by decomposition in the same write that creates them, which is the inverse of the pointer a superseded item already carries. An item left waiting on one replaced by nothing has that wait resolved in the set the gate decides, what waits on what being part of what is approved. Without the repointing, an item waiting on a superseded sibling waits on something that will never be current, forever, and every instrument reads it as an ordinary dependency hold about to lift itself.

What the set answers is checked at the same gate and as mechanically. Decomposition names, per [requirement](../02-the-interview.md) of the intent, the item that answers it; a requirement is one statement of the reading the requester confirmed. A requirement one item answers alone is assigned to it whole. One the split spreads over several items is assigned to none of them: decomposition derives one requirement per item, stating that item's share in the requester's terms, and each is a requirement record of its own, attached to the intent, pointing at the one it was derived from, and written by intake at decomposition's call. The derived requirements are part of the set the gate decides, beside what waits on what. A requirement is answered by the item assigned it, or by the items answering every requirement derived from it, so the whole the requester confirmed is covered exactly where each share is. A set that leaves a requirement named by no item and derived into none is rejected there, and so is one that leaves a derived requirement named by no item.

And so is one with an item that answers no requirement, whole or derived — work nobody asked for, whose criteria at Spec would trace to nothing. Every item decomposition writes therefore answers a requirement whole or carries a derived share of one, the item that creates a service and each step of a migration included. What it costs is a set rejected for a share decomposition failed to derive, which the re-decomposition then derives.

[_Spec_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/03-the-six-patterns.md)'s per-item check reads the derived requirement where there is one, and a re-decomposition supersedes a derived requirement with the item that carried it, the pointer [_The interview_](../02-the-interview.md) gives a superseded reading. What the derivation costs is a statement the requester never confirmed standing where a confirmed one did: a share drawn wrong passes a gate the score auto-passes, every criterion for a split requirement names the share and never the whole, and the [acceptance round](../02-the-interview.md) is where the requester next reads the whole.

A requirement decomposition judges unanswerable is named by no item and marked with a tagged reason, intake writing the mark as it writes the record, which is the treatment [_Spec_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/03-the-six-patterns.md) already gives a sentence fitting no pattern. What the check costs is bounded by where the gate fires: a decomposition yielding one item fires none, every requirement of that intent is that item's by construction, and a requirement decomposition marked unanswerable there is stopped by nothing, the count of them on the intent being what shows it.

Decomposition has a gate of its own, named for the stage and scored like every other, and it fires where the stage yielded more than one item. What is approved is the set: how many items, which service each changes, and what waits on what. A rejection re-decomposes the set rather than sending one item back, because what the gate decides on is the decomposition and not an item, and each round counts against the attempt limit on the intent rather than on an item — the items of a rejected round are superseded and their replacements start at nothing, so a count kept on them would never reach a limit however many rounds were spent. It is the parameter the interview already counts its rounds against, one stage later and on the same record, in a field of its own beside the rounds and never the same field: the two are different stretches of work, an owner answering an escalated interview clears that count alone, and one field would spend an interview's rounds out of decomposition's budget. Intake writes it, called by decomposition, which is the one write decomposition makes to an intent rather than to items. While rounds remain, a human re-decomposes by hand with [Edit in place](../../03-gates/03-actions-at-each-gate.md), the gate still deciding and the set still editable. An intent that exceeds the limit gets **escalated** written on it and appears in Work as an escalation (12) — the second of the two things that write that value, and the two are told apart by which of the intent's counts exceeded the limit rather than by the value itself.

Decomposition also runs again on an intent in progress, which is what three other sections send a problem to: a group [_Reports_](../01-intake/02-reports.md) says the grouper preferred to make, a spec that comes back too large, and a change [_No single item may break a contract_](../../07-contracts/02-no-single-item-may-break-a-contract.md) says cannot ship as one. A [rework request](../../03-gates/06-going-back-up.md) naming decomposition re-decomposes the set, as does a gate's reject naming it, and what either produces is decided at this gate whatever it yields — the one-item condition is the first decomposition's, and a re-decomposition of work in progress always fires, because what it decides is which existing items are superseded rather than how many are proposed. The intent goes to re-decomposing and every unmerged item of it stops while that firing is open, the way they stop while the interview is: without it a sibling merges between the rework request and the verdict, and the set approved would supersede a release. What stops them is the intent's state, which [_The interview_](../02-the-interview.md) makes a precondition on the gate component and the merge queue too and not on [_Dispatch_](../05-dispatch.md) alone: an item already approved at Merge to master merges with no agent on it. The item that sent it back stops with them, its stage unchanged and its attempt booked on the intent, so it resumes where it was if the re-decomposition does not replace it. The items it replaces are superseded and point at their replacements, the record shape a rejected round already uses; what differs is that an item in progress has spent artifacts, a build, and an environment, and superseding it throws all of them away.

A merged item is out of reach, since a rework request may be raised no later than the merge to master, so the re-decomposition leaves shipped work alone and declares the new set's order against it, which is what [_When an intent's items do not all ship_](../04-when-an-intents-items-do-not-all-ship.md) already says about the siblings that got there first. Each round counts against the same re-decomposition count, so a set that keeps being re-decomposed escalates the intent rather than dividing forever, and its items stop there rather than resuming. They appear in Work under it until a human takes one over (12) or drops them, the factory not going on to build a decomposition it has just said it cannot make. Against a decomposition that fires once per intent this costs a gate every builder implements as re-firing and a set that can change under work already approved. What it buys is that a wrong decomposition is repaired on the intent that caused it rather than restarted as a new one with the interview, the grouped reports, and the timeline left behind.

One verdict covers the whole decomposition, however many services it changes. Splitting it up — one verdict per service, each holder deciding only the items in theirs — would rebuild here the wait [_Who owns a contract_](../../07-contracts/07-who-owns-a-contract.md) refused: approvals that must all arrive before anything proceeds, in a graph where a four-service decomposition is ordinary, and the attempt limit turning a long enough wait into escalations (12). A holder who wants to decide at decomposition for their own service gets that with a safeguard (9) on their area, which is what a safeguard does everywhere else. The drawback is that they first see the work one stage down, with the authoring already done — never blind, since the item links back to its intent and Work shows the sibling timelines under the same decision, but reading a spec rather than a proposal.

Decomposition writes the items, and the gate decides over records that already exist. Every other [artifact gate](../../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) does — Spec, [Implementation plan](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/03-implementation-plan.md), [Tasks](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/04-tasks.md), and Implementation each decide over an artifact already written — and the alternative gives the Decomposition decision fields describing the set it proposes, which is what an item already records. It would also split one record's writer in two: a decomposition yielding one item fires no gate, so items would appear at approval where a gate fired and at decomposition where none did.

A rejected set is superseded rather than discarded. Each rejected item points at the items of the re-decomposition that replaced it — a list, since a re-decomposition may replace four items with two — written by decomposition in the same write that creates the replacements, so what was decomposed wrong is readable beside what replaced it rather than only in the decision's feedback. Where a re-decomposition replaced an item with nothing the pointer stays unwritten, and what says why is the item's superseded stage beside the decision that rejected the set. What the pair of choices costs is item records that no gate approved, a superseded set per rejected round, and a service record decomposition wrote for an item the re-decomposition did not replace, which no item in progress then names: every reader of items — [_Work_](../../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) first — has to tell an item in progress from one of those.

The Spec gate cannot do this job — it is per item, so approving one item's spec never ratifies the decomposition that produced four. Without a gate here, a wrong decomposition is discovered several specs later and re-decomposing means abandoning items that were already approved. So the gate fires where there is a set to ratify and nowhere else: a decomposition that yields one item produced none, and putting a gate in front of every intent, most of them single-item, would be a decision about nothing. Scoring it was the cheaper defence and it is the wrong one: a gate the score auto-passes is still a gate every builder implements and every intent passes through. What the condition costs is the decomposition that should have yielded four and yielded one, which now reaches a human one stage down as a spec too large, at [_Spec_](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md) rather than here.
