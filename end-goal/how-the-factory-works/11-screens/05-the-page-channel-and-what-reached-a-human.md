# The page channel, and what reached a human

Factory reports the [page](../08-operations/07-pages.md) channel beside the human's load, from the page events [the log](../../deferred.md) already holds and over the same factory-owned span the rest of its numbers use. Four numbers: pages per service and per named human, the share unacknowledged when they widened, the interval from acknowledged to answered, and how many pages a human fired at [Ops](01-work-ops-factory-people.md) on their own judgment. [_Pages_](../08-operations/07-pages.md) says what limits the narrow channel, that a page nobody needed makes its recipient slower to answer the next one, and that is a behaviour rather than a rule the factory enforces. So the one human-facing channel the design insists must stay rare was also the only one nothing measured, and an owner could not see it degrade until somebody slept through a real page. These are read the way the [auto-approve and undo pair](04-what-the-factory-auto-approved-and-what-was-undone.md) is read: numbers nothing decides on, that an owner argues with. Nothing here fires a page, narrows [the condition](../08-operations/07-pages.md), or changes [the hours a service pages within](../08-operations/07-pages.md), each of which is authored or decided where it is defined.

Beside them the screen reports what reached whom. The human's load says how long the factory stood waiting on a human, which is a [close event](../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md)'s time against its open event's, and that interval contains a delivery nobody has ever looked at. So it is reported split at the first delivery the transport accepted, which the [delivery record](../08-operations/07-pages.md) names: the part before the split is the channel's and the part after it is the human's. A row no delivery was ever accepted for is reported as a fault of the channel and against nobody. The part after the split is split once more, at the first [acknowledgement](../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) where the row is a decision one holder acknowledged: before it the wait was a shared duty's, with the row in front of every holder and taken by none, and after it one human's. That is the distinction the interval could not make before, and it is not a rare one: a wrong address, a chat integration that stopped, or a [People](01-work-ops-factory-people.md) declaration naming somebody who left produces a long wait charged to a human who was never told. The notifier's own [last check](../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) record does not cover it, since it says the component ran and not that this row reached this person.

What it costs is that the split is only as good as what the transport reports back. A transport that accepts a message and never delivers it reads here as delivered, which is the failure this cannot see and the reason the four numbers above are read beside it rather than instead of it. The counts are computed over the one factory-owned span like the rest, so an owner who suspects the channel changed reads one number over both halves of the change.
