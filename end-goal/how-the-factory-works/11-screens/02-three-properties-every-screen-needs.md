# Three properties every screen needs

**Two audiences.** Everything on [the four screens](01-work-ops-factory-people.md) serves whoever holds a duty from [the owner's list](../../what-humans-do.md), however many people that is. Duties 4 and 5 — report a bug, complain — belong to end users, who never open this product. The way in is thin and embedded in the deployed software; what they send is a [_report_](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md), and it appears in Work under the intent it was grouped into rather than as a timeline of its own.

**Designed for silence.** When the factory is working, there is nothing to do and the screens are empty. Empty must not be mistaken for broken: the home view at zero shows a digest of what the factory shipped, decided, and auto-approved while nobody was looking. A digest computed from records the factory writes is empty in both cases, though: a quiet week and a factory that stopped writing produce the same page. So the home view also reads every [last check](../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) record, the record each of the components below overwrites every pass, carrying the interval that pass runs on. It shows a row for each one past the interval it names with a further pass owed, whatever wrote it. A [readiness reading](01-work-ops-factory-people.md) is a third case that reads the same at zero: nothing has stopped, so a fleet not yet complete shows a row there before anything is wrong, and every install carries it until the fleet is.

Every one is read, because they are not one per component: the [health monitor](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) writes one per service; the [deployer](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) one per target of a persistent [environment](../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md), which is production and the ones a customer defines and never a candidate's, and one for [the platform](../05-environments/02-an-environment-per-candidate/README.md); and [_Drift detection_](../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) one per production target. Five are single: the [notifier](../08-operations/07-pages.md)'s, one each for the pass over [the constraints in force](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md), the pass over [the advisory feed](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/03-advisories.md), and the pass over [the deprecation list](../07-contracts/08-deprecation.md), and [dispatch](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md)'s for the pass that argues [a fleet proposal](../10-fleet/07-a-fleet-proposal.md). Every one and not the newest of a class, because that aggregation is the one that hides a partial stop. A health monitor still checking one service and stopped on another reads as current, and the stopped service's release is unmeasured with no rollback coming. That is the state a [page](../08-operations/07-pages.md) fires on, and the aggregate shows it as healthy. So each record is read against its own interval and nothing is aggregated over a class at all, for the reason [_current release_](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md) already refuses to let one target report for the rest: one member of a class may not stand for the class. That rule answers by taking the strictest member, and this one by reading every member on its own.

The row names what wrote it and what it checks, as in "the health monitor has not checked *payments* since 04:10", rather than showing a duration, so nothing on the page asks the reader to know how to read a number. Each record is read against the client's clock and re-read while the view is open, over the subscription [_The screens as software_](03-the-screens-as-software.md) gives the client, so a record nothing overwrites crosses its interval on an open screen without a reload. Where that subscription drops, the rows give way to the disconnected state and no age is shown: a client that went on rendering ages from a store it could no longer reach would read healthier the longer it stood, which is the reading the rows exist to prevent.

The rows are on the view whether or not it is at zero, and the digest is the part that appears only at zero: a stopped [notifier](../08-operations/07-pages.md) is exactly what leaves gates opening and rows arriving, so a signal shown only on an empty page would be withheld in the case it was written for. What that costs: a component whose pass merely ran late puts a named row on the home view until its next pass clears it, and it sits above whatever is genuinely waiting there — chosen over a partial stop that shows nothing at all.

**Push, not poll.** Gates and escalations leave the product by mail or chat. Otherwise the factory's speed is limited by how often a human remembers to check. The same rule holds inside the product: a screen already open learns that a record changed from the subscription [_The screens as software_](03-the-screens-as-software.md) gives the client, never from a reload, so a board is true as long as it is connected and says so when it is not. A [_page_](../08-operations/07-pages.md) is the third channel and the narrow one, for a wait where production is worse until a human ends it.
