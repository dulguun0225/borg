# Work, Ops, Factory, People

Four screens, each answering a different question, and what is on each.

- **Work** — one [item](../01-one-pipeline.md) is one timeline. [Intent](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md), [spec](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md), [plan](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/03-implementation-plan.md), [tasks](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/04-tasks.md), implementation, [rollout](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md), and the [numbered release](../06-releases/04-the-release-number.md) it ends in, in the order they happened, with each [gate](../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) shown inline at the point where it fires. At the [Implementation](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/README.md) gate the implementation is shown as the [build](../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md)'s diff against master, the one place the product renders code, for the reason [_What the factory does not build_](../../what-the-factory-does/04-what-the-factory-does-not-build.md) gives. At every gate the [vector](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) is shown while a human decides and the number beside the verdict once it is written, and the [close event](../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) records when the row was opened, both for the reason [_Where a gate is, and what decides it_](../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) gives. [Decomposition](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/01-decomposition.md) is the one gate above a timeline rather than on it: an intent that became four items shows one decision with four timelines under it, and shows as [partly delivered](../02-intent-into-items/04-when-an-intents-items-do-not-all-ship.md) where those timelines did not all reach a release. Features and bugs are the same kind of item. A project is a grouping of work, not a separate place.

  Board and list views answer "where is it stuck", which includes [the merge queue](../05-environments/03-the-merge-queue.md) and the [windows](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) a service has open: who holds each, what is under watch, and what waits. A thing waits where the windows are all open, an [error budget](../08-operations/05-service-level-objectives.md) is exhausted, a [rollback](../06-releases/06-rollback.md) is holding, a [_fleet entry has no credential to reach_](../10-fleet/05-an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold.md), a credential is at its [_spend ceiling_](../10-fleet/08-a-spend-ceiling.md), no [fleet entry](../10-fleet/README.md) covers the stage the item reached, that stage's role has no [role prompt version in force](../10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md), or a human is holding. Each stop the factory itself causes is a row and not an absence: [dispatch writes one per item and stage](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md), naming its cause and the values it was met on. So the screen shows an item the factory stopped as stopped on a named cause, with a link to where at Factory the record that lifts it is written. That is what keeps a new install with no fleet entry written, and an upgrade that added a stage, from rendering as work a human is thinking about.

  Filtered to what is waiting on a human, it is the home view and has the badge count, because answering the factory is the daily job. What waits on a human is pending gates, [UAT](../../what-humans-do.md) assignments (7), the factory's [interview](../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md) questions (3), and [escalations](../03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) where the factory admits it is stuck (11, 12). The factory's own [holds](../03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) are in it too, where the named cause is a record only a human writes: a fleet entry that does not exist, a role prompt with no version in force, a credential nothing can reach, a ceiling only an owner raises or clears. So are dispatch's two [constraint](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md)-caused stops, what lifts each being an owner's write too. A credential nothing can reach routes by the rule [_An account that runs out is a hold_](../10-fleet/05-an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold.md) sets rather than to the owner by default, to whoever People records as having lent the name the row carries, which is the same shape as a safeguard naming who answers its rows. A [_ceiling_](../10-fleet/08-a-spend-ceiling.md) reached routes to the owner, raising or clearing one being the owner's, so no row of either kind reaches a human who cannot act on it. Those holds reach the badge and fire no [page](../08-operations/07-pages.md): a badge is what an owner is already reading, and what pages is decided where each wait is defined.

  A pending gate is a [_decision_](../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) whose open event has neither a close event nor an abandonment, which is what the [notifier](../08-operations/07-pages.md) delivers and what People answers "who gates what" from. A row an edit superseded, a supersession ended, or an escalation stopped carries an abandonment and is not pending, so nothing here reads the item's stage to tell a live wait from a dead row. Which human a row waits on is the [duty](../../what-humans-do.md) the design names where one is named: confirming [criteria](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md) (6) at Spec, UAT (7) at the [Merge to master](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/07-merge-to-master.md) gate. It is the owner everywhere else, which is where a [page](../08-operations/07-pages.md) already widens when nobody is recorded. A [safeguard](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) (9) adds the human and [names who its rows route to](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md), so the check reaches the person who authored it rather than the owner by default — a compliance officer who safeguards a regulated area answers their own rows. Where the safeguard names nobody the rows go where every unheld row goes, to the owner, and no thirteenth duty is needed for either: routing needs a reachable human and not an obligation the twelve would have to score.

  That filter is not a fifth screen: everything it would contain is attached to an item, and where a thing is stuck is the question Work already answers. What [_Three properties every screen needs_](02-three-properties-every-screen-needs.md) puts on the view besides the filter is a row per [last check](../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) record past the interval it names with a further pass owed, and at zero the digest. That is attached to no item and is not in the filter or in the badge count: the badge counts what waits on a human, and a component whose pass ran late waits on nobody. Those rows are how a stop that pages nothing reaches a screen a human already reads, the same records Ops holds: every stop the [notifier](../08-operations/07-pages.md) cannot report is one of them, its own above all, the channel that would carry that page being the thing that stopped.

  Beside those rows sits a **readiness reading**, also attached to no item: per role, whether a [fleet entry](../10-fleet/README.md) matches it and a [role prompt version in force](../10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md) exists, and the age of the oldest item [dispatch](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) holds unmatched. Unlike a last check row, a role with no matching entry reaches the badge, because that is a wait on a human and not a pass that merely ran late. A first install shows a row waiting there before anything is wrong, and every install carries the reading until [a fleet proposal](../10-fleet/07-a-fleet-proposal.md) leaves every role covered.
- **Ops** — deployed software per [environment](../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md): which release of each service is running, what [contracts](../07-contracts/01-two-versioned-things.md) it publishes, health, [incidents](../08-operations/06-incidents.md), the rollouts in progress and the [control](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) each is measured against, where the [strategy](../03-gates/02-the-rollout-strategy.md) keeps one, and every [last check](../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) record with the age of each, all of them and not only the ones past their interval. This is the screen that answers what is running rather than what needs a human.

  Beside each service it reports what that service is actually being watched for: the [size in force](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) with the finest size its traffic reaches beside it, the confidence and power that reading was taken at, and whether [passed](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) is reachable at all. Two readings never close: the one against the service's own recent history, and an explicit threshold where a safeguard set one. For each, it also reports the size and [average run length](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) in force, with the [crossings](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) that run length admits on a service where nothing changed. That is the whole rate at which that service's releases can be rolled back for no reason, on the screen rather than the confidence alone. A service missing one of [the four fields the deployer populates](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) shows unmeasured beside its releases, the same record [_the analysis window_](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) already carries.

  This is the one place a human sees a service watched for less than the evidence asked for, or watched with no way to reach passed at all, so that every one of its windows runs the whole length of [its cap](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md). Neither of those is a stop, and neither reaches Work or fires a [page](../08-operations/07-pages.md): the release is under watch and nothing waits on a human, which is exactly why weak protection would otherwise be invisible. Which release is running is the [deploy record](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md)'s answer, per target where its completion per [target](../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) differs, so that a rollout stalled on one target is visible before anything pages, and where [_Drift detection_](../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) disagrees with it that is what the screen shows, over the record and not beside it.

  A [mitigation](../../deferred.md) standing on a target is shown here for as long as it stands.

  An acting screen, not watch-only: it can [undo a change after it shipped](../../what-humans-do.md) (10), rolling back while the build it would return to is still running or raising the [revert](../06-releases/06-rollback.md) after; instruct a mitigation and end one already standing; mark a rollback as [not caused by the release](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md); and fire a [_page_](../08-operations/07-pages.md) on a human's own judgment.
- **Factory** — the machine itself. It holds [gate and risk policy](../09-gate-policy/README.md), thresholds, strategy safeguards, environments, the [_agent fleet_](../10-fleet/README.md) and the credential and [effort](../10-fleet/01-what-an-agent-runs-on.md) each entry runs on, the [_role prompts and skills_](../10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md) with the version of each in force, and the [_fleet proposals_](../10-fleet/07-a-fleet-proposal.md) waiting on a disposition. It holds the [constraints](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) in force with the three widest reaches, listed by reach. Those are the permanent things the factory works inside, read here rather than found through the requests they arrived with, supplied here where one arrives on its own, a design system's upload among them, and withdrawn here where a withdrawal is written. Beside them it lists the registries watched for [advisories](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/03-advisories.md), and beside that, the registries a service resolves from that no feed is named for. A constraint that arrived with a single intent is read and withdrawn at Work, on its intent.

  The same screen reports the numbers: throughput, rework rate, gate rejection rate, and cost per feature. It reports how many [_reports_](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) are waiting to be grouped, and how many the way in refused for exceeding a rate, per service and over the whole channel — the way in being the thin report channel the factory ships inside every deployed service. It shows whether the [page](../08-operations/07-pages.md) a report marking harm can cause is turned off. It reports how many items are stopped at [dispatch](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) now and under which of its six causes. So a fleet with no entry for a stage, a role prompt awaiting its gate after an upgrade, an unreachable credential, one at its ceiling, and an item a [constraint](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) stops at dispatch are each a number on the screen that holds the record lifting them, rather than a shortage an owner infers from timelines. It reports the [mutation score](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/03-what-the-encoding-rests-on.md) per service beside the existing counts. It reports criteria withdrawn per service and per author beside [the count in force](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/02-in-force-and-withdrawal.md), and beside them the count [unreliable](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/02-in-force-and-withdrawal.md) per service. It reports the hours a mitigation has stood, per target. It reports, per service, the [product licence](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) an owner authored and the licences its current release resolved.

  It reports how many gates a [resolved factor](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) put a human at, a factor decided for a human whatever the number is rather than weighed into it, grouped by which factor the vector names. Grouping is what makes a fault in whatever supplies one read as a single group rising rather than as the score having changed its mind everywhere at once. A group standing at a steady height is not by itself a fault: an [intent grouped from reports](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) and a [version with no author](../10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md) resolve by design and never stop, and a [toolchain with no extractor](../07-contracts/06-what-a-consumer-declares.md) stands until somebody ships one. So what an owner reads for an outage is a group that rose, and grouping is what keeps the standing ones from burying it.

  It reports what each agent is doing and how well, and the human's own load: how many rows wait on a human now, how long a row a human decided waited, and how long it was open in front of them before the verdict, per duty and per named human. Beside the load it reports what each human approved and how often it was later undone (10) or rolled back, the same pair [the screen reports for the factory](04-what-the-factory-auto-approved-and-what-was-undone.md), per human. Beside that pair it reports how many of a human's approvals were of their own edit, the self-approval count [_Where a gate is, and what decides it_](../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) writes wherever no second holder of the row's duty exists. What limits cost per feature is the [_spend ceiling_](../10-fleet/08-a-spend-ceiling.md) where an owner authored one on the credential, a limit on what the factory spends through it that a [hold](../03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) marks when reached, and the quota on the [provider account](../10-fleet/01-what-an-agent-runs-on.md) behind it otherwise, shown as unbounded where none is authored. Beside it Factory reports the burn rate, units spent so far against the period in force, and when spending at that rate projects to exhaust the ceiling. That is why the number and the entry naming the credential are on one screen.

  The numbers are queries computed at read time over one span the factory owns and nobody authors, the same span [the digest](02-three-properties-every-screen-needs.md) reports over. Throughput is releases per service, gate rejection rate is rejects over firings per gate row, and rework rate is items with more than one [attempt](../03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) at any [stage](../01-one-pipeline.md) over items reaching a release. Cost per feature is what the intent and its items spent on models, and nothing of the substrate. Where the owner has authored a rate for every model a feature ran on, it is one converted total across models and time; where one is missing, the figure is labelled units per intent, per model version, and the screen says the factory does not measure cost.

  Beside cost per feature Factory shows each intent's [outcome](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md): the [acceptance round](../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md)'s verdict, the before-and-after report rate, or nothing where the factory raised the intent, absence being the answer and not a gap.

  It counts neither the [environment per candidate](../05-environments/02-an-environment-per-candidate.md) nor the [full fleet a release keeps](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) while a window could return to it, which is the largest cost a rollout has and lands on hosting the owner pays for outside the factory. Environment-hours per item and instance-hours per release are reported beside it, per service and per item — the recorded facts [_An environment per candidate_](../05-environments/02-an-environment-per-candidate.md) and [_What stands for a rollback_](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/02-what-stands-for-a-rollback.md) keep of that hosting, carrying the converted amount where the service record's hosting rate is in force and units only where none is authored. The [refused report count](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) is one exception and is a counter kept by the report store, the store the way in writes into, because the record a query would count is the write the rate exists to refuse. [The auto-pass rate a threshold was authored at](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) is the other, read from the [policy version](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) that authored it rather than computed over the span, a reference recomputed over a moving span not being one.

  The human's load is two reads of rows the [decision log](../../deferred.md) already holds. The rows waiting are [open events](../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) with neither a close event nor an abandonment that name a duty or a human, which is the reading the decision's own section gives and needs no join against the item's stage. How long a row waited is its close event's time against its open event's, over rows a human closed, grouped by the duty or human the open event names. So no wait record the design refuses elsewhere is added, and the holds the factory sets over a record it never writes stay uncounted. The count of items stopped at dispatch is a third such read, over rows the same log holds: a hold whose second row has not been written, grouped by the cause the first names. The count of gates a resolved factor put a human at is a fourth, and it reads open events rather than holds: those whose [vector](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) records a factor resolved rather than valued, grouped by which factor and computed over the same span as the rest, so the number is a rate the owner can compare against the span before it.

  What it costs: the wait and the deciding are one interval — a row opened on Friday night and decided on Monday reads as the weekend, so the number is how long the factory stood waiting on a human and never how long a human spent. What one factory-owned span costs: an owner who suspects the factory's behaviour changed reads one number computed over both halves of the change. Two of them are the exception and have to be, because nothing else measures a [role prompt](../10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md): gate rejection rate and rework rate are also readable grouped by the role prompt version in force, which the [factor vector](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) on each decision names, so a version that made things worse is visible as two rates side by side. It is the weakest measurement on this screen, a rate needing many items to move where a small install produces few, and [_Reach is what decides one_](../10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/02-reach-is-what-decides-one.md) accepts that: a version is improved on a human's judgment at its gate, and these two rates are the slow confirmation.

  Not stage definition: the stages are the factory's own, and a role prompt and a skill are read here rather than authored, [a gate](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/09-a-role-prompt-or-a-skill.md) being where a version is decided and a [safeguard](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) (9) all an owner writes. That gate's row is decided here as well, and it is the only decision any screen holds that belongs to no item: its row names a version and no item at all, so Work has nowhere to put it, one item being one timeline, and this screen already shows the version in force beside it. The three actions are the row's own, Approve, Reject with feedback and [Edit in place](../03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md), and reading a version here while deciding it somewhere else is what would leave the gate that reaches a human for every role prompt version, on any threshold an owner would author, with nowhere to decide it.
- **People** — humans, the duties each holds, who gates what, who does user acceptance testing (7), who lent a credential [_the fleet_](../10-fleet/README.md) runs on, and who installed [_Drift detection_](../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md), those last two being what routing needs where no duty owns the row. The duties are also what a [_page_](../08-operations/07-pages.md) routes on, there being no rotation to declare beside them. Declared, not enforced: the model routes work today, and is the [seam](../../deferred.md) authentication attaches to later. The one field here the factory does enforce is the [_spend ceiling_](../10-fleet/08-a-spend-ceiling.md) an owner may author beside a lent credential, a limit on what is spent through it that holds work when reached. Beside the credential the declaration also holds a rate an owner may author per model version and effort, which Factory converts into cost per feature and reports there rather than here.

  A People row may also hold no duty and lend no credential: a row added only so a human can read the four screens without gating, approving, or otherwise acting anywhere. Every acting call at Work, Ops, Factory, and People refuses that row rather than each screen checking it on its own, and the row reads all four. Until [seam 5](../../deferred.md#security-comes-last) is enforced its name is claimed the way every other row's is, which the [actor](../../deferred.md#security-comes-last) field already records.

  A row's name is what an actor or a [page event](../08-operations/07-pages.md) resolves to and never what either stores: from the first record each holds an opaque per-person key instead, and this declaration is the one place that key maps to a name, kept outside [the chain](../../deferred.md#security-comes-last) so it can be changed independently of it. Erasing a person deletes the mapping and nothing else: the key stands on every record it was ever written to, so the chain, its links, and its counts are undisturbed, and what those records name is gone. Every screen resolves a name through this mapping rather than holding one, and the mapping is therefore the one record [the erasure list](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) must reach for an identified human, beside a redaction's own targets.
