# The screens as software

The four screens are one client, built and versioned with the factory and upgraded with it. Versioning the client with the factory does not version the tab a human already has open: an upgrade is applied by whoever hosts the install, and a screen loaded before it goes on calling and rendering after it. So every call the client makes carries the factory version it was built from, beside the principal below, and the factory refuses a call whose version is not its own. The client renders that refusal as a required reload and never as a failed action, so a screen open across an upgrade is stopped by it rather than carried through it, and no record is rendered by a client built for a shape it was not written in. What that costs: a screen cannot be upgraded alone, and a human mid-edit at a gate loses the edit to the reload, which is why the refusal is on every call and not on the load alone. An [Edit in place](../03-gates/03-actions-at-each-gate.md) the factory accepted before the upgrade is already a version in the chain and is not lost.

Every place a human can be on them is an address, which is an item, a decision, a service on an environment, or a constraint. So a row the notifier delivers by mail or chat opens at the row and not at the home view, and going back returns to where the human was. Reopening after a refresh shows what the store holds now rather than what the screen held, the screen holding nothing a record does not.

What the store holds now is also what an open screen holds: the client keeps one subscription per address the human is on, carrying every change to the records that address renders, so nothing on a screen is true only as of its load. That is what makes [_push, not poll_](02-three-properties-every-screen-needs.md) hold inside the product as well as out of it: a gate row a sibling holder closed, a rollout whose rollback fired, and a hold dispatch lifted change on the screen before a human acts on them. A subscription that drops puts the screen in a disconnected state: what it held stays readable and is marked stale, every action on it is refused until the subscription is re-established, and re-establishing it re-reads the address whole rather than resuming from what the screen held. What that costs: every open client holds a subscription the store serves, and a disconnected screen refuses an action a human could have taken on a store that was fine and a network that was not.

A verdict is written only against the open event the row was rendered from: on submit the client re-reads that open event, and where a close event or an abandonment has reached it since the row was drawn, the screen shows that state and sends nothing. So the refusal [the log holds for a second close](../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) is met at the screen, before a human has written into a row already decided or already ended.

Who a human is on them is what they declare at [_People_](01-work-ops-factory-people.md). The declaration is carried on every call as the principal that seam 5 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../../deferred.md) puts there, and nothing enforces it until that seam is built. The client is where authentication attaches then, once, and no screen holds a session the store does not.

The screens are held to what the factory holds a product's screens to. The [design system](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) the product ships with is the one the screens are built from, and each of the four declares a [state machine](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/04-the-screen-state-machine.md) the way every screen the factory builds does, held in the client's own build rather than in a spec version the factory never writes for itself. Empty, loading and failed are among its states, as _Spec_ requires of every screen, and disconnected is a fourth the subscription above adds; each of the four screens declares all of them, the client being exempt from nothing it holds a product to. The predicates [a run on a candidate environment](../05-environments/04-what-the-candidate-environment-decides/01-the-third-outcome.md) can decide are decided over each of those declared states: a contrast floor, a name on every control, a focus order, a target size. That is what makes an accessibility regulation reachable on the factory's own screens and not only on what it builds, and what it rests on is the enumeration rather than whichever states somebody happened to open.

Every time shown is a record's own, written in UTC where the record was written, and rendered in the viewer's locale and zone by the client and by nothing else; the text is in one language until an owner supplies another, translation being content the product ships and not a record.

The write direction has one rule too. A calendar value a human authors, an hour of day or a date, is stored with the time zone it was authored in, named as the IANA time zone database names one and sent by the client beside the value, and every reader of the value computes in that zone and in no other. Five fields are such values: the [hours a service pages within](../08-operations/07-pages.md), the date a [constraint](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) binds from, its review date, the zone a constraint's deadline is counted in, and the date a [spend ceiling](../10-fleet/08-a-spend-ceiling.md)'s period starts from. What is computed from one is an instant, written in UTC like every other time and rendered in the viewer's zone like every other time, so two holders in two zones read one window and one deadline. What it costs is a zone on every such field, and a zone an author chose wrongly, which nothing observes.
