# What an agent runs on

An agent reaches its model through a **provider account**, and the factory stores a reference to that account's credential rather than the credential itself — seam 3 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../../deferred.md), which the fleet is the first thing to use. The [resolver](../../deferred.md) is a file on disk today. A fleet entry has seven fields: a model, an effort, a role, a scope, the name of a credential, the **processing location** the credential resolves to, and the classes of material the entry may be handed. The processing location is the provider and the region, written by the owner beside the credential, so the provider is now a named party wherever an entry names one. The model names its version, because the [_per-author prior_](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) is kept per version and a version that behaved differently under one name would average two authors into one prior. The operations an agent may perform belong to the [role](../01-one-pipeline.md), not the entry: an owner may narrow them on the entry and never widen them.

**Effort** is how long the model works before it answers. Some providers offer it as a setting and some do not, which is why naming it names no provider: it is a dial and not a name. The factory does not check that the credential's provider offers what the entry asks for, the way it does not check a quota — it discovers both by reaching for them, and an entry asking for an effort nobody offers fails at the same place an exhausted account does. What it costs: the prior cannot see it — two entries on one model at two efforts share one prior, which [_Paid for is not authored by_](04-paid-for-is-not-authored-by.md) settles and states the cost of.

The account itself is not a record. A record would need fields the factory cannot populate — quota and balance are facts at the provider, whose name the factory does not record — and a quota field would have [_A spend ceiling_](08-a-spend-ceiling.md) compared against a number the factory never reads, where it compares against what the factory recorded itself. The credential name is the whole of the handle. What that costs: two entries whose credentials resolve to one account share a quota the factory cannot see — they are exhausted at the same moment and recorded as two unrelated waits, and only the owner knows they were one account.

An owner writes the entry, at [Factory](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), and no component in [the pipeline](../01-one-pipeline.md) writes back to it: the factory reads the [substrate](../../what-humans-do.md) and never writes it. Four of the seven fields decide that: a model somebody is willing to run, the effort they are willing to have it run at, a credential somebody lends, and the processing location that credential resolves to. The other three are chosen among values the factory already defines. The roles are the factory's own [stages](../01-one-pipeline.md), plus the two put on an [intent](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) before any item exists, [the grouper](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) before any intent does, and the role that argues [a fleet proposal](07-a-fleet-proposal.md). None of them is a deploy: the [deployer](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) performs every deploy and no entry is ever put on one. The scope is drawn on the same lines a [safeguard](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) (9) is drawn on; an entry for the role that argues a fleet proposal carries none, no scope narrowing what argues over the whole fleet. The classes of material are narrowed from what [_One pipeline_](../01-one-pipeline.md) hands an agent: the intent's statement, reports, an owner's answer, the repository, run output, constraints, and an incident's copied [failure records](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md). A factory that wrote its own entries would be choosing whose lent account pays for the next stage and how long it runs, which is the authority [_A spend ceiling_](08-a-spend-ceiling.md) keeps with the owner: an owner authors what may be spent through a credential, and the factory never does. What it may do instead is argue: [_A fleet proposal_](07-a-fleet-proposal.md) names a fleet and no credential, and the write stays where it is.

[Context assembly](../01-one-pipeline.md) reads the classes of material at every dispatch: a class the entry does not name is withheld before the selection rule selects anything, and the [input manifest](../01-one-pipeline.md) records each withheld source as excluded with the entry as the reason, beside what the rule excluded. So a run handed everything and a run an owner narrowed differ on a record, and the classes are the other half of the control the processing location is: one binds where an item is processed, the other what is sent there. What it costs is an agent working without material its stage would have handed it, and a manifest that lands a human at the gate the way any exclusion does.

[Dispatch](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) honours both halves of a scope, the item's [area chain](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/02-what-an-item-names.md) and its service's [project](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md); an agent checking its own scope would be the agent deciding where it may go. What the entry costs is that the fleet does not grow itself: an item whose stage and [area](../../what-humans-do.md) match no entry is stuck at dispatch. It does not wait for a proposal to say so: [dispatch writes the hold](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md), naming the stage and the scope no entry covered, so the item shows in [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) stopped on that cause rather than as a [timeline](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) that does not advance, and Factory counts the items stopped that way. What the entry costs is therefore the owner's write, and never the diagnosis before it.

Every run of an agent writes an **agent run record**, the counterpart to the [input manifest](../01-one-pipeline.md): where the manifest names what was withheld before the agent started, the run record names what was sent and where. One is written per run of any agent: a stage, a round of [the interview](../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md), [the grouper](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md), an [evaluation-set run](02-a-model-under-a-name.md), or a [decomposition](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md). The component that performed the run writes it. It carries the credential name, the units the provider returned, the period they fall in, the converted amount where the owner had authored a rate for that model version and effort on the [_People_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) declaration, the sources the agent was handed, and the processing location the credential resolved to. What that costs: a rate corrected later does not reprice what the record already wrote.

The account may be an organisation's or a person's own, and the factory takes either, because a reference resolves the same way whichever it is. The early factory is where that matters. An owner installing one before it has proved anything has a personal subscription and no organisation agreement to spend against, and a design that made them wait for procurement in order to watch the factory run once would impose the proven case's requirements on the unproven one. So a personal account attaches through no second mechanism: same field, same resolver, same record.

What differs is downstream of the account and not in the factory at all — its quota, who may revoke it, and whose money it spends. Three things follow, and each is a drawback of the personal account rather than a feature of it: whose name ends up on the work, what an exhausted account does, and what happens when one is taken away.
