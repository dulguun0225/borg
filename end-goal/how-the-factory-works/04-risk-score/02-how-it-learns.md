# How it learns

The score is learned, not fixed. Every bad decision feeds back and refines it: an [auto-passed](../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) change that a human undoes (10), a low-risk change its analysis window rolled back, a gate the factory would have passed but a human rejected. Outcome feedback is the sharpest signal but not the only one — any source that improves the score is admissible, and the input set stays open.

A supplied value moves on both ends of its own row, and [gate policy](../09-gate-policy/01-what-is-in-it.md)'s table is what says where the second end is: every row there states what goes wrong at each end, and both are evidence. One end is something going wrong — a rollback, a human's undo, a [crossing](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) of the boundary the [health monitor](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) could have seen and did not, an [item](../01-one-pipeline.md) the [attempt limit](../03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) gave up on. Which parameter a rollback or an undo moves is a fact of the record rather than a judgment about the regression: one on a release whose window closed [passed](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) is evidence about the power, the exit that rules a regression out having cleared one it should have caught, and one on a release whose window timed out is evidence about the size, nothing having been ruled out at all. The other is the parameter costing more than it returns, which is observable for most of them: attempts nothing has ever needed, a [cap](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) far longer than any window of that service takes to resolve, a [window's size](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) finer than its traffic can rule anything out at, a [power](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) whose windows run to the cap on volume a lower one would have closed passed within it. That last is the power's own observable and not the size's, which matters because the two are read off the same windows: a service whose windows all run to the cap is telling the score either that its size is finer than its traffic supports or that its power is higher than its traffic supports, and only the second is what the size in force being coarsened already answers. So the two are read apart — the size's own end is the finest size the traffic reaches, which the window computes and reports, and the power's is a volume a lower rate would have closed within — and reading one event as both ends at once would move two supplied values against a single piece of evidence. Reading only the first end would make every value move one way and stay there, and a value that only tightens ends up costing what it was set to protect against — the clearest case being an [analysis window](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) so fine it never resolves, watching every release for the whole cap and catching nothing.

Calibration also reads each factor's distribution over the decisions that named it, against that factor's own history. A factor whose distribution has moved is measuring something other than what the formula was calibrated on: an agent that learned what the factor rewards, an area whose shape changed, a fault in what supplies it. Outcomes alone discover that slowly, one release at a time against hundreds of decisions already taken. A factor found drifted takes the treatment [an unavailable factor](01-factors-at-least.md) takes, resolved and not valued, a human deciding whatever the formula returns and the vector naming it, until a recalibration is in force at that gate. A recalibration writes a [score version](01-factors-at-least.md) differing in the weights, and _Factors, at least_ says where such a version is in force at once and where it waits on an owner. What it costs is what that treatment always costs: a drifted factor puts humans at gates across the factory at once, and what tells that apart from the score changing its mind is the count [_Factory_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) reports per resolved factor, which this one enters like any other.

The [per-author prior](01-factors-at-least.md) is exempt from that reading and gets one of its own, because its distribution moving is what it working looks like: it narrows as outcomes arrive, so read against its own history every earned narrowing is a drift, and the treatment would put humans at gates factory-wide for the score having learned something. What replaces it is the reading a learned value can fail — the prior each held-out decision was taken on, against what that release's window then closed. A prior that no longer separates the held-out releases whose windows failed from the ones whose windows passed is drifted whatever its distribution did, and takes the same treatment until it is recalibrated. It is read on the held-out sample and nowhere else, for the reason the threshold rises on nothing else: outside the sample the prior itself decided which changes a human read, so the outcomes there are the ones its own decisions selected. A prior standing drifted stops the sample from selecting on that author at all, the resolution being one the sample may never pass, so no new held-out evidence about that prior arrives while it stands: what recalibration reads is the held-out decisions already recorded, and recalibration is the only exit. That is stated rather than left to be found, because the check and the evidence for it come from the same place. What it costs: two directions, both toward the gate. A service too quiet to close a window on evidence produces no held-out outcome either, so the one factor the score itself moves goes unchecked exactly where the width rule [_Factors, at least_](01-factors-at-least.md) states is already holding the prior wide — no check where there is no evidence, and nothing standing in for one. And an author whose prior is found drifted is read at the gate until somebody recalibrates, with the sample no longer able to produce the evidence that would clear them.

Two of the window's parameters have no second end here and are stated as such rather than left to be discovered. The [power](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) is not a third, which is worth saying because it is the confidence's opposite number and the reader will expect it to behave like one. What shows a power too high is not a counterfactual: a window running to the cap on volume a lower one would have closed passed within it, read off that service's own windows. No outcome says [the confidence a comparison must reach](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) was too high. What would show it is a rollback that turned out unnecessary, and the one thing that establishes a failed release was fine is a human [marking the rollback as not caused by the release](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md). That says the comparison was confounded, nothing about how confident it should have been, so it excludes the rollback from what learns and teaches the confidence nothing. And the other end of the [item-size target](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) is cost per feature and rework rate, both of which are [_Factory_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md)'s to report, cost per feature converted to one number only where the owner authored rates. Beside them the target reads [environment-hours](../05-environments/02-an-environment-per-candidate.md) per item and [instance-hours](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/02-what-stands-for-a-rollback.md) per release as they arrive, figures the design already records rather than values it would have to estimate. Both are allowed to move one way because neither compounds: the traffic a comparison needs grows as the log of the confidence, where it grows as the inverse square of the size. So an owner may have to author over one of them, which [authoring being an override](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) is exactly the case for.

Cost per feature read here is the same figure [_Factory_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) shows, and it moves only on intents that reached an [outcome](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) at close. Where none has, the item-size target's cost end holds still rather than moving on a number nothing yet grounds.

The [risk threshold](../09-gate-policy/01-what-is-in-it.md) is a third exception and not for want of an observable. Its own row states the loose end plainly — gate more and the human work the factory exists to remove comes back — and how often a row puts a human at it is a count, needing no outcome and no counterfactual. What it is not is evidence about safety, and the difference from the two above is the difference between protection gone worthless and protection gone expensive: a window too fine to resolve rules nothing out, so loosening it gives up nothing, where a human at a gate really does read the change and really does stop some of them. Loosening the threshold on its cost alone would be the score deciding that human review is not worth its price, which is a judgment about what the factory is for rather than a reading of what happened — and the design already answers a cost complaint the other way, by having the owner author the value (8). So the threshold moves down on outcomes and up on one thing only, which is the next paragraph.

Scoring on the author reinforces itself if left alone: a distrusted author is gated more, gated work draws more scrutiny, more scrutiny finds more faults, and distrust is confirmed. The factory holds out a random sample — auto-passing what it would have gated, under the longest analysis window there is — to keep unbiased signal on the authors and areas it has stopped trusting. How much it holds out is the **held-out sample rate**, authored with the rest of [gate policy](../09-gate-policy/01-what-is-in-it.md) (8) rather than chosen by the score, for the reason every other bound there exists: the rate decides how often a change the score wanted gated reaches production with nobody at the gate, and a mechanism that sets its own rate of doing so is bounded by nothing. Where an owner authors none the score supplies it, the same division the [window's size](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) uses, and a [safeguard](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) (9) is a ceiling over it — sampling less is a human kept at a gate.

A held-out window that timed out is not evidence for raising a threshold. The sample exists to produce an outcome, and the exit that reports no outcome reports none here either: counting it as good would put back exactly what the sample was built to remove — an outcome recorded only for what the factory let through, this time as silence read as success. What that costs is the direction it leaves: a quiet service produces held-out releases that teach nothing, so on a service too quiet to close a window on evidence the threshold can fall and cannot rise — the ratchet _How it learns_ already names in the other parameters, and the reason an owner may have to author over it. The [per-author prior](01-factors-at-least.md) reads the same silence the same way, and that is what keeps the ratchet to one thing: a prior that narrowed on unresolved windows would fall underneath the threshold at the same time and from the same quiet, so more changes would clear a fixed authored threshold while the threshold itself was the only thing an owner was watching. Two mechanisms drifting toward auto-pass on the same absent evidence is what a held-out sample cannot correct, because a service producing no outcomes produces none for the sample either.

The sample is also the only evidence there is for raising a threshold, which is more than the paragraph above asks of it. A change that was gated and turned out well is consistent with three things at once: it was never risky, the human at the gate caught what would have made it risky, or its author was more careful for knowing a human would read it — and no record tells those apart, so none of them is evidence the gate was unnecessary. What does tell them apart is a change the score wanted gated and did not gate, and the sample is the only thing that produces one. So a factory whose sample never selects has a threshold that can fall and cannot rise: it tightens on every outcome it gets wrong and never loosens on one it gets right, the same shape as a [window](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) whose size only ever narrows. That is worth saying because the sample reads like a fairness mechanism for authors the factory has stopped trusting, and it is also the only reason half the calibration is possible at all.

The sample is the score's own to hold out of, and it reaches nothing an owner safeguarded. A safeguard (9) can only add, and a gate held always-on is a human added; a sample that could pass one would be the single mechanism in the design that removes a human from a gate — exactly what a safeguard exists to prevent. So what the sample may auto-pass is what the score itself would have gated on a vector that resolved nothing — never the human [a resolved factor](01-factors-at-least.md) put there, whether the score could not compute that factor or computed a value the design resolves on, the score being in no position to auto-pass a gate its own number never decided. The drawback: the areas an owner distrusts most are the ones it learns least about.

The selection is recorded on the decisions rather than as a set of its own, in two fields that must not be one. **Held out** says the score selected this [item](../01-one-pipeline.md), written on every decision on it from the selection onward, so an item selected once auto-passes every gate the score itself would have gated — never one [a resolved factor](01-factors-at-least.md) put a human at — and reaches production with a human removed at each of them. **Why it auto-passed** says, at this firing, whether the auto-pass came from the number being under the threshold or from the selection, and reads threshold at a gate the score would have passed anyway. The health monitor runs the window to the [cap](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) on the selection and not on the provenance, because what the sample is evidence about is a [release](../06-releases/02-the-release-record.md) reaching production with a human removed somewhere above it. A held-out release also takes a strategy that keeps a [control](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) wherever there is a build to start one from, whatever the vector's impact half would have picked: the sample exists to produce outcome evidence, and a deploy [without a control](../03-gates/02-the-rollout-strategy.md) leaves only [the weak fallback](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md). What that costs: a second set of instances for a window that runs to the longest [exit](../08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) there is, and a sample measured better than the population it represents — so its evidence supports that a comparison was available, not that an unsampled release on the same author would have shown the same. A service's first release has no control whatever the score prefers, so the sample cannot usefully select one.

One reading is over the number rather than over a factor, and the sample is the only place it can be computed: the share of held-out releases whose windows failed within each band of the number, published per service and factory-wide beside the realized auto-pass rate at the [threshold](../09-gate-policy/01-what-is-in-it.md) in force, each band carrying the count of resolved held-out windows behind it. Off the sample the gate the score asked for is the reason there is no outcome to count, which is what makes the bands incomparable there. What it answers is the question nothing else in this section asks: whether the number ranks anything at all. Factors argued for, a formula published, and every parameter calibrated are together consistent with a number no better than a constant, and bands whose failure shares do not rise with the number say exactly that — a finding that the formula is worth nothing rather than mis-weighted, and one no reading of the inputs produces. Without it an install can run for a year on such a formula, every decision recorded as taken under a [score version](01-factors-at-least.md) that ranked nothing. It is also the reference the [risk threshold](../09-gate-policy/01-what-is-in-it.md) is authored against, [_Factors, at least_](01-factors-at-least.md) being where what the pair of rates distinguishes is stated. What it costs: on a small install the bands hold few releases each, so one failure moves a share and the reading is weakest where the sample is thinnest, which the count beside each band states rather than hides.
