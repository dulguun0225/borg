# What is not in it

Three things are next to gate policy and are not it. **The score.** An owner authors what the number is compared against, never how it is computed — the formula is published so a human can disagree with it, and the [vector](../04-risk-score/README.md) behind it is learned from outcomes. **The stages.** A gate is where policy applies; what is between the gates is the factory's own — including [what an agent is told](../10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md) there, which the factory writes, versions, and puts through a gate of its own, and which an owner reaches only by adding a safeguard at that gate (9). **The form a criterion takes.** The [six patterns](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/03-the-six-patterns.md) are a closed set and the factory owns the form, which is what lets it end the interview — an owner confirms criteria (6) and does not author the shape one may have.

A [_strategy_](../03-gates/02-the-rollout-strategy.md) default is a field on production's environment record beside gate policy. Twelve more are fields on the service record, beside the window limit and the analysis window's parameters. Five of them are the explicit health threshold, the [service level objective](../08-operations/05-service-level-objectives.md), the [fraction of its instances a release keeps](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) while a rollback could return to it, the service's **maximum concurrent kept fleets**, bounding how many such fleets it may hold at once, and the [size and average run length](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) of the reading against a service's own recent history. Three more are the [hours within which the service pages](../08-operations/07-pages.md), the rate a [rollback proof test](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) runs at, and the periods of a [change freeze](04-stopping-the-factory.md). The last four are the [backlog cap](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md), the [search budget](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md), the [bake volume](../03-gates/02-the-rollout-strategy.md), and the [mutation floor](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/07-merge-to-master.md).

Each is supplied where nothing is authored, and a safeguard (9) moves each one way only: the bake volume and the mutation floor it may raise and never lower, the rest it may lower and never raise. A safeguard on the strategy default or the threshold is the owner's duty (9). Six are authored outright instead, with nothing supplied: the objective, the fraction, the maximum concurrent kept fleets, the hours, the proof test rate, and the freeze. The first five leave a safeguard nothing to constrain, and a safeguard (9) on the sixth may add a period or lengthen one and may never shorten one. The fraction's default is all of them, fixed rather than supplied, because the score does not model capacity. The hours' default is every hour, because nothing the factory observes says which service may wait for morning, and no proof test runs at all where an owner authors no rate. A [_spend ceiling_](../10-fleet/08-a-spend-ceiling.md) is a limit on what the factory spends through one credential, reaching which is a [hold](../03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md). It is a field of the [_People_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) declaration that records who lent the credential, and is authored outright for the same reason: its evidence is cost, which the score does not learn from, so nothing supplies a value and there is nothing for a safeguard to constrain. A [maximum concurrent candidate environments](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/06-deploy-to-candidate-environment.md), a field of the factory-wide settings record, is authored outright on the same ground. A service at its kept-fleets cap stops deploying rather than losing a recovery a window could still call for, and [_Ops_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) shows the stop. None of these is authored here — each is authored and is not among the eleven, like retention below.

An area's [hazard severity](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/03-hazard-severity.md) is authored outright as well, on the area record beside the item-size target. It is authored for the reason the objective is: nothing the factory observes says what harm the software can do. No safeguard constrains it either, there being no supplied value to clamp, and the [area chain](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/02-what-an-item-names.md) already does what one would, the value in force being the highest named anywhere on that chain.

The [mutant cap](../05-environments/02-an-environment-per-candidate.md) is authored the same way: a fixed default on the service record, leaving a safeguard nothing to constrain. So is the cap on a release's distinct [failure record](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) keys, except that a safeguard (9) may lower it and never raise it. So is the bound above which a criterion is [unreliable](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/02-in-force-and-withdrawal.md), except that a safeguard (9) may raise it and never lower it, lowering being what takes a criterion out of the gate.

**Retention is authored and is not one of the eleven.** An owner may set how long the [_report store_](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) keeps a report and how long the decision log is kept, both fields of the factory-wide settings record. A third field of that record is the **backup retention**, how far back a backup may reach, authored outright with nothing supplied: no outcome teaches how far back a restore should reach, so there is nothing for the score to supply. Its one reader is the [erasure list](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md)'s retirement, a row going once no backup within it could put back what the row names. Where they author neither of the first two, both are kept for the life of the install. Cutting the log reaches no value in force: [what an owner authored](02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) is the field of the record its scope names, and the policy version only records it for the trail, so a retention setting shortens the trail and never widens what the factory may do unattended. The report channel's two rates sit beside them, authored the same way and bounding what arrives rather than what is kept; where an owner authors neither rate, arrival is unbounded, because no outcome teaches a rate and so there is nothing for the score to supply. How long the numbers [the health monitor](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) reads are kept is authored nowhere: that store is outside [the graph](../../what-the-factory-does/01-tight-integration.md), and its floor is a requirement on the host that section states. The service record carries a retention field of its own: how long a [schema-change snapshot](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/01-a-schema-change.md) is kept, authored outright with nothing supplied. A safeguard (9) may lengthen it and never shorten it. What it retains is a copy of production data the factory made, and an owner's erasure obligations reach it.

Shortening decision-log retention is decided rather than written. It takes a human at a gate row of its own, routed to a human other than the one who authored the shorter value, the treatment [_A safeguard's withdrawal_](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/10-a-safeguards-withdrawal.md) already gets, for the same reason: removing a protection is decided and not merely written, and here the protection is the evidence a shorter value destroys. The shorter value is not in force until the row approves it. The row names each author whose [per-author prior](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) stands drifted and whose held-out decisions the cut would remove, a prior [_How it learns_](../04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md) restarts when they go. Lengthening is not gated: a longer value is in force on write, adding protection the way any authored value already may. Neither an authored value nor a safeguard may take decision-log retention under [the retention floor](02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md), which a records-retention constraint may also write.

Truncating the log means cutting it back to a checkpoint, and the [chain](../../deferred.md) restarts there. The truncation appends a chained row of its own, naming who authored the retention value, the value it enforced, the boundary it cut to, and the policy version and score version in force at the cut. So what was removed and under what authority stays readable, and a decision after the cut naming a version before it is still read against what it was decided under. But nothing before the checkpoint is evidence any more, and the score loses its oldest outcomes, the ones a narrow [per-author prior](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) was built from — so an owner truncating for storage widens the priors of the authors the factory knows best, and nothing on the screen says so. The checkpoint is not a field: it is the oldest record the log still holds, and storing it beside the log would be one fact in two places able to disagree. Everything else in [the graph](../../what-the-factory-does/01-tight-integration.md) is kept, because deleting any of it breaks a link a record already points at — which is what one graph exchanges for the customer's own disk. Kept means the records: what is removable inside a kept record is its text, by [a redaction](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md), which destroys words and never a link.

A **legal hold** suspends this. It is a record with one writer, [Factory](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), the same writer a [safeguard](02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) and a [halt](04-stopping-the-factory.md) already have, naming a subject and a reason. While it stands, truncation and [report expiry](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) are refused wherever it reaches. It ends only at a gate row of its own, held by a human always and routed away from the human who wrote it, the treatment [_A safeguard's withdrawal_](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/10-a-safeguards-withdrawal.md) already gets, for the same reason: ending a hold removes a protection and is decided rather than merely written. A [redaction](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) inside a hold's reach is refused while the hold stands, and the refusal is recorded. That conflict, a hold standing against an erasure the owner owes, is counsel's to resolve and not this document's.
