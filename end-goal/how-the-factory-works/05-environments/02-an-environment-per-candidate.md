# An environment per candidate

Every item in progress gets an environment of its own, created from master plus that item's candidate and torn down when the item merges, is dropped, or is superseded by a [re-decomposition](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md). It is the item's and not the candidate's, so a rebuild composes no second one: the three teardown events are the item's, as [_Records, and one long-lived branch_](01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) already has it, and one environment per candidate is what the arrangement looks like at any moment rather than what is counted. Nothing is shared, so nothing queues behind anything: a candidate being repaired delays nobody, and a candidate waiting on a human delays only itself. Nothing shares because nothing else is candidate-fed — the environments a customer defines receive master's releases alone, so no persistent slot exists for candidates to take turns on.

The environment is composed for the candidate running in it — the [_current releases_](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md) of its dependencies, what is running rather than what is newest, never another service's candidate, and wherever those dependencies are, a service in another project included. Which services those are, [_Which producer a consumer reaches_](../07-contracts/11-which-producer-a-consumer-reaches.md) derives. That is what makes a twelve-service project testing twelve items at once safe, and it is only safe because no item may break a contract. What it was composed from is a field the deployer writes at creation and rewrites when the environment is recomposed at a re-verification, naming each dependency and the release of it that was current then. No deploy record is written for a dependency put in place that way, a deploy record being what a gate decided and there being one gate here, over the item's own build. The cost is that a field the deployer writes about its own work can disagree with what is actually on that environment, and nothing checks it — the drift detector reads production targets only.

Its [store](../07-contracts/09-the-store-is-a-contract-too.md) is created with it, and what that store holds is stated. The deployer applies the service's schema to it the way a production deploy would: every change master carries plus the item's own, the same step and the same actor [the deploy record names](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md). So an item's schema change runs first where a failure costs a candidate and not an incident. What the store starts with is authored per service on the [service record](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md), beside [the explicit threshold](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) and at the same cost: a snapshot the owner has anonymised, or synthetic rows they supply, and where they author nothing it starts empty. It is stated rather than silent because two checks read the store's state: the [criteria](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md), and the store [declarations in force](../07-contracts/06-what-a-consumer-declares.md) decided against it. A check over a store nobody seeded decides over whatever the run happened to write. What a candidate's store cannot rehearse is production's scale: a change that completes in milliseconds over the seed can hold a lock for minutes over production's rows, and what catches that is [the reading against the service's own recent history](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md), not this run.

A non-production value set of the configuration is authored per service on the service record, beside what the store starts with, and the deployer writes it onto the candidate environment at composition, so a candidate never holds a production value. A service whose owner authors none runs its criteria against unset values, and the production value an item changes is exercised only by its window, never by its candidate.

[Mutation of the build](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/03-what-the-encoding-rests-on.md), the check on whether an encoding could have failed, adds a cost of its own. Each mutant tested at [_Implementation_](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/README.md) is a build and a deploy onto the item's one environment, run serialised with the seeded store restored between them, so mutation multiplies composition and environment-hours by the mutant count. A **mutant cap** per item bounds it, authored on the [service record](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) with a fixed default.

Torn down for good on those three events, an environment is also reclaimed meanwhile from an item that is running nothing. An item stopped above Implementation, sent back to [Spec](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md), waiting on its intent's [interview](../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md), or [escalated](../03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) with nobody having taken it over, holds no run its environment is serving, and the environment is recomposable, being created from master plus the candidate and composed from dependencies' current releases, which is what [_The merge queue_](03-the-merge-queue.md)'s re-verification already rebuilds. The deployer tears one down where the item's stage says nothing can be running on it and composes one again when the item next reaches [Deploy to candidate environment](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/06-deploy-to-candidate-environment.md); the branch and the builds persist, the environment being the one part that can be discarded and composed again. Without reclamation the [platform](../03-gates/02-the-rollout-strategy.md)'s room is consumed monotonically by items waiting on humans, every wait the design leaves unbounded: an owner's silence on an interview, an escalation nobody takes, the re-authoring above an item sent back, each holding one environment. Ten such items on a platform with room for ten hold every later item at that gate indefinitely, no [page](../08-operations/07-pages.md) fires, and the factory can free nothing itself. What reclamation costs is composition paid twice on an item that resumes, and that the deployer reads whether a run is in progress before tearing down rather than assuming it.

What composing one takes is written, not estimated: the environment record carries when the deployer began composing it and when the run could start, written by the deployer as it does the work, so [_Factory_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) reports composition time per service beside throughput — a number an owner reads before deciding whether the platform is the bottleneck, and the one the throughput argument above rests on. What an install has to provide is stated the same way, as a shape rather than a figure: the factory itself, which is one process and one store, and then per item in progress one environment holding that service's build, its store, and the current release of each dependency in the transitive closure — so the platform an operator provisions is linear in items in progress with the closure as the coefficient, and the bound on items in progress is the room the platform has, held at [_Deploy to candidate environment_](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/06-deploy-to-candidate-environment.md) when it runs out. The figure is the install's, because the closure and the release are: the design refuses to state a capacity for the reason it refuses to model one below.

How much room the platform has is read rather than modelled, on the same terms as composition time. The deployer keeps a [last check](../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) for the platform beside the ones it keeps per persistent target, overwritten each pass with three counts: how many candidate environments the factory's records hold as standing, how many the platform reports holding for the factory, and the room the platform reports where it reports one. [_Factory_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) shows the three beside composition time, so an owner reads how near the install is to the hold at [_Deploy to candidate environment_](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/06-deploy-to-candidate-environment.md) before items stop there, rather than inferring it from throughput after they have. A candidate environment the platform holds and the records mark torn down is a teardown that failed, and the deployer tears it down again on its next pass, keyed on the environment the way [its restart](../08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md) is keyed on the release and the target, so a leak does not consume the room for good. Where the platform reports no room figure, the two counts are what Factory shows and the hold at the gate stays the discovery: the factory reads what the platform reports and computes nothing over it, which is the refusal below holding. It is the one record of the deployer's that [_Drift detection_](../08-operations/08-drift-detection.md)'s reading of last checks leaves alone, for the reason a record per candidate target was refused there: a deployer stopped composing is a stalled timeline in [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) already.

The cost is infrastructure per item in progress rather than one shared environment per service. It is the factory's cost and not a human's: where the score or a safeguard puts a human on a candidate, that human's delay is that item's alone, where a single shared environment would have imposed it on every item behind them.

What an environment per candidate costs in hosting is counted rather than modelled. **Environment-hours** is composition start to teardown, summed across every compose-and-reclaim cycle an item's environment goes through, read from the timestamps the deployer already writes on the environment record at each. Where the service record's [environment-hour rate](../08-operations/05-service-level-objectives.md) is in force at a given write, the deployer writes the converted amount for that span beside the timestamps, fixed at the write and never repriced later. [_Factory_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) reports it beside composition time, per service and per item. The refusal this paragraph held is overturned: it argued a bound would need the factory to know what an environment costs on the hosting the owner pays for outside the factory, but environment-hours is a recorded fact of a record that already exists and not a model of what capacity costs. What an owner may bound is authored at [_Deploy to candidate environment_](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/06-deploy-to-candidate-environment.md), beside the platform's own room.
