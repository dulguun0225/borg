# Reclaiming an environment

Torn down for good on the three events [_An environment per candidate_](README.md) names, an environment is also reclaimed meanwhile from an item that is running nothing. An item stopped above Implementation, sent back to [Spec](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md), waiting on its intent's [interview](../../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md), or [escalated](../../03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) with nobody having taken it over, holds no run its environment is serving, and the environment is recomposable, being created from master plus the candidate and composed from dependencies' current releases, which is what [_The merge queue_](../03-the-merge-queue.md)'s re-verification already rebuilds. The deployer tears one down where the item's stage says nothing can be running on it and composes one again when the item next reaches [Deploy to candidate environment](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/06-deploy-to-candidate-environment.md); the branch and the builds persist, the environment being the one part that can be discarded and composed again. Without reclamation the [platform](../../03-gates/02-the-rollout-strategy.md)'s room is consumed monotonically by items waiting on humans, every wait the design leaves unbounded: an owner's silence on an interview, an escalation nobody takes, the re-authoring above an item sent back, each holding one environment. Ten such items on a platform with room for ten hold every later item at that gate indefinitely, no [page](../../08-operations/07-pages.md) fires, and the factory can free nothing itself. What reclamation costs is composition paid twice on an item that resumes, and that the deployer reads whether a run is in progress before tearing down rather than assuming it.
