# The verdict

Merge to master is where a candidate becomes a release and gets its number. Everything from there is machine: numbering, strategy selection, rollout, monitoring, rollback.

The verdict is score-gated like every other gate, so it is not the last place a human decides and there is no last one: the same score decides at each gate whether a human decides there, and a safeguard (9) puts one back. Where one is there, what they are doing is [UAT](../../../what-humans-do.md) (7) — the environment is no longer named for it, because in the steady state nobody is there. Where it auto-passes, the verdict is the factory's own, taken against acceptance criteria authored from a reading of the [intent](../../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) that the person who asked confirmed at [the interview's last round](../../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md). The criteria are confirmed by a human (6) only where the score or a safeguard put one at [Spec](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md), which in the steady state it does not, an intent grouped from [reports](../../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) excepted. Its source is resolved rather than weighed there, so a human is at that gate whatever the number is.

The alternative was to separate that verdict by origin, permanent for human-originated features, auto-passable for factory-originated fixes, and that is the second, invisible path the pipeline forbids, separated by a worse predictor than the score's own factors. Scoring it costs this: a change can reach production with nobody having watched it run. The analysis window covers half of that and says so: it catches a change that misbehaves, never one that behaves perfectly and is the wrong thing. What catches a wrong thing built well is the person who asked confirming the factory's reading of the intent at [the interview's last round](../../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md), which is not score-gated, and the criteria, where the score or a safeguard put a human at the [Spec gate](../../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md) to confirm them (6). The same list includes end users (4, 5) and [a human undoing the change after it shipped](../../../what-humans-do.md) (10); an owner who wants more adds any of the four back per service or per area with a safeguard. None of the four is present on every item.

The first is absent wherever nobody asked: an intent the factory raised itself takes no [last round](../../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md), its evidence being the request, so an item decomposed from a detector's intent has the first on no path at all, and reaches production with none of the four wherever the second is absent too. The second is absent in the steady state except on the reports path, where the source resolves a human to Spec and it is present on every item. The last two are after production. Which of them an item gets is therefore read off the decisions that fired and not assumed from the source: a detector's intent never carries the first, and whether it carries the second is whether that item's Spec decision closed on a human's verdict, which the row says and no rule about the source predicts. A number above the threshold usually puts one there and does not always: an item the [held-out sample](../../04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md) selected auto-passes the gates the score would have gated, and its **Why it auto-passed** field is what tells that from a number under the threshold. A safeguard and a [resolved factor](../../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) each put one there whatever the sample did. So the path with neither of the first two is a detector's item whose Spec row closed without a human, and that is the path an owner adding a safeguard is buying the check for.
