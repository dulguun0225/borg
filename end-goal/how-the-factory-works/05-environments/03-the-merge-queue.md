# The merge queue

Candidates verified in parallel cannot all fast-forward, because each was built on a master that has since moved. So merging is a queue: a candidate entering it re-verifies against master plus every candidate ahead of it, and fast-forwards only if that passes. A failure invalidates the speculation behind it, and those candidates re-verify against the master that actually resulted.

A candidate that fails its own re-verification — against the master that actually resulted, not against a speculation ahead of it — is rejected by the queue: the item [goes back up](../03-gates/06-going-back-up.md) [the pipeline](../01-one-pipeline.md) to Implementation, the queue naming no other stage and there being none between, and counts an attempt there against [_the limit_](../03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) — the rework is real work, which is what the limit bounds. That is the stop the [Merge to master](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/07-merge-to-master.md) gate can no longer give, having already approved, and without it a candidate failing here would have no path. Candidates behind a failure count nothing, for the same reason they re-verify without an attempt counted against them: they failed because of someone else.

That it failed on its merits is established rather than assumed, because a re-verification is never a repeat of the run that passed: the build is new and the environment recomposed. So a criterion that passed on the candidate environment and fails here changed its answer between two runs that differ, and what differed is on the record — each run's results name [the composition it was performed against](01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md), the [seed and value-set versions](02-an-environment-per-candidate/01-the-store-and-the-configuration.md) among the releases it names, and each build its commit. Such a criterion is run once more, over the re-verification's own build and composition — once, and never until green. A repeat that disagrees is the disagreement over one build that makes a criterion [undecided](04-what-the-candidate-environment-decides/01-the-third-outcome.md), and the way back is authoring the criterion's encoding again — the check [_Implementation_](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/05-implementation/README.md) authors beside the code to decide it. The score learns from it the way it learns from a [reject](../03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md), an encoding that cannot hold its answer being a defect of the item's own build.

A failure that repeats is real, and what the score learns turns on the criterion and the two compositions: an [unreliable](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/02-in-force-and-withdrawal.md) one teaches nothing. Where they match, the candidate failed against the master it will actually merge into, and the score learns as from a reject. Where a dependency's release moved between the runs, the score learns as from a [hold](../03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) and the [per-author prior](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) does not move: what changed is a release the author's work never saw, and a prior kept per model version and factory-wide would carry one moved dependency's noise onto every item that model authors.

The rejection the queue writes into the log names which reading it was, and the moved release where there is one, so the one rejection [_Factory_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md)'s gate rejection rate cannot see is legible on the item's timeline in [_Work_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md). What this costs is one more run per disagreeing criterion and three exits where one stood. The attempt counts on every exit, so an item a moving dependency keeps rejecting still walks toward an [escalation](../03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) — deliberately: work the environment will not let finish is work a human should see, and the limit bounds spend, which the rework is. What the three exits remove is the booking of that movement as the author's defect. On the third reading the attempt and the prior part ways: counted like a reject, taught like a hold, a combination no gate produces. The two record different things: the limit what was spent, the prior who authored what failed, and here what failed is not what was authored.

A re-verification also compares one field no criterion reads: where the two builds name a [design system](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) constraint record and the records differ on a component or a token the candidate's build uses, the candidate fails whatever its criteria answered. An item drafted before a design-system move and merging after it would otherwise leave a departure outside the [move's conformance items](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md), which were enumerated once, when the move arrived. The rejection takes the reading a moved dependency takes, whatever else failed beside the move: the score learns as from a hold, the per-author prior does not move, the attempt counts, and the log entry names the replaced record, with the moved release where one also moved — an owner's move being no defect of the author's. The comparison is of two fields, and a build naming no design-system record compares as nothing.

The queue restores what one shared environment used to provide — the commit that was verified is the commit that merges. It costs compute on speculative runs that a failure ahead of them throws away, which is the better of the two: the shared environment imposed the same serialization on human time, and this imposes it on machine time.

Its order is settable, like the queue at any gate. Reordering changes when a candidate re-verifies, never what it has to pass. The settable order is one priority field of the item, written through [dispatch](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) with [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) as the caller, and it orders every queue an item waits in as an item — this one and the queues at the gates up to and including Merge to master. It orders nothing after the merge: numbered releases waiting to deploy are ordered by the release number and by nothing else, [_the revert a rollback is waiting on_](../08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) excepted. So an owner who rushes an item to the front here has rushed it at every gate it has left, and has no way to reorder a deploy — an urgent fix behind three numbered releases waits for them.

The queue is a component and not a record. Its membership is the items whose [stage](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/02-what-an-item-names.md) says Merge to master approved them, whose fast-forward has not happened, and whose [intent's state](../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md) permits it, its order is [tier](../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md), then that priority within it, then the time of the approval in [the log](../../deferred.md), and its speculative re-verifications are its own state, read by nothing outside it. The two outcomes that outlast a run are records already: a fast-forward writes the release record, and a failure on the candidate's own merits is the rejection the queue writes into the log, with the queue as caller and actor and no decision opened — the arrangement [_Records, and one long-lived branch_](01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) already makes. What that costs is that nothing records how long a candidate waited, so why something took a day is reconstructed from the approval time against the release record's own.

Master's head the queue reads from git, the system that holds it. Deriving it from the highest-numbered release record was refused because the two systems move at one event with nothing spanning them: a queue that stops between the fast-forward and the record write leaves a derived head one commit stale, and the next candidate would merge a change no release names inside its own release. The next number is a query and not a field — one above the highest among that service's release records, numbers never reused and a rolled-back release keeping its own, the queue's per-service ordering being what stops two candidates reading one — because a counter beside the records would be the same fact written twice at one event, and a lost counter write mints a duplicate.

What guards that query where the ordering cannot is stated at [_What the queue reads before it mints_](05-what-the-queue-reads-before-it-mints.md): a duplicate refused where the records are kept, master read against the records at every start and before every mint, and how a number is minted after a restore.
