# The analysis window

The **analysis window** is a record, one per production deploy of a release its service has not watched before, whichever attempt that is, and one per deploy [the search](03-overlapping-windows.md) calls for, over a build nothing has watched. So a rollback opens none, the release it returns to having been watched already, and neither does a redeploy of one already watched. Watched rather than current, because a release that exits failed never completes its deploy and so never becomes [_current_](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md), and the window that failed it has to have been opened over something. The health monitor writes it, opened when that deploy record is written and closed once at exactly one of four exits, because the health monitor is what evaluates every exit and the deployer never advances that record again on the deploy's own account. A rollback does advance the records of every release it undid, and that is a later event the health monitor called for rather than the deploy finishing.

The window names the deploy it was opened over and, through it, the release, the service, and the control; where the deploy is [the search's](03-overlapping-windows.md), whose record names no release, it names the build instead. It also names whether passed is available to it, the size per quantity, the confidence, power, and cap resolved at the open, and the set of targets the boundary was allocated over at the open. It names the size and run length in force for the other two readings that could fail this release, the own-history reading and the explicit threshold, beside the [policy version](../../what-the-factory-does/02-traceability.md) and [score version](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) in force. It names the [emission version](01-the-health-monitor.md) each arm's series were read at, and where the two differ, which quantities were outside its set for that reason. It names the **boundary version**: the construction that turns the size, confidence, power, and run length into a boundary, and allocates it over the set, is the factory's, shipped with it and authored by nobody, and a version of the factory that changes it changes what every exit means. A reading at an exit is not interpretable against anything but the boundary it was actually read against.

The boundary version is on the window at the open because it cannot be added to a history written without it, the rule [_Traceability_](../../what-the-factory-does/02-traceability.md) states for the policy and score versions. A mechanism that learns from exits reads exits at the boundary version in force and none at another, since `passed` and `failed` under two constructions are not one currency: the [per-author prior](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md), the [window limit](03-overlapping-windows.md), the size and power the score supplies, and the [held-out bands](../04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md). So an upgrade that changes the version restarts each where it starts, the prior at an unseen author's width, the window limit at 1, the supplied size and power at what the product ships, the bands empty. That is the treatment evidence already gets when a [truncation](../04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md) of the log removes it. [A rollback's target](03-overlapping-windows.md) and the last known-good release read exits at every version, because they ask whether any window failed a release and not at what rate it was read. What that costs is stated so that such a change is made rarely: it puts humans back at gates across the install and serializes every service until new exits arrive, and the [install event](../../deferred.md) the upgrade writes names the version change so the owner can read why.

A service missing one of [the four fields the deployer populates on its service record](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) opens a window that records only that it measures nothing for that service, since what the field would have supplied is what a reading needs to exist at all.

What that costs: the health monitor is the only thing that closes a window, so a monitor that stops running leaves windows open, reaches the window limit, and holds that service's production deploys. That is a wait on the factory while the monitor is running and something else while it is not, and the two are told apart the way [_Drift detection_](08-drift-detection.md) already tells them apart for itself: a check that silently stops is worse than the bug it catches. So the health monitor writes a last check record per service and the deployer one per target of a persistent [environment](../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md), the same record the drift detector keeps, carrying the interval its pass runs on and recording on its last pass that no further pass is owed, both as [_Drift detection_](08-drift-detection.md) sets them. That record is read by [Ops](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) and by [the home view](../11-screens/02-three-properties-every-screen-needs.md). Without it a stopped monitor and a service with nothing to report are the same row in [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), and the component that is stopped is the one whose stopping disarms the [rollback](../06-releases/06-rollback.md) (the factory's only authority to act on production without a human) for every release after it. What the record costs is a write per pass on two components that otherwise write only at events.

The window is also how long the factory may act on the health monitor alone. Inside it, a **crossing** of the boundary — what sequential analysis calls a statistic reaching the stopping boundary set for it — rolls the release back with no human involved; outside it, the same crossing raises an [item](../01-one-pipeline.md). It is the factory's own authority and is limited by evidence, where [undoing a change after it shipped](../../what-humans-do.md) (10) is a human's and is limited by reversibility — a rollback while the build it would return to is still running, a revert once it is gone. That is why there is no second named window: what limits a human's undo is that build's instances, which the strategy either keeps or does not, and a window with no parameter and no limit of its own is a second name for something already named. A rollback deploy opens no window of its own: the build being restored has been watched already, and a fresh window would compare it against the release just failed and restore that one.

The window is a volume condition, not a clock — and not a volume computed in advance either. The comparison is evaluated as traffic arrives, against a boundary that is valid at every point it is read. That much is forced by what the factory already does: a threshold set for one look at a fixed sample is not the threshold for a thousand looks at a growing one, and reading a fixed-horizon test continuously is how a factory rolls back healthy releases all day. The boundary is what makes continuous reading legitimate rather than a fault nobody traces to its cause.

| Exit | What closes the window | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| **failed** | the comparison crosses the boundary against the release | rolled back, no human involved — while a [mismatch](08-drift-detection.md) stands, none is performed, and [_Drift detection_](08-drift-detection.md) says what happens instead |
| **passed** | the comparison rules out a regression of the size worth catching | closed early, on evidence |
| **timed out** | neither, by the cap | closed unresolved |
| **skipped** | a rollback aimed below it undid this release | closed with the release neither failed nor running |

Three of the four close without failing the release, and no rule in this document reads
them as a group. Their content is opposite in each of the two directions a rule can read
them. `passed` ruled a regression out where `timed out` ruled nothing out, so a mechanism
learning from an exit — [the per-author prior](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md),
[the window limit](03-overlapping-windows.md) — that admitted both would read silence as
evidence. And `skipped` left the release neither failed nor running where the other two
left a build still serving, so a query asking what can be returned to — [a rollback's
target](03-overlapping-windows.md), the [fallback comparison](01-the-health-monitor.md) —
that admitted all three would name a release whose instances the rollback ended. No one
grouping is right for both readings, so _closing without failing_ is reserved as a phrase
and every rule over exits names the exits it admits, one at a time. What it costs is
repetition, and a fifth exit would have to be written into each such rule by hand — which
is the point, a new exit falling on no side by default.

An exit is more often several records than one, so the window's close is the last durable
step of it and never the first, on the rule [_Tight
integration_](../../what-the-factory-does/01-tight-integration.md) states for every event
that writes more than one record. Failed is the longest. The
[deployer](01-the-health-monitor.md) writes the [rollback](../06-releases/06-rollback.md)'s
deploy record, the deploy record of every release the rollback undid advances to rolled
back, those releases' windows close skipped,
[intake](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) writes the revert intent, and this
window closes failed last. Each step is keyed the way [the merge
queue](../05-environments/03-the-merge-queue.md)'s record write is: the deploy record on
the release and the target, the intent on the failed release its evidence names, a
window's close on the window. So a start replays every step already taken and writes
nothing at any of them. What the order buys is that a stop anywhere inside a failed exit
leaves the window open, which is the state [the health monitor's
restart](../../one-process.md) already reads and the cap already bounds. Closed first, the
same stop would leave a release the factory had failed serving production with no rollback
deploy record, no [hold](../03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md), no
[mismatch](08-drift-detection.md), and nothing that would ever retry. Where the failed exit
finds no release to return to, the order is the same: the revert intent and the
[page](07-pages.md) precede the close. While a [mismatch](08-drift-detection.md) stands, no
rollback is performed either, and [_Drift detection_](08-drift-detection.md) says what
happens instead. Passed and timed out take it too, the control torn
down and the window closed after. What it costs is a window readable as open for as long as
the exit it is taking runs.

What passed rules out, it rules out at a rate. That rate is the **power** — the probability that a regression of the size the window is actually watching for crosses the boundary rather than reaching passed — authored per service beside the size and the confidence, and resolved at the open like them. It is a probability of catching and not of missing, the direction the confidence already reads in, so a higher number is more protection in both. Passed is available only while the control is readable. A comparison rules out a regression relative to the control, so a control that is itself failing lets a bad release pass — the one reading nobody corrects afterwards, because nothing rolled back and nothing is left to mark. The health monitor already reads every service against its own recent history, and it reads the control the same way: a window whose control is crossing that reading cannot exit passed, and stays open until the control is readable again or the cap closes it timed out. That is also what closes the hole a shared-state change leaves, where both arms degrade together and the comparison stays flat. Failed stays available: a release worse than a failing control still improves production when rolled back, and the rollback's own confound is corrected afterwards by a human [marking it as not caused by the release](03-overlapping-windows.md). What it costs is a second reading per window on instances the monitor is already measuring, and a service whose control is failing holding its next deploy to the cap — the safe direction, since what it has is a comparison that can say nothing.

What an owner authors is that size, the confidence required, and the power required at that size, with the rest of [gate policy](../09-gate-policy/README.md) (8), safeguarded per service the way explicit thresholds are (9); where they author nothing the score supplies all three. The size is one value per quantity, three for a service: the smallest change in the request rate, in the error rate, and in the latency quantile that the comparison has to detect. It is not one number, because a detectable change is not commensurable across the three: at one volume the relative change a boundary can rule out in an error rate near zero is far coarser than in a latency quantile, so one scalar would be read at the hardest quantity's floor and every quantity would inherit it. An owner authoring a size names the quantity it is for, and the score supplies each they left. The confidence stays one number, being held over the set, and the power is per quantity as the paragraph below states.

Two boundaries need two rates. The confidence bounds how often a release that is fine is rolled back, and only the power bounds how often a release that is not reaches passed, closes the window early, and ends the only watch there is on it. Neither rate is readable without the other, the traffic a comparison needs being set by the two together and by the size, so a design carrying no such parameter at all would leave that arithmetic undefined rather than merely unstated, and two factories running the same size and confidence would exit passed at different points and both conform. An owner who authors two of the three is not in that position: the score supplies the one they left, the way it supplies any parameter nobody authored. Every mechanism that reads a passed close as evidence that nothing was there inherits that rate, which is why it is authored rather than left to whatever the boundary arithmetic happened to imply: [the per-author prior](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md), [the window limit](03-overlapping-windows.md), and the [score](../04-risk-score/README.md)'s own calibration. A safeguard on the size is a minimum level of protection, the score may ask to catch something smaller and never something coarser, and on the power it is a floor, the direction a floor under the confidence already sets. So a safeguard can only add protection, the way one at a gate can only add a human.

The duration is measured and never set, and that is where the throughput comes from: a release that is plainly fine shows it early and its window closes. It gets there by being fine rather than by being watched against a coarser boundary, which is the change not to make — the traffic a comparison needs scales as the inverse square of what it must detect, so cutting the window tenfold makes the boundary three times coarser. The size is the only one of the three that enters that arithmetic squared: the confidence and the power enter it together and far more slowly, which is why loosening either buys little traffic back and coarsening the size buys a great deal. Speed obtained that way runs out quickly. Speed obtained from evidence does not.

The confidence is over the set of quantities and not per quantity: what it bounds is how often a release that is fine is rolled back, because a crossing on any one of the three quantities [the health monitor reads](01-the-health-monitor.md) starts the same rollback, the same [hold](../03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) on the service's deploys, and the same [incident](06-incidents.md). Read per quantity, 95% authored means about one good release in seven rolled back over three quantities, and the figure moves every time a quantity is added without anything the owner wrote changing — a number whose meaning depends on arithmetic done somewhere else is the number nobody can argue with. So the health monitor reads each quantity at the stricter boundary that holds the authored confidence over the set, which is its arithmetic to do once rather than every owner's to do by hand. The power takes no such adjustment and is held per quantity, because the two exits are opposite logical shapes: failed is a disjunction — a crossing on any one quantity fails the release — where passed is a conjunction, every quantity having ruled its own regression out, so a regression on one quantity reaches passed only if that quantity's own boundary missed it and reading three cannot raise the rate. An explicit threshold a [safeguard](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) sets stays outside that set, and not for being free of a rate. Absolute describes the number the threshold names and not the reading that tests it: whether a service's error rate or latency quantile exceeds a fixed number is decided from a finite count of requests, so a service whose true value sits anywhere near that number crosses it eventually however good the service is, and read continuously and forever it crosses with probability approaching one. So the threshold is read the way [the reading against a service's own recent history](01-the-health-monitor.md) is — against a boundary valid at every point, with an average run length in force — and not as a count compared against a number. It stays outside the set because each reading carries its own rate and neither pays for the other: folding the threshold in would loosen the comparison the owner did not touch to pay for the check they added, which is the reverse of what a safeguard does. What the threshold's own boundary costs is lateness — it crosses once the volume behind the reading supports the run length in force, and not when the raw count first passes the number — which lands hardest on [a service's first release](01-the-health-monitor.md), where an explicit threshold is the only reading there is. What the stricter per-quantity boundary costs is traffic — a stricter boundary per quantity needs more of it, a fifth or so more over three — so a service near the edge of closing a window on evidence times out instead, and is watched at the coarser size the paragraphs below already resolve for a service whose traffic cannot reach what the evidence asks for. That cost lands as weak protection reported as weak, which the window already does, and not as a rate the owner did not author.

The set the confidence is held over is wider than the three quantities. The comparison is
[evaluated per target](01-the-health-monitor.md), so the set is the three quantities on
every production [target](../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) the
rollout is planned to reach, the list the [environment
record](../05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) holds at the open, and
a crossing on any one of them starts the same rollback. The boundary is allocated over
that whole set at the open, by the arithmetic above, and does not move as targets are
reached. A boundary sized for one target while the first is read, and tightened as each
next one arrives, has already spent on the first what the later allocation cannot take
back, so the rate over the rollout would be roughly the allocations summed and not the
number the owner authored. The window names the target set the allocation was made over,
so an exit stays readable against the boundary it was read against after the environment
record changes. A target the rollout never reaches, the release failing or the cap closing
the window first, keeps its allocation unspent. Without any of that an environment of four
targets would roll a good release back about four times as often as one of one, with
nothing the owner wrote having changed. The power stays per quantity and per target, for
the reason it stays per quantity: passed is a conjunction, every quantity on every target
having ruled its own regression out. What it costs is what the per-quantity boundary
already costs, more traffic per target as targets are added, and it lands from the first
target rather than as each is reached: a rollout that fails on the first target has been
read at a boundary paid for targets it never reached.

Three readings can roll a release back — the comparison against the [control](01-the-health-monitor.md), the reading against a service's own recent history, and an explicit threshold where a safeguard set one — so the authored confidence bounds one of the three and never the factory's own rate of rolling back a release that was fine. What the other two contribute over a window is that window's volume divided by each reading's run length in force, which is arithmetic on values already resolved at the open rather than an unknown, so [Ops](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) reports the three together per service and not the confidence alone. Without that an owner authoring 99% has authored a number governing one path to a rollback out of three, and reads it as governing all of them — which is the same defect the confidence being read per quantity has, one level up.

A boundary is not arguable the way a vector of factors is, and the score's own rule is that a number nobody can argue with is one nobody will trust. What an owner argues with is the size, the confidence, and the power, all three authored and all three open to a [safeguard](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md), and the size and run length of each reading that never closes, on the same terms; the boundary, and the volume at which a comparison may close passed instead, are arithmetic on those. That is the same shape as the score — a published formula over inputs a human can disagree with.

A cap limits it in elapsed time, authored the same way. The condition is a traffic-volume one, but what ends a window that will never reach its volume cannot be — so a service too quiet to receive that much traffic times out every time. There the window is weak protection and is reported as weak: a service called twice a night is not made safe by watching it until lunchtime. Timing out on traffic too thin to rule anything out is weak evidence; timing out on traffic the comparison never received at all is none. The volume the health monitor counts comes from the same emission it is measuring, so an arm that stopped emitting and an arm nobody called look alike, and the other arm is what tells them apart. A release arm silent beside a control that is serving is [a crossing on the request rate](01-the-health-monitor.md) and exits failed, never timed out. Volume is nothing only where both arms received none, a service nobody called or the store [gone quiet](01-the-health-monitor.md) under both. So the window records the volume each arm received, and where both are nothing the exit says nothing about the release. [The score](../04-risk-score/README.md)'s own rule is what governs, and it was written for this: a factor that silently reads as absent reads as low risk, and the protection a failure would remove is the protection that failure is evidence for needing. Such a window stays admissible for one thing only, [a rollback's target](03-overlapping-windows.md), which asks whether any window ever failed that release and not whether anything measured it. What that admission costs is bounded by the arm reading: the release it names is one that served every request the control served, or one no request reached while nothing reached the control either.

A service's first release ends there too and for a different reason: it has no comparison to rule anything out, so passed is not an exit available to it — an explicit threshold a safeguard set can still fail it, but nothing can clear it early. With a window limit of 1 on a service that has never shipped, that cap is also how long the second release waits. The [sample the score holds out](../04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md) runs to the cap rather than stopping where the boundary would allow — [auto-passing](../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) a change the score wanted gated is where the factory is most openly guessing, so it takes the longest watch available, which on a quiet service is not much.

The window reads the [producer](../07-contracts/01-two-versioned-things.md)'s own numbers and nothing else, one window excepted below. A [consumer](../07-contracts/01-two-versioned-things.md) that breaks does so in its own error rate, on its own schedule, and what catches it is that service's own health monitor raising an item — after the producer's window closed, with nothing rolling back automatically. Reading another service's numbers would require every consumer's calls to be recorded and attributed across services, which is the recorded-usage approach [_What a diff cannot see_](../07-contracts/05-what-a-diff-cannot-see.md) refuses; the window keeps that refusal rather than reopening it. The item is what replaces it, together with the [contract rules](../07-contracts/04-enforcement.md) that make the schema half mechanical, the producer's own [per-field observables](../07-contracts/05-what-a-diff-cannot-see.md), and a human undoing the change after it shipped (10) while it can still be undone.

The one window that reads more is a [brownout](../07-contracts/08-deprecation.md)'s, and it does not reopen the refusal. A brownout's effect lands wherever the hidden read is: a disabled operation still called errs in the producer's own error rate, which the window already reads, but a field a consumer parses and now fails on errs in that consumer's numbers alone, and the producer's own never show it. So beside the producer's own numbers its window reads a reading already taken: the health monitor reads every service against its own recent history — the reading the control check above uses — with no call recorded and nothing attributed across services. Any service crossing that reading while a brownout's window is open fails it. What that costs is a false failure whose rate rises with the install's size — a service breaking for its own reasons fails a brownout that touched nothing of its — and that rate is a number rather than an unknown, being every service's traffic over its own run length in force for as long as the brownout's window is open, which is the one place a run length authored per service adds up across services. It fails in the safe direction: an element restored that nobody read, and a removal blocked that a human must re-raise.

The parameters are learned. How fast a problem would appear is already what the score reads to pick a strategy, and the same evidence sets the size and the power wherever an owner has not. Only a rollback or a human's undo traceable to the health monitor counts as that evidence, and not a rollback a human [marked as not caused by the release](03-overlapping-windows.md). A change undone for being the wrong feature says nothing about what the monitor should have caught, and feeding those back would shrink the size for owners who changed their minds, raise the power on releases the monitor had nothing to catch on, and lengthen every window with either. Which of the two one such event sets is the exit its release's window took, which [_How it learns_](../04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md) states once for both: a window that closed passed cleared what it should have caught, so the event is about the power, and one that timed out ruled nothing out at all, so it is about the size. Which quantity's size is the one the evidence names: a rollback's [incident](06-incidents.md) names the quantity the reading crossed on, and a human's undo names none, so it moves the size of every quantity. The score learns from what the health monitor could have seen, the same way it learns from a [reject](../03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) and not from a hold.

That evidence sets what is worth catching, and one thing more is needed: whether anything can be caught. The size in force is the coarser of the two, per quantity — what the evidence asks for, and the finest size this service's own traffic can rule anything out at inside the cap, which is arithmetic over the volume its windows have actually received and over the confidence in force. That arithmetic is done for each quantity on its own, which is what lets a quantity conclude at the size the evidence asked for while another is watched coarser. On latency that finest size has a second floor: the width of the histogram bucket [the health monitor](01-the-health-monitor.md) reads the quantile from, since a change smaller than a bucket is a change the kept series cannot show. The power does not enter that arithmetic and never coarsens the size, which is what keeps the three parameters from trading against each other: what it decides is whether passed is reachable at the size in force and at what volume, never what that size is.

Where the traffic cannot support the power in force at that size inside the cap, passed is not an exit available to that window, which runs to the cap the way a first release's does and a window over an unreadable control already does. The rule is the same whoever set the rate, an owner, the score, or a [safeguard](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md): a floor under the power spends the early exit and never the size the service is watched for, and the invariant that a safeguard can only add protection holds without an exception to it. What that costs is stated rather than left as an implication, because it is everything a passed close buys elsewhere. Such a service runs every window to the cap, so its [window limit](03-overlapping-windows.md) stays at 1, its releases narrow no [per-author prior](../04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md), and its [held-out sample](../04-risk-score/02-how-it-learns.md) produces nothing a threshold can rise on. That is the position a service too quiet to close a window on evidence is already in, reached here by whoever asked for the rate.

A size finer than that is a size nothing is ruled out at, so the window times out every time, protects nothing, and holds the next deploy for the whole cap; asking for it would be watching longer and catching less. What is worth catching and whether anything can be caught are different questions, and only the first is about harm; that is why reading the traffic for the second does not reopen the restriction above on what counts as evidence. What it costs: a service can be told, truthfully, that it is being watched for something coarser than the evidence asked for. So [Ops](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) reports the size in force per service and per quantity with what the traffic reaches beside each, the confidence and power that reading was taken at, and whether passed is reachable at all — the one place a human sees a service being watched for less than the evidence asked for. And a service whose traffic falls far enough is watched at the size it was installed with and no coarser, which is the weak protection _The analysis window_ already says such a service gets. This is also what keeps a learned size from ratcheting: every miss makes it finer and nothing makes it coarser, so without the second question a service that missed twice would need sixteen times the traffic it has and never close a window on evidence again.
