# Service level objectives

The reading against a service's own recent history says whether that service is failing now. It says nothing about how often it has failed, and a service that fails a fraction of its requests every day crosses nothing: those failures are in the history it is read against.

A **service level objective** is a target an owner authors on the [service record](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) — the proportion of a quantity that must be good over a stated period, 99.9% of requests served without error over thirty days being the ordinary shape. The factory computes what is left of it from the emission the health monitor already reads, and that remainder is the **error budget**. It does two things. While the budget is exhausted, that service's production deploys are held, and the hold lifts itself when the period rolls forward far enough to restore it; nothing is decided and no [_page_](07-pages.md) fires, which is the shape the hold a [declared dependency that is not current](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/08-deploy-to-production.md) sets already has. The objective is also one of the four detectors [_Intake_](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md) has that read what runs. It raises an intent on the service when the budget is exhausted, or when the **burn rate**, the share of the budget spent per hour, exhausts it before the period ends at either reading, the last hour or the period so far. The long reading sees a service failing the same fraction every day; the short one sees a fault an hour old. The evidence that keys the raise is the service and the objective's period, so one intent stands per period. The raise reaches [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) as an unrefined intent and never as a page: an escalation on it pages, the way [_Pages_](07-pages.md) treats one on an item raised from an incident. A period the store does not cover, read as [_The health monitor_](01-the-health-monitor.md) states, leaves the budget uncomputed, and an uncomputed budget holds the way an exhausted one does, lifting itself once the store covers the period: a budget taken as intact over records that are not there is an absent input read as evidence.

It is authored and never supplied, which is the opposite of the division the window's size and the [window limit](03-overlapping-windows.md) use, and the reason is that the score learns from the factory's own outcomes: a target inferred that way is set to whatever the service already achieves and never binds. What an objective states is what the customer promised the people using the software, and the factory observes nothing that says what that is. Where an owner authors none there is no budget and nothing is held — that reading and the window are the whole of what protects that service. It is authored on the service record beside the explicit threshold and is not one of [gate policy](../09-gate-policy/README.md)'s eleven rows, at the same cost that threshold carries.

Beside the service level objective, the service record takes two further owner-authored rates: currency per environment-hour and currency per instance-hour, pricing the hosting a candidate and a kept fleet consume outside the factory. Converted at the write, they turn the recorded [environment-hours](../05-environments/02-an-environment-per-candidate/README.md) and [instance-hours](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/02-what-stands-for-a-rollback.md) figures into money the same way [a rate authored per model version](../10-fleet/08-a-spend-ceiling.md) turns spend into cost per feature, and where none is authored those figures stay units only. What it costs: an install billed per environment authors the same number twice, once here and once with its hosting provider.

What the hold costs: the fix for whatever exhausted the budget is itself a production deploy. So two items pass the hold: a revert, which passes the hold [a rollback leaves](03-overlapping-windows.md) for the same reason, and an item whose intent a detector raised on that service, the health monitor's at a crossing or the objective's own. The second is what makes the hold passable on a service that crossed nothing: the health monitor raises nothing there, and without the objective's raise the hold would stand with no item able to lift it. A request an owner raises (1) on that service does not pass; the route is the objective's intent, which exists whenever the budget is exhausted. Without the exception the budget would hold hardest exactly where production is worst. What the exception costs: a fix making things worse takes the same path, so a service can spend what is left of its budget on its own repairs, bounded by the [attempt limit](../03-gates/05-the-attempt-limit.md) and by nothing else.
