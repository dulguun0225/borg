# Pages

One **notifier** delivers everything waiting on a human out of the product, on three channels: mail, chat, and the page. It routes all three the same way, on the [_People_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) declaration, by the [duty](../../what-humans-do.md) the row belongs to, or by the named human where the row belongs to none, so the routing is implemented once rather than beside each thing that waits. Its callers are the components that create a wait: the [gate component](../01-one-pipeline.md) when it opens a decision a human must close, intake when it writes a round of [_interview_](../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md) questions or an intent escalated, [dispatch](../02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) when it writes an item escalated, and the health monitor at a [failed](02-the-analysis-window.md) exit that finds no release to return to. An owner may fire one from [Ops](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) as well. The drift detector calls nothing, so the notifier reads its store itself. A **page** is the narrow channel: what qualifies for one is a wait where the deployed software is worse until a human ends it, and nothing else fires one. It is the only channel that widens and the only one that writes into [the log](../../deferred.md), a record per delivery there being a log sized by how often the factory notifies.

What a delivery on any of the three writes instead is a **delivery record**, one per row the notifier delivers and the notifier's alone to write: the row, the channel, the recipient it resolved from People, whether the transport accepted the send, and when. It is overwritten at each attempt the way a [last check](08-drift-detection.md) record is, so what exists is one record per waiting row rather than one per delivery. A delivery that changes no state is still the only evidence there is of the one thing nothing else records, that this row reached this person. Without it a notifier fault, a wrong address, a chat integration that stopped and a human who is slow are one number on [Factory](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), and [_The page channel, and what reached a human_](../11-screens/05-the-page-channel-and-what-reached-a-human.md) is what reads them apart. What it costs is a record per waiting row and a write per attempt, neither of them in the log, a delivery not being a read of evidence.

What one component costs: a fault in it stops all three channels at once and the factory falls back to whoever remembers to open Work, which is the poll [push-not-poll](../11-screens/02-three-properties-every-screen-needs.md) was written to remove. So the notifier writes a last check record too, beside the health monitor's and the deployer's, and nothing pages when the notifier is what stopped: the channel that would carry that page is the thing that stopped. What makes it visible instead is [Ops](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) reading those records beside the drift detector's own, and [the home view](../11-screens/02-three-properties-every-screen-needs.md) reading every one of them against the interval each names, whether or not it is at zero — a poll in both places, which is what push-not-poll cannot reach, because a component cannot announce its own silence. What reaches a human without the poll is [the drift detector](08-drift-detection.md)'s one delivery of its own, to an address the owner wrote into its store, fired when it finds the notifier's last check stale or every factory last check stale at once, the whole process having stopped. The notifier in turn pages whoever installed the detector when the detector's own last check is stale, so each of the two processes watches the other.

A page is not a record; it is the name for the sequence of page events on one row. A **page event** is one append to the log naming the row, the human reached, and which of four it is, reached, acknowledged, widened, or answered, each with an [actor](../../deferred.md). The first delivery reaches every human holding the duty at once; unacknowledged it widens exactly once, to the owner; there is no narrower first recipient and no second widening. Acknowledged is written when a human says they have the row, which is an act in the product and nothing else — it ends no wait, decides nothing, and stops only the widening. The same act on a row that is a [decision](../03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) appends an acknowledgement to the decision as well, one act writing both, and a gate row that pages nobody still takes the acknowledgement, which _Where a gate is, and what decides it_ sets out. Answered is written when the row stops waiting, and the component that ends the wait calls the notifier at the same write it ends it with.

Widening on unanswered instead would make a human already working the problem indistinguishable from one asleep, because every page whose remedy takes longer than the delay would widen — so the channel that exists to be rare would be loudest exactly where somebody is already on it. What acknowledgement costs is a state a human can set and then walk away from, which is why it stops the widening and never the page: the row still waits, still shows in [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), and still reads as production being worse until somebody ends it. A caller that fails to make the closing call leaves a page unanswered against a row already resolved, which Ops shows as a wait with nothing waiting on it.

One wait ends where nothing calls, and it is the one the notifier already reads for itself: a mismatch is cleared by a human inside [the drift detector](08-drift-detection.md), which writes into a store no factory component may write and calls nothing. So that page's answered event is written by the notifier the next time it reads that store and finds the mismatch cleared, and its time is the time of that read rather than of the clearing. Every other caller makes the closing call.

That condition is already the document's, and two refusals above follow from it rather than being added to it. A [declared dependency](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) that is not its service's current release holds the production deploy, nothing is decided, and the hold lifts itself when the dependency is current again — there is no wait for a human to end. An owner's silence at [_The interview_](../02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md) consumes no compute, which is why no limit applies to that wait; a page would be the same mistake made more loudly.

Eight things meet that condition today. Six are here, one is in [_Rollback_](../06-releases/06-rollback.md), where a failed exit finds no release to return to and the failed release goes on serving, and the eighth is in [_An account that runs out is a hold_](../10-fleet/05-an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold.md), where a target credential cannot be reached while the health monitor is calling for a rollback. The condition is what governs: the list follows from it and is not the test.

An escalation (12) on an item the factory raised from an incident — the factory saying it cannot fix a defect that is live. A human deciding at a gate on a revert while the rollback that removed the defect still holds: the service is running the build that was restored, master still contains the defect, and nothing ships past that human. And a mismatch [_Drift detection_](08-drift-detection.md) found, which holds that service's production deploys until a human clears it. The fourth is a window past its cap that nothing has evaluated, which is what a stopped health monitor looks like from outside. The release under watch is unmeasured, the rollback that would undo it is not going to happen, the service's production deploys are held at the window limit, and no mechanism the factory has ends any of it, the component that would being the one that stopped. Its own last check record is what makes the state readable, and [_Drift detection_](08-drift-detection.md) reading that record is what fires the page. The fifth is a rollback the health monitor called for whose [deploy record](../06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md) is still not complete on every target at the deployer's next [last check](08-drift-detection.md) for that environment: production serves a release the factory has already failed, and the mechanism that would remove it did not finish. That is the state the eighth reaches by one particular cause, a target credential nothing can reach, and this reaches it by every other cause a rollback can stop on — the deployer stopped, a target that accepts the shift and never completes it. In all five, production is worse than it should be and no mechanism the factory has will improve it.

The sixth is an open incident whose crossing has not stopped, with no open window — the deployed software is worse until a human ends it, whatever the factory has since raised from it. It pages whoever holds (2), and the factory having raised an intent on that incident does not make this page one that only informs.

The first is not special for having come from an incident. An item [_After the analysis window_](04-after-the-analysis-window.md) raises is not one, and takes the same shape as an intent grouped from end-user [_reports_](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) (4, 5); a defect an end user reported is that shape exactly. An intent the [objective](05-service-level-objectives.md) raised on a budget spent or being spent takes that shape too. An item from any of those four, stopped at the attempt limit, is the factory giving up on a defect while the software containing it is live, so it meets the condition without being on the list. What separates these is never which of intake's three sources the intent came from: the refusal that an escalation on a feature item has nothing live that is worse already separates on whether something live is worse, and that is the whole of the test.

One wait meets a condition of its own and is admitted beside these rather than under them: a deadline a [constraint](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) put on an intent, where a law started a clock. The software is no worse for the hour, and the customer is — a breach notified late is a breach notified late whatever production is doing — so it pages whoever holds (2) for that constraint when the deadline is written, at any hour, and is answered when the intent ends or the human marks the obligation met. It is the one page the condition above does not cover, admitted because the condition was written for what the factory can make worse and a statutory period is worse on its own; what it costs is a page class whose remedy the factory cannot perform, so the page delays nothing the factory would otherwise have done, and every one of them reaches a human at whatever hour the trigger happened to arrive.

An [advisory](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/03-advisories.md)'s [remediation period](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) passing is admitted on the same ground and is not the raise itself. The raise reaches Work as an unrefined intent and pages nobody, but the period an owner authors per severity is a clock the way a law's is. Passing it while the intent is still open pages whoever holds (2) for that advisory, at any hour, answered the same way a constraint's deadline is.

A report carrying the field a reporter sets on [_reports_](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md), marking that it describes harm to a person, is admitted on the same ground: the condition above was written for what the factory can make worse, and a person already being harmed is worse on its own, the way a statutory period is. The intent it raises pages nobody through grouping, which still takes no gate, but the mark does: whoever holds (2) receives it at any hour, the owner reached if nobody does. It is one page per intent and never per report: a later report attaching to an intent already paging raises nothing further. What bounds a stranger's use of it is a cap of its own and not the report channel's rates, whose [per-source key](../02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) is a session a stranger renews at no cost. The cap is how many intents a service's marked reports may page per interval, a field of the [factory-wide settings record](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) with a shipped default, which an owner may lower and a safeguard (9) may lower and never raise. Past the cap a marked intent waits at Work as every marked one does, a human still resolved to Spec on it, and one page per interval goes out instead, naming the service and how many marked intents arrived past the cap. So a real person's page behind a stranger's volume is delayed to the interval and never dropped, and the human reached reads that volume is what they are looking at. It is answered the way a constraint's deadline is, when the intent ends or a human marks it addressed. Cost, beside the first exception's own: a page a stranger can cause and a remedy the factory cannot perform, and under the cap a person's page that waits behind a stranger's.

Whether this page fires at all is a field of the [factory-wide settings record](../09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md), on by default, so an owner who will not be woken by a stranger turns it off. [Factory](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) says so where it is off.

A rollback the factory performed on its own is reported, not requested, and reporting is not paging — **the factory does not page to inform.** Nothing else waiting on a human meets the condition either: a [UAT](../../what-humans-do.md) assignment (7) delays its own item and holds its own item's environment, an escalation on a feature item has nothing live that is worse, a deploy queued behind its service's window limit is waiting on the factory, and a [_fleet entry with no credential to reach_](../10-fleet/05-an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold.md) stops work rather than making anything live worse — the row most often mistaken for a page and still not one. Those wait in Work, and mail or chat delivers them.

A page has no duty of its own — inventing a thirteenth would be a mechanism where routing already exists. It reaches whoever holds the duty the row belongs to, the same routing that showed them the row in Work.

Not every row belongs to a duty. A mismatch [_Drift detection_](08-drift-detection.md) found holds the production deploy gate, which none of [the twelve](../../what-humans-do.md) owns: confirming criteria (6) and performing UAT (7) are the duties at a gate, and [_What the candidate environment decides_](../05-environments/04-what-the-candidate-environment-decides/02-the-verdict.md) puts the UAT one at the [Merge to master](../03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/07-merge-to-master.md) gate. Installing the drift detector is [substrate](../../what-humans-do.md) outside the twelve, so the page reaches whoever [_People_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) records as having installed it, and widens to the owner where nobody is recorded. That is what [_What the fleet is not_](../10-fleet/09-what-the-fleet-is-not.md) already does for a lent credential (routing needs a named human rather than a duty number), and it is why a thirteenth duty stays refused: a duty would score an obligation the twelve deliberately leave unscored, where a named human only has to be reachable.

A duty may be held by more than one human, and a page reaches all of them at once. There is no rotation naming which one it reaches first: a rotation would be a People declaration enforced by nothing, and what a stale one does — widen to every holder — is what happens here without it. The parameter's failure mode is the behaviour that replaces it, so the parameter is what can be dropped. Unacknowledged, a page widens once more, to the owner. The drawback: a page nobody needed reaches everybody, accepted so that none is ever lost, and acknowledgement narrows it only where somebody is awake to send one.

An unanswered page never makes the factory act alone, because the state it is paging about is stable. Where a rollback has run, the service runs [the rollback's target](03-overlapping-windows.md) and not the one just failed, so the wait delays the fix and never the recovery; where none has, what is live is what was live when the page fired, and the wait delays the fix there too.

That split is also what decides whether a page waits for morning. Where production serves a release the health monitor has called for a rollback on and the rollback has not run, the software is worse now and worse for every hour of the wait, and the page fires whenever the condition arises, on every service. That is a failed exit with nothing to return to, a target credential that cannot be reached, and a window past its cap with the health monitor stopped. Where the rollback has run, or nothing was live to remove, the software is worse until a human ends the wait and no worse for the hour at which they do: the revert at a gate, an escalation on an item raised from an incident, a mismatch the drift detector found. The factory tells the two apart from what it already holds, the release the production deploy record names and whether a rollback the health monitor called for on it has run. Nothing authors the urgency and nothing scores it; the score computes impact for every decision it makes, but which of the two a wait is turns on state and not on likelihood.

For the second kind an owner authors on the [service record](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md), beside the explicit threshold and the objective, the hours within which that service pages. The hours carry the time zone they are read in, which [_The screens as software_](../11-screens/03-the-screens-as-software.md) requires of every authored calendar value. A wait of the second kind arising outside them goes out by mail and chat at once, waits in [Work](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) as every row does, and pages at the next hour the service allows in that zone; a holder in another zone reads the same window, rendered in their own. Where an owner authors none, it pages at any hour, which is what every service did before there was anything to author. The hours are authored and never supplied, for the reason the [objective](05-service-level-objectives.md) is: nothing the factory observes says which service the customer minds losing overnight, and a value inferred from outcomes would be set to whatever the service already gets. What it costs is another authored value that goes stale the way a People declaration does, and a page that waited until morning on a service whose hours were authored too narrow. The owner who authored them chose that over a page reaching every holder at 02:40 for work that could not have needed doing before morning, which is the thing that makes the next page slower to answer.

A bound on how long an [incident](06-incidents.md)-raised item may be worked before a human is reached is admitted on the same ground: an owner authors it on the [service record](../02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md), with a fixed default, beside the objective and the hours a service pages within. Passing it while the item is still open pages whoever holds (2), answered the same way a constraint's deadline is. Cost: an item the pipeline could not finish in time pages a human who has, besides undo (10), the two [mitigations](../../deferred.md) a human may perform at Ops.

A human may fire one on their own judgment, from [_Ops_](../11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), which is the parallel a human's undo (10) already has: the rollback the factory would perform, on a human's judgment instead of the health monitor's. Nothing scores it and no limit applies to it. What limits it is that a page nobody needed makes its recipient slower to answer the next one, which is a behaviour rather than a rule, so [_The page channel, and what reached a human_](../11-screens/05-the-page-channel-and-what-reached-a-human.md) counts what this channel is doing instead of asserting that it stays rare.

A page is an event on the row it belongs to — who was reached, when it widened, who answered — each recording an actor, which is seam 1 of [_Deferred, but not designed out_](../../deferred.md) and nothing new. The hours are the one authored thing in it, and they are on the service record; who holds a duty is a People declaration and the widening is the factory's own, so gate policy stays at the rows it has.
