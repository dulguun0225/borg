# Deferred, but not designed out

One thing is sequenced away rather than answered: security. It is a decision about order, and what it has to satisfy is that waiting creates no requirement the factory cannot meet when it arrives — met by leaving fields populated in the meantime. Software the factory did not build was sequenced away here once and is not any longer: [_A service that already exists_](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/01-a-service-that-already-exists.md) adopts one through [the pipeline](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md), because a customer with software already running is the ordinary first customer rather than a later one.

## Security comes last

The factory should be free and easy to play with at the start and tighten as the human world demands it. That is a sequencing decision, not permission to build something that cannot be secured later. It sequences the five seams below; it does not cover the product's own release channel, which is not deferred and is stated next.

Five seams are nearly free now and expensive to retrofit:

1. **An actor on every record** — every [gate decision](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md), edit, approval, [undo of a shipped change](what-humans-do.md) (10). No authentication, no enforcement, just the field, always populated. From the first record the field holds an opaque per-person key and never a name, and the same is true of every other field that names a human on the log seam 2 keeps, a [page event](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/07-pages.md)'s recipient included. Identity cannot be added to a history written without it, and a name cannot be added to one that only ever held a key. Beside the actor, a second field populated from the first record says how the key was obtained: claimed, while nothing verifies a caller, and verified, once seam 5 does. It is there for the reason the actor is: afterwards, a key the product accepted without proof reads the same as one it checked, and rows are never migrated, so a history written without the field could never say where verification began. With it, which records a second party may rely on is read row by row from the chain rather than guessed from a date, and a per-author prior learned from claimed rows is learned from rows that say so. [_People_](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) maps a key to a name outside the chain, so an erasure there deletes the mapping and leaves the chain, its links, and its counts standing. The reasoning is the one above, applied one level down. What it costs is one more field on every record, that the field marks the unverified history without shortening it, and that every screen resolves a name by a second read, through the mapping that is now the one record an erasure request must reach.
2. **One append-only decision log, each record chaining its predecessor.** It is the audit trail and the [risk score](how-the-factory-works/04-risk-score/README.md)'s training data at once, and must not become two systems. Append-only is a promise its writer makes about itself; the chain is what makes it evidence instead — one field, populated from the first record, because a chain laid over history written without one starts at the retrofit. A second field cannot wait for the same reason: a payload format version on every row, because rows are never migrated. A later factory version reading an early row is told which shape that row holds rather than inferring it from which fields are present. The [factor vector](how-the-factory-works/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md)'s set of factors is open, so an inferred reading takes an absent factor as unavailable or as zero, either a decision that was never made. Where the head is anchored is answered by the one process already outside the trust domain. The drift detector of [_Drift detection_](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) records the head in its own store each pass and verifies the chain still holds the head it recorded, extended and nothing else. A chain rewritten and recomputed then verifies clean inside the store and disagrees with the record outside it. That is evidence against the factory, not against the owner, who hosts the detector and its store alike — against the party holding every store, a self-hosted product anchors nothing, and what the log offers an auditor is the owner's records, not proof against the owner.

   The [policy](how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/README.md) and [score version](how-the-factory-works/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) a decision was made under are named on the row appended when the [gate](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) fires, for the reason the actor is there: what a decision was made under is not recoverable from a history recording only the decision. A decision is two rows, the second closing the first, because a row is chained when appended and a verdict written onto a chained row afterwards is a rewrite — so those two versions sit on the open event alone, and the close event carries the verdict and its actor. The open event names the artifact version under decision by identifier and by the content digest [the artifact store](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) keeps on it, as [_Where a gate is, and what decides it_](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) states, so the chain covers the text decided over and not the name alone. One chain means one writer: every component that decides anything calls the log rather than writing into it, because five writers would be five implementations of the chain and five heads.

   The log holds eight shapes, not one. A shape is what a row's payload format version declares, and [_Records and their writers_](records.md) lists every row the log writes. Every format version the writer accepts declares which of these shapes it serialises, and the writer refuses a row under none, so every row that list gives the log is one of these shapes or is not written. The first is a decision. The second is a [_page event_](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/07-pages.md). The third is a wait the factory could not compute when a gate fired, an unreachable credential and [the backlog cap's stop](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) among them; [_What a gate may change_](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) gives the pair of rows every wait takes. The fourth is a [rework request](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/06-going-back-up.md), the row written when an author sends its [item](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) back with no gate fired. The fifth is [the merge queue's rejection](how-the-factory-works/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md), one row naming which of the queue's readings it was and the moved release where there is one; nothing ends it, so it is not a wait, and no gate fired, so it is not a decision. The sixth is a truncation, appended when the log enforces its retention setting. It names the actor who authored the value, the value it enforced, the boundary it cut to, and the policy version and score version in force at the cut, because a log shortened for storage and a log cut to remove evidence otherwise read the same. The seventh and eighth are the two rows a decision is read against: the [policy version](what-the-factory-does/02-traceability.md) appended at each owner write with [_Factory_](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) as caller, or [_People_](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) for a write to its declaration other than the key-to-name mapping, and the score version appended by the score as its values move.

   Those two are in the chain because a decision names them by identifier and nothing else. A version rewritten in place would change what every decision naming it is read against while the log verified clean, the decision's own row untouched and only its meaning moved. A value the score learned from outcomes exists nowhere but the row that carries it, so there is nothing outside the chain to check a rewrite against. Chained, a rewrite of either is the rewrite the drift detector's check exists to catch. A decision names the policy version and score version it was decided under, and a truncation names the pair in force at the cut; the page event, the wait, the rework request, and the queue's rejection name neither, not being decisions. What the two cost is a chained row per authored write and per supplied value that moved, most differing in one number, and a retention shorter than a version's life. The cut removes a version a decision still names, and the truncation row naming what was in force at the cut keeps that decision readable — a decision the cut removed reads as the rest of what it removed.

   What that costs is that every reader of the trail filters on which shape it has. The score is told to ignore the page event, the wait, the truncation, and the queue's rejection the way it already ignores a [hold](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md), there being no factor vector on any of them to learn against. It reads a rework request only as an outcome on the author, the one [_Going back up_](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/06-going-back-up.md) says the [per-author prior](how-the-factory-works/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) takes. Within the decision it reads, it joins the two rows, since the [factor vector](how-the-factory-works/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) it learns against is on one and the outcome it learns from is on the other. How long the log is kept is a setting an owner authors, stated with the rest of what they author in [_Gate policy_](how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/03-what-is-not-in-it/README.md). Enforcing it removes the oldest records and never the head, so the detector's check reads through a truncation undisturbed — unless the cut reached past the head the detector recorded, retention shorter than the gap since its last pass, which reads as a rewrite until a human weighs the truncation row.

   That one field is a hash and not an identifier of the predecessor, because every property above turns on the chain covering content and not order alone. Its value is a hash over the row's own payload and over the same field on the row appended before it, and the head is that field on the newest row. The payload format version already on every row fixes both the canonical serialisation of the payload and the hash algorithm, so a row re-serialised by a later factory version hashes to what the earlier version wrote, and an upgrade reads as an extension rather than a rewrite. The drift detector recomputes forward from the head it recorded and compares, which is what makes an edit in place of any row's payload a mismatch instead of a chain that still verifies. An identifier of the predecessor would leave all of that unenforced. A rewritten payload would leave every chain field valid and the head unchanged, and the version a decision names would move under it while the log verified clean. What that costs is a serialisation and an algorithm neither of which may move without a new payload format version to carry the move, so a row written under an older one is verified under the older rule for as long as it is kept.

   A ninth shape joins the eight: an **install event**, written by the log with [the install's first-start step](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/07-a-fleet-proposal.md) as caller, at every upgrade and at every start after the factory's records are restored from a backup. It names the event, an upgrade or a restore, the actor, and which version of the factory shipped it. At an upgrade it also names the changes the first-start step applied to the factory's own store and the snapshot it took before a removal among them, as [_One process_](one-process.md) states. What it costs is one more shape the log's readers filter on, the treatment the wait and the truncation already get.

   A tenth shape joins the nine: a **read event**, appended by the log when the log itself is read and when stored report text a [redaction](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) could reach is read — a report, its statement, or an artifact version quoting it. It names the principal and what was asked for, never a screen opened. What it costs is a log that grows with reads of evidence, bounded by naming only those.

3. **Secrets by reference.** [Artifacts](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) and [specs](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md) contain names, never values — they get copied, diffed, and given to agents. So does every credential the factory records: the one an agent reaches its own model through, [_The fleet_](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/README.md) using this seam before any artifact does, and the one on an [_environment record_](how-the-factory-works/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) that seam 4 resolves at every deploy. The credentials a service itself reaches the outside world with are a third kind: held by name in its configuration and resolved through the same resolver at deploy. One resolver answers all three, and it can be a file on disk today. What that costs is that nothing separates them: whoever can read the resolver reaches the models, the deploy targets, and a service's own credentials alike, so until policy attaches at seam 4 this separates storage and not reach. What a [stage](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) hands an agent is filtered against what this resolver returns for the item's reach before it is handed over, the rule [_One pipeline_](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) states.
4. **A named seam between the deployer and deploy targets.** However it is implemented, the [deployer](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md), the component that performs every deploy, reaches a [deploy target](how-the-factory-works/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) through a small set of named operations, and no agent reaches one at all. Deploying is not a [stage](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) an agent is dispatched to, so the actor on a [deploy record](how-the-factory-works/06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md) is the deployer and never an agent. The deployer is a program the install runs and can check; which model an agent is, by contrast, is only what its [fleet entry](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/README.md) says. That seam is where policy attaches later; without it, production access is spread through the codebase. What it attaches to on the other side is the deployer's own scope and the credential on the [_environment record_](how-the-factory-works/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) it resolves, declared and enforced by nothing until this seam is built. What that costs is that an agent's [_scope_](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) says nothing about a deploy target: where a deploy may go is authored on the environment, never on a fleet entry.

   That no agent reaches a deploy target rests on [dispatch](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md): no [role](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) is a deploy, and dispatch matches an item's stage against the roles the factory defines, so no agent is ever put on one. Beside that ground now sits the operation list [_One pipeline_](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) puts on every role, the operations an agent in it may perform, which an owner may narrow on a fleet entry and never widen. The list is declared now the way this seam is and enforced with it: no sandbox, no egress rule, and no credential issued per [candidate](how-the-factory-works/06-releases/03-what-a-build-is-called-and-when.md).

   A **mitigation** is a named class at this seam with two operations and not three: shifting traffic off a target, and changing the instance count of a release the factory deployed. Restoring a release outside [_the analysis window_](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) is not added — a [revert](how-the-factory-works/06-releases/06-rollback.md) once one exists is the answer there. Neither operation makes a build. Both act on releases the pipeline already verified, which is why one path holds: the second path the design refuses is a change reaching production without a gate, and a mitigation changes where a release runs and how many instances of it do, never what runs. The deployer performs one on a human's instruction from [Ops](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) and writes a **mitigation record**, naming the actor under seam 1, the operation, the target, and the [deploy record](how-the-factory-works/06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md) it modifies, which [the drift detector](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) reads as intended state. A mitigation stands until a human ends it at Ops, is shown there while it stands, and how long each has stood is a count on [Factory](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md).

5. **A principal on every call.** Seam 1 puts an actor on a record; this puts one on the call that reaches a seam, carried from the entrance to whatever the call reaches. A human at a [screen](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) calls as the [_People_](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) row they say they are; an agent calls as its [fleet entry](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/README.md) plus the [dispatch](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) that put it on the [item](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md), so the [_scope_](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) it was dispatched under travels with every call it makes; a component calls as itself. The resolver of seam 3 and the operations of seam 4 take the principal on every call and record it beside what was asked for, deciding nothing on it — populated, self-asserted, enforced by nothing, the treatment seam 1 gets. It is a seam because the other four have nothing to test without it. The resolver is addressed by credential name, so when an agent at the [Spec](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md) stage in one [project](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) names another project's production deploy credential, the resolver answers it, there being no caller for a policy to read. The actor seam 1 records is whatever the caller supplied, so the caller a policy would need is missing there too, one record later. Adding the caller at the retrofit means touching every call site, the spread through the codebase seam 4 was declared to avoid, moved one seam over. With the principal on the call, verification attaches once, where the call enters the factory: a human authenticated at a screen, an agent's dispatch checked against its scope. Every call and record downstream inherits it, so seam 1's field becomes verifiable without a second retrofit.

   The way in that [_Reports_](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) ships inside every deployed service calls as the deploy that placed it, and that one principal is checked from the first record rather than recorded now and enforced later. At every deploy the deployer mints a token for the way in, hands it to the service in its configuration beside the service's own credentials, and writes a digest of it on the [deploy record](how-the-factory-works/06-releases/05-the-deploy-record/README.md), never the token itself. The way in sends the token on every submission; the [report store](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/02-reports.md) digests it, finds the deploy record carrying that digest, and takes the service and the environment from that record and from nothing the submission says. Both ends are the factory's own, so there is no person to authenticate and nothing to retrofit, which is why this principal does not wait on the field below. What it costs is a digest on every deploy record and a token in every deployed service's configuration, readable by whoever can read that configuration, which impersonates that one deploy and no other.

   What it costs is a field threaded through every call the factory makes, and what it buys until enforcement is the shape and not the security: a record written before the retrofit still holds a name the product accepted without proof. Enforcement is a one-way field on the [factory-wide settings record](how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md): off at install, and turned on once by the owner at [_Factory_](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), chained as a policy version the way every owner write already is. While it is off, [_People_](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) and [_Factory_](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) show every actor as claimed. A rule requiring the field on before any production environment record is created is refused: a one-person install runs to production on a claimed name by design, and the claimed-or-verified field on every actor already makes the trail say so. A [constraint](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) of the document kind, the kind a human enforces at a gate rather than a diff or a run, may require the field on; an item within its reach then waits at [dispatch](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md), as an item already waits when no fleet entry covers its stage, and clears when the field turns on. What that costs is that an install can ship to production with every actor a claimed name, and the trail says so.

[One pipeline](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) is not a sixth seam — it is the architecture the five attach to, chosen for coherence rather than safety: a single path is a single place to put policy. What makes that work is that nothing routes around it, including incidents — the emergency action [_One pipeline_](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) allows is approve now, not skip, so a rushed yes is a yes with an actor on it and not a gap in the record. That is where most single-path designs fail.

The drawback is that a single path is a single point of failure. Almost nothing outside the pipeline disagrees with the pipeline, so a subverted one ships a change with a valid approval on it. What replaces the second path is mostly downstream of the deploy and reads what the factory wrote: the log for detection, the actor for attribution, and a human undoing a change after it shipped (10) — inside the [_analysis window_](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md) — for the undo. [_Drift detection_](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) is the one thing that disagrees from outside, over two facts — what is actually running against the record naming it, and the log's chain against the head it recorded — the whole of what a separate second path is worth.

## The product's release channel

[**The builder**](what-the-factory-does/05-what-the-customer-runs-it-under.md) publishes a digest and a signature over each release of the product. Install and upgrade verify a release against both before the factory's first start on it; a verification that fails stops the start. The key that verifies ships with the product and is rotated only by a signed release naming its successor. One link is left that nothing verifies: the first install trusts the key it was handed.

The channel keeps every release reachable for as long as an install can be upgraded from it. [_One process_](one-process.md) supports no skipped version, so an install reaches the current release through every release between, one at a time, and a release it cannot fetch is one it can never pass. Releases therefore leave the channel from the oldest forward and never from the middle: no release leaves while a release before it is still on the channel, and a release found defective is superseded by the one after it and never withdrawn. That is a requirement on the builder, the way a build's artifact staying fetchable is one [_Rollback_](how-the-factory-works/06-releases/06-rollback.md) puts on the host. What it costs is that the builder hosts every release from the oldest one still on the channel forward, and that a defective release stays fetchable for an install that has to pass through it.

A release carries more than code: the [role prompts](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md), [skills](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md), [evaluation sets](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/02-a-model-under-a-name.md), [starting priors](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/02-a-model-under-a-name.md), [the list of allowed predicate kinds](how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/01-what-is-in-it.md), and [design systems](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md) it ships with. A **shipped-bundle identity** is one digest over that whole set, naming which release it shipped in. It is written on every [ungated entry](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/03-what-an-agent-is-told/README.md) [the artifact store](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) writes at install and at an upgrade's first start, on every score version and policy version written at either event, and on the install event above.

A release also carries the product's own [resolved set](how-the-factory-works/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) and [notice file](how-the-factory-works/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md), and the coverage the set was resolved under, written by the builder from the build that produced the release in the shape a [build](how-the-factory-works/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) record gives a service's. The shipped-bundle identity covers both. The product is the one software in an install that no [service](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) record names, so the [advisory](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/03-advisories.md) detector matches the running release's set against the advisory feeds beside every service's current release, on the same pass and keyed by source the same way. A match raises no intent, since no item can be cut for software the factory does not build. It is a row on [the home view](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/02-three-properties-every-screen-needs.md) naming the advisory and the release, standing until an upgrade's install event names a release whose set no longer matches or the feed withdraws it; past the [remediation period](how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) it pages as any advisory does. A release whose set could not be derived, or whose coverage leaves out something a watched feed speaks to, lists the product on [Factory](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) as unassessed beside the services. What it costs is a feed named for each registry the product resolves from, and a fix the factory cannot ship itself: the row stands until the owner upgrades.
