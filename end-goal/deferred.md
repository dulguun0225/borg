# Deferred, but not designed out

One thing is sequenced away rather than answered: security. It is a decision about order, and what it has to satisfy is that waiting creates no requirement the factory cannot meet when it arrives — met by leaving fields populated in the meantime. Software the factory did not build was sequenced away here once and is not any longer: [_A service that already exists_](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/01-a-service-that-already-exists.md) adopts one through [the pipeline](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md), because a customer with software already running is the ordinary first customer rather than a later one.

## Security comes last

The factory should be free and easy to play with at the start and tighten as the human world demands it. That is a sequencing decision, not permission to build something that cannot be secured later.

Five seams are nearly free now and expensive to retrofit:

1. **An actor on every record** — every [gate decision](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md), edit, approval, [undo of a shipped change](what-humans-do.md) (10). No authentication, no enforcement, just the field, always populated. Identity cannot be added to a history written without it.
2. **One append-only decision log, each record chaining its predecessor.** It is the audit trail and the [risk score](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score/README.md)'s training data at once, and must not become two systems. Append-only is a promise its writer makes about itself; the chain is what makes it evidence instead — one field, populated from the first record, because a chain laid over history written without one starts at the retrofit. A second field cannot wait for the same reason: a payload format version on every row, because rows are never migrated — a later factory version reading an early row is told which shape that row holds rather than inferring it from which fields are present, and the [factor vector](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md)'s set of factors is open, so an inferred reading takes an absent factor as unavailable or as zero, either a decision that was never made. Where the head is anchored is answered by the one process already outside the trust domain: the drift detector of [_Drift detection_](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#drift-detection) records the head in its own store each pass and verifies the chain still holds the head it recorded — extended and nothing else — so a chain rewritten and recomputed verifies clean inside the store and disagrees with the record outside it. That is evidence against the factory, not against the owner, who hosts the detector and its store alike — against the party holding every store, a self-hosted product anchors nothing, and what the log offers an auditor is the owner's records, not proof against the owner. The [policy](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md) and [score version](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) a decision was made under are named on the row appended when the [gate](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) fires, for the reason the actor is there: what a decision was made under is not recoverable from a history recording only the decision. A decision is two rows, the second closing the first, because a row is chained when appended and a verdict written onto a chained row afterwards is a rewrite — so those two versions sit on the open event alone, and the close event carries the verdict and its actor. One chain means one writer: every component that decides anything calls the log rather than writing into it, because five writers would be five implementations of the chain and five heads. The log holds six shapes, not one — a decision, a [_page event_](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#pages), a wait the factory could not compute when a gate fired, an unreachable credential among them, a truncation appended when the log enforces its retention setting and naming the actor who authored the value, the value it enforced, the boundary it cut to, and the policy version and score version in force at the cut, because a log shortened for storage and a log cut to remove evidence otherwise read the same — and the two rows a decision is read against: the [policy version](what-the-factory-does.md#traceability) appended at each owner write with [_Factory_](how-humans-do-it/11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) as caller, and the score version appended by the score as its values move. Those two are in the chain because a decision names them by identifier and nothing else: a version rewritten in place would change what every decision naming it is read against while the log verified clean, the decision's own row untouched and only its meaning moved, and a value the score learned from outcomes exists nowhere but the row that carries it, so there is nothing outside the chain to check a rewrite against. Chained, a rewrite of either is the rewrite the drift detector's check exists to catch. A decision names the policy version and score version it was decided under, and a truncation names the pair in force at the cut; the page event and the wait name neither, not being decisions. What the two cost is a chained row per authored write and per supplied value that moved, most differing in one number, and a retention shorter than a version's life: the cut removes a version a decision still names, and the truncation row naming what was in force at the cut keeps that decision readable — a decision the cut removed reads as the rest of what it removed. What that costs is that every reader of the trail filters on which shape it has, and the score is told to ignore three of them the way it already ignores a [hold](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/04-what-a-gate-may-change.md) — and, within the shape it does read, to join the two rows, since the [factor vector](how-humans-do-it/04-risk-score/01-factors-at-least.md) it learns against is on one and the outcome it learns from is on the other. How long the log is kept is a setting an owner authors, stated with the rest of what they author in [_Gate policy_](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md#what-is-not-in-it); enforcing it removes the oldest records and never the head, so the detector's check reads through a truncation undisturbed — unless the cut reached past the head the detector recorded, retention shorter than the gap since its last pass, which reads as a rewrite until a human weighs the truncation row.
3. **Secrets by reference.** [Artifacts](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) and [specs](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec.md) contain names, never values — they get copied, diffed, and given to agents. So does every credential the factory records: the one an agent reaches its own model through, [_The fleet_](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md) using this seam before any artifact does, and the one on an [_environment record_](how-humans-do-it/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) that seam 4 resolves at every deploy. One resolver answers both, and it can be a file on disk today. What that costs is that nothing separates the two: whoever can read the resolver reaches the models and the deploy targets alike, so until policy attaches at seam 4 this separates storage and not reach.
4. **A named seam between the deployer and deploy targets.** However it is implemented, the [deployer](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-health-monitor) — the component that performs every deploy — reaches a [deploy target](how-humans-do-it/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) through a small set of named operations, and no agent reaches one at all: deploying is not a [stage](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) an agent is dispatched to, so the actor on a [deploy record](how-humans-do-it/06-releases/05-the-deploy-record.md) is the deployer and never an agent — a program the install runs and can check, where which model an agent is is only what its [fleet entry](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md) says. That seam is where policy attaches later; without it, production access is spread through the codebase. What it attaches to on the other side is the deployer's own scope and the credential on the [_environment record_](how-humans-do-it/05-environments/01-records-and-one-long-lived-branch.md) it resolves, declared and enforced by nothing until this seam is built. What that costs is that an agent's [_scope_](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) says nothing about a deploy target: where a deploy may go is authored on the environment, never on a fleet entry.
5. **A principal on every call.** Seam 1 puts an actor on a record; this puts one on the call that reaches a seam, carried from the entrance to whatever the call reaches. A human at a [screen](how-humans-do-it/11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) calls as the [_People_](how-humans-do-it/11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) row they say they are; an agent calls as its [fleet entry](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md) plus the [dispatch](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items/05-dispatch.md) that put it on the [item](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md), so the [_scope_](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) it was dispatched under travels with every call it makes; a component calls as itself. The resolver of seam 3 and the operations of seam 4 take the principal on every call and record it beside what was asked for, deciding nothing on it — populated, self-asserted, enforced by nothing, the treatment seam 1 gets. It is a seam because the other four have nothing to test without it: the resolver is addressed by credential name, so an agent at the [Spec](how-humans-do-it/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec.md) stage in one [project](how-humans-do-it/11-screens.md#work-ops-factory-people) that names another project's production deploy credential is answered, there being no caller for a policy to read; and the actor seam 1 records is whatever the caller supplied, the same absence one record later. Adding the caller at the retrofit means touching every call site, the spread through the codebase seam 4 was declared to avoid, moved one seam over. With the principal on the call, verification attaches once, where the call enters the factory — a human authenticated at a screen, an agent's dispatch checked against its scope — and every call and record downstream inherits it, so seam 1's field becomes verifiable without a second retrofit. What it costs is a field threaded through every call the factory makes, and what it buys until enforcement is the shape and not the security: a record written before the retrofit still holds a name the product accepted without proof, and the seam bounds that to before rather than forever.

[One pipeline](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) is not a sixth seam — it is the architecture the five attach to, chosen for coherence rather than safety: a single path is a single place to put policy. What makes that work is that nothing routes around it, including incidents — the emergency action [_One pipeline_](how-humans-do-it/01-one-pipeline.md) allows is approve now, not skip, so a rushed yes is a yes with an actor on it and not a gap in the record. That is where most single-path designs fail.

The drawback is that a single path is a single point of failure. Almost nothing outside the pipeline disagrees with the pipeline, so a subverted one ships a change with a valid approval on it. What replaces the second path is mostly downstream of the deploy and reads what the factory wrote: the log for detection, the actor for attribution, and a human undoing a change after it shipped (10) — inside the [_analysis window_](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#the-analysis-window) — for the undo. [_Drift detection_](how-humans-do-it/08-operations.md#drift-detection) is the one thing that disagrees from outside, over two facts — what is actually running against the record naming it, and the log's chain against the head it recorded — the whole of what a separate second path is worth.
