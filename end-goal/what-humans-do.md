# What humans do

Non-exhaustive owner's list. The factory does everything else. It is a list of duties, not of people: an owner may hold all twelve or delegate any of them, and [_People_](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) is where each attaches to a named human — a designer holding (2), (6), and (11), a compliance officer holding (2) and a safeguard (9) over a regulated area. The numbering is how the rest of the document refers to them: a bare `(7)` or `(11, 12)` anywhere in it is a duty from the list below, and nothing else is written that way. Whoever holds a duty gets its gate rows and its user acceptance testing assignments (7) in their own view of [_Work_](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md).

Writing that record is the owner's and not a duty of its own: it distributes the twelve, and a duty over it would be one of the twelve deciding who holds the twelve. Nothing enforces it, and nothing has to — a [_page_](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/07-pages.md) or a gate row with no holder recorded widens to the owner, the person who would have written the row. That write is the residue segregation leaves: the rule in [_Where a gate is, and what decides it_](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md) stops a human approving a version they authored, and no rule reads the write that decided who holds a row's duty. The party who assigns every approver is not separated from approving. What bounds that is the chain: each such write appends a [policy version](how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) naming who holds what, so the roster in force at any row is read from the log rather than from today's declaration, and a holder removed to approve one row and restored after leaves both versions around the close.

What falls outside is substrate rather than work, and the design names three: hosting the factory, installing [_Drift detection_](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/08-drift-detection.md) beside it, and composing [_The fleet_](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/README.md) — the entries the factory dispatches against and the credentials they run on. The third covers composing an entry as well as lending a credential, because writing a model, an [effort](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/01-what-an-agent-runs-on.md), a [role](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md), and a [scope](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md) onto an entry is a write the factory never makes and no more one of the twelve than supplying the credential is. It also covers reading a [_fleet proposal_](how-the-factory-works/10-fleet/07-a-fleet-proposal.md) and giving it a disposition — the same obligation from the other side: the factory argues for a fleet, the owner decides, and deciding is substrate because writing is. What that leaves is a factory that chooses and a human who consents rather than chose — the word in this sentence used to be *choosing*, narrowed to *writing* when the factory was given something to argue with. What it costs is that an owner accepting a proposal they cannot check has composed a fleet in name and not in fact. None of the three is something a human does with an [item](how-the-factory-works/01-one-pipeline.md), a rule, or a running system, which is what the twelve are. Each is still an obligation, and nothing here scores one — least of all a proposal, which waits at [_Factory_](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md) with no [page](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/07-pages.md) and no number saying how long it has waited. Upgrading the factory is part of hosting it, not a fourth: a self-hosted product is brought up to date by whoever runs it — the same person, the same kind of obligation.

**Originate [intent](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/README.md)** — the factory cannot know what is wanted until told:

1. Request features.
2. Supply constraints. A constraint names what it binds — the factory, a [project](how-the-factory-works/11-screens/01-work-ops-factory-people.md), an area, or one intent — and binds every item inside that from then on. A constraint with one of the three widest reaches is permanent like the rules (8): the factory works inside it and never gets out. Laws and regulations are permanent constraints; so is a **design system** — the reusable decisions every screen is built from, its tokens, its components and the states each may be in, and the rules for using them, neither artwork nor the tool the artwork is drawn in — and an owner supplies one per project or picks from the ones the factory ships with. The narrowest reach is one intent: the constraint arrives with a single request and binds every item [decomposed](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/03-decomposition/README.md) from it, a screen design among them — every item, because one request yielding four binds all four. Nothing removes a constraint on its own: it binds until withdrawn, so an owner who never withdraws one has the factory building against it years later. What a constraint may carry beyond its reach — a date it binds from, a date it is to be reviewed by, and a period the factory's own conduct is bound to once a trigger occurs, the shape a law takes — is set out in [_Constraints and the design system_](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/01-intake/01-constraints-and-the-design-system.md).
3. Answer the factory's [interview](how-the-factory-works/02-intent-into-items/02-the-interview.md), for as many rounds as it asks, until the intent is refined — and one round more once every item has shipped, saying whether what shipped is what was asked for.

**Feed back as end users** — routine, in end-user terms, not engineering terms:

4. Report bugs.
5. Complain ("this button is too slow").

**Verify against intent** — at a [gate](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/01-where-a-gate-is-and-what-decides-it.md), so as often as the [score](how-the-factory-works/04-risk-score/README.md) or a safeguard (9) says:

6. Confirm the [acceptance criteria](how-the-factory-works/03-gates/07-what-particular-gates-decide/02-spec/README.md) are the right ones. Unit tests are today's encoding of them; what a human is checking is the criteria, not the test code.
7. Perform user acceptance testing (UAT).

**Set the rules** — permanent, not shrinking:

8. Author gate policy and risk thresholds. The duty is one line here and eleven parameters in [_Gate policy_](how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/README.md), which is the set. None has to be authored for the factory to run: what an owner leaves alone, the score supplies.
9. Add a safeguard — a gate held always-on, or a parameter narrowed — on any subject [_A safeguard is a record_](how-the-factory-works/09-gate-policy/02-one-shape-across-all-of-them.md) names — a stage, a service, a project, an area, a contract element, gate policy's own list, or the drift detector's last check. The subject follows the mechanism the safeguard binds, which is why a service is among them: the [window limit](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) and the [analysis window](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/02-the-analysis-window.md)'s parameters are per service, and so is an explicit [health threshold](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/01-the-health-monitor.md). An **area** is an owner-declared grouping of the software — coarser than a file, free to cut across services — and names "the payments path" wherever a safeguard, a score factor, or a gate an owner added back has to be narrower than a project. An area lies inside one area or one project, so declared areas form a chain rooted at a project, and a safeguard drawn anywhere on that chain reaches the work below it. The cost is that an owner cannot narrow their way out of a safeguard: declaring a finer area never reduces human work.
10. Undo a change after it shipped — one the factory auto-approved: a [rollback](how-the-factory-works/06-releases/06-rollback.md) while the [build it would return to](how-the-factory-works/08-operations/03-overlapping-windows.md) is still running, a revert after.

**Backstop the factory** — only where it falls short, shrinking as it improves:

11. Help author a spec or an implementation plan the factory cannot produce properly on its own — up to writing it together with the factory.
12. Take over issues the factory cannot fix on its own.
