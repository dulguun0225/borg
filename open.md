# Open

**Is a spend ceiling a ninth gate policy parameter, or are the two bounds the whole answer?** Two rows of [_Gate policy_](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md#what-it-holds) state their cost in spend — [_the attempt bound_](how-humans-do-it/03-gates.md#the-attempt-bound) burns it before anyone sees the item, and [_the interview_](how-humans-do-it/02-intent-into-items.md#the-interview)'s round bound spends round after round on an intent that never converges — and Factory reads cost per feature off what was spent. Nothing caps it. Those two bounds are what stand between a circling item and unbounded cost, and neither is named as that.

What turns on it is whether a ceiling can satisfy the three claims [_One shape across all of them_](how-humans-do-it/09-gate-policy.md#one-shape-across-all-of-them) makes of every parameter, because a row that fails one of them does not fit. The score supplies what an owner does not, and what outcome would teach a default here is not obvious: the score learns from vetoes and rollbacks, and a ceiling's evidence is cost per feature. A pin can only add, which for a ceiling means a pinned one may only be lower. Scope follows the mechanism, and the mechanism that consumes spend is an agent running — so the candidates are a [_fleet entry_](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md), a stage, or an area, and each bounds a different thing: an entry bounds one credential's spend however many items it takes, a stage bounds one stretch of work across every agent that attempts it, and an area bounds a customer's exposure where they care about it.

Either answer costs something. A ceiling that stops an agent part-way is the shape [_An account that runs out is a hold_](how-humans-do-it/10-fleet.md#an-account-that-runs-out-is-a-hold) already describes — a hold, burning nothing against the attempt bound and teaching the prior nothing — so the mechanism is built and the question is whether the factory should be the second thing reaching for it, next to a provider quota it neither sets nor reads. Deciding against a ceiling costs saying so where the spend is priced, so that a reader meeting "burns spend" is told what that spend runs into and that nothing else is coming.
